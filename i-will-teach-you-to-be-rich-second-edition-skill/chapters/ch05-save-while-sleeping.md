# Chapter 5: Save While Sleeping

## Core Idea
Good intentions decay; defaults persist. Sethi frames automation as a one-time design task that removes repeated decisions from the moments when people are busy, tempted, or anxious. The Automatic Money Flow routes income through checking to retirement, savings goals, bills, and credit-card payoff according to a tested schedule. The setup should include a cash buffer, linked accounts, and due-date alignment so automation does not create overdrafts or failed transfers. Link accounts, align due dates, and schedule the transfers and payments that make saving, investing, bills, and debt payoff happen before discretionary money is available.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Power of Defaults**: Configure the desired behavior once so it happens without a fresh decision. Use this when a goal matters but gets lost in busy weeks.
- **Automatic Money Flow**: Paycheck enters the system, retirement is captured, checking filters money to savings and investments, bills are paid, and the card balance is cleared. Use this as the operating system for personal finance.
- **90-minute maintenance model**: After setup, review statements and unusual transactions periodically rather than manually directing every dollar.
- **Buffer for irregular income**: Calculate bare-bones monthly needs, build roughly three months of that amount before aggressive investing, and use the buffer to smooth good and bad months.

## Key Concepts
- **Pay yourself first**: Move savings and investing before discretionary spending can consume the paycheck.
- **Checking buffer**: A temporary cushion that protects against timing errors and failed transfers.
- **Bill-date alignment**: Move due dates or schedule transfers so the system has predictable timing.
- **Irregular-income buffer**: Savings that converts volatile income into a stable monthly spending allowance.
- **Review loop**: Automated does not mean unattended; inspect bills, fees, errors, and unusual spending.

## Mental Models
- Use automation to move decisions from emotionally “hot” moments to a calm setup session.
- Think of checking as a routing hub, not a permanent storage location.
- Design for the future self who is distracted, busy, or less motivated.

## Anti-patterns
- **Automating without a cash buffer**: A timing mismatch can cause overdrafts or failed payments.
- **Setting transfers and never reviewing statements**: Errors and fraud can remain hidden.
- **Using a fixed-income schedule for volatile income**: Freelancers need a reserve and tax set-aside first.
- **Saving only what is left at month-end**: The system makes “nothing left” the default.

## Worked Example
For a monthly paycheck, the book’s representative flow is: salary and 401(k) first; a few days later, transfers to named savings goals and a Roth IRA; then bills and the card payment. The dates are intentionally staggered so a late paycheck can be corrected before a transfer posts. Sethi also emphasizes that the first cycles should be watched for errors, because “automatic” describes the intended behavior, not immunity from a bad account number or an insufficient buffer. Once the flow works, maintenance can shrink to periodic statement review and adjustment when income or goals change. The exact dates should be adapted to the user’s pay cycle and due dates.

## Key Takeaways
1. Link every account needed for the flow.
2. Automate savings, investing, bills, and card payments in a safe order.
3. Leave a buffer and review the first few cycles closely.
4. Once targets are met, spend the permitted remainder on the Rich Life.
