# Glossary

**85 Percent Solution** — Act on a sound-enough plan, then improve it after it is running (Ch 00, Ch 03, Ch 07).

**Automatic Money Flow** — A scheduled route from income to retirement, checking, savings goals, bills, card payoff, and guilt-free spending (Ch 05).

**Asset allocation** — The chosen mix of stocks, bonds, cash, and other asset classes in a portfolio (Ch 07, Ch 08).

**Big Wins** — A small number of actions with disproportionate financial impact, such as automation, investing early, fee reduction, debt payoff, and negotiation (Ch 00, Ch 04).

**Conscious Spending Plan** — A four-bucket allocation for fixed costs, investments, savings goals, and guilt-free spending (Ch 04, Ch 05).

**Credit utilization** — Revolving debt relative to available credit; a factor in credit scoring (Ch 01).

**Dollar-cost averaging** — Investing equal amounts at regular intervals; useful for recurring income and behavior, though not always optimal for a lump sum (Ch 07).

**Expense ratio** — An annual fund operating cost deducted from assets (Ch 06, Ch 07).

**FIRE** — Financial Independence, Retire Early; a higher-savings route to reaching a point where investments can fund life (Ch 00, Ch 07, Ch 08).

**Guilt-free spending** — Planned discretionary money that can be spent without guilt after goals are funded (Ch 04, Ch 05).

**HSA** — Health Savings Account; potentially tax-advantaged account for eligible people with qualifying health coverage (Ch 03).

**Invisible money script** — A learned belief about money that influences decisions outside awareness (Ch 00, Ch 01, Ch 03, Ch 05, Ch 06).

**Ladder of Personal Finance** — Priority sequence from employer match and expensive debt through retirement accounts, HSA, and additional investing (Ch 03).

**Marginal tax bracket** — The tax rate applied to the next slice of income, rather than the entire income (Ch 08).

**Rebalancing** — Restoring a portfolio to its target asset mix after market movement (Ch 07, Ch 08).

**Rich Life** — A personally defined life of choices, experiences, relationships, time, and security funded by money (Ch 00, Ch 08, Ch 09).

**Roth IRA** — Retirement account funded with after-tax money and governed by current contribution and withdrawal rules (Ch 03).

**Survivorship bias** — Mistaking the performance of survivors for the performance of the full original group (Ch 06).

**Target-date fund** — Diversified fund that changes allocation over time and generally rebalances automatically (Ch 07, Ch 08).

**Time in the market** — Staying invested for the long term rather than trying to predict short-term price changes (Ch 06, Ch 07).

**Total compensation** — Salary plus bonus, equity, benefits, flexibility, title, leave, and other employment terms (Ch 09).

**Value-based spending** — Paying for what matters and cutting what does not, rather than applying the lowest price to everything (Ch 04).
