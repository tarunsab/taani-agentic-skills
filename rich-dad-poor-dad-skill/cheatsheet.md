# Rich Dad Poor Dad — Cheatsheet

## Start with the cash-flow question

For the decision in front of you, ask:

1. Does this reliably put money in my pocket, take money out, or do both at different times?
2. What income will pay for it: earned, portfolio, or passive?
3. What costs, taxes, debt service, maintenance, and time does it require?
4. What would make the decision fail, and how would I limit the downside?
5. What skill or evidence do I still need before committing?

## The core map

| Question | Read it through |
|---|---|
| How do I stop depending only on wages? | Build an asset column while preserving necessary earned income. |
| How do I know whether a purchase helps? | Follow the cash flow, not the label. |
| Why does a raise disappear? | Expenses and liabilities may be expanding with income. |
| What should I learn next? | Accounting, investing, markets, law, sales, or systems. |
| Why am I not acting? | Diagnose fear, cynicism, avoidance, habit, or arrogance. |
| Is this opportunity real? | Verify the numbers, the market, the people, the downside, and the exit. |

## Three income types

- **Earned income**: money received for work or services.
- **Portfolio income**: money associated with paper assets such as stocks and bonds.
- **Passive income**: money associated in the book with assets such as income-producing real estate or businesses.

The book's direction is to learn how to convert earned income into portfolio and passive income. That is a conceptual model, not a guarantee that passive income is effortless or risk-free.

## Skill audit

Rate current confidence from 1–5, then choose the weakest skill that blocks the next decision:

- Reading income statements and balance sheets
- Understanding cash flow and debt service
- Evaluating investments and markets
- Understanding applicable law and tax advice
- Communicating and selling
- Negotiating
- Managing cash flow, systems, and people

## Start-small action

Choose one action that creates evidence without creating an unbounded commitment:

- Read and reconcile your own cash-flow statement.
- List every liability and its monthly outflow.
- Open or review the account that will hold a future asset purchase.
- Interview a qualified practitioner about a skill or market you do not understand.
- Analyze one opportunity using the asset-column worksheet.
- Make one offer only after defining your maximum downside and exit conditions.

## Safety check

The book favors action and financial intelligence, but action without verification is not financial intelligence. Before using leverage, a corporation, a tax strategy, or a concentrated investment, check current rules and obtain qualified advice. Preserve liquidity for obligations you cannot avoid, and do not treat a book's anecdote as a forecast for your circumstances.
