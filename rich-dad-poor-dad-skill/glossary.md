# Rich Dad Poor Dad — Glossary

## Asset

In the book's practical cash-flow definition, something that puts money into a person's pocket. The classification depends on the actual income and expenses associated with ownership, not only on the object's market label.

## Asset column

The part of a personal balance sheet that holds things intended to produce income, appreciate, or have a ready market. The book's instruction to “mind your own business” means building this column even while working for an employer.

## Balance sheet

A statement showing assets and liabilities. In the book's teaching approach, it helps reveal whether a person's apparent wealth is supported by productive assets or offset by obligations.

## Cash flow

The movement of money into and out of a person, household, or business. Kiyosaki uses cash-flow patterns to make financial behavior visible and to distinguish assets from liabilities.

## Corporation

A legal and organizational structure discussed as a way to conduct business, handle expenses, and manage liability and tax questions. Its use depends on current law and professional advice; it is not automatically beneficial.

## Earned income

Income received for work or services. The book contrasts it with portfolio and passive income and argues that relying only on earned income can keep a person tied to continued labor.

## Financial intelligence

The practical ability to understand numbers, evaluate opportunities, and use accounting, investing, markets, and law to make decisions. It is presented as learnable rather than as a fixed trait.

## Liability

In the book's practical definition, something that takes money out of a person's pocket. A purchase can be desirable and still be a liability if its ongoing cash flow is negative.

## Passive income

Income associated in the book with assets such as real estate or businesses. “Passive” does not mean free of management, risk, tax, or effort; investigate the actual arrangement.

## Portfolio income

Income associated with paper assets such as stocks and bonds. The book treats it as one route for converting earned income into money generated by assets.

## Rat Race

A recurring pattern in which work produces income, income is absorbed by taxes and expenses, and the need for the next paycheck remains. The book uses the phrase to describe dependence on a wage-and-expense loop.

## Work to learn

A career filter that values the skills, exposure, and feedback a role develops in addition to its immediate pay. The book emphasizes broad business skills such as selling, communication, accounting, and systems.

## Financial freedom

The book's desired state in which passive and portfolio income can cover living needs, making work a choice rather than the sole condition of survival. The amount and conditions required are personal and must be calculated, not assumed.
