# The Compounding Habit Lifecycle

A reference guide for managing the complete lifecycle of a habit experiment from initial observation to graduation and stacking.

## 1. The Observation Phase
Before designing new habits, gather empirical data on what is currently happening.
- **The Scorecard**: Record daily routines in `Current Habits.md`.
- **Non-Judgmental Framing**: Label behaviors as `helpful`, `neutral`, or `harmful` relative to current long-term goals. Every habit serves an immediate need; identifying that need is the first step toward behavior change.

## 2. The Diagnosis Phase
When an agent or user reviews the baseline, examine the four stages of the habit loop:
1. **Cue**: Is the trigger obvious and unambiguous?
2. **Craving**: What anticipated feeling or state does the brain expect?
3. **Response**: How much physical and cognitive friction stands between cue and action?
4. **Reward**: Does the action deliver an immediate satisfying payoff?

## 3. Experiment Design Guidelines
Every proposed experiment must satisfy:
- **Identity Vote**: State who the user is becoming, not just what they want to achieve.
- **The Two-Minute Rule**: Define the smallest possible version that keeps the habit alive on low-energy days.
- **Habit Stacking**: Attach the new habit to an established anchor: `After [Current Habit], I will [New Habit]`.
- **Environment Prime**: Prepare the physical space so the desired action has the least friction.
- **Recovery Protocol**: If a day is missed, return to the routine at the next available opportunity. Never miss twice.

## 4. Graduation and Stacking
A habit is considered graduated when:
- It has been practiced consistently across a minimum of 21–60 days.
- The behavior requires minimal cognitive overhead or willpower to initiate.
- The review confirms `phase: completed` and `result: successful`.

Once graduated:
1. It is recorded in `Current Habits.md` as an established routine.
2. It becomes available as an anchor cue for future habit stacks.
3. The next experiment can be designed without increasing overall daily willpower expenditure.
