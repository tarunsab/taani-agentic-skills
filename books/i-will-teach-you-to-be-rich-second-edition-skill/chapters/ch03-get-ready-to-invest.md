# Chapter 3: Get Ready to Invest

## Core Idea
Investing is not a lottery ticket or an expert-only activity. Sethi separates account selection from investment selection so the first task is simply to create the containers that will receive future contributions. The Ladder of Personal Finance prioritizes the employer match, expensive credit-card debt, a Roth IRA, additional 401(k) contributions, an eligible HSA, and then taxable investing. The sequence prevents a person from spending weeks comparing funds while missing an immediate match or allowing high-interest debt to grow. Open the right accounts, capture available tax advantages and employer matching, and climb a simple priority ladder while keeping high-interest debt under control.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Ladder of Personal Finance**: First capture the employer 401(k) match; then pay off high-interest credit-card debt; fund a Roth IRA; increase the 401(k) toward its limit; consider an HSA if eligible; then use taxable or other accounts. Use this to decide what to fund next.
- **85 Percent Solution for account selection**: Pick a reputable low-cost brokerage with sensible funds and move on. Use this when comparing providers is blocking the first contribution.
- **Tax-wrapper comparison**: A 401(k) can defer taxes, a Roth IRA uses after-tax contributions but shelters future qualified growth, and an HSA can provide multiple tax advantages when used correctly. Use this to compare account location before chasing investments.
- **Automation as commitment**: Set even a small recurring contribution so the account and habit exist. Increase it as cash flow improves.

## Key Concepts
- **401(k) match**: Employer contribution that should generally be captured before other discretionary investing.
- **Roth IRA**: Tax-advantaged account funded with after-tax money; qualified withdrawals of contributions/growth follow current rules.
- **HSA**: Account available only with qualifying health coverage; contributions, growth, and qualified medical spending can receive favorable tax treatment.
- **Robo-advisor**: Algorithmic investment service emphasizing convenience; compare its ongoing fee and features with direct low-cost providers.
- **Contribution minimum**: A small automatic deposit can satisfy an account minimum and establish the habit.

## Mental Models
- Use the **ladder** to reduce choice overload: fund the next high-value rung, not every account simultaneously.
- Treat account type and investment choice as separate decisions: first place money in an appropriate wrapper, then choose a fund.
- When debt and investing compete, compare the debt rate, risk, tax benefits, and the cost of delaying compounding; a hybrid can be rational for student loans.

## Anti-patterns
- **Waiting for a large starting balance**: Delays learning and compounding when a small deposit would start both.
- **Investing aggressively while revolving card debt grows**: Guaranteed high interest can overwhelm uncertain market returns.
- **Choosing by interface alone**: A polished app does not justify a materially higher ongoing fee.
- **Treating savings as long-term investing**: Cash stability and growth investments solve different problems.

## Worked Example
The ladder begins with an employer match because it is an immediate return, then turns to expensive revolving debt, then to tax-advantaged accounts. Sethi repeatedly distinguishes opening and funding an account from choosing the actual fund, so a new investor can make progress without solving every investment question on day one. Once the first three rungs are in place, an eligible investor can evaluate an HSA as an investment vehicle for unused medical dollars. The sequence prevents a person from optimizing a sophisticated account while missing a simpler, higher-return action, and it makes the next decision visible when money is limited.

## Key Takeaways
1. Open the 401(k), identify the match, and contribute enough to capture it.
2. Make an explicit plan for high-interest debt rather than ignoring it.
3. Open a Roth IRA and automate even a modest contribution.
4. Evaluate HSA eligibility only after the earlier rungs are handled.
