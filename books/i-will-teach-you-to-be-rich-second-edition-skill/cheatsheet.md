# Decision Cheatsheet

## If/then rules

| Situation | Do this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| You are researching endlessly | Apply the 85 Percent Solution | Action starts learning and compounding |
| You carry card debt | Pay minimums everywhere, then attack one balance with a fixed extra payment | Prevents drift and directs effort |
| A bank or card charges a fee | Call and ask for a waiver or lower rate | High upside for minutes of work |
| You have an employer match | Contribute enough to capture it | Immediate compensation |
| You have expensive revolving debt | Prioritize payoff before aggressive investing | The interest cost is high and certain |
| You do not know where money goes | Build four buckets and name savings goals | Makes trade-offs visible without permanent penny tracking |
| Saving depends on willpower | Automate transfers after payday | Defaults beat motivation |
| You want a simple portfolio | Use a low-cost target-date fund | Diversification and rebalancing are bundled |
| You want more control | Use a small set of broad index funds | Allocation, not stock picking, drives the structure |
| A fund or adviser boasts recent results | Check long-term, net-of-fee performance and survivorship | Recent winners are weak evidence |
| An asset class grows above target | Redirect new contributions to lagging areas | Rebalances without unnecessary selling |
| Market headlines are frightening | Stay with the long-term plan unless your goal changed | Timing is unreliable and best days can be missed |
| A purchase is predictable | Estimate full cost, divide by months, automate a sinking fund | Time creates options and prevents debt |
| A partner’s spending causes conflict | Point to the shared goal and plan, not the person | Reduces defensiveness |
| A salary offer arrives | Research, show value, negotiate total compensation, and practice | Preparation creates leverage |
| You want to buy a home | Compare total ownership cost with renting and opportunity cost | Sticker price is incomplete |
| You are saving more but feel no purpose | Translate “freedom” into a street-level outcome | Specific motivation supports sustained action |

## Starting allocation guide

- Fixed costs: roughly 50–60% of take-home pay.
- Investments: roughly 10%.
- Savings goals: roughly 5–10%.
- Guilt-free spending: roughly 20–35%.

Treat these as diagnostic ranges. If fixed costs dominate, look for a structural Big Win or earn more; do not punish yourself for failing an unrealistic spreadsheet.

## Ladder quick order

1. Employer match.
2. High-interest card debt.
3. Roth IRA.
4. More 401(k).
5. Eligible HSA after earlier priorities.
6. Taxable and other investing.

Keep an appropriate cash buffer and verify current tax rules, contribution limits, and withdrawal rules.

## Fast smells

- You know the exact savings-account rate but not your monthly investing contribution.
- You are comparing funds by last year’s return.
- You pay only the minimum because it is the required amount.
- A “simple budget” has so many categories that you stopped using it.
- A financial product is complex, expensive, and justified mainly by a polished interface.
- You call a home an investment without counting maintenance, taxes, insurance, repairs, and opportunity cost.
- You are proud of saving more but cannot name what the money is for.
