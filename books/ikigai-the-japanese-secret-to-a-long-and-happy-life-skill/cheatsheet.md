# Ikigai — Cheatsheet

## Find the signal

| Ask | Look for |
|---|---|
| What makes the day worth beginning? | A purpose, role, craft, relationship, or contribution. |
| When do I lose track of time? | Activities that create flow and reveal possible ikigai. |
| What makes the practice sustainable? | Ritual, appropriate challenge, social support, movement, and rest. |
| What makes life fragile? | One source of income, one relationship, one identity, or one coping habit. |

## Flow decision rule

1. **Too easy / boredom** → add a small challenge or new constraint.
2. **Appropriately difficult** → define a concrete task, remove distractions, and continue.
3. **Too difficult / anxiety** → reduce the task or build the missing skill.
4. **Vague goal** → replace it with a process and a finish line for today.

## Daily design

- Keep one meaningful activity active even when formal work ends.
- Choose a modest form of movement that can be repeated most days.
- Use social ties as infrastructure, not as an emergency repair.
- Eat with moderation cues such as smaller portions or stopping before overfull; adapt for health needs.
- Spend some time learning, interacting, or doing something novel so the mind does not run only on automatic pilot.
- Protect a distraction-free block for the activity most connected to purpose.

## Resilience check

| If you notice | Ask |
|---|---|
| Rumination about the past or future | What is controllable in this moment? |
| Perfectionism | What is beautiful or useful in the incomplete version? |
| One-point failure | What redundancy or second support can I cultivate? |
| Fear of every risk | Can I make a small, bounded experiment? |
| A draining obligation | Is it a chosen value or a fragility I should remove? |

## Ten-rule quick reference

Stay active; slow down; do not overfill; keep good friends; move; smile; reconnect with nature; give thanks; live in the moment; follow your ikigai.
