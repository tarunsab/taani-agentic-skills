# Glossary

**Accountability** — Naming the problem plainly and accepting responsibility for the response (Ch 1).

**Action before feeling** — Taking a reasoned first step before motivation or comfort arrives (Ch 2, Ch 4).

**Adjustment shock** — Anxiety, guilt, or hypervigilance triggered by a positive change that exceeds the familiar baseline (Ch 4).

**Antifragile mind** — The book’s model of a mind that can strengthen through proportionate, meaningful adversity (Ch 4).

**Baseline** — A familiar emotional or behavioral level to which the system tends to return (Ch 1, Ch 4).

**Cognitive distortion** — A biased or incomplete reasoning pattern that turns a stimulus into an unreliable conclusion (Ch 4, Ch 7).

**Comfort zone** — The range of experiences the body and mind recognize as safe and familiar (Ch 1, Ch 4).

**Core commitment** — The subconscious objective a person repeatedly protects through their behavior (Ch 2).

**Core need** — The underlying need hidden beneath a protective commitment, such as trust beneath control (Ch 2).

**Emotional backlog** — Unprocessed feelings that accumulate until they demand attention or release (Ch 5).

**Emotional intelligence** — Understanding, interpreting, and responding to emotions in a healthy way (Ch 4).

**Emotional validation** — Allowing a feeling to be human and understandable without agreeing with its interpretation (Ch 6).

**Extrapolation** — Projecting a current condition into the future as if it will define one’s entire life (Ch 4).

**False dichotomy** — Treating two extreme possibilities as the only available outcomes (Ch 4, Ch 7).

**Faulty inference** — Drawing a false conclusion from evidence that may itself be real (Ch 4).

**Fight-or-flight** — A defensive survival state that narrows attention toward threat and reduces higher-order functioning (Ch 5).

**Inner child** — A psychologically meaningful representation of an earlier self carrying old needs, fear, and adaptations (Ch 5, Ch 6).

**Inner peace** — A grounded, present-centered state to which one can return beneath mental turbulence (Ch 7).

**Instinct** — A quiet, present-focused response that moves one toward or away from what is actually happening (Ch 3).

**Logical lapse** — A break in reasoning where fear makes the imagined climax feel like the final conclusion (Ch 4).

**Microshift** — A tiny repeated change that gradually becomes a new pattern and comfort zone (Ch 4).

**Negative association** — A learned link between a desired outcome and danger, guilt, vulnerability, or rejection (Ch 1).

**Positive disintegration** — The breakdown of an old self-concept that creates the conditions for a more capable one (Ch 1, Ch 5).

**Presence** — Meeting the current day and actual experience instead of escaping into past or future stories (Ch 7).

**Principle** — A foundational cause-and-effect rule used to govern conduct over time (Ch 6).

**Psychic thinking** — Treating assumptions about other minds, alternate lives, or the future as knowable facts (Ch 4, Ch 7).

**Self-concept** — The accumulated idea of who one is, shaped by social input, experience, and confirmation bias (Ch 1).

**Self-mastery** — Responsibility for one’s interpretations, habits, emotional responses, and direction (Ch 7).

**Self-sabotage** — A pattern that obstructs a conscious aim while meeting an unconscious need (Ch 1, Ch 2).

**Suppression** — Unconscious denial of emotion that eventually disrupts behavior or erupts (Ch 7).

**Trigger** — An experience that activates unresolved pain, a limiting belief, or a neglected need (Ch 3).

**Upper limit** — A person’s tolerance for positive feeling, success, or unfamiliar improvement (Ch 1, Ch 2).

**Worry** — Rehearsal of feared possibilities used as a protective strategy but often experienced as preparation without a plan (Ch 3, Ch 4, Ch 7).
