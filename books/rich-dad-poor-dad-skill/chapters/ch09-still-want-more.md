# Lesson 9: Still Want More? Here Are Some To-Dos

## Train opportunity recognition

The chapter turns the earlier ideas into a list of practices: look for bargains, make offers, study changing neighborhoods or markets, shop for value during downturns, and look in places other buyers ignore. The common mechanism is repeated observation plus willingness to speak with people and test a possibility. A bargain alone is not enough; the book stresses that change can be what makes a bargain profitable. Treat each observation as a hypothesis that requires current data and due diligence.

## Think in structures and relationships

The suggestions include finding a buyer before a seller, using options or agreements, grouping purchasing power, thinking bigger about the whole opportunity, and learning from history. These are ways of changing the structure of a transaction rather than accepting the first affordable fragment. They also introduce legal, financing, and counterparty risks that the book's anecdotes do not fully model. Use a qualified professional before copying a structure involving contracts, leverage, ownership, or tax treatment.

## Action beats waiting

The chapter's final message is that action is required before feedback or financial reward can occur. The safe translation is not “act without thinking”; it is “move from passive reading to a bounded action that produces evidence.” Set a time, choose the smallest reversible step, define the stop condition, and record what you learn. Inaction is itself a choice, but action should be sized so that one mistake does not damage the user's financial base.
