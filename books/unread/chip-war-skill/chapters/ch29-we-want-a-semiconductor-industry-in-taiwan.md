# Chapter 29: “We Want a Semiconductor Industry in Taiwan”

## Core Idea

K. T. Li recruited Morris Chang to move Taiwan from low-margin assembly into advanced semiconductor fabrication. Chang’s radical idea was a pure foundry: manufacture chips designed by customers without competing with them. The Taiwanese state supplied 48 percent of TSMC’s startup capital, secured Philips as a technology partner, pressured local investors, and provided tax benefits. Chang then staffed the company with U.S.-trained engineers and made neutrality its value proposition to fabless designers. TSMC democratized chip authorship while concentrating the digital printing press: many designers could use the foundry, but the economics of yield and scale favored one dominant manufacturer.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Pure-play foundry**: fabricate customers’ designs while never designing competing chips.
  - When to use: when design complexity and fab cost are rising, and customers need a trusted manufacturing partner.
- **Democratized design, concentrated fabrication**: lower entry costs for designers while scale economies intensify manufacturing concentration.
  - When to use: when a platform separates creative work from capital-intensive production.

## Key Concepts

- **Morris Chang**: founder of TSMC and architect of the foundry model.
- **TSMC**: Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company.
- **Foundry**: neutral manufacturer of chips designed by other firms.
- **Fabless firm**: chip designer without its own fabrication plant.

## Mental Models

- Look for neutral infrastructure that lets competitors share a production layer.
- Separate the economics of design entry from the economics of manufacturing scale.
- Treat government finance as an architecture-shaping input, not just a subsidy.

## Anti-patterns

- **Making the manufacturer a customer’s competitor**: it discourages IP sharing and dependence.
- **Assuming privatization means independence from the state**: TSMC began as a Taiwanese state project.

## Worked Example

TSMC’s government provided 48 percent of startup capital, Philips invested $58 million and transferred technology, and the government solicited the remainder from Taiwanese business families. Chang promised not to design chips, giving fabless firms a reliable alternative to second-class capacity at integrated chip companies.

## Key Takeaways

1. Use neutrality to attract customers who would otherwise fear imitation.
2. Build scale where yield and capital costs compound.
3. Design industrial policy around a business model, not only a facility.
