# Chapter 34: Running Faster?

## Core Idea

Andy Grove warned that offshoring manufacturing could cause the United States to lose future industries, while many policymakers argued that America should simply “run faster” technologically. The case for globalization was strong: U.S. firms retained leadership in chip design software and much semiconductor equipment even as fabrication moved to Asia. But the strategy assumed U.S. leadership was guaranteed and treated dependence as harmless. A Defense Department study by Richard Van Atta warned that access to cutting-edge chips would soon depend on foreign countries. The chapter’s test is empirical, not ideological: globalization can improve efficiency while eroding the domestic capacity needed in a crisis.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Run faster**: preserve leadership by continuing to innovate rather than restricting trade or rebuilding every production step at home.
  - When to use: when a country retains a credible lead and dependencies are substitutable.
- **Offshoring lockout**: abandoning production in a “commodity” sector can remove the learning base for future industries.
  - When to use: when today’s manufacturing capability feeds tomorrow’s technology.

## Key Concepts

- **Offshoring**: relocating production abroad.
- **Van Atta study**: 2007 assessment of semiconductor globalization and defense dependence.
- **EDA firms**: U.S. companies dominating advanced chip-design software.
- **Validated end-user**: status that eased some export controls for SMIC.

## Mental Models

- Measure leadership by control of bottlenecks, not corporate profits alone.
- Ask what happens under a worst-case disruption, not only in peacetime.
- Distinguish efficient interdependence from resilient interdependence.

## Anti-patterns

- **Repeating “globalization” without mapping dependencies**.
- **Assuming current lead guarantees future lead**: the U.S. had already lost DRAM and lithography ground.

## Worked Example

The 1999 Taiwan earthquake took a week to restore most TSMC fabs and a month for consumer markets to normalize. Van Atta’s 2007 warning argued that the Pentagon would soon depend on foreign access to advanced chips, even though Washington believed the U.S. was running faster.

## Key Takeaways

1. Verify the “run faster” thesis against capability data.
2. Preserve learning in strategically adjacent manufacturing.
3. Model rare disruptions before declaring supply chains safe.
