# Chapter 27: War Hero

## Core Idea

The 1991 Persian Gulf War became the first major public demonstration of the semiconductor-enabled military transformation. Paveway bombs, Tomahawk missiles, stealth aircraft, sensors, and communications systems destroyed Iraqi targets with unprecedented precision and speed. The Paveway itself had evolved through repeated generations: fewer components, greater reliability, lower cost, and more features. Its success reflected the same logic as Intel’s microprocessors—widely understood, easy to use, and cheap enough for routine training. The victory validated Perry’s offset strategy and exposed the Soviet Union’s technological inferiority. “Silicon over steel” moved from a planning concept to visible battlefield reality.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Affordable precision**: a capability changes warfare when it is accurate, versatile, reliable, and cheap enough to use repeatedly.
  - When to use: when judging whether a demonstration weapon can scale operationally.
- **System validation**: a technology strategy is proven only when multiple components work together under real conditions.
  - When to use: when a lab or test range cannot reveal the value of the complete network.

## Key Concepts

- **Persian Gulf War**: conflict that publicly validated the offset.
- **Paveway**: laser-guided bomb whose design dated to Vietnam.
- **Tomahawk**: cruise missile using terrain and computing for guidance.
- **Precision munition**: weapon guided accurately to a target.

## Mental Models

- Use the “training affordability” test for adoption.
- Trace performance back through generations of components and manufacturing.
- Judge a military revolution by outcomes against a capable opponent.

## Anti-patterns

- **Confusing a spectacular demonstration with a new weapon class**: the result depended on decades of incremental improvements.
- **Assuming accuracy replaces strategy**: the war validated technology, not every political use of force.

## Worked Example

Two F-117s destroyed Baghdad’s telephone exchange with Paveway bombs while Tomahawks struck other targets. Air Force studies found laser-guided planes hit thirteen times as many targets as comparable planes without guided munitions.

## Key Takeaways

1. Scale precision through affordability and practice.
2. Evaluate networks of systems, not isolated devices.
3. Use real conflict to test whether technical advantage changes outcomes.
