# Chapter 43: “Call Forth the Assault”

## Core Idea

Xi Jinping’s martial call to “assault the fortifications” of core technology reflected China’s dependence on foreign chips, software, materials, and tools. China’s aggregate semiconductor market share was only about 6 percent across the supply chain, with less than 1 percent in design software and no leading-edge fabrication. Beijing responded with Made in China 2025 and the state-backed Big Fund, aiming to reduce imported production from 85 percent in 2015 to 30 percent by 2025. The policy mixed subsidies, acquisitions, talent recruitment, joint ventures, and pressure on local governments. It also carried a governance problem: provincial officials built redundant fabs, while politically connected firms often underperformed. China wanted technological independence, but the book argues that deep integration—not isolation—had enabled the earlier successes of Taiwan, Korea, Japan, and ASML.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Shock brigades and special forces**: concentrate state capital and organizational force on strategic technology bottlenecks.
  - When to use: when market forces alone cannot fund a capability with national-security value.
- **Subsidy-governance test**: judge industrial policy by technology, yield, customers, and learning—not money committed.
  - When to use: when a state is financing many competing projects.

## Key Concepts

- **Made in China 2025**: plan to reduce China’s dependence on imported chips.
- **Big Fund**: state-backed semiconductor investment vehicle.
- **SMIC**: China’s leading foundry, still behind TSMC.
- **Market share**: China’s small position across software, IP, tools, materials, design, and fabrication.

## Mental Models

- Map the whole supply chain before claiming national independence.
- Treat capital allocation and governance as separate from technical capability.
- Ask whether a policy integrates into an ecosystem or tries to replace it wholesale.

## Anti-patterns

- **Equating state funding with venture capital**: forced investment by state entities lacks Silicon Valley’s selection logic.
- **Building a fab in every province**: duplication destroys scale and learning.

## Worked Example

China’s Big Fund drew money from the Ministry of Finance, state banks, China Tobacco, and municipal vehicles. Some analysts called it venture capital, but the source notes that a cigarette company funding chips is structurally different from investors selecting firms for commercial returns.

## Key Takeaways

1. Target chokepoints, not slogans.
2. Make subsidies conditional on operational learning and customers.
3. Recognize that an anti-U.S. supply chain is harder than a larger role in the existing one.
