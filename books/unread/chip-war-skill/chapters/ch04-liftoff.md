# Chapter 4: Liftoff

## Core Idea

Early integrated circuits were too expensive and unfamiliar for ordinary consumers, so the U.S. military became the crucial first customer. Texas Instruments used the Minuteman II missile guidance computer to prove that chips could survive demanding conditions and deliver more computing power in less space. The Pentagon’s willingness to buy expensive early units gave producers revenue, engineering feedback, and a reason to improve reliability. At the same time, the space program created a public and technical showcase: Apollo’s guidance computer depended on integrated circuits even though the components were costly. This was a launch market, not a permanent business model; civilian scale would later be required to sustain Moore’s Law.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Launch customer**: a demanding early buyer absorbs high initial costs while helping a new technology mature.
  - When to use: when a new component is too expensive for mass markets but solves a mission-critical problem.
  - How: use the buyer’s requirements to harden the technology, then pursue higher-volume customers.
- **Military-civilian transition**: defense procurement can seed capabilities that later diffuse into consumer markets.
  - When to use: when public spending creates a technology whose eventual market is much larger than defense.

## Key Concepts

- **Minuteman II**: an early missile system using TI integrated circuits in its guidance computer.
- **Apollo guidance computer**: a compact computer whose chip-based design supported lunar missions.
- **Reliability**: the ability of a chip to operate under vibration, heat, and other stress.
- **Moore’s Law**: the expected cadence of increasing component density.

## Mental Models

- Ask “who can afford the prototype?” before asking who will buy the mature product.
- Treat a hard operating environment as a test platform for manufacturing quality.

## Anti-patterns

- **Assuming early defense demand is the final market**: military volume was insufficient to fund all later R&D.
- **Confusing visibility with causation**: Apollo publicized chips, but process improvements and production discipline made them cheaper.

## Worked Example

TI’s missile guidance work showed why integrated circuits were strategically useful: they reduced the size and weight of the computer while increasing reliability. Apollo then placed a chip-based guidance computer inside a spacecraft, forcing engineers to solve problems that consumer electronics would later inherit.

## Key Takeaways

1. Use a launch customer to bridge prototype economics.
2. Capture the reliability lessons from demanding environments.
3. Plan early for the larger market that must eventually carry the technology.
