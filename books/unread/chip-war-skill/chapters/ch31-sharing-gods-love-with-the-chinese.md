# Chapter 31: “Sharing God’s Love with the Chinese”

## Core Idea

Richard Chang founded SMIC in Shanghai hoping to bring world-class fabrication to China through his experience at Texas Instruments. China’s government supplied tax benefits and subsidies, while Chang recruited foreign-trained engineers, especially from Taiwan and the United States. This “one old staffer brings along two new ones” approach transferred practical knowledge faster than state-owned joint ventures that kept Chinese workers at basic tasks. SMIC reached within a few years of the frontier but struggled against TSMC’s scale, accumulated know-how, and customer trust. Samsung’s success meanwhile showed how sustained investment during downturns can win the DRAM game. The chapter asks whether globalization creates shared prosperity or gives China a path to acquire capabilities that may later challenge the system.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **One old staffer brings along two new ones**: use experienced foreign-trained workers to accelerate local capability.
  - When to use: when a new fab lacks tacit manufacturing knowledge.
- **Game of chicken in DRAM**: all firms must keep investing through overcapacity because stopping first sacrifices the next node.
  - When to use: when scale and continuous process improvement determine survival.

## Key Concepts

- **SMIC**: Semiconductor Manufacturing International Corporation.
- **Chartered Semiconductor**: Singaporean foundry that tried to follow TSMC.
- **Technology domestication**: converting imported expertise into local capability.
- **Fab capacity**: production volume available at a given process technology.

## Mental Models

- Separate technology transfer that trains local staff from transfer that merely locates a foreign fab.
- In capital races, ask who can keep spending when prices make investment painful.
- Treat subsidies as effective only when paired with operational learning.

## Anti-patterns

- **Counting a factory as an industry**: NEC’s Shanghai joint venture left Chinese workers with limited expertise.
- **Buying capacity without customers or yields**: money cannot by itself create a competitive foundry.

## Worked Example

SMIC raised more than $1.5 billion, hired hundreds of foreign engineers, and initially had 393 overseas engineers alongside 650 local engineers. By the end of the 2000s it was only a couple of years behind leading foundries, but it still lagged TSMC and depended on continuous government support.

## Key Takeaways

1. Transfer tacit knowledge through people and routines.
2. Pair subsidies with real production and customer feedback.
3. Expect scale incumbents to defend their lead through downturns.
