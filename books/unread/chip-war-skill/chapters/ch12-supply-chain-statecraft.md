# Chapter 12: Supply Chain Statecraft

## Core Idea

Texas Instruments’ decision to build assembly plants in Taiwan, Singapore, and elsewhere shows how semiconductor investment became foreign policy. K. T. Li recognized that Taiwan needed both economic growth and stronger ties to the United States; TI needed lower-cost labor and a secure regional base. The resulting factories created jobs, transferred know-how, and helped turn potential “dominoes” toward Communism into integrated U.S. allies. The same logic operated in Singapore, Malaysia, and other Asian economies, where assembly absorbed workers moving from farms to cities. Supply chains thus did more than reduce costs: they distributed dependence in ways that supported a political order.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Supply-chain statecraft**: use production networks to create economic interests that reinforce security relationships.
  - When to use: when alliance durability depends on employment, investment, and shared industrial capability.
  - How: place factories where they solve local economic problems while connecting them to the sponsor’s technology and customers.
- **Economic security link**: commercial presence can become an indirect security guarantee.
  - When to use: when formal military commitments are uncertain or politically costly.

## Key Concepts

- **K. T. Li**: Taiwanese economic official who used semiconductors to upgrade Taiwan.
- **Texas Instruments**: early U.S. firm that built major assembly capacity in Asia.
- **Domino theory**: U.S. fear that economic and political instability would spread Communist influence.
- **Technology transfer**: movement of skills and process knowledge through investment and employment.

## Mental Models

- Map factories alongside military bases to see the political geography of production.
- Ask what a country gains besides wages: skills, suppliers, customers, and security ties.

## Anti-patterns

- **Seeing offshoring as a one-way extraction**: host economies used factories to learn and move up the value chain.
- **Treating investment as separate from defense**: production ties influenced the credibility of political commitments.

## Worked Example

TI’s first Taiwan visit began badly, but Li later understood that a plant could create jobs, bring technical knowledge, and deepen U.S. links. TI approved the facility in 1968; it assembled its first devices in 1969 and shipped its billionth unit by 1980.

## Key Takeaways

1. Evaluate supply chains for their alliance effects, not only their labor savings.
2. Use foreign investment to build local capability deliberately.
3. Recognize that economic dependence can be a form of deterrence.
