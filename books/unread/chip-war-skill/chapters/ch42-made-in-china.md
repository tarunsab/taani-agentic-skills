# Chapter 42: Made in China

## Core Idea

Xi Jinping’s concern was not whether China had impressive internet companies but whether those companies rested on foreign hardware. China’s software, data centers, surveillance systems, and cloud services relied on imported chips, especially U.S.-designed processors made by allied fabs. Xi called this dependence a “vital gate” held by others and connected semiconductor capability to national rejuvenation and information security. The goal was to move from low-profit assembly toward “core technologies” that other countries could not live without. China studied the paths of Japan, Taiwan, and South Korea: subsidize capital, bring back overseas talent, require partnerships to transfer know-how, and exploit rivalry among foreign firms. The chapter sets up self-sufficiency as both an economic and security project.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Vital gate**: a core component can control a large downstream system even when the visible product is domestic.
  - When to use: when evaluating technological dependence beneath a successful platform.
- **Core technology upgrading**: move from assembly to irreplaceable design, IP, tools, and fabrication.
  - When to use: when a country captures labor income but not the largest share of value or leverage.

## Key Concepts

- **Chinese Dream**: Xi’s national rejuvenation program.
- **Core technology**: capability the rest of the economy cannot easily replace.
- **Vital gate**: supply-chain control held by external producers.
- **One Belt, One Road**: Chinese infrastructure and network expansion strategy.

## Mental Models

- Look below software platforms to the chips that execute them.
- Separate market size from control over the input that makes the market work.
- Use prior Asian industrialization strategies as a comparative playbook.

## Anti-patterns

- **Mistaking a domestic app ecosystem for technological independence**.
- **Focusing on assembly volume while importing the irreplaceable component**.

## Worked Example

China spent more money importing semiconductors than oil in many years, even while its internet firms and AI surveillance systems expanded. Most PCs paired Windows with Intel chips, making the foreign hardware dependency visible beneath domestic software.

## Key Takeaways

1. Identify the “vital gate” behind a successful digital sector.
2. Upgrade toward components that create leverage, not just jobs.
3. Treat talent and partnerships as part of industrial policy.

## Connects To

- **Ch 43**: Beijing organizes the response as an assault on core technology.
- **Ch 51**: the U.S. weaponizes the same gates against Huawei.
