# Chapter 10: “Transistor Girls”

## Core Idea

The chapter exposes the labor system behind falling chip prices: men disproportionately designed early chips, while women—many of them immigrants—assembled and tested them. Charlie Sporck brought factory discipline to Fairchild, treating productivity, weak unions, and stock options as mutually reinforcing tools. When California could not provide enough low-cost labor, Fairchild moved assembly to Hong Kong, then to Singapore, Malaysia, Taiwan, and elsewhere in Asia. Offshoring reduced costs and created jobs that helped stabilize America’s allies, but it also relied on gendered assumptions and low wages. The international division of labor became a core mechanism of Moore’s Law and of U.S. influence in Asia.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Assembly offshoring**: retain high-value wafer fabrication while moving labor-intensive packaging and testing to lower-cost locations.
  - When to use: when production can be modularized and labor cost dominates the step.
  - How: preserve process control, transfer wafers or components, and build local capacity around the assembly site.
- **Supply-chain statecraft**: make investment and employment support political alignment.
  - When to use: when allies need jobs and the sponsor needs durable regional ties.

## Key Concepts

- **Assembly and testing**: attaching, wiring, and checking chips after wafer fabrication.
- **Offshoring**: moving a production stage across borders to reduce cost or access labor.
- **Productivity**: output per worker or machine, central to cost decline.
- **Fairchild**: first major U.S. chip firm to offshore assembly in Asia.

## Mental Models

- Decompose a product into steps before deciding what “national production” means.
- Treat labor pools and political stability as linked supply-chain inputs.
- Ask who bears the social cost of a productivity strategy.

## Anti-patterns

- **Calling cost reduction purely technical**: labor relations, wages, gender assumptions, and migration shaped the result.
- **Assuming offshoring is politically neutral**: factories changed employment and alliance incentives.

## Worked Example

Fairchild opened a Hong Kong facility in a rented sandal factory and assembled 120 million devices in its first year. The company kept wafer production in California but used lower labor costs to hire engineers who could manage high-quality assembly, and other U.S. firms soon followed across Asia.

## Key Takeaways

1. Map labor and assembly into the technology story.
2. Offshoring can improve both cost and quality when it funds better supervision.
3. Treat employment effects as part of geopolitical strategy.
