# Chapter 50: Fujian Jinhua

## Core Idea

Fujian Jinhua tried to enter the DRAM market with more than $5 billion in Chinese government funding, but lacked the specialized know-how that takes decades to build. It partnered with Taiwan’s UMC, recruited Micron employees, and obtained confidential layouts, lithography-mask processes, testing information, and yield data. The alleged theft showed why process knowledge is more valuable than a finished chip, while Chinese court actions against Micron and Veeco showed how legal and political systems could reinforce the transfer. The Trump administration used export controls on U.S. equipment, coordinated with Japan, to cut Jinhua off from the tools it needed. Production halted within months. The case demonstrated that a concentrated tool oligopoly can impose a decisive choke on a state-backed competitor.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Know-how acquisition ladder**: license, recruit, poach, or steal the process knowledge needed to move from equipment ownership to reliable production.
  - When to use: when a late entrant has money and tools but lacks yields.
- **Equipment choke**: restrict access to a small set of irreplaceable fabrication tools.
  - When to use: when design or production equipment is concentrated among allied suppliers.

## Key Concepts

- **Fujian Jinhua**: Chinese DRAM project halted by U.S. export controls.
- **UMC**: Taiwanese foundry accused of helping transfer Micron DRAM know-how.
- **Yield data**: information about which process conditions produce working chips.
- **Equipment oligopoly**: a few firms controlling essential semiconductor machinery.

## Mental Models

- Value process files by the time and capital they replace.
- Track the tools required to turn stolen knowledge into commercial output.
- Coordinate controls across allied suppliers or the choke leaks around one country.

## Anti-patterns

- **Assuming patents capture the whole advantage**: tacit process and yield knowledge were central.
- **Using one-country controls in a multinational tool market**.

## Worked Example

Micron employee Kenny Wang downloaded 900 confidential files to Google Drive and later moved to UMC. After U.S. officials coordinated with Japan, Jinhua was barred from buying tools from Applied Materials, Lam Research, and KLA; its production soon stopped.

## Key Takeaways

1. Protect yield and process knowledge as strategic IP.
2. Identify the equipment that makes a capability executable.
3. Use allied coordination to make export controls effective.
