# Chapter 41: How Intel Forgot Innovation

## Core Idea

Intel entered the 2010s with unmatched profits, R&D resources, x86 dominance, and a history of manufacturing leadership. It then lost ground in two dimensions: Nvidia’s parallel GPUs captured AI workloads, and TSMC’s neutral foundry model outlearned Intel’s integrated design-and-manufacturing structure. Intel’s attempted foundry business lacked customer trust because Intel competed with the firms it wanted to serve. Management also delayed EUV and repeatedly missed 10nm and 7nm process schedules. The company still had a chance to recover, but without it the United States would lack a domestic producer of leading-edge processors. The warning is institutional: a company can possess talent and money yet prioritize margins over innovation.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Parallel-processing shift**: use GPUs to run many instances of the same calculation simultaneously, making AI economically practical.
  - When to use: when workloads repeat calculations across large data sets.
- **Integrated-model tradeoff**: combining design and fabrication can optimize a product, but a neutral foundry gains scale and customer focus.
  - When to use: when deciding between internal coordination and ecosystem learning.

## Key Concepts

- **CPU**: general-purpose processor executing calculations serially.
- **GPU**: parallel processor suited to graphics and AI.
- **AI accelerator**: chip specialized for machine-learning workloads.
- **Intel foundry**: failed 2010s attempt to sell Intel manufacturing to outsiders.

## Mental Models

- Compare a workload’s computation pattern before choosing CPU or GPU.
- Treat customer neutrality as a competitive capability for a foundry.
- Watch for margin metrics that suppress emerging-market experiments.

## Anti-patterns

- **Optimizing the balance sheet at the expense of process leadership**.
- **Entering foundry services without changing the competitor mindset**.

## Worked Example

Intel spent over $10 billion a year on R&D, yet its foundry won only one major customer and its EUV adoption lagged. Nvidia, meanwhile, used GPUs and CUDA to become central to AI while cloud companies began designing their own specialized chips.

## Key Takeaways

1. Match architecture to workload, not to incumbent identity.
2. Fund low-margin options before they become the only growth path.
3. Give a foundry one clear promise: trusted manufacturing.
