# Chapter 40: “There Is No Plan B”

## Core Idea

By the mid-2010s, decades of optical workarounds had pushed 193-nanometer lithography near its practical limits. Tony Yen and TSMC’s R&D leaders concluded that EUV was the only route to continue transistor scaling: there was no Plan B. TSMC and Samsung could afford the risk because they had scale, while GlobalFoundries canceled its 7nm program after spending $1.5 billion and deciding a medium-sized foundry could not make it profitable. The consequence was another concentration of leading-edge fabrication from four plausible firms to three. The chapter shows that technological progress can continue while economic access to the frontier narrows.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **No Plan B technology bet**: commit to a difficult platform when all alternatives are temporary workarounds.
  - When to use: when the current process can extend the runway but cannot sustain the long-term trajectory.
- **Scale threshold**: a technology may be technically viable but commercially unavailable to firms below a certain volume.
  - When to use: when node transitions require multibillion-dollar R&D and capacity investment.

## Key Concepts

- **193nm workaround**: optical tricks extending deep-ultraviolet lithography.
- **7nm node**: advanced process generation associated with EUV adoption.
- **GlobalFoundries**: foundry that exited leading-edge logic.
- **FinFET**: 3D transistor architecture enabled by earlier node scaling.

## Mental Models

- Ask whether a workaround preserves the trajectory or only delays the wall.
- Count firms that can fund the next node, not firms that can build the current one.
- Treat scale as a prerequisite for learning, yield, and cost recovery.

## Anti-patterns

- **Confusing technical possibility with profitable production**.
- **Relying on endless optical tricks** after each workaround adds cost and delay.

## Worked Example

GlobalFoundries had acquired EUV tools for its Fab 8 but halted the program in 2018, abandoning 7nm after $1.5 billion in development and an estimated comparable amount still required. TSMC, Intel, and Samsung remained able to pursue the frontier.

## Key Takeaways

1. Identify the next enabling platform before the current one saturates.
2. Test whether the business can carry the R&D and capacity burden.
3. Expect frontier production to consolidate.
