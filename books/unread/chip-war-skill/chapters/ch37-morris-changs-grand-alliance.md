# Chapter 37: Morris Chang’s Grand Alliance

## Core Idea

As the founder generation retired, semiconductor companies became more professional but often less willing to make extreme long-term bets. AMD eventually separated its design and fabrication businesses, creating GlobalFoundries, while rising process complexity made each technology node more expensive. TSMC faced a financial crisis and a 40nm delay, but Morris Chang returned as CEO, rehired workers, and increased capital spending rather than accept stagnation. He called the ecosystem around TSMC its “Grand Alliance”: customers, IP providers, equipment makers, and materials suppliers all benefited from the neutral foundry. Because TSMC was not competing with customers’ chip designs, it could coordinate standards and capture their combined R&D momentum.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Grand Alliance**: use a neutral foundry to align the innovation of many customers and suppliers.
  - When to use: when an ecosystem needs a common manufacturing platform.
- **Invest through the downturn**: add capacity and R&D when weaker competitors are cutting back.
  - When to use: when scale and learning will compound after demand recovers.

## Key Concepts

- **GlobalFoundries**: foundry spun out of AMD’s fabs.
- **FinFET**: 3D transistor structure controlling leakage at small nodes.
- **Node**: generation of manufacturing technology.
- **Grand Alliance**: TSMC’s customer-and-supplier ecosystem.

## Mental Models

- Think of a neutral platform as a coordinator of other firms’ innovation.
- In a downturn, compare the cost of capacity with the cost of losing the next node.
- Evaluate integrated manufacturing against scale-based learning.

## Anti-patterns

- **Cutting investment to satisfy short-term markets**: Chang saw stagnation as the real risk.
- **Treating a supplier’s customer as a competitor**: neutrality is the foundry’s strategic asset.

## Worked Example

During the 2008–09 crisis, Chang fired his successor, rehired workers, and announced multibillion-dollar increases in capital spending. He argued that too much capacity was safer than too little as TSMC pursued Apple and the smartphone market.

## Key Takeaways

1. Build a platform around neutrality and shared standards.
2. Invest when rivals are forced to retrench.
3. Treat yield, scale, and ecosystem coordination as one advantage.
