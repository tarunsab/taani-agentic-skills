# Chapter 30: “All People Must Make Semiconductors”

## Core Idea

China had a large workforce and a scientific elite but lost decades of semiconductor development to Communist suspicion of foreign connections and Mao’s Cultural Revolution. The regime sent scientists to farms, attacked expertise, and demanded self-reliance even when domestic industry lacked the tools and materials to make advanced chips. Neighboring Taiwan, Hong Kong, South Korea, and Southeast Asia instead used foreign investment to turn rural labor into industrial capability. After Mao, Deng Xiaoping made science and technology central to the Four Modernizations, but slogans such as “Made in China” could not substitute for accumulated process knowledge. China’s electronics assembly therefore grew on imported silicon, leaving a structural gap between assembling devices and producing the components that made them valuable.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Self-reliance trap**: refusing foreign technology before domestic capability exists can deepen dependence by blocking learning.
  - When to use: when a state wants autonomy in a complex global industry.
- **Assembly-to-fabrication ladder**: move from low-value assembly toward design and fabrication through skills, suppliers, capital, and partnerships.
  - When to use: when a country is a manufacturing platform but captures little profit.

## Key Concepts

- **Cultural Revolution**: Maoist upheaval that damaged China’s science and semiconductor capability.
- **Four Modernizations**: Deng-era program placing science and technology at the center of development.
- **Made in China**: policy aspiration to replace imported systems with domestic production.
- **Imported silicon**: dependence on foreign chips despite domestic electronics assembly.

## Mental Models

- Distinguish workforce scale from specialized industrial capability.
- Ask whether self-reliance policies increase or reduce access to learning.
- Treat assembly as a possible first rung, not evidence of leading-edge fabrication.

## Anti-patterns

- **Politicizing expertise out of existence**: advanced industries cannot be built by suppressing trained specialists.
- **Assuming a decree creates a supply chain**: tools, materials, and tacit knowledge must accumulate.

## Worked Example

China’s 1965 integrated circuit was followed by the Cultural Revolution, during which scientists were sent to rural work and the industry lost a decade. By 1975, one party leader reported that only one in 1,000 domestic semiconductors met standards, while neighboring economies were gaining factories and skills.

## Key Takeaways

1. Protect scientific talent during industrial upgrading.
2. Use foreign integration to climb the capability ladder.
3. Do not mistake device assembly for control of the value chain.
