# Chapter 52: China’s Sputnik Moment?

## Core Idea

U.S. restrictions on Huawei and other Chinese firms may have created a Chinese “Sputnik moment,” accelerating Beijing’s willingness to fund semiconductor development. YMTC received extraordinary support and continued operating during Wuhan’s COVID lockdown, while other projects such as Wuhan Hongxin collapsed amid fraud and weak execution. China cannot realistically reproduce every link in the global supply chain: EUV alone requires decades, tens of billions of dollars, and hundreds of thousands of precise components. A more plausible strategy is selective independence—use RISC-V, build mature-node logic and power chips, and gain NAND capacity while remaining dependent in some frontier areas. Subsidies may increase China’s market share without closing its technology gap. The central uncertainty is whether pressure creates genuine capability or simply more waste.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Sputnik effect**: external technological pressure can mobilize a rival’s state resources and accelerate its strategic program.
  - When to use: when evaluating whether restrictions will deter or catalyze capability-building.
- **Selective decoupling**: reduce dependence at specific choke points rather than attempt total autarky.
  - When to use: when complete technological independence is economically impossible.
- **Mature-node strategy**: build older, cheaper processes for cars, power management, and other non-frontier uses.
  - When to use: when access to leading-edge tools is restricted.

## Key Concepts

- **YMTC**: China’s leading NAND producer.
- **Wuhan Hongxin (HSMC)**: failed semiconductor project associated with fraud.
- **RISC-V**: open-source instruction-set architecture.
- **Silicon carbide/gallium nitride**: materials useful for power-management chips.

## Mental Models

- Separate capability breadth from leading-edge parity.
- Ask whether a policy changes incentives faster than it changes technology.
- Use a frontier-complexity test before assuming a stolen design can be reproduced.

## Anti-patterns

- **Assuming every subsidy is productive investment**: HSMC and Tsinghua Unigroup show how money can be wasted.
- **Demanding total autarky as the only success condition**: selective reduction of dependence is more plausible.

## Worked Example

During Wuhan’s lockdown, special trains carried YMTC workers into the city so NAND production could continue. In the same city, HSMC used fake credentials and an old-facility copy to attract funds before failing, illustrating the range of outcomes produced by state urgency.

## Key Takeaways

1. Treat sanctions as a possible mobilization trigger.
2. Target selective, achievable reductions in dependence.
3. Evaluate subsidies by yields, customers, and learning.
