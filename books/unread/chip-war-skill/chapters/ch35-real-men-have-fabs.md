# Chapter 35: “Real Men Have Fabs”

## Core Idea

Jerry Sanders’s defense of AMD’s fabs captured an older Silicon Valley culture in which design and manufacturing belonged together. By the 2000s, however, the economics of logic and memory fabs had diverged from the economics of analog and radio-frequency chips. Advanced logic or DRAM fabs cost around $20 billion and need continuous node transitions, while many analog chips remain profitable on mature processes requiring far less capital. Memory production had already concentrated in East Asia, and logic fabrication was following it as American firms became fabless. Sanders’s insistence was cultural as much as technical: “real men have fabs” defended identity after the economic case had weakened. The right question is which chip category and scale justify owning fabrication.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Category-specific fab economics**: decide whether to own a fab based on node cadence, capital intensity, scale, and product differentiation.
  - When to use: when choosing integrated manufacturing versus a foundry.
- **Logic-memory-analog split**: group chips by economic and technical behavior before making policy or business comparisons.
  - When to use: when a single “semiconductor industry” label hides divergent supply chains.

## Key Concepts

- **Logic chip**: processor for phones, PCs, and servers.
- **Memory chip**: DRAM or NAND storage.
- **Analog chip**: sensor, power, or signal-management chip often built on mature nodes.
- **Fab**: fabrication plant for semiconductor wafers.

## Mental Models

- Use the “$20 billion and two-year node” test for advanced fabrication.
- Ask whether differentiation comes from shrinking transistors or from design and application.
- Separate cultural attachment from operating economics.

## Anti-patterns

- **Applying advanced-logic economics to every chip category**.
- **Keeping a fab to defend status** when the business cannot sustain its capital needs.

## Worked Example

Advanced DRAM and logic fabs cost about $20 billion, while mature analog fabs require roughly one-quarter as much capital. AMD stayed integrated under Sanders but later spun out its fabs, while many fabless firms used TSMC.

## Key Takeaways

1. Choose manufacturing structure by category and scale.
2. Treat mature-node production as a different strategic asset.
3. Challenge identity-based arguments with capital and yield data.
