# Glossary

**Academic eligibility** — The GPA and test conditions required to play college football (Ch 8).

**Access** — Entry to the school, relationships, routines, and resources that let ability become visible (Ch 3, 11).

**All-American** — A national recognition used in the recruiting market to rank elite high-school players (Ch 2).

**Blind side** — The side of a right-handed quarterback's body he cannot see while preparing to throw; more broadly, a hidden exposure with disproportionate downside (Ch 1).

**Character Courses** — BYU correspondence courses Michael uses to replace failing grades and meet an eligibility gate (Ch 8).

**Context gap** — Missing vocabulary, prior instruction, or lived experience that makes a test measure access more than capacity (Ch 3, 8).

**Crystallized intelligence** — Knowledge accumulated through instruction and experience, contrasted in the book with solving a new problem on the spot (Ch 8).

**De facto segregation** — Separation produced by housing, schooling, wealth, and choice rather than an explicit legal rule (Ch 3).

**Fluid intelligence** — The ability to respond to a novel problem; the book uses Michael's adaptive learning to complicate a low early school profile (Ch 8).

**Franchise player** — A high-value player a team may retain under a special compensation designation; left tackles increasingly receive this treatment (Ch 9).

**Gap play** — A running play in which each blocker owns a gap and the runner follows the opening created at a named lineman's hip (Ch 6).

**Getting fit** — Tim Long's technique of striking with the hands inside the defender's breastplate while driving the hips and feet (Ch 6).

**Hurt Village** — The Memphis housing project whose poverty, weak institutions, and informal networks shape Michael's early environment (Ch 3, 11).

**Institutional memory** — The way past exclusion or policy choices continue to shape current trust and behavior (Ch 10).

**Learning-disabled classification** — A formal educational finding that changes available instruction and testing accommodations (Ch 8).

**Left tackle** — The offensive lineman assigned to protect the blind side of a right-handed quarterback; the book presents it as scarce quarterback insurance (Ch 1, 2, 9).

**Market for football players** — The scouting, recruiting, salary, and draft system that turns projected football value into price and opportunity (Ch 2, 9).

**Motive audit** — A check of who provided support, why, when, and whether the support was an inducement or ordinary care (Ch 8).

**NCAA investigation** — An inquiry into whether benefits to a recruit violated college athletics rules (Ch 7, 8).

**One-play asymmetry** — A role in which one rare failure can outweigh many successful repetitions, as a sack can define a tackle's game (Ch 9).

**Opinion leader** — Coach O's term for a recruit whose credible experience can influence other recruits' choices (Ch 7, 10).

**Pocket time** — The time a quarterback has before pressure forces a throw, scramble, or sack; fractions of a second matter (Ch 5, 9).

**Prototype** — A market's model of the desired player, such as the unusually large, long, agile left tackle (Ch 2, 9).

**Protective instinct** — The impulse to shield people or territory, valuable in football but potentially dangerous when uncontrolled off the field (Ch 1, 12).

**Recognition lag** — The delay between a capability becoming strategically important and the market or public assigning it a high value (Ch 5, 9, 13).

**Sifting machine** — Tom Lemming's information network for reducing millions of players to a small annual set of recruitable prospects (Ch 2).

**Support system** — The practical and relational infrastructure—food, bed, rides, clothes, tutoring, medical care, and advocacy—that converts potential into sustained opportunity (Ch 3, 7, 8).

**Survival ecology** — An environment in which concealment, running, hoarding, or immediate protection can be rational responses to instability (Ch 11).

**Visual learner** — Coach O's practical description of Michael's demonstrated ability to retain spatial arrangements and physical demonstrations better than abstract playbook language (Ch 8, 10).

**West Coast offense** — A disputed label for a passing lineage; in the book's contrast, Walsh's branch spreads the field and emphasizes short, timed, repeatable routes (Ch 5).
