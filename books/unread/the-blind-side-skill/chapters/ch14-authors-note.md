# Author's Note

## Core Idea

The Author's Note explains that the book began with the author's old schoolmate Sean Tuohy, but that the significance of Michael Oher was initially invisible to him. Only after Lewis learned how much the left tackle had come to matter in the NFL, and heard that college coaches saw Michael as a future professional protector, did the personal story connect to the strategic and economic story. His wife Tabitha's reaction pushed him to treat the connection as a book rather than a passing anecdote. The reporting then widened across Tuohy family conversations, coaches, players, agents, school staff, Memphis residents, foster-care sources, archives, and old games, so the football explanation and the social account could test one another. Michael initially withheld his history and answered little, but later called Lewis directly and became a much more important source; this change complicates any simple account of who “owns” the story. The note is therefore both acknowledgment and method statement: the narrative is built from triangulated testimony, observation, records, and the author's effort to learn a technical world from its participants. Its candor also marks a limit—this is a reported interpretation shaped by access, memory, selective disclosure, and the author's interest in the hidden value of the left tackle, not a complete or neutral record of every life involved.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Reporting triangulation**: test a consequential account across people with different positions, incentives, and kinds of access.
  - When to use: when memory is partial or a single narrator benefits from one interpretation.
  - How: combine firsthand testimony, institutional records, expert explanation, observation, and contradictory cases.
- **Narrative hinge**: find the technical or economic fact that makes a personal story newly legible.
  - When to use: when a seemingly private event has wider structural significance.
  - How: identify the fact that changes the stakes, then connect it to the people whose lives embody the change.
- **Source-boundary disclosure**: state how access was obtained and where the account remains uncertain.
  - When to use: whenever a narrative depends on cooperation, memory, or protected information.
  - How: name the source path, the changes in access, and the limits that should temper confidence.

## Key Concepts

- **Triangulation**: comparing testimony and evidence from multiple vantage points.
- **Narrative hinge**: the left-tackle market's rise, which makes Michael's individual story structurally important.
- **Source access**: the relationships that determine which memories, records, and expert explanations become available.
- **Selective memory**: the possibility that a subject remembers public facts more readily than private experience—or protects the private experience.
- **End notes**: source clarifications that extend the book's football, salary, naming, and biblical references.

## Mental Models

- Treat a reported book as a **constructed evidence graph**: each major claim should have more than one kind of support when possible.
- Ask what the author's **access path** makes visible and what it may leave out.
- Separate **provenance** (“how did this enter the story?”) from **truth** (“what best explains the evidence?”).

## Anti-patterns

- **Treat gratitude lists as proof**: acknowledgments show access and assistance, not that every claim has been independently established.
- **Mistake a subject's silence for absence of experience**: withholding can be evidence about safety, trust, or memory.
- **Assume triangulation eliminates bias**: many sources can share the same institutional blind spot.

## Worked Example

Lewis first hears about Michael from Sean and ignores the story because it seems like a private family anecdote. The later discovery that left tackles are unusually valuable changes the question, while conversations with coaches, agents, players, teachers, Memphis residents, and Michael himself supply different pieces of the answer. The sequence illustrates how a narrative hinge can redirect research, but also why the resulting account should be read with its selection path in mind.

## End Notes and Source Clarifications

- The football notes distinguish the lead blind-side rusher in a 4–3 defense from the outside right linebacker who fills that role in a 3–4.
- Ben Alamar's research is used to support the importance of passing attacks and pocket time, while also explaining why football lacks clean individual statistics: twenty-two players and interacting assignments share each play. The note's Kurt Warner stopwatch example warns that a sack credited to a line may also reflect a quarterback holding the ball too long.
- The “West Coast offense” label is historically disputed. The note separates Sid Gilman's field-spreading influence, Don Coryell's deeper and riskier branch, and Walsh's wider, shorter, more timed and repeatable system.
- Other notes supply context rather than new arguments: the long resistance to the forward pass, a correction about players' identities, a caveat that defensive-end salary data combines both sides, and the reporting detail that Bobby Spivey's legal-guardian status helped open Michael's early history. The title's Moses reference comes from Exodus, where faltering speech is linked to reluctance to speak before power.
- The dropped image assets included defensive-alignment diagrams; the prose explanation is retained, but the diagrams themselves are not reproduced.

## Key Takeaways

1. Follow the technical hinge that reveals why an individual story matters beyond itself.
2. Use multiple source types and make the source path visible.
3. Treat silence, memory, and institutional records as evidence with limits, not as transparent windows.
4. Preserve uncertainty where the evidence cannot support a stronger claim.
