# Chapter 8: Character Courses

## Core Idea

The NCAA investigation treats the Tuohys' care as a possible recruiting violation, while the family's experience suggests that the rules are looking for improper gifts inside a life that needed food, clothes, shelter, education, and protection. Michael's fragmented answers make the investigation difficult: his family names, addresses, foster homes, and childhood memories are incomplete, and an authority figure's phrasing changes the answer he can produce. The investigators are not simply foolish—their job is to establish whether benefits were exchanged for a college commitment—but the book shows how a compliance frame can mistake the surface transaction for the underlying deprivation. At the same time, academic eligibility becomes the immediate bottleneck: Michael works intensely with Miss Sue, raises his senior grades to all A's and B's, and still falls short of the NCAA threshold. Psychological testing then reveals a learner who has memorized words without decoding them, yet learns from every new problem and has an IQ in the ordinary range once experience and instruction have changed. The BYU Character Courses, repeated ACT attempts allowed after an LD classification, and oral/visual tutoring are rule-bound but ethically uncomfortable workarounds that make college possible. Sean's reading of *The Charge of the Light Brigade* shows the operative teaching method: convert unfamiliar language into a football scene Michael can see, then connect courage and assignment to his own role. The chapter closes with the football version of the same lesson: use bottles, chairs, or other physical objects to make a play visible before expecting verbal code to guide action.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Motive audit**: distinguish ordinary care from an inducement by asking who gave what, when, why, and what the recipient could realistically return.
  - When to use: when support and institutional incentives overlap.
  - How: document resources and motives without assuming either innocence or guilt from appearance.
- **Contextual learning diagnosis**: compare performance on decontextualized tasks with performance when vocabulary, examples, or oral explanation are supplied.
  - When to use: when low scores coexist with effort or strong performance in another channel.
  - How: observe how the learner uses feedback, then provide a foundation rather than merely lowering the standard.
- **Visualize the assignment**: represent an abstract sequence with objects, demonstrations, or a familiar scene.
  - When to use: when symbols and verbal labels do not register under pressure.
  - How: build the scene, name each moving part, rehearse it, and check recall in action.

## Key Concepts

- **NCAA investigation**: the inquiry into whether benefits or recruiting rules were violated.
- **Academic eligibility**: the GPA/ACT conditions required to play college football.
- **Learning-disabled classification**: a formal finding that changes which educational and testing accommodations are available.
- **Character Courses**: BYU correspondence courses used to replace failing grades with new grades.
- **Fluid intelligence**: responding to a new situation on the spot.
- **Crystallized intelligence**: knowledge accumulated through prior experience and instruction.
- **Context gap**: a missing foundation that makes a familiar test appear impossible.
- **Visual learner**: Coach O's practical label for Michael's demonstrated ability to retain visible arrangements and movements.

## Mental Models

- Think of a compliance interview as a **model of reality**; test what the model leaves out before treating its outputs as the whole case.
- Use **feedback sensitivity** as evidence of learning capacity: the ability to apply a solved problem to the next one matters more than a single score.
- Convert words into **spatial rehearsal** when a task will ultimately be performed in space and time.

## Anti-patterns

- **Assume silence is proof of deception or incapacity**: Michael often freezes under authority and supplies the answer he thinks will end the intrusion.
- **Treat eligibility loopholes as education**: the Character Courses solve a gatekeeping problem but do not by themselves create literacy or intellectual interest.
- **Explain a complex play once on a chalkboard**: Michael can say he understands and still have no usable memory of the assignment.

## Worked Example

Sean turns Tennyson's poem into an Ole Miss–LSU scene by replacing an unfamiliar unit of distance with a familiar half-mile, explaining the battlefield as a stadium, and connecting the poem's courage to a left tackle's duty. Michael first watches the NBA game, then leans toward the reading, asks whether the soldiers all die, and finally turns off the television to ask what a league means. The lesson becomes memorable because it is visual, emotional, and tied to football. Coach O later applies the same logic to plays, using mustard and ketchup bottles to stand for the moving defenders and blockers.

## Key Takeaways

1. Audit support and inducement separately when care and institutional incentives overlap.
2. Treat low performance as a diagnostic prompt, not a complete identity.
3. Teach the missing foundation and use feedback to reveal learning capacity.
4. Make abstract instructions visible, spatial, and rehearsable.
