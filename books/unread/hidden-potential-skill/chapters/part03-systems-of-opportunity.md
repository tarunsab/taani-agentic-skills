# Part III: Systems of Opportunity — Opening Doors and Windows

## Core Idea

Personal character skills and scaffolding matter, but they cannot compensate for systems that never offer a fair chance to practice or be seen. Grant’s evidence linking tax records and patents shows that children from the top 1% of family income were ten times more likely to become inventors than children from below-median-income families, even when early math ability was comparable. Geography and role models mattered too: moving to a more innovative area increased patenting odds, and girls benefited especially from exposure to female inventors. Part III therefore moves from individual growth to institutional design in schools, teams, hiring, and admissions. Its systems-level question is how to replace early sorting with cultures, processes, and opportunities that reveal progress, collective intelligence, and late-blooming capability. Good systems open doors, provide windows after rejection, and remove barriers that make potential invisible.

## Source Sections

- **Opening Doors and Windows** — Opportunity is distributed through family, geography, role models, and institutional access.
- **Schools** — Build a culture that assumes every student is teachable and provides professional teachers, looping, early support, play, and interests.
- **Teams** — Create cohesion, balanced voice, and channels for ideas from every level.
- **Selection** — Contextualize difficulty, inspect trajectories, use live work samples, and allow do-overs.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Assumptions → values → practices**: Use to diagnose culture. Deep beliefs shape what a system rewards, which shapes daily routines.
- **Opportunity for all**: Design for progress across the distribution, not only for people who display early brilliance.
- **Systemic scaffolding**: Build support, voice, and second chances into the institution so access does not depend on one exceptional mentor.

## Key Concepts

- **Lost Einstein**: A person with potential who lacks the opportunity to develop or display it.
- **Culture**: Shared assumptions, values, and practices that shape behavior.
- **Opportunity gap**: Unequal access to the conditions that turn ability into achievement.
- **Role-model exposure**: Access to people who make a possible future visible.
- **Collective intelligence**: A group’s ability to solve problems together.
- **Potential-aware selection**: Evaluation that considers context, learning, trajectory, and work samples.

## Mental Models

- Move from **heroic exception to repeatable system**.
- Inspect the **upstream assumption** before changing a downstream practice.
- Ask who is missing because the system only recognizes polished results.

## Anti-patterns

- **Sorting by starting point**: Treating early scores, credentials, or polish as complete forecasts.
- **Gatekeeper dependence**: Making one boss, teacher, or screener the sole route to opportunity.
- **Equal treatment without equal access**: Using identical standards when people had radically different chances to prepare.

## Limitations & Boundaries

Better systems cannot guarantee equal outcomes or remove every difference in motivation, preparation, or circumstance. Finland’s model has local ingredients and later faced declining motivation and test performance, so its practices are not a plug-in recipe. Context, trajectory, and voice improve institutional judgment but do not eliminate uncertainty.

## Worked Example

Chetty’s innovation evidence makes the opportunity problem visible. Affluent children with high math scores were more than twice as likely to become inventors as equally scoring low-income children, and moving from New Orleans to Austin was associated with a 37% rise in the probability of patenting. Exposure to nearby inventors helped explain the pattern, with female role models especially important for girls. The implication is not that wealth or geography creates genius directly; it is that systems distribute guides, practice, aspirations, and permission to try.

## Key Takeaways

1. Design conditions that reveal growth rather than reward only visible head starts.
2. Make support and voice structural, not dependent on luck.
3. Give people a chance to show capability in the present.
4. Treat opportunity as an institutional responsibility.
