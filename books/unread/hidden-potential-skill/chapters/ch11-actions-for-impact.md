# Actions for Impact

## Core Idea

This closing action map converts the book’s arguments into forty compact rules. It preserves the book’s three section-level principles plus the lettered actions beneath them: build character skills, set up scaffolding, and build systems of opportunity. The actions emphasize matching learning methods to tasks, starting before readiness, filtering advice, choosing excellence over perfection, and tracking progress. They then move outward to deliberate play, recovery, rerouting, teaching, coaching, credible expectations, and responsibility for future generations. The final group applies the same judgment to schools, teams, and selection systems so that opportunity is not reserved for early stars.

## Frameworks Introduced

### I. Build character skills

1. **Build character skills** — Grow the skills that let you make yourself and others smarter.
2. **Become a creature of discomfort** — Match the method to the task, not merely to your preference.
3. **Use it or never gain it** — Enter the arena before you feel ready.
4. **Seek discomfort** — Treat awkwardness as a sign that growth is happening.
5. **Set a mistake budget** — Set a minimum number of errors to invite trial and learning.
6. **Become a sponge** — Seek new knowledge and perspectives for growth, not ego.
7. **Ask for advice, not feedback** — Ask what to do better next time.
8. **Filter sources** — Weigh care, credibility, and familiarity before absorbing input.
9. **Be the coach you hope to have** — Be candid in content and respectful in delivery.
10. **Strive for excellence, not perfection** — Accept nonessential flaws and protect high standards.
11. **Enlist judges** — Use independent 0–10 ratings and ask how to improve.
12. **Be your own last judge** — Release work that represents you well.
13. **Use mental time travel** — Ask what your past self would think of today’s progress.

### II. Set up scaffolding to overcome obstacles

14. **Set up scaffolding** — Look outward for temporary support matched to the obstacle and moment.
15. **Turn practice into play** — Build enjoyable, structured skill challenges.
16. **Compete against yourself** — Let improvement, not an opponent’s bad day, define winning.
17. **Vary the routine** — Alternate skills, tools, methods, and constraints.
18. **Schedule rest and recovery** — Use breaks to sustain passion, ideas, and memory.
19. **Back up to move forward** — Change routes when the current method has stalled.
20. **Find a compass** — Seek credible direction before a complete map exists.
21. **Seek multiple guides** — Collect landmarks and turning points from several journeys.
22. **Find a side gig** — Use a meaningful detour and small wins to refuel.
23. **Teach what you want to learn** — Use recall and explanation to strengthen competence.
24. **Build confidence by coaching others** — Give the advice you need to follow.
25. **Use high and low expectations wisely** — Prove credible believers right and ignorant doubters wrong.
26. **Be a good ancestor** — Remember who is counting on the path you open.

### III. Build systems of opportunity

27. **Build systems of opportunity** — Open doors for people who are underrated and overlooked.
28. **Don’t waste a brain** — Assume intelligence takes many forms and every student can grow.
29. **Professionalize education** — Train, trust, and support teachers as professionals.
30. **Keep teachers with students** — Use looping so relationships and learner knowledge compound.
31. **Let students explore and share interests** — Make agency and enthusiasm contagious.
32. **Transform groups into teams** — Align members around shared responsibility for a meaningful mission.
33. **Choose prosocial leaders** — Prefer listeners and mission-focused contributors over babblers and ball hogs.
34. **Use brainwriting** — Generate and evaluate ideas independently before group selection.
35. **Replace ladders with lattices** — Give ideas multiple routes to sponsorship and voice.
36. **Remove unnecessary credentials and experience requirements** — Do not confuse pedigree with future potential.
37. **Account for degree of difficulty** — Interpret performance in the context of access and obstacles.
38. **Use trajectories** — Preserve the slope of improvement, not only an average.
39. **Reimagine interviews and do-overs** — Let candidates share passions, demonstrate strengths in relevant work samples, and retry when the first snapshot was noisy.
40. **Redefine success** — Measure progress and the distance traveled, not only the peak.

## Key Concepts

- **Character**: Learned capacity to live by values under pressure.
- **Scaffolding**: Temporary support matched to an obstacle.
- **Progress**: Growth in capability, motivation, or contribution over time.
- **Opportunity**: Access to conditions that allow potential to become visible.

## Mental Models

- **Inside out**: Build discomfort tolerance, absorptive capacity, and selective standards.
- **Support and route**: Add play, guides, teaching, coaching, and recovery when effort stalls.
- **System design**: Make equity, voice, trajectory, and second chances routine.

## Anti-patterns

- **Waiting for readiness**: It delays the practice that creates readiness.
- **More effort as the universal fix**: Some obstacles need a new method, support, or route.
- **Single snapshots and shortcuts**: They reward head starts and hide distance traveled.

## Limitations & Boundaries

These actions are compact defaults distilled from the book, not a universal checklist or a promise of outcomes. The book calls them its top forty because the three section principles are included alongside thirty-seven detailed actions. Adapt the intensity, timing, and support to the task, person, institution, and risk.

## Key Takeaways

1. Grow yourself through discomfort, useful input, and selective imperfection.
2. Design motivation and support so persistence does not depend on exhaustion.
3. Teach, coach, and build structures that let more people demonstrate potential.
4. Use progress and contribution as the final measures.
