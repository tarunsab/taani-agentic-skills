# Chapter 8: Mining for Gold

## Core Idea

Collective intelligence is not the sum of the smartest individual brains; it is the team’s capacity to bring out and combine useful thinking from everyone. The Chilean mine rescue began with many capable people but little cohesion, and its success depended on leadership practices, team processes, and systems that welcomed ideas from outside the core group. Research summarized by Grant finds that collective intelligence tracks prosocial skill, balanced participation, and a meaningful shared mission more than individual IQ. A team becomes more than a group when members know their outcomes depend on one another, share knowledge, and coach across roles. Brainwriting improves on brainstorming by separating idea generation and initial evaluation from group selection, while a lattice system prevents one boss from becoming a single point of failure for ideas. Psychological safety matters, but the chapter emphasizes the system around voice: multiple routes, parallel plans, and careful judgment can turn a young outsider’s suggestion into a life-saving intervention.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Collective intelligence**: Use when a problem needs varied expertise. Build a team whose members contribute, combine, and improve one another’s thinking.
- **Cohesion through interdependence**: Align members around a meaningful shared outcome and make the contribution of each role visible.
- **Prosocial leadership**: Select leaders who listen, put the mission above ego, and create conditions for others to contribute.
- **Brainwriting**: Have people generate ideas separately, pool them, evaluate them independently, and only then meet to select and refine.
- **Lattice system**: Provide multiple channels across levels and teams so one gatekeeper cannot kill an idea or silence a contributor.

## Key Concepts

- **Collective intelligence**: A group’s ability to solve analytical and creative problems together.
- **Prosocial skill**: Behavior that mobilizes other people’s strengths for a shared outcome.
- **Cohesion**: Shared identity and responsibility grounded in interdependence.
- **Babble effect**: Mistaking airtime and assertiveness for competence when selecting leaders.
- **Bad apple**: One nonprosocial member whose ego or sabotage degrades the group.
- **Balanced participation**: Broadly distributed contribution across members.
- **Brainwriting**: Individual-first idea generation and evaluation.
- **Psychological safety**: A climate in which people can speak up without status punishment.
- **Lattice**: A multi-route organizational structure for ideas, sponsorship, and voice.

## Mental Models

- Ask **who has not spoken or been asked?** before trusting the room’s consensus.
- Treat the loudest person as **one data point**, not the default leader.
- Separate **creative variance** from collective judgment; both are necessary.

## Anti-patterns

- **Expert-only assembly**: Limiting the solution space to established authorities.
- **Brainstorming by default**: Letting status, noise, and conformity suppress ideas.
- **Babble-based promotion**: Elevating people who dominate discussion but undermine collaboration.
- **Single-gatekeeper hierarchy**: Making one manager’s no sufficient to kill a proposal.
- **Confusing psychological safety with no judgment**: Voice needs a process for vetting feasibility and risk.

## Limitations & Boundaries

Experts remain valuable; the argument is for a wider and better process, not for discarding expertise. Brainwriting generates variance and still needs collective judgment, while lattice routes and parallel plans cost time and coordination. Psychological safety makes voice possible but does not make every proposal feasible or correct.

## Worked Example

When the Chile rescue’s Plan A was too slow, 24-year-old engineer Igor Proestakis proposed rapidly widening an existing borehole with a cluster hammer. André listened, asked questions, and let the proposal become Plan B alongside Plan A despite its technical risks. After the drill bit shattered on reinforcing rods, Igor used another route in the lattice to pitch an industrial claw that could extract the obstruction; the mining minister approved it after local listeners ignored him. Five days of failed attempts ended when the claw cleared the hole, allowing the capsule to rescue all 33 miners. The result depended on a system that made low-status voice reachable and kept multiple plans alive.

## Key Takeaways

1. Build teams around interdependence and prosocial contribution, not prestige alone.
2. Use brainwriting to protect diversity before applying group judgment.
3. Select leaders for listening and mission focus, especially with proactive teams.
4. Create multiple paths for ideas to reach a sponsor and survive a local no.
