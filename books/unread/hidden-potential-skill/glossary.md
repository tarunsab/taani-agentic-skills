# Glossary

**Absorptive capacity** — Ability to recognize, value, assimilate, and apply new information (Ch 2).
**Advice** — Forward-looking input about what to do better next time (Ch 2).
**Ambition** — The outcome a person wants to attain (Ch 0).
**Aspiration** — The person one hopes to become, rather than only an outcome to win (Ch 0, Ch 10).
**Babble effect** — Mistaking airtime and assertiveness for competence when choosing leaders (Ch 8).
**Brainwriting** — Individual-first idea generation and evaluation followed by group selection and refinement (Ch 8).
**Burnout** — Emotional exhaustion that accumulates under overload (Ch 4).
**Character** — Learned capacity to prioritize values over instincts, especially under pressure (Ch 0, Part I).
**Character skills** — Proactivity, determination, discipline, emotional self-regulation, and prosocial capacities that produce and sustain progress (Ch 0, Ch 1, Ch 6).
**Coach effect** — Confidence and motivation gained by giving others guidance that one also needs (Ch 6).
**Credibility** — Relevant expertise that makes a source’s judgment informative (Ch 2, Ch 6).
**Curse of knowledge** — Difficulty explaining basics after forgetting what it was like not to know (Ch 5).
**Deliberate play** — Structured skill-building made enjoyable through challenge, novelty, and recreation (Ch 4, Ch 7).
**Deliberate practice** — Structured repetition with clear goals and immediate feedback (Ch 4).
**Degree of difficulty** — Access, constraints, and obstacles surrounding a performance (Ch 9).
**Distance traveled** — Growth relative to a person’s starting point and route, not only the final peak (Ch 0, Ch 9).
**Do-over** — A second chance after a noisy or unrepresentative first performance (Ch 9, Ch 10).
**Early intervention** — Targeted support supplied when a learner first shows difficulty (Ch 7).
**Familiarity** — A source’s knowledge of the person’s capabilities and context (Ch 2).
**False negative** — Rejecting someone who could have succeeded (Ch 9).
**False positive** — Accepting someone who will not perform as expected (Ch 9).
**Flooding** — High-intensity exposure to a feared activity (Ch 1).
**Good ancestor** — A steward who improves conditions for future generations (Ch 6).
**GPT** — Grade-point trajectory; the slope of grades over time, proposed as a selection signal (Ch 9).
**Growth mindset** — Belief that ability can improve, which needs supportive culture and scaffolding to matter (Ch 6, Ch 10).
**Harmonious passion** — Joy in the process of practice rather than compulsive pressure for an outcome (Ch 4).
**Hidden potential** — Capacity for greater achievement that is not visible from an initial snapshot (Ch 0, Ch 9).
**Impostor syndrome** — Fear that others will discover one’s inadequacy despite evidence of capability or growth (Ch 10).
**Imperfectionism** — Selective discipline about which flaws to fix and which to accept while pursuing excellence (Ch 3).
**Interleaving** — Alternating related skills, tools, or problems during practice (Ch 4).
**Languishing** — Stagnation and emptiness that can further drain focus and motivation (Ch 5).
**Lattice system** — Multiple organizational routes for ideas and sponsorship across levels and teams (Ch 8).
**Learned industriousness** — Effort becomes rewarding when effortful behavior is praised (Ch 1).
**Learning styles** — A popular but unsupported claim that fixed personal modes should determine instruction (Ch 1).
**Looping** — Keeping a teacher with the same students across multiple grades (Ch 7).
**Mental time travel** — Imagining how one’s past self would evaluate current progress (Ch 3).
**Minimum lovable product** — An early result that is genuinely valued, not merely minimally functional (Ch 3).
**Mistake budget** — A planned minimum number of errors used to invite experimentation (Ch 1).
**Multiple guides** — Several partial sources whose landmarks can be assembled into a personal route (Ch 5).
**Overblown implications effect** — Treating one poor performance as proof of global incompetence (Ch 3).
**Peter Principle** — Promotion based on success in one role until a person reaches a role beyond their abilities (Ch 9).
**Personality** — Predisposition or default tendency for thinking, feeling, and acting (Ch 0).
**Potential-aware selection** — Evaluation using context, trajectory, learning, and live demonstrations, not only pedigree (Ch 9).
**Proactivity** — Taking initiative to seek information, create opportunities, and act before being directed (Ch 0, Ch 2).
**Prosociality** — Using one’s skills to collaborate and strengthen other people and the shared mission (Ch 2, Ch 8).
**Scaffolding** — Temporary, tailored support that provides a foothold beyond current reach (Ch 0, Part II).
**Side-gig detour** — A meaningful alternate project that supplies small wins and restores momentum (Ch 5).
**Stereotype threat** — Performance disruption caused by fear of confirming a negative group stereotype (Ch 9).
**Spaced repetition** — Revisiting material after breaks to deepen recall and reduce forgetting (Ch 4).
**Task-matched learning** — Choosing a learning method because of the task’s demands (Ch 1).
**Tutor effect** — Competence gained by teaching desired knowledge to others (Ch 6).
**Trajectory** — Direction and rate of change over time (Ch 5, Ch 9).
**Underdog effect** — Motivation to disprove low expectations from an uninformed doubter (Ch 6).
**Wabi sabi** — Honoring beauty and usefulness in inevitable imperfection (Ch 3).
**Work sample** — Direct demonstration of a candidate’s skill on a relevant task (Ch 9).
