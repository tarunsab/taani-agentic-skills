# Cheatsheet

## Decision rules

| If you notice… | Do this | Because… |
|---|---|---|
| A learner is comfortable but not improving | Match the method to the task and add a small real use | Preference is not effectiveness |
| Someone is waiting to feel ready | Start with a first-day version and a mistake budget | Readiness grows through practice |
| Feedback is vague or defensive | Ask, “What is one thing I can do better next time?” | Advice points forward |
| Input conflicts | Check care, credibility, and familiarity | Not every source has earned influence |
| A project is over-polished | Set acceptable and aspirational targets; ask judges for scores | Precision beats “do your best” |
| A plateau feels permanent | Back up and test a new route | New methods often worsen performance before improving it |
| Motivation is flat | Take a purposeful detour and collect a small win | Progress is motivational fuel |
| Confidence is low | Coach or teach someone facing a similar problem | Giving advice activates resources |
| One person dominates a group | Switch from brainstorming to brainwriting | Independent voices reduce status and conformity effects |
| One boss can kill an idea | Add lattice routes to multiple sponsors | A single no should not close the frontier |
| A candidate looks ordinary | Inspect difficulty and trajectory; use a live work sample | Potential is not the same as polish |

## Scaffolding test

1. What exact obstacle is blocking progress?
2. Who or what can supply the missing foothold?
3. Is the support tailored to this obstacle?
4. Is the timing pivotal?
5. How and when will responsibility return to the learner?

## Practice defaults

- Use task-matched methods: reading/writing for analysis; listening for sound and emotion; doing for communication and memory.
- Make practice deliberate play: a subskill, a challenge, a score or time, and novelty.
- Compete with the previous version of yourself.
- Interleave related skills instead of repeating one routine until it feels easy.
- Schedule breaks before burnout or boreout; rest protects passion, ideas, and memory.

## Evaluation defaults

- Preserve rise over run; do not collapse growth into an average.
- Compare performance with relevant peers and the access they had.
- Use past performance only when the old and new roles share the needed skills.
- Treat one failure as a snapshot, not a verdict.
- Let the candidate judge whether the interview represented them; offer a do-over when it did not.

## Team and system smells

- Loudest voice promoted → babble effect.
- Brilliant individual, weak collaboration → missing prosocial glue.
- Team assigned but not interdependent → group, not team.
- High test scores plus misery → performance has displaced well-being.
- Early score treated as destiny → hidden potential is being filtered out.
