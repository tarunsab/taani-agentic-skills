## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### Potential is distance traveled
Judge potential by how far someone can travel, not only by the height of the peak they have already reached. Grant contrasts visible starting advantages with growth made possible by opportunity, motivation, and character skills. Initial ability and background still matter, but they are poor proxies for future capacity when people have had unequal access to practice, coaching, or time. Use trajectory, degree of difficulty, and capacity to learn as complements to present performance.

### Character skills are learned capacities
Character is not simply a moral code or a personality trait; it is the learned capacity to live by values under pressure. The recurring skills are proactivity, determination, discipline, emotional self-regulation, and prosocial contribution. They can be developed in adults as well as children: in Grant's cited randomized West African entrepreneur study, five days of personal-initiative training produced about 30% higher profits over two years, nearly triple the gain from traditional cognitive-skills training. Use character skills to explain how people create opportunities, persist through obstacles, and raise the performance of others.

### Scaffolding creates temporary lift
Scaffolding is support that gives a learner a foothold beyond current reach and then transfers responsibility back to the learner. Effective scaffolding is supplied by people or systems, tailored to the obstacle, offered at a pivotal moment, and temporary. It may be a coach, a peer-teaching structure, a deliberate-play exercise, a detour that restores momentum, or a selection process that lets a candidate show what they can do. Ask: what obstacle is present, what support matches it, when is it useful, and how will independence grow?

### Become a creature of discomfort
Comfort is not a reliable learning guide. Match the method to the task: reading and writing support critical thinking, listening supports emotional or language comprehension, and doing supports memory and communication. Put the skill into use before feeling ready, deliberately seek the awkward edge, and make mistakes visible enough to learn from them. The point is not reckless exposure; it is appropriately designed practice that makes discomfort a signal of growth.

### Become a human sponge
Absorptive capacity is the ability to recognize, value, assimilate, and apply new information. The sweet spot is proactive information seeking plus a growth-oriented filter: seek new perspectives, but do not absorb indiscriminately. Ask for advice rather than backward-looking feedback, and weigh sources by care, credibility, and familiarity. A good sponge also releases what it has learned so others can grow; learning is both uptake and contribution.

### Be an imperfectionist
Strive for excellence, not zero defects. Wabi sabi means honoring inevitable imperfection while selecting which flaws are acceptable and which threaten the core purpose. Replace vague “do your best” goals with precise, difficult targets; use judges for quality control, then make yourself the final judge of whether the work represents you. Track progress with mental time travel so rising expectations do not erase the distance already covered.

### Turn practice into play
Harmonious passion is joy in the process rather than pressure to achieve an outcome. Deliberate play combines structure for skill development with novelty, challenge, and fun; it is not cosmetic gamification added to a task one still hates. Compete with your past self, interleave related skills, vary tools or constraints, and schedule breaks. Rest protects passion, incubates ideas, and strengthens memory through spacing.

### Treat stagnation as a routing signal
Progress is often nonlinear. A plateau can mean that the current method has reached its limit, so backing up and temporarily performing worse may be necessary to learn a better method. Use a compass—credible directional guidance—rather than waiting for a complete map, gather pins from multiple guides, and test a route. When languishing drains motivation, a side project or hobby can provide small wins and fuel a return to the main road.

### Tie bootstraps together
The strongest version of bootstrapping is interdependent: people use their existing resources with and for one another. The tutor effect improves competence when you teach what you want to learn; the coach effect improves confidence when you give others the advice you need to follow. Credible believers can raise expectations, while ignorant naysayers can trigger an underdog effect; knowledgeable doubters can instead become threatening. Purpose expands resilience when other people or future generations are counting on the work.

### Build systems that reveal potential
Schools, teams, and selection systems should give people a fair chance to demonstrate growth. A culture flows from assumptions to values to practices: Finland’s assumption that every student is teachable supports teacher professionalism, looping, early intervention, play, and individualized interests. Collective intelligence comes from cohesion, prosocial contribution, balanced participation, and systems such as brainwriting and lattice paths for voice. Hiring and admissions should contextualize difficulty, inspect trajectories, use real-time work samples, and offer a do-over where the first snapshot is noisy.
