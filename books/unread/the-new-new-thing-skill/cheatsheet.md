# Cheatsheet

## Fast judgment rules

| Situation | Ask / rule | Failure smell |
|---|---|---|
| A novel idea appears | What changed, why now, and what small push makes adoption possible? | Novelty is being treated as demand. |
| A product is successful but its capability is commoditizing | Build the successor internally before a rival does. | The core team says the profitable product cannot be threatened. |
| Founder sees the concept but not the operation | Add a translator/operator with authority, not just a title. | The company has vision and capital but no adoption path. |
| Engineers want to build a beautiful system | Test a buyer's willingness before optimizing the architecture. | A technically remarkable pilot has no user. |
| A public story is doing the selling | Split story, proxy, mechanism, and falsifier. | “You do the math” hides untested assumptions. |
| A team is working extreme hours | Confirm what new evidence the intensity is buying. | Loyalty and options replace customer learning. |
| Software controls the physical world | Use least privilege, manual fallback, logs, and a known-safe state. | A high access mode exists but no one can explain causality. |
| A sensor or metric says all is well | Ask what the system cannot measure; keep human inspection. | Important signals are dismissed because they are not digitized. |
| An incumbent owns the route to market | Find the chokepoint, underbelly, or interface that changes the basis of competition. | The plan assumes technical merit alone defeats distribution power. |
| A valuation rises on future belief | Treat it as contingent financing, not earned value. | Wealth or price becomes the only evidence of success. |

## Minimal decision tree

1. **Is there a real asymmetry?** If not, refine the problem before adding technology.
2. **Can a bounded experiment expose timing or demand?** If yes, run it before scaling.
3. **Does the experiment work technically but fail commercially?** Change the proposition, not merely the implementation.
4. **Does success threaten the current product?** Protect the internal successor from the core's incentives.
5. **Does adoption require translation?** Add an accountable market/operator layer.
6. **Does automation act on the physical world?** Require fallback, observability, and human signals before increasing control.
7. **Are people staying because they believe or because they are trapped?** Recheck incentives, evidence, and exit conditions.

## Distinctions to preserve

- Imagination is a source of possibilities; execution and demand decide whether they matter.
- Commitment is not the same as sunk cost.
- A proxy metric is not the outcome it predicts.
- More sensors are not the same as more understanding.
- A founder's conceptual authority is not operational competence.
- A public market price is not a durable business model.
