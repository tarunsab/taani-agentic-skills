# Cheatsheet

For study and reasoning only; use current clinical and legal guidance for real care.

## Decision Rules

| Situation | Choose / ask | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Major trauma with several injuries | Run **ATLS: Airway → Breathing → Circulation → Disability → Exposure** | Fix the fastest lethal threat first, not the most dramatic-looking wound. |
| Patient is crashing | Use **damage control** before definitive repair | Stabilization preserves the chance of a later, safer operation. |
| Organ support is proposed | Name the **bridge endpoint** | A ventilator, inotrope, or VAD buys time; it is not automatically a cure. |
| Action and inaction both threaten life | Compare harms, benefits, reversibility, and the patient’s values | A “well-informed gamble” is still a gamble; do not hide uncertainty behind percentages. |
| Child is hospitalized for a long time | Add family, routine, play, school, and honest explanation | Emotional security is part of care, not a reward after physiology improves. |
| Donation may be possible | Wait until death is understood; involve the SNOD; start with the person’s wishes | Too early overwhelms; too late can lose testing and preservation time. |
| Organ must be allocated | Treat it as **matching, not queuing** | Use predefined compatibility, urgency, geography, and outcome criteria; check inputs independently. |
| Donor retrieval is approaching | Protect both clocks: family farewell and organ ischemic limit | Dignity and viability are simultaneous requirements. |
| Recipient is not ready | Do not cross-clamp or accept the heart early | The safest place for the donor heart is not the cooler while the recipient chest is unprepared. |
| Transplant is complete | Think **new function + lifelong vulnerability** | Monitor rejection, infection, medication toxicity, trauma, and ordinary-life recovery. |

## Tells & Smells

- “It’s only asthma” despite worsening energy, weight, breathing, or function → reassess the trajectory.
- A stable monitor beside a warm, unresponsive body → explain the brain-death paradox; do not use appearance as the diagnosis.
- A famous or forceful operator → check judgment, consent, humility, teamwork, and patient dignity.
- A donor described only as a resource → restore name, family, ritual, and agency.
- A child who looks cheerful → still ask about fear, despair, and loss of control.
- A matching result → treat it as a probability shift, never a guarantee against rejection.

## Fast Sequence

1. **See the person.**
2. **Identify the immediate threat.**
3. **Choose the least-worst reversible step.**
4. **Make the mechanism and uncertainty explicit.**
5. **Coordinate the handoff before the irreversible move.**
6. **Plan the human aftermath, not only the technical success.**
