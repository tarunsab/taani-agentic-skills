# Glossary

**Advanced Trauma Life Support (ATLS)** — A systematic trauma approach using the primary survey Airway, Breathing, Circulation, Disability, Exposure, ordered by the fastest lethal threat (Ch 1).

**Acquired immunologic tolerance** — A state in which an immune system accepts tissue from a particular donor after exposure during development; foundational to later transplant research (Ch 7).

**Allocation algorithm** — A computer process that ranks recipients using factors such as size, age, blood group, tissue type, urgency, geography, and predicted survival (Ch 7).

**Brain death / neurological death** — Irreversible loss of brain and brain-stem function despite continued circulation maintained by intensive care (Ch 5, Ch 7).

**Bridge to transplant** — Temporary mechanical support intended to keep a patient alive until a suitable donor organ is available (Ch 6, Ch 8).

**Cardioplegia** — Cold preserving solution used to stop and protect a donor heart during retrieval (Ch 11).

**Cardiopulmonary bypass** — Extracorporeal support that temporarily performs the circulation and oxygenation work of the heart and lungs during surgery (Ch 6, Ch 12).

**Cold ischemic time** — Time an organ remains without warm blood flow while cooled for transport; the limit is organ- and patient-specific (Ch 7, Ch 9, Ch 11).

**Coma dépassé** — The historical term for “irreversible coma,” soon associated with brain death in ventilated patients (Ch 5).

**Damage-control surgery** — Rapid stabilization of catastrophic injury by stopping hemorrhage and contamination before definitive reconstruction (Ch 3).

**Deemed consent** — Presumed consent to organ donation unless a person opts out, subject to law and family rules (Ch 14).

**Dilated cardiomyopathy** — Weakening and enlargement of the heart muscle that impairs forward pumping and causes fluid congestion (Ch 2).

**Family-centred care** — Care that treats family presence, relationships, and emotional needs as part of a child’s care rather than a disruption (Ch 4).

**HeartWare / LVAD** — A continuous-flow left ventricular assist device that diverts blood from the left ventricle to the aorta through an implanted pump and external driveline (Ch 6, Ch 8).

**Hyperacute rejection** — Rapid antibody-mediated destruction of a transplanted organ by preexisting recipient antibodies (Ch 13).

**Inotrope** — A drug that changes the force of heart-muscle contraction, used in the book to support failing circulation (Ch 2, Ch 4, Ch 10).

**Last offices** — Final nursing care that washes, dresses, and restores the deceased person’s recognizable human presentation for the family (Ch 11).

**Life support** — Temporary assistance that replaces a failing organ’s function and buys time for recovery, transfer, or definitive treatment (Ch 3).

**Moment of honor** — A respectful pause before or after organ retrieval to acknowledge the donor, family, gift, and staff (Ch 11).

**Organ scarcity** — The persistent mismatch between people who could benefit from transplantation and organs available (Prologue, Ch 7, Ch 14).

**Palliative treatment** — Treatment that may extend life or relieve suffering without removing the underlying long-term vulnerability (Ch 6, Ch 10).

**Return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC)** — Return of a detectable pulse after cardiac arrest (Ch 3).

**Specialist nurse in organ donation (SNOD)** — A professional who supports families and coordinates donation from end-of-life conversations through retrieval and follow-up (Ch 5, Ch 7, Ch 9).

**Tissue typing** — Testing donor and recipient characteristics to estimate immunological compatibility and rejection risk (Ch 7).

**Ventricular assist device (VAD)** — A mechanical pump supporting a failing heart ventricle; pediatric options carry different stroke and bleeding risks (Ch 6, Ch 8).
