# Chapter 19: Revelations

## Core Idea

Mitnick uses his established contact at the Social Security Administration to test whether Eric Heinz’s identity is real, then follows the result through family records and public documents. The discovery that an infant with the same name died years earlier explains why Eric’s father reacts with anger and why the alias is so difficult to verify. Further call and utility records connect Eric’s movements to the investigation and reveal that the FBI is preparing evidence against Mitnick. The chapter’s strongest method is triangulation: a name is not trusted until family, government, financial, and residential records agree. Its ethical failure is equally important: the same method reaches deeply private information and demonstrates why access to administrative databases must be tightly controlled.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Identity triangulation**: Test a claimed identity across independent records and relationships.
  - When to use: Authorized fraud, insider-risk, or account-recovery investigations.
  - How: Start with minimal data, use approved sources, corroborate contradictions, and protect unrelated personal information.
- **Contradiction-driven search**: Let a mismatch between a person’s story and a record determine the next verification step.
  - When to use: When evidence is incomplete but a claim has a specific testable implication.
  - How: State the implication, check it independently, and update confidence without overclaiming.

## Key Concepts

- **Stolen identity**: A real person’s identifying details used by someone else.
- **Public utility record**: A service account that can reveal a residence.
- **Pager cloning**: Duplicating the routing identifier of another pager; included as a historical risk, not a method to use.
- **Search warrant evidence**: Information investigators gather to justify a legal search.

## Mental Models

Use “identity is a bundle of relationships”: a name, address, family, employer, and account history should cohere. Treat every new record as an update to a model, not as permission to access another system.

## Anti-patterns

- **Using one database as an oracle**.
- **Collecting more personal data than the investigative question requires**.
- **Taking a contradiction as proof of a larger theory without independent confirmation**.

## Worked Example

Mitnick asks Ann to check Eric’s government record, contacts the supposed father, confirms the death through an uncle, and obtains a death record. He then combines this with call patterns and Eric’s repeated moves. The identity hypothesis becomes strong because different sources agree, not because one disclosure is dramatic.

## Key Takeaways

1. Corroborate identity across independent, authorized sources.
2. Use contradictions to choose the next test, not to justify unlimited access.
3. Minimize and protect personal data unrelated to the question.
