# Chapter 11: Foul Play

## Core Idea

Adam’s death shifts Mitnick’s hacking from an abstract challenge into a grief-driven investigation. The family notices anomalies that do not fit a simple overdose story: the shoes appear to have been tied by someone facing Adam, and the physical evidence suggests another person may have administered the fatal dose. Because the police initially decline to pursue the case, Mitnick uses phone records and informal research to follow people around Adam, mixing genuine concern with the dangerous confidence that he can become a vigilante investigator. Years later, a confession confirms that Uncle Mitchell panicked after Adam overdosed and helped move the body rather than calling for emergency aid. The chapter’s limitation is clear: a compelling theory can be right and still be reached through methods that create new legal and ethical harm.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Anomaly-led investigation**: Begin with details that do not fit the accepted explanation.
  - When to use: When a narrative contains physical or behavioral inconsistencies.
  - How: Record the anomaly, generate alternatives, seek corroboration, and distinguish suspicion from proof.
- **Motive under grief**: Emotional urgency can supply energy while reducing restraint.
  - When to use: When a personal loss makes ordinary channels feel too slow.
  - How: Use independent investigators and evidence controls rather than private escalation.

## Key Concepts

- **Foul play**: The possibility that a death was caused or concealed by another person.
- **Anomaly**: A fact that conflicts with the current explanation.
- **Vigilante investigation**: Private pursuit of a case outside legitimate authority.
- **Corroboration**: Independent evidence that strengthens or weakens a hypothesis.

## Mental Models

Use “inconsistency before conclusion”: the clue earns investigation, not certainty. Think of grief as a force multiplier for both care and risk.

## Anti-patterns

- **Treating intuition as evidence**: It can identify where to look but cannot establish what happened.
- **Bypassing legitimate investigators because they seem indifferent**: Private action can compromise evidence and expose others.
- **Using personal technical power to settle a personal loss**: Emotional stakes make scope control more important, not less.

## Worked Example

Mitnick notices that Adam’s shoes and the location of the puncture do not fit his father’s account of a self-administered overdose. He follows phone and relationship clues, then later hears Mitchell describe the overdose and the decision to move Adam’s body. The conclusion is source-grounded, but the route also shows why anomalies should be handed to trained investigators rather than pursued through unauthorized access.

## Key Takeaways

1. Record anomalies precisely before deciding what they mean.
2. Use corroboration and independent review when personal emotion is high.
3. A correct theory does not retroactively authorize the method used to reach it.
