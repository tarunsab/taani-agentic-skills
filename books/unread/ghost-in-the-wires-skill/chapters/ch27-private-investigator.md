# Chapter 26: Private Investigator

## Core Idea

Mitnick moves to Denver, “hiding in plain sight” under the Eric Weiss identity and finding work at a law firm. He tailors résumés to roughly match job requirements, limits the number of references he must maintain, rehearses a plausible biography, and uses an answering service to support the story. He then travels to South Dakota to search public records and build a more durable identity, taking advantage of friendly staff, weak separation between work areas, and unsecured official materials. The chapter shows that identity fraud succeeds through ordinary credibility signals—professional dress, a business card, a job, a helpful act—not through one spectacular forgery. It also shows the risk to institutions: hospitality and convenience can turn a low-suspicion visitor into someone with prolonged access to sensitive records.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Cover-story consistency**: A persona must remain coherent across résumé, interview, references, records, and everyday conversation.
  - When to use: Defensively in hiring and vendor onboarding.
  - How: Verify employment, education, identity, and references through independent channels.
- **Hospitality attack surface**: Friendly, loosely supervised service environments can grant prolonged access.
  - When to use: In physical-security assessments.
  - How: Separate public assistance from restricted records, escort visitors, and secure blank official forms.

## Key Concepts

- **Hiding in plain sight**: Living under an ordinary-looking cover rather than staying physically isolated.
- **Answering service**: A live call-handling service used to support a reference story.
- **Vital statistics**: Government records used in the identity scheme.
- **Believability stack**: The cumulative effect of clothing, documents, employment, and helpful behavior.

## Mental Models

Use “credibility is compositional”: people trust a bundle of small signals. Treat unsupervised access to records and blank forms as a high-severity control failure.

## Anti-patterns

- **Hiring from a single polished narrative**.
- **Letting staff work alone with sensitive records and official stationery**.
- **Assuming a helpful visitor is safe because they perform a small favor**.

## Worked Example

At the law firm, Mitnick gives a few imperfect test answers so he will not appear overqualified, then provides a reference through an answering service. In South Dakota, the Registrar gives a friendly private investigator access to a microfiche room, where he also sees blank certificates and the official embosser. The defensive lesson is to verify each claim and protect the process that creates credentials.

## Key Takeaways

1. Verify every part of a cover story independently.
2. Supervise access to records, blank forms, seals, and credentialing equipment.
3. Small acts of helpfulness can be used to accelerate trust.
