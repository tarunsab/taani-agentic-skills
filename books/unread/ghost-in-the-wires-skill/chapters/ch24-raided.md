# Chapter 23: Raided

## Core Idea

Mitnick’s early-warning system alerts him that FBI agent Ken McGuire has been near his workplace and his apartment. He interprets the signal, warns Lewis, removes incriminating material, and returns to work before the agents arrive. The FBI searches his apartment, plays a recording of calls, and tries to persuade him to cooperate against Lewis; because the agents have not arrested him, he leaves and consults his father. The raid reveals both sides’ strategies: investigators rely on recordings and pressure, while Mitnick relies on preparation, relocation of material, and real-time observation. It also exposes a major failure in his own judgment—he erases data at Teltec and later realizes that his employer may be seeking a deal with the government.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Early-warning tripwire**: A defined signal alerts a team when a known event or actor enters a monitored area.
  - When to use: Defensively for physical security or incident response.
  - How: Limit the data, validate alerts, rehearse response, and avoid converting monitoring into retaliation.
- **Evidence-pressure interview**: Investigators reveal selected evidence and offer cooperation as a way to divide collaborators.
  - When to use: As a legal-process case study, not as a tactic to imitate.
  - How: Seek counsel and preserve rights; do not improvise under pressure.

## Key Concepts

- **Early-warning system**: Mitnick’s scanner-and-computer alert for a known agent’s phone.
- **Search warrant**: Legal authorization used in the apartment raid.
- **Cooperator pressure**: An attempt to make one participant testify against another.
- **Data wiping**: Erasing material from a system; in the memoir, a risky response that can have legal consequences.

## Mental Models

Use “alert, assess, act” rather than panic: an alert is a signal requiring validation, not a verdict. Think of evidence handling as an independent legal process; concealment can create a second problem.

## Anti-patterns

- **Building a monitoring system without a lawful purpose**.
- **Destroying or moving evidence**.
- **Assuming a colleague’s loyalty under legal pressure**.

## Worked Example

The alarm shows McGuire’s phone near Teltec and a call to a pay phone near Mitnick’s apartment. Mitnick warns Lewis, cleans his apartment, and later faces agents who play a recording and ask him to cooperate. The sequence shows why incident response should be planned in advance and why legal counsel, not improvisation, should guide a person facing a search.

## Key Takeaways

1. Define what an alert means and what response is lawful before deploying it.
2. Separate incident response from evidence destruction.
3. Expect incentives and pressure to change relationships during an investigation.
