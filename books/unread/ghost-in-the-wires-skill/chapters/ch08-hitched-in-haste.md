# Chapter 7: Hitched in Haste

## Core Idea

Bonnie becomes a source of affection, stability, and practical support while Mitnick’s hacking becomes a secret second life. He describes lying about evening classes, using her phone line, and spending his available time on systems instead of the relationship. When the Santa Cruz Operations raid arrives, Bonnie and Mitnick face the legal consequences together, but his decision to marry partly to prevent her testimony turns commitment into a defensive maneuver. The chapter makes the personal cost unmistakable: money saved for a wedding goes to legal retainers, his mother and grandmother absorb financial and emotional strain, and Bonnie is pulled into a case she did not create. Mitnick also learns that a simple social-engineering call can be more decisive than sophisticated code, while the criminal system and public reputation can magnify a technical trespass.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Obsession displacement**: A rewarding technical activity crowds out time, honesty, and obligations until the surrounding life becomes a support structure for the activity.
  - When to use: When a “hobby” requires secrecy and consumes nearly every waking hour.
  - How: Measure time, concealment, financial cost, and effects on close relationships.
- **Simple-path principle**: The easiest successful path may be human persuasion, not technical sophistication.
  - When to use: Defensively, when prioritizing controls.
  - How: Test ordinary support calls, account changes, and employee verification.

## Key Concepts

- **SCO/Xenix**: Santa Cruz Operations’ Unix-based operating system project.
- **Summary probation**: A probation arrangement without regular reporting to an officer.
- **Leverage marriage**: Mitnick’s own description of marrying partly for legal and custodial advantages.
- **Cost transfer**: Burden shifted from the actor to family, partner, employer, or institution.

## Mental Models

Use “relationship as a leading indicator”: secrecy, isolation, and resource diversion reveal a problem before an arrest. Think of the human layer as the shortest path into a technical environment.

## Anti-patterns

- **Using a partner as an unconsenting legal shield**: It treats a relationship as equipment.
- **Assuming a sophisticated system requires a sophisticated attack**: A helpful employee can defeat a strong technical design.
- **Measuring harm only by files changed**: Financial, emotional, and legal consequences count.

## Worked Example

Mitnick reaches SCO by persuading a secretary to reveal an account name and change its password to one he supplies. He locates source code but does not take it because the transfer would be too slow. Even without data removal, the company raids the apartment, Bonnie is questioned, and the couple spends their savings on lawyers. The episode shows why “nothing was damaged” is an inadequate risk measure.

## Key Takeaways

1. Audit the cost an obsession imposes on people who did not consent to it.
2. Test human support workflows as seriously as technical controls.
3. Define harm broadly: access, fear, expense, and loss of control all matter.
