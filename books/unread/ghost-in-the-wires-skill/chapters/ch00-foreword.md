# Foreword: Steve Wozniak

## Core Idea

Steve Wozniak frames Kevin Mitnick’s story through the tension between technical ability, motive, and consequence. He remembers Mitnick as someone who broke into corporate systems for the challenge, did not destroy files, and did not use or sell the financial information he could reach, while still acknowledging that the behavior was unauthorized. That distinction explains why the memoir cannot be read as a simple hero story: curiosity may be genuine, but access without consent still creates risk for owners, employees, and investigators. Wozniak also emphasizes Mitnick’s later ability to turn the same fascination toward security work, public explanation, and friendship. Use this framing when evaluating an unusual technical talent: ask what the person is trying to learn, what harm the method creates, and whether the work is authorized.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Motive–impact–authorization test**: Separate why someone acts, what their action changes, and whether they had permission.
  - When to use: When a technically impressive act is being praised or condemned too broadly.
  - How: Name the motive, inventory actual effects, then check consent and scope.

## Key Concepts

- **Challenge motive**: Acting to solve a difficult technical puzzle rather than to obtain money.
- **Non-destructive access**: Reaching a system without intentionally deleting or damaging files; not the same as harmlessness.
- **Technical wizardry**: Skill that can be used either to defeat or improve security.
- **Myth-making**: Public narratives that simplify a complicated person into a symbol.

## Mental Models

Think of motive, impact, and authorization as three separate axes; a favorable motive cannot cancel unauthorized access. Use the “translation” model: the same capability becomes constructive when placed inside a consented security engagement.

## Anti-patterns

- **Equating non-destruction with permission**: The owner still loses control and may face disclosure, disruption, or liability.
- **Reducing a person to a media label**: A headline can hide both the technical facts and the human cost.

## Worked Example

Wozniak recalls driving a route whose apparently random GPS trace spelled “FREE KEVIN” on television. The stunt illustrates how technical play can carry a social message and why Wozniak’s support was personal, not merely an endorsement of intrusion. It also foreshadows the memoir’s later reversal, in which Mitnick’s public identity changes from wanted hacker to security professional.

## Key Takeaways

1. Evaluate capability, motive, impact, and authorization separately.
2. Do not confuse an absence of obvious damage with ethical legitimacy.
3. Look for ways to redirect curiosity into consented defensive work.
