# Chapter 6: Will Hack for Love

## Core Idea

Mitnick returns to computing through Lenny DiCicco, who demonstrates that access to a connected system can be gained through a friendly security guard and a persuasive institutional role. The chapter then shifts to formal learning: a technical school gives Mitnick a path toward legitimate computer work, while hacking continues in the background. He is eventually expelled after an unexplained computer incident, and later receives a job offer from a telephone company that is publicly undercut by a false press release. The episode is important because Mitnick denies creating the release, illustrating how a reputation can generate assumed actions and how one person’s deception can contaminate another person’s identity. The chapter’s title also captures the personal cost: his relationship with Bonnie becomes entangled with the secrecy and time demands of the obsession.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Access-by-association**: A person’s familiarity with an employee can substitute for a real access check.
  - When to use: When reviewing visitor, escort, and contractor processes.
  - How: Require independent identity and authorization at each transition.
- **Reputation cascade**: A known label causes observers to attribute unrelated events to the same person.
  - When to use: During incident response or media/legal review.
  - How: Separate evidence from prior reputation and test alternative explanations.

## Key Concepts

- **ARPANET**: The connected research network Lenny reaches through Hughes.
- **Assembler language**: Low-level programming that gives close control of machine details.
- **Circumstantial evidence**: Evidence that suggests involvement without directly proving the act.
- **Myth of the hacker**: A public identity that can outlive and distort specific facts.

## Mental Models

Use “trust does not travel” when a visitor is introduced by a friendly employee. Use a base-rate check in investigations: reputation can be a lead, but it cannot be the conclusion.

## Anti-patterns

- **Letting one employee vouch for the whole visit**: Association is not authorization.
- **Assuming the most notorious person caused every related event**: This creates false positives and weakens investigations.
- **Hiding a consuming activity inside a relationship**: Secrecy transfers the cost to people who did not choose the risk.

## Worked Example

Lenny arranges for Mitnick to enter Hughes as a DEC employee and reach a VAX connected to ARPANET. The session produces little useful information, but the access itself is treated as a triumph. Later, a false bank press release is blamed on Mitnick after he mentions a lost job offer, even though he denies creating it. These paired incidents show both sides of access-by-association: a real breach can be enabled by trust, and a reputation can also create an imagined breach.

## Key Takeaways

1. Verify every visitor independently, even when a known employee is enthusiastic.
2. Investigate evidence before using reputation as an explanation.
3. Treat secrecy and obsession as relationship risks, not private quirks.
