# Cheatsheet

## Core distinctions

| Distinction | Use it to ask |
|---|---|
| Capability vs authorization | Who granted permission, for what, and until when? |
| Possibility vs occurrence | Did we demonstrate a weakness, observe an event, or prove history? |
| Correlation vs attribution | Are signals independent, and what exactly do they identify? |
| Rapport vs identity proof | What trusted record verifies the person? |
| Legal result vs public story | Which timeline and source are we describing? |

## Human request decision rule

| Signal | Safe response |
|---|---|
| Urgency, role, jargon, or familiarity | Pause; use a known callback and independent role check |
| Request for sensitive data | Minimize disclosure; verify necessity and recipient |
| Request to bypass a normal workflow | Require owner approval and record the exception |
| “I am correcting an error” | Treat it as a new change request |
| Second person confirms using the same source | Find an independent source |

## Identity proofing

1. Verify at enrollment, recovery, privilege change, and retirement.
2. Compare records from independent origins across time and context.
3. Protect recovery channels as carefully as primary credentials.
4. Treat a business card, confident story, or friendly familiarity as context—not proof.

## Incident reasoning

1. Preserve the observation and its timestamp.
2. List at least two plausible explanations.
3. Grade each source for independence, authority, and reliability.
4. Set a response threshold before certainty arrives.
5. Separate containment, investigation, notification, and attribution.

## Authorized test gates

| Gate | Required question |
|---|---|
| Owner | Who authorized the work? |
| Scope | Which systems, accounts, dates, and methods are included? |
| Safety | What data is prohibited, and how is test data handled? |
| Stop | What event pauses the test, and who can call it? |
| Report | How will feasibility, observation, and attribution be labeled? |

## Signals worth reviewing

- Dormant accounts, access paths, or recovery channels change.
- Unexpected storage, device, call, or login patterns appear.
- A public claim begins to drive operational decisions.
- A person maintains many unexplained identities or references.
- A legacy service trusts location or familiarity instead of authentication.

## Quick formula

`capability + authorization + scope + accountability = legitimate security work`

`observed event + independent corroboration + documented authority = defensible conclusion`
