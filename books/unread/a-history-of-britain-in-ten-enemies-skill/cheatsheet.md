# Cheatsheet

## Decision rules

| If you observe… | Ask / do… | Book anchor |
|---|---|---|
| A group becomes cohesive during danger | Identify which internal differences the enemy frame suppresses and who gains authority | Ch 0, 2, 3 |
| A famous patriotic story explains a victory too neatly | Check source date, motive, numbers, terrain, weather, and competing accounts | Ch 1, 4, 5, 8, 10 |
| A state hires foreign fighters | Model payment, land, leverage, settlement, and the moment the ally becomes a ruler | Ch 2, 4 |
| Rivals unite against one enemy | Design the postwar settlement before calling the coalition a nation | Ch 1, 3, 8 |
| An island is called naturally safe or special | Audit ships, food, tides, weather, landing sites, and retreat | Ch 1, 4, 5, 10 |
| An empire calls itself civilized or defensive | Trace land, labour, treasure, monopoly, and who absorbs the harm | Ch 1, 5, 6, 7, 9 |
| A hero dominates the memorials | Add the sailors, workers, civilians, prisoners, and sick people missing from the story | Ch 5, 6, 8, 10 |
| A payment buys temporary peace | Ask whether it removes the threat or finances the next demand | Ch 3 |
| A revolutionary claim says “all” | List who is excluded in law and practice | Ch 7, 9, 11 |
| A former enemy becomes an ally | Find the new threat, institution, or shared practice that changed the category | Ch 4, 8, 10, 11 |

## Recall chronology

1. Prehistory: migration, Beaker people, Celts, and no pure “indigenous” Britain.
2. 55/54 BC: Caesar lands, meets resistance, and withdraws.
3. AD 43–84: Claudius, Caratacus, Boudicca, Watling Street, and Hadrian's frontier.
4. c. 410–597: Roman withdrawal, Saxon settlement, Augustine's mission.
5. 793–1066: Viking raids, Alfred, Danelaw, Cnut, Stamford Bridge.
6. 1066–1453: Norman conquest, Harrying, Hundred Years' War, Joan of Arc.
7. 1492–1739: Spanish conquest, Atlantic slavery, Drake, Armada, asiento, Jenkins' Ear.
8. 1568–1902: Dutch revolt, company empires, Anglo-Dutch wars, Camperdown, Boer Wars.
9. 1587–1815: North American settlement, Revolution, War of 1812, New Orleans.
10. 1854–2022: Crimea, Great Game, revolution, Cold War, and the Russian invasion of Ukraine.
11. 1170–1998: Ireland's Norman rule, plantation, famine, partition, Troubles, Good Friday Agreement.
12. 1871–after 1945: German empire, world wars, Blitz, Trautmann, and reclassification.

## Myth checks

| Remembered as… | Also check… |
|---|---|
| “Caesar conquered Britain” | He made two expeditions and withdrew; logistics and tribal alliances mattered |
| “Harold simply took an arrow in the eye” | Bayeux image and later accounts conflict; source motives matter |
| “The Armada ended Spanish invasion” | Three later armadas failed; 1588 involved planning, fireships, guns, and storms |
| “Agincourt was tiny English versus a giant French host” | Modern estimates, Welsh proportion, V-sign story, and ransom records complicate it |
| “The Light Brigade proved British character” | The charge followed a misinterpreted order; the poem made error noble |
| “The Blitz Spirit was universal” | Cardiff courage coexisted with terror, class privilege, looting, and abuse |

## Minimal application loop

1. Name the enemy claim and the national benefit it promises.
2. Separate evidence, legend, interpretation, and joke.
3. Map who gains unity, land, money, or authority.
4. Record the absent cost and the people excluded.
5. Ask what would make today's enemy tomorrow's partner.
