# Glossary

**1066** — Year of Stamford Bridge and the Norman landing at Hastings; used as a hinge between Viking and Norman rule (Ch 3, Ch 4).

**Act of Union** — 1707 union of England, Wales, and Scotland, followed by the 1800 union with Ireland (Ch 9, Ch 10).

**Alfred the Great** — Wessex king who used retreat, fortresses, troop rotation, a settlement with Guthrum, and naval reform (Ch 3).

**Anglo-Saxon Chronicle** — Annalistic source from the 800s onward; important but West-Saxon-biased and mixed with legend and error (Ch 2).

**Armada** — Spanish invasion fleet of 1588; defeated through English harassment, fireships, Spanish mistakes, and storms (Ch 5).

**Asiento** — Contract licensing British merchants to sell slaves in Spanish America (Ch 5).

**Balance of Power** — British policy of preventing one European state from becoming the dominant bully (Ch 8).

**Blitz / Blitz Spirit** — German bombing campaign and the later memory of British civilian endurance; the chapter also records terror and inequality (Ch 10).

**Blitzkrieg** — German “Lightning War,” combining air attack and tanks for rapid breakthrough (Ch 10).

**Bloody Sunday** — 1972 Derry shooting in which fourteen Catholic protestors were killed by British soldiers (Ch 9).

**Boston Massacre** — 1770 confrontation in which British troops killed five people after being provoked by a mob (Ch 7).

**Boston Tea Party** — 1773 destruction of 342 chests of East India Company tea in protest against monopoly and taxation (Ch 7).

**Britannia** — Personified island and warrior guardian, later a symbol of defiance and naval power (Ch 0, Ch 1).

**Boudicca** — Iceni queen who united tribes, destroyed Roman settlements, and lost at Watling Street (Ch 1).

**Calgacus** — Pict leader whose anti-Roman speech appears in Tacitus; Deary notes it may have been invented (Ch 1).

**Cassivellaunus** — Celtic leader who briefly united tribes against Caesar before alliances and Roman pressure defeated him (Ch 1).

**Cnut the Great** — Danish king who ruled England, Denmark, and Norway and became the first temporary unifier of England (Ch 3).

**Danegeld** — Money paid to Vikings to leave England; short-term relief that could reward recurring extortion (Ch 3).

**Danelaw** — North-eastern English territory retained by Danish rule after Alfred's settlement with Guthrum (Ch 3).

**Divide and conquer** — Strategy of exploiting rival groups so an invader can defeat a coalition piecemeal (Ch 1).

**Entente Cordiale** — 1904 British-French agreement that ended their long pattern of belligerence (Ch 4, Ch 10).

**Enemy's enemy** — Alliance of convenience between groups that normally distrust each other; not necessarily a durable friendship (Ch 1, Ch 3, Ch 8).

**East India Company** — Chartered company with armed forces, colonial authority, monopoly interests, and records of slavery, famine, and opium trade (Ch 6, Ch 7).

**Fishguard** — 1797 Welsh landing where weather, drunkenness, empty cannon, and local resistance defeated French troops (Ch 4).

**Fireships** — Burning vessels used to scatter the Spanish Armada at Calais (Ch 5).

**Flota de Indias** — Spanish West Indies or Silver Fleet convoy carrying colonial wealth (Ch 5).

**French Revolution** — 1789 upheaval whose liberty language and Terror reshaped European politics (Ch 4).

**Good Friday Agreement** — 1998 settlement that formally ended the Troubles and enabled power sharing (Ch 9).

**Great Game** — Anglo-Russian rivalry for influence in Central Asia during the nineteenth century (Ch 8).

**Hadrian's Wall** — Roman frontier marking the northern limit of sustained conquest in Britain (Ch 1).

**Harrying of the North** — William's punitive devastation of northern England after rebellion (Ch 4).

**Home Rule** — Irish demand for self-government that preceded partition and the Irish Free State (Ch 9).

**Joan of Arc** — French leader whose victories and martyrdom helped turn regional loyalties into French national identity (Ch 4).

**Largs** — 1263 Scottish victory and settlement that marked the end of much Norse influence in the islands (Ch 3).

**Light Brigade** — Cavalry sent against Russian guns at Balaclava after a misinterpreted order (Ch 8).

**Magna Carta** — 1215 peace agreement between King John and barons, later enlarged into a democracy myth (Ch 1).

**Mercenary blowback** — Risk that hired foreign troops acquire land, leverage, and interests of their own (Ch 2).

**Night of the Long Knives** — Reported Saxon massacre of British leaders at a peace conference (Ch 2).

**Normans** — Viking-descended rulers from northern France who conquered England in 1066 (Ch 3, Ch 4).

**Operation Sea Lion** — German plan to invade Britain, dependent on air and naval superiority that Germany failed to secure (Ch 10).

**Pale** — Fortified English zone around Dublin; source of “beyond the pale” (Ch 9).

**Plantation of Ulster** — Transfer of Catholic Irish land to English and Scottish Protestant settlers (Ch 9).

**Picts** — Northern peoples who resisted Roman rule through guerrilla warfare (Ch 1).

**Roman afterlife** — Continuation of Roman influence through Christianity, papal authority, tithes, and church politics after the army left (Ch 1).

**Source bias** — Distortion created by a hostile, interested, later, or politically positioned witness (Ch 0, Ch 1, Ch 2, Ch 4, Ch 10).

**Splendid Isolation** — British preference for avoiding binding alliances while defending imperial interests (Ch 8).

**Stamford Bridge** — Harold Godwinson's 1066 victory over Harald Hardrada and Tostig (Ch 3).

**Watling Street** — Battle where Roman terrain and formation tactics defeated Boudicca's larger force (Ch 1).

**William the Conqueror** — Norman duke who won Hastings and imposed land transfer, castles, and coercive rule (Ch 4).

**Vikings** — Scandinavian raiders, traders, settlers, and slave dealers whose pressure helped unify Britain and Ireland (Ch 3).

**Wolfe Tone** — Protestant founder of the United Irishmen who sought French-backed rebellion against English rule (Ch 9).
