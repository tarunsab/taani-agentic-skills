# Glossary

**Abstraction** — A reusable boundary that hides complexity while exposing useful capability (Ch 8).

**A/B test** — A randomized comparison of a control and treatment on a defined outcome (Ch 6).

**Activation energy** — The friction between deciding to work and taking the first concrete action (Ch 3).

**Bus factor** — How many key people can become unavailable before a team cannot continue (Ch 10).

**Canary** — A limited rollout used to detect a problem before broad exposure (Ch 4).

**Continuous delivery** — A release practice that keeps changes small, tested, observable, and reversible (Ch 4).

**Economic denominator** — The denominator in a ratio that connects activity to meaningful value or outcome (Ch 5).

**Estimate** — A forecast of likely work under stated uncertainty, distinct from a desired target (Ch 7).

**Fail fast** — Reject invalid input, configuration, or state near its source before damage spreads (Ch 9).

**Fungibility** — The condition that multiple people can perform an important task (Ch 10).

**Growth mindset** — The view that skills can be developed through effort, challenge, and feedback (Ch 2).

**Idempotence** — Repeating an operation produces the same result as performing it once (Ch 9).

**Implementation intention** — An if-then plan connecting a cue to a specific action (Ch 3).

**Iteration speed** — The time from making a change to receiving trustworthy feedback (Ch 4).

**Leverage** — Value or impact produced per unit of time invested (Ch 0, Ch 1).

**Long click** — A search behavior used as a proxy for a result satisfying the user (Ch 5).

**Maker’s schedule** — Long, uninterrupted blocks for construction, debugging, and design (Ch 3).

**Milestone** — A measurable checkpoint that demonstrates progress toward a project goal (Ch 7).

**MVP** — The smallest credible experiment that maximizes validated learning, not merely the fewest features (Ch 6).

**Operational burden** — The recurring work required to run, maintain, support, and transfer a system (Ch 9).

**Pareto principle** — The observation that a minority of causes can account for a majority of effects (Ch 1).

**Post-mortem** — A blameless analysis of what happened, why, and how to prevent or recover next time (Ch 10).

**Practical significance** — A change large enough to matter for the decision and its scale (Ch 6).

**P95/P99** — High-percentile latency measures that expose tail experience rather than the average (Ch 5).

**Reentrant process** — A process that can safely resume after interruption (Ch 9).

**Rollback** — A fast return to a known-good version after a harmful change (Ch 4).

**Technical debt** — Deferred design, test, or maintenance work that creates future cost (Ch 8).

**Target** — A desired business outcome or date, distinct from an engineering forecast (Ch 7).

**Validation** — Evidence gathering that tests a consequential assumption before large commitment (Ch 6).

**Work in progress** — Started but unfinished work that consumes context and capacity (Ch 3).
