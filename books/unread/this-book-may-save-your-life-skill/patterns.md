# Patterns and Techniques

## Interconnected-system check
**When to use**: A symptom or intervention seems confined to one organ.
**How**: Ask which neighboring systems share blood flow, nerves, pressure, hormones, or microbial ecology; trace the likely feedback loop before choosing a fix.
**Trade-offs**: The model catches cascading failures but can encourage overinterpretation if every ordinary symptom is treated as systemic.

Rajan repeatedly uses surgery and anatomy to show that local events propagate: bleeding changes circulation and breathing, gut microbes signal the brain, and sleep changes metabolism and immunity. Use the pattern to widen the frame, not to replace clinical assessment.

## Pattern-over-product diet
**When to use**: Comparing diets, supplements, or “superfoods.”
**How**: Build a repeatable pattern around diverse plant foods, fiber, wholegrains, nuts, seeds, fermented foods, and suitable sources of omega-3; reduce ultra-processed foods, excess alcohol, smoking, and tobacco exposure.
**Trade-offs**: The book rejects a single perfect diet and acknowledges food access and privilege; a healthy pattern can still include occasional treats.

## Work with the body clock
**When to use**: Sleep timing, jet lag, weekend lie-ins, or night shifts are unstable.
**How**: Anchor waking and sleeping times, use bright natural light soon after waking, dim light and stimulation before bed, and use darkness/noise control for daytime sleep after a night shift.
**Trade-offs**: Chronotypes differ, and short-term shift work cannot be made biologically identical to daytime work.

## 20/20/20 screen break
**When to use**: Sustained near-screen work or eye fatigue.
**How**: Every 20 minutes, look at an object at least 20 feet away for up to 20 seconds, preferably with some outdoor natural light.
**Trade-offs**: It reduces near-focus strain but cannot prevent every eye disease or substitute for an eye examination.

## Knees-higher-than-hips defecation
**When to use**: Straining, prolonged toilet sitting, or difficult stool passage.
**How**: Raise the feet on a stable stool or lean forward so the torso is roughly 35–60 degrees to the hips; relax rather than force the pelvic floor.
**Trade-offs**: The book draws on a small 2003 position study; individual bowel patterns vary and persistent change needs assessment.

## Breath reset
**When to use**: Short-lived stress or panic escalation.
**How**: Slow the breath with relaxed posture and diaphragmatic movement; Rajan also describes the mammalian diving reflex from cold water on the face as a physiological reset.
**Trade-offs**: This is not a treatment for serious breathing difficulty, chest pain, or an underlying anxiety or lung disorder.

## Sensory mismatch correction
**When to use**: Motion sickness, anticipated needle pain, or a visually confusing movement.
**How**: Align inputs where possible: look at the horizon in a moving vehicle, look away during a cannula, or move with changing visual information to train balance.
**Trade-offs**: Prediction and distraction can modulate some experiences but do not eliminate every pain or vestibular disorder.

## Microbiome diversity loop
**When to use**: Supporting gut, immune, or skin ecology.
**How**: Increase variety and fiber gradually; include plant foods and fermented foods; preserve useful microbial exposure; use antibiotics only for appropriate bacterial disease.
**Trade-offs**: The book stresses that microbiome science remains incomplete and that “boosting” immunity can be harmful.

## Bladder threshold reset
**When to use**: Frequent urgency caused by habitual “just-in-case” voiding, after appropriate medical review.
**How**: Gradually lengthen intervals toward normal capacity rather than restricting fluids; relax the pelvic floor during urination and consider structured bladder retraining.
**Trade-offs**: Urinary pain, blood, poor flow, incontinence, or persistent urgency can signal infection, obstruction, nerve problems, or another condition.

## Red-flag inspection
**When to use**: A recurring body output or sensory symptom changes.
**How**: Observe stool color/form, urine appearance, breath odor, tongue changes, floaters/flashes, lumps, bleeding, and functional decline; separate common variation from sudden or persistent change.
**Trade-offs**: These observations are clues, not diagnoses. Escalate rather than self-treat when the pattern is sudden, severe, persistent, or associated with systemic illness.

## Maintenance before miracle
**When to use**: A health claim promises a single shortcut.
**How**: First check the durable basics—sleep, movement, nutrition, hydration, tobacco/alcohol, stress, hygiene, and sun protection—then consider a narrowly targeted hack with a stated mechanism and limit.
**Trade-offs**: Maintenance is slower and less marketable, but the book presents it as the more reliable default.
