# Glossary

**Adaptive immune response** — Learned, targeted defense using B cells, T cells, antibodies, and immune memory (Ch 13).

**Alveoli** — Microscopic lung air sacs surrounded by capillaries where oxygen and carbon dioxide exchange occurs (Ch 4).

**Angiogenesis** — Growth of small blood vessels; Rajan presents aerobic exercise as one way this may support coronary blood supply (Ch 3).

**Anosmia** — Loss of the sense of smell, often temporary after infection but sometimes a marker requiring review (Ch 8).

**Barotrauma** — Injury or discomfort caused by pressure imbalance, especially between the middle ear and the environment (Ch 7).

**Bristol Stool Chart** — Pictorial scale used to relate stool form to likely constipation or diarrhea patterns (Ch 1).

**Chronotype** — A person's genetically influenced tendency toward earlier or later sleep and activity timing (Ch 12).

**Circadian rhythm** — Roughly 24-hour biological cycle coordinated by the suprachiasmatic nucleus and environmental light (Ch 12).

**Clinical death** — Cessation of breathing and blood flow that can sometimes be reversed (Ch 14).

**Cognitive reserve** — Brain capacity to resist the functional effects of aging, supported in the book by mental and social stimulation (Ch 2).

**Concussion** — Mild traumatic brain injury caused by the brain being shaken inside the skull; repeated injuries increase risk (Ch 2).

**Defecation** — Coordinated emptying of the rectum through involuntary and voluntary sphincter control (Ch 1).

**Enteric nervous system** — Neuronal network embedded in the gastrointestinal tract that helps regulate digestion and communicates with the brain (Ch 1).

**Epiglottis** — Flap that helps protect the airway during swallowing; the shared throat is an evolutionary trade-off for speech (Ch 1).

**Eustachian tube** — Passage linking the middle ear to the nasal/sinus area and helping equalize pressure (Ch 7).

**Glymphatic system** — Brain waste-clearance pathway active during sleep, using cerebrospinal fluid movement (Ch 12).

**Homeostasis** — Coordinated internal balance among interdependent body systems (Introduction).

**Innate immune response** — Fast, broad defense formed by barriers, inflammation, phagocytosis, and interferons (Ch 13).

**Ischemia** — Temporary or prolonged loss of blood flow to tissue, associated in the book with severe pain and tissue injury (Ch 3, Ch 11).

**Macula / fovea centralis** — Central retinal regions specialized for detailed vision, making them high-value but vulnerable points (Ch 6).

**Microbiome** — Community of microorganisms living in or on the body; the book treats it as an ecological partner and functional organ (Ch 1, Ch 10, Ch 13).

**Nociceptor** — Receptor that detects potentially damaging stimuli; the brain constructs the experience of pain from such inputs and context (Ch 2).

**Otoliths** — Calcium-based “ear stones” that move in vestibular organs and help signal head position and movement (Ch 7).

**Peristalsis** — Alternating muscle contractions that propel food through the digestive tract independently of gravity (Ch 1).

**Proprioception** — Sense of joint position and body orientation without visual monitoring (Ch 5).

**Rectoanal inhibitory reflex (RAIR)** — Anal sampling mechanism that helps distinguish gas from solid rectal contents (Ch 1).

**Retinal detachment** — Separation of the retina from supporting tissue; a sudden rise in floaters with flashes is a warning sign (Ch 6).

**Sleep inertia** — Groggy, heavy feeling after abrupt awakening, especially from deep sleep (Ch 12).

**Suprachiasmatic nucleus** — Brain clock of roughly 20,000 neurons that coordinates circadian timing (Ch 12).

**Takotsubo cardiomyopathy** — Stress-induced weakening of the heart muscle, also called broken-heart syndrome (Ch 3).

**Vagus nerve** — Major nerve linking brain and body, including gut signaling and the stress-reducing diving reflex (Ch 1, Ch 2).

**Valsalva maneuver** — Exhaling against a closed airway to raise abdominal pressure during defecation (Ch 1).

**Vestibular apparatus** — Inner-ear system of canals and chambers that supports balance and coordination (Ch 7).

**Visceral intuition** — A felt judgment emerging from brain–gut processing of prior experience and partly unconscious information (Ch 1).
