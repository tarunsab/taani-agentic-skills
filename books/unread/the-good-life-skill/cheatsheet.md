# Cheatsheet

## Decision rules

| If… | Do… | Because… |
|---|---|---|
| You are choosing between a visible reward and a relational investment | Ask what the reward is for, then protect the relationship that gives it meaning | Money/status adapt; connection has durable health and meaning effects |
| A connection leaves you better than alone | Appreciate it and schedule recurring contact | Good relationships need exercise, not wishful maintenance |
| A relationship feels depleting | Ask whether it needs repair, a boundary, less contact, or safe exit | “Depleting” is a prompt for judgment, not an automatic command |
| You feel a fast surge of anger, fear, or shame | Run W.I.S.E.R.: Watch → Interpret → Select → Engage → Reflect | Emotion is a signal; the first impulse is not the only response |
| You keep thinking “later” about a call or visit | Put a specific recurring plan on the calendar now | Time is finite and unknown; later often disappears |
| You are online and merely observing | Try active contact, then check how you feel | Passive comparison tends to reduce well-being; active connection can lift it |
| Work stress follows you home | Name it, decompress briefly, then ask and listen | Unacknowledged emotions spill into irritability or withdrawal |
| Family members seem fixed in old roles | Look for one changed or unexpectedly kind behavior | The “You Always / You Never” story blocks corrective experience |
| A friend or colleague seems unlike you | Ask a genuine question and follow the answer | Curiosity builds understanding, weak ties, and opportunity |

## Quick social-fitness audit

1. List the people who shape your life, including casual and work contacts.
2. For each, note contact frequency and energizing/depleting effect.
3. Mark support received/given: safety, growth, confiding, identity, romance, practical help, fun.
4. Strengthen one good tie, nudge one neglected tie, and choose one difficult tie for reflection.
5. Recheck yearly or after a major life transition.

## W.I.S.E.R. prompts

| Stage | Ask |
|---|---|
| Watch | What is happening in the whole context? What is my body doing? |
| Interpret | What is at stake? What am I assuming? What else could be true? |
| Select | What outcome do I want? What options, resources, timing, and constraints exist? |
| Engage | Can I practice, choose a workable setting, and adjust as I go? |
| Reflect | What helped or harmed? What will I repeat or change? |

## Thresholds and study signals

- **~40%** — cited estimate for the share of happiness associated with intentional activity; treat as a hopeful estimate, not a personal equation.
- **~50%** — association between social connection and increased odds of surviving a given year in the large review cited by the authors.
- **~85%** — accuracy with which untrained observers’ emotion ratings predicted which couples remained together in one study.
- **~Half** — waking moments in a large smartphone-sampling study spent thinking about something other than the current activity.
- **Age 50 → 80** — relationship satisfaction predicted later mental and physical health better than middle-aged cholesterol in the Harvard Study analysis.

## Tells and smells

- “I’ll reconnect when life slows down.” → relationship neglect disguised as planning.
- “My partner should be everything.” → other friendships, interests, or community may need rebuilding.
- “That’s just how I am / how my childhood made me.” → influence mistaken for fate.
- “I left work at work.” → check the evening behavior, not the intention.
- “We never fight.” → ask whether peace is hiding distance or unspoken vulnerability.
- “I’m connected online.” → compare active, embodied contact with passive consumption and mood afterward.
