# Glossary

**Affective forecasting** — Predicting how a future event or choice will make you feel; the book emphasizes that people often misjudge connection and overestimate social discomfort (Ch 2).

**Attachment style** — A learned pattern for seeking, giving, or avoiding closeness and comfort, visible in childhood and relevant to adult relationships (Ch 7).

**Corrective experience** — A later, positive relationship or event that contradicts an earlier harmful expectation and makes new ways of relating possible (Ch 8).

**Eudaimonia / eudaimonic happiness** — Enduring well-being grounded in meaning, purpose, and a sense that life is valuable, rather than momentary pleasure (Ch 1).

**Energizing relationship** — A connection that generally leaves a person feeling more alive, connected, and belonging than before the interaction (Ch 4).

**Generativity** — Extending concern, care, creativity, and influence beyond the self toward people, purposes, and future generations (Ch 3, 9, conclusion).

**Hedonia / hedonic happiness** — Fleeting pleasure or a good moment, contrasted with the durable value of eudaimonic well-being (Ch 1).

**Loneliness** — The painful gap between the social contact a person wants and the contact they actually experience; it is subjective, not simply living alone (Ch 1, 4).

**Prospective longitudinal study** — Research that follows people through time and records life as it unfolds rather than relying mainly on later recall (Ch 1).

**Reflective listening** — Listening without immediate commentary, then restating a partner’s experience without judgment and checking whether it is accurate (Ch 7).

**Secure base** — A trusted relationship that provides safety and comfort while allowing a person to explore, take risks, and return for support (Ch 7).

**Social clock** — Cultural expectations about when events such as leaving home, partnering, or having children are supposed to occur (Ch 3).

**Social fitness** — The ongoing attention, exercise, and reflection required to maintain relationships that support well-being (Ch 4).

**Social universe** — A personal map of relationships organized by frequency of contact and the felt quality of being energized or depleted (Ch 4).

**Stuckness** — A repetitive pattern of thoughts, feelings, or interactions that narrows options and keeps a person from moving forward (Ch 6).

**Weak tie** — A casual or peripheral relationship that may provide daily uplift, bridges to new people and information, or access to opportunities (Ch 10).

**W.I.S.E.R. model** — Watch, Interpret, Select, Engage, Reflect: a way to slow emotional reactions and choose a more purposeful response (Ch 6).
