# Essay 13: 101 Things More Worth Thinking About than Whatever’s Consuming You

## Core Idea

This essay redirects attention away from the obsession, grievance, or uncertainty currently consuming it and toward questions that enlarge a life. The prompts move across desired daily experience, self-respect, memory, work, relationships, mortality, creativity, purpose, embodiment, culture, and love. Wiest repeatedly chooses concrete presence over abstract rumination: what you will create, eat, and share today; what your future self would say; what your patterns reveal; and what “enough” means. The list also treats attention as formative—what you read, listen to, admire, resist, and repeatedly think about helps construct the self. It is best used as a deliberately broad reflection inventory, not as a demand to answer every prompt at once.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Attention redirection**
  - When to use: Rumination has narrowed your world to one problem or fear.
  - How: Choose a prompt that reconnects you with the present, a value, a relationship, a capability, or a future action.
- **Pattern over story**
  - When to use: You are trying to understand yourself or another person through a single event.
  - How: Examine repeated actions, relationships, and choices rather than the image or explanation attached to them.
- **Enough and objective**
  - When to use: “More” has no stopping point and fulfillment keeps receding.
  - How: Define what enough money, love, productivity, and purpose mean, then direct minutes toward the larger objective.
- **Conceive the realized self**
  - When to use: You cannot picture the person or life you are trying to build.
  - How: Describe how that self thinks, loves, gives thanks, and acts; use the image as the first step toward embodiment.

## Key Concepts

- **Attention**: The limited mental resource that can be invested in rumination or in life-building questions.
- **Pattern**: What repeated behavior reveals more reliably than a person’s imagined character.
- **Big objective**: A general direction for the limited time available.
- **Enough**: A personally defined threshold that prevents endless seeking.
- **Future self**: A later perspective used to evaluate present action and priorities.
- **Shadow quality**: A trait admired or hated in others that may reveal one’s own accepted or resisted qualities.
- **Micro-wonder**: An ordinary sensory detail that restores contact with lived experience.
- **Conception**: Forming a clear image of the most realized self before acting into it.

## Anti-patterns

- **Rumination monopoly**: Letting one problem consume attention crowds out action, relationship, and wonder.
- **Abstract self-improvement**: Thinking about change without naming a small daily behavior keeps the future imaginary.
- **Image-based judgment**: Evaluating people by a constructed emotional picture ignores repeated behavior.
- **Undefined “more”**: Without enough or an objective, the mind turns every achievement into another demand.

## Key Takeaways

1. Redirect attention toward questions that produce agency and connection.
2. Use patterns, not isolated feelings, to understand people and yourself.
3. Define enough before endless wanting defines it for you.
4. Remember what is already present and what you once wished to have.
5. Begin with a vivid conception of the person you want to become.
