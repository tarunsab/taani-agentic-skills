# Essay 23: Everything Is Here to Help You: How Intrinsically Motivated People Become the Best Versions of Themselves

## Core Idea

Wiest calls the belief that everything is here to help us a powerful liberation because it changes the meaning of events, feelings, and obstacles. A person can see themselves as a victim of what happens or as someone given opportunities to grow, see differently, and expand; the same fork applies to discomfort, which can be treated as suffering or as a signal to learn from. This is the difference between extrinsic motivation—experience imposed from outside—and intrinsic meaning, in which the person participates in creating the interpretation and response. Wiest does not claim certainty about a higher plan; she argues that people who create happier lives often choose the useful interpretation anyway. Since suffering is unavoidable, the practical alternative is to give difficulty a purpose and grow through it rather than sit in discomfort without meaning.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Everything-as-helpful lens**
  - When to use: An obstacle or painful feeling is consuming you as proof that life is against you.
  - How: Ask what it can teach, change, or make visible, then act on the lesson without pretending the event was pleasant.
- **Intrinsic versus extrinsic meaning**
  - When to use: Other people’s ideas, products, or authority have become the source of your direction.
  - How: Reclaim interpretation and responsibility for creating meaning in the life actually being lived.
- **Purpose for suffering**
  - When to use: Pain feels pointless and therefore unbearable.
  - How: Choose a purpose that makes the difficulty a path toward growth, peace, or a more capable self.

## Key Concepts

- **Intrinsic motivation**: Meaning and direction generated through one’s own interpretation and chosen response.
- **Extrinsic motivation**: Behavior organized around forces, judgments, or desires imposed from outside.
- **Victim lens**: The assumption that events only happen to and diminish the person.
- **Opportunity lens**: The assumption that events can supply information, growth, or a new choice.
- **Purpose of suffering**: A meaning framework that makes unavoidable difficulty usable.

## Mental Models

- When certainty about cosmic purpose is unavailable, choose the interpretation that increases agency and humane action.
- Ask “What can this help me see?” rather than “Why is this happening to me?”—without denying harm.
- Treat a feeling as a signal, not as a sentence imposed by the world.

## Anti-patterns

- **Using purpose to romanticize harm**: A helpful interpretation does not make abuse, loss, or injustice good.
- **Forfeiting power to external authority**: Victimhood can become a way of letting other people’s goals direct life.
- **Waiting for proof of a plan**: The essay treats higher purpose as uncertain; action cannot depend on certainty.

## Key Takeaways

1. Choose an interpretation that makes growth and responsibility possible.
2. Turn discomfort into information without pretending it is pleasant.
3. Create meaning where certainty is unavailable.
4. Let hard experiences develop you rather than define you as powerless.
