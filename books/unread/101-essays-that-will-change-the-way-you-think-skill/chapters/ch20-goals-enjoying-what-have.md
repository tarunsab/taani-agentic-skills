# Essay 20: Goals to Set That Are More About Enjoying What You Have Than Chasing What You Don’t

## Core Idea

Milestones mark evolution but do not automatically create emotional fulfillment, so Wiest proposes goals that deepen appreciation for the life already available. The list moves from finishing what is half-used and caring for current friendships to noticing body function, enjoying inexpensive pleasures, documenting ordinary days, and giving purpose to existing work. It also includes practical order—assigning every belonging a home, living within means, spending less for a period, and doing important work early—because material and temporal structure can make appreciation easier. Several goals replace control and acquisition with meaning: love people as they are, create traditions, give things significance, and let go of what no longer serves. The overall method is to redirect ambition from “more possessions or a different life” toward a more present, intentional, and inhabitable life.

## Goal Set

1. Resume unfinished books, food, clothing, apologies, friendships, and projects.
2. Appreciate people as they are instead of judging or fixing them.
3. Invest in existing friends rather than counting toward a larger social total.
4. Record one thing the body enabled each day.
5. Learn to enjoy simple food, outdoor time, sleep, friendship, and good work.
6. Keep a one-sentence journal of days.
7. Infuse purpose into current work rather than waiting for ideal work.
8. Create personal holiday traditions.
9. Try a spend cleanse using what you already have.
10. Give every useful or beautiful belonging a home.
11. Live within means so higher income does not merely reproduce spending habits.
12. Call your mother when you have the privilege.
13. Become someone who gives things meaning rather than seeking objects to supply it.
14. Do the most important task in the morning.
15. Practice releasing small objects and associations that no longer serve you.
16. Pace activity by tracking breath, relaxation, presence, and quality over quantity.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Appreciation goal**
  - When to use: Resolutions are focused only on changing circumstances or acquiring more.
  - How: Set a practice that increases contact with what is already present.
- **Journal of days**
  - When to use: A full diary is unsustainable but you want to see the shape of a year.
  - How: Write one or two sentences before bed to preserve the day’s existence.
- **Spend cleanse**
  - When to use: Immediate consumption is crowding out more important goals.
  - How: Use what you own for a defined period and observe both restraint and sufficiency.
- **Home for each belonging**
  - When to use: Physical disorder makes attention and calm harder.
  - How: Keep what is purposeful or beautiful and assign it a return place.
- **Breath-paced living**
  - When to use: Speed has disconnected action from presence.
  - How: Use the ability to feel your breath as a cue to slow down and restore quality.

## Key Concepts

- **Milestone**: A marker of evolution, not a guaranteed source of fulfillment.
- **Appreciation practice**: A goal designed to deepen enjoyment of existing conditions.
- **Percentage habit**: The tendency for spending behavior to persist as income changes.
- **Purpose infusion**: Bringing meaning to current work instead of waiting for a perfect role.
- **Physical home**: A consistent place that makes order easier to maintain.
- **Quality over quantity**: Evaluating accomplishment by presence and depth rather than volume.

## Anti-patterns

- **Resolutions as escape**: Pursuing a different life can prevent inhabiting the current one.
- **Social tallying**: A larger network is not a reliable substitute for close connection.
- **Income without habit change**: More money does not automatically create financial freedom.
- **Speed as productivity**: Moving beyond breath and awareness can reduce the quality of the life being built.

## Key Takeaways

1. Make goals that increase appreciation, not only acquisition.
2. Use tiny records and physical order to make the present visible.
3. Turn current work, friendships, and belongings into sites of meaning.
4. Track the quality of action through breath, pace, and presence.
