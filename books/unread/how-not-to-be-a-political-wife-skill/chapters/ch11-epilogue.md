# Epilogue: What Remains

## Core Idea

The epilogue begins with Michael leaving politics in July 2024 after losing Surrey Heath and moving toward work at The Spectator, a role Vine connects to a childhood wish he once had for a magazine subscription. The marriage has already ended: they separated in 2021, divorced in 2022, and had spent years effectively living in separate bedrooms. Vine does not describe the divorce as a simple victory over Michael; she says they are now a close family, that the children understood the separation, and that lockdown exposed truths they had both been avoiding. Her sliding-door reflection is that opposing politics earlier might have preserved the marriage, but her deeper failure was suppressing challenge and becoming a bad partner while trying to be supportive. The final diagnosis is that politics is an addictive culture whose hunger for power can corrupt hearts and minds, while kindness is the remaining counterweight.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Hindsight without total exoneration.** Vine can now see choices that were hidden by excitement, fear, and the political schedule, but she does not use hindsight only to condemn Michael. She identifies her own people-pleasing, silence, self-blame, and failure to challenge as part of the marriage’s history. This does not erase the culture’s pressure; it makes responsibility shared and specific. A useful retrospective account names both agency and constraint.

**Politics as addiction.** The epilogue compares power to an appetite that promises one more achievement, office, or victory. Like the Turkish Delight offered to Edmund in *The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe*, politics can make betrayal feel justified by a small immediate reward. The metaphor explains why people continue after the costs are obvious and why winning one contest creates the need for another. It is a cultural diagnosis, not a claim that every politician is personally identical.

**Kindness as an operating principle.** The epigraph’s call for kindness answers the book’s recurring scenes of mockery, betrayal, status anxiety, and instrumental friendship. Kindness does not mean avoiding disagreement or excusing harmful decisions. It means remembering the human being, spouse, child, voter, colleague, and opponent beneath the role. In Vine’s account, it is the most credible alternative to a system that treats people as symbols and prey.

## Key Concepts

- **Divorce as a political consequence:** Vine says the real cause was politics and the way it changed their lives, not a single affair or personal villain.
- **Good separation:** The family remains close, the children are adults, and the former spouses can speak more frankly once the political role no longer organizes every exchange.
- **Sliding doors:** A different early boundary might have produced a different marriage, but the counterfactual cannot be proven; it is a tool for responsibility and grief.
- **Shared mechanisms, different marriages:** Vine notes that other political marriages also failed, while recognizing that their details and causes were not identical.
- **The rejected role:** She ends by asking what she wants, where she wants to live, and whether she wants to be a wife at all, rather than accepting “political wife” as a permanent identity.

## Mental Models

**The post-role audit.** When a public role ends, ask what remains of the relationship, family, work, home, health, and personal preferences. The answer may include genuine affection and a workable family even if the marriage cannot continue in its old form. This audit prevents the role’s end from being mistaken for the end of every relationship. It also helps identify which losses belonged to politics and which were choices within the marriage.

**The hunger loop.** Power offers status or purpose, demands sacrifice, produces new threats, and then promises that the next victory will make the sacrifice worthwhile. Because the reward is temporary, the loop restarts at a higher cost. The epilogue’s addiction metaphor explains the inability to stop even when a family is exhausted. A boundary interrupts the loop by making “enough” a legitimate political and personal outcome.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating divorce as proof that one spouse alone caused the family’s failure.
- Using hindsight to rewrite uncertainty as if the right decision was obvious at the time.
- Assuming political success can compensate indefinitely for lost health, trust, or safety.
- Making kindness equivalent to silence or surrender.

## Worked Example

Vine’s final reflection is the worked example. She imagines saying no to politics in the early years, then admits that the more immediate failure was her refusal to challenge Michael honestly and her inability to be the partner she wanted to be. Separation and divorce create space for a different relationship: the children can say that the marriage had effectively been over for years, and the former spouses speak more candidly without the office between them. The outcome does not redeem the costs, but it demonstrates that leaving a role can preserve family connection. The practical question is not only whether a partnership survives public life, but which parts of the relationship can be saved once public life stops directing it.

## Key Takeaways

- A relationship can end while family connection and affection remain.
- Retrospective responsibility should include self-critique as well as criticism of the political system.
- Public ambition can become an appetite that makes stopping feel like failure.
- Kindness is compatible with boundaries, truth, disagreement, and leaving.
