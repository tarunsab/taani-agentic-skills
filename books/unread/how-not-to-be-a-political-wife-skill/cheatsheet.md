# Cheatsheet

## Ten anti-lessons

1. **Lesson One:** Becoming one in the first place.
2. **Lesson Two:** Stay trapped in the incredulous feeling of not knowing how you arrived there.
3. **Lesson Three:** Allow yourself to be hijacked into a life for which you are unprepared.
4. **Lesson Four:** Assume fairness still functions in political vocabulary.
5. **Lesson Five:** Be the grown-up who is not impressed by empty trappings of power.
6. **Lesson Six:** Have children who are both seen and heard.
7. **Lesson Seven:** Step into the political coven and cease to be a person; become an object or prey.
8. **Lesson Eight:** Believe there is such a thing as a friend in politics.
9. **Lesson Nine:** Think you know better than politicians how to run things.
10. **Lesson Ten:** Trust no one.

## Fast questions

| Situation | Ask |
|---|---|
| A partner is offered political office | What changes for work, money, home, children, health, and the right to say no? |
| A political friend asks for silence | Is this protection, or compliance being renamed as loyalty? |
| Access feels glamorous | What is the price in time, privacy, finances, and family exposure? |
| A public attack begins | What must be corrected, what can be ignored, and what protects children now? |
| A campaign is winning | Who governs the next day, with which people, plan, and fallback? |
| A spouse’s health is failing | What support and independent space are needed before endurance becomes collapse? |
| A journalist asks for confirmation | Which facts are verified, what are the incentives, and who bears the consequences? |

## One-line model

Keep a self, tell the truth early, make the family’s costs visible, protect children from symbolism, and never confuse political access with friendship.
