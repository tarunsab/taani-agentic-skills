# Glossary

**Additional Costs Allowance (ACA)** — The parliamentary allowance discussed in the expenses episode as support for an MP’s additional residence costs. Vine presents the family’s use of it as legitimate in context, while acknowledging why the public saw the surrounding purchases as an abuse of public money.

**Barlby Road** — The North Kensington family home where Vine and Michael built their marriage, raised the children, entertained friends, and later returned after the first move to Surrey. It becomes both a practical address and an emotional anchor.

**Brexit** — The shorthand for Britain leaving the European Union. In the memoir it is at once a constitutional choice, a campaign, a friendship rupture, a family argument, and a long after-effect rather than a single event.

**Cameron Court / Camelot** — Vine’s names for the social and political world around David and Samantha Cameron, including Oxford friends, advisers, spouses, donors, journalists, and the media’s romanticized version of the group.

**Chatham House rule** — The social convention that permits frank discussion without attributing remarks publicly. Vine describes it as part of the trust that made the early circle feel open, though the circle still had tribal boundaries.

**“Cuckoo in the nest”** — Vine’s recurring outsider feeling: occupying a family, class, newsroom, or political setting while fearing that she does not truly belong there.

**“The frog”** — The metaphor for gradual normalization. A person can become accustomed to political exposure, pressure, and compromise so incrementally that the temperature is dangerous before it is recognized.

**Life After Victory** — The post-referendum planning that Vote Leave was expected to have. Vine uses its absence or incompleteness to show the danger of winning a high-stakes campaign without a settled governing plan or personnel.

**Lady Macbeth** — The gendered label applied to Vine after her intervention in Michael’s leadership decisions and a leaked email. She treats it as a way of turning a spouse into a sinister political archetype while obscuring the agency of the male politician.

**The Notting Hill Set** — The media label for the West London and Oxford-connected Conservative network associated with Cameron and Osborne. Vine separates the label’s mythology from the varied people and households inside it.

**Political wife / Westminster WAG** — Vine’s deliberately unstable role-name for the spouse expected to support a politician, host, absorb scrutiny, and remain personally invisible. The memoir’s title rejects the assumption that this should be a person’s whole identity.

**“Politics friends”** — Steve Hilton’s blunt distinction between the people one works with for political advantage and the relationships that survive outside the contest for office. The phrase becomes a warning about confusing proximity, loyalty, and friendship.

**Sanctioned eavesdropper** — Vine’s description of her position as a journalist close enough to overhear political life while also participating in it as Michael’s spouse. It captures the book’s insider-outsider stance.

**“Swarm”** — The nickname used for Rachel Whetstone in Vine’s account, reflecting her forceful, networked presence in the early circle. Her story also illustrates how quickly a political tribe can turn social exclusion into discipline.

**Toxic interface** — Vine’s phrase for the boundary where politics, media, family life, and public appetite meet. A domestic detail can become a national story, and a political dispute can enter a child’s school or home.

**Vote Leave** — The campaign organization in which Michael became a prominent public face alongside Boris Johnson, Dominic Cummings, Gisela Stuart, and others. Vine’s account emphasizes both its persuasive message and its lack of a fully worked-through governing aftermath.
