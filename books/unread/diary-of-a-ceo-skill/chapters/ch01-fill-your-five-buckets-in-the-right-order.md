# Chapter 1: Fill Your Five Buckets in the Right Order

**Pillar**: I — The Self | **Law**: 1 | **Source basis**: Law 1 body

## Core Idea

Bartlett models professional potential as five interconnected buckets: knowledge, skills, network, resources, and reputation. He argues that the buckets normally fill from left to right: knowledge applied becomes skill; knowledge and skill create value that expands a network; network opens access to resources; and sustained value and resources build reputation. Because the first two buckets survive a professional “earthquake”—being fired, technological disruption, or a company failing—he treats them as the foundation of longevity. The short-term temptation is to choose money, title, status, or visible reputation before acquiring the knowledge and skill needed to carry the role, but that builds a career on weak foundations. Bartlett illustrates the cost with Richard, a flourishing junior employee who accepted a lucrative CEO title without the experience to support it; the company collapsed within 18 months and Richard had to search for a more junior role. The law is not anti-money or anti-status; it is a sequencing rule that treats learning and capability as the highest-yielding investment because they cascade into the other buckets.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **The Five Buckets**
  - When to use: When choosing a job, course, book, project, or dream.
  - How: Assess and deliberately fill, in order, (1) what you know, (2) what you can do, (3) who you know, (4) what you have, and (5) what the world thinks of you.
- **Professional earthquake**
  - When to use: When testing whether your career can withstand disruption.
  - How: Ask which assets a shock could remove. Protect the knowledge and skills that cannot be taken away or unlearned, then use them to rebuild network, resources, and reputation.

## Key Concepts

- **Knowledge**: What you know and the first bucket to invest in.
- **Skill**: Applied knowledge—what you can do.
- **Network**: The people who know and can connect with you.
- **Resources**: Money, access, and material capacity.
- **Reputation**: What the world thinks of you.
- **Delayed gratification**: The patience to take a learning-rich path before a visibly rewarding one.
- **Foundation**: Knowledge and skill, the two buckets a professional earthquake cannot empty.

## Mental Models

- **Bucket order** — A role with slightly less immediate cash but much more knowledge and skill may be the higher-paying choice over a career horizon.
- **House on sand** — A title without the capability to perform it is fragile status, not durable progress.

## Anti-patterns

- **Skipping to resources or reputation**: Ego can make money, title, or status look like progress even when the ability to deliver has not been built.
- **Confusing current visibility with future resilience**: Network, resources, and reputation can be disrupted; knowledge and skill are the durable base.

## Worked Example

Richard was offered nearly double his salary, equity, a move to New York, and a CEO role despite having no management experience. The offer existed, so Bartlett supported his move, but within 18 months the company had lost key staff, run out of money, and closed amid management controversy. Richard was left far from home and looking for a more junior role. The example makes the sequencing rule concrete: the visible buckets were full enough to grant the title, but the first two buckets were not yet developed enough to sustain it.

## Key Takeaways

1. Invest first in knowledge and the ability to apply it.
2. Judge opportunities by what they teach and let you practise, not only by salary or title.
3. Treat knowledge and skill as career insurance against disruption.
4. Let network, resources, and reputation be consequences of value rather than substitutes for capability.
