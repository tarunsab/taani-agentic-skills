# Chapter 22: You Must Become a Plan-A Thinker

**Pillar**: III — The Philosophy | **Law**: 22 | **Source basis**: Law 22 body

## Core Idea

Bartlett argues that a backup plan can reduce the focus and emotional commitment available to a primary goal, especially when success depends heavily on effort. He tells Nando Parrado’s survival story: after the Andes plane crash, Parrado chose to move forward through impossible terrain because returning to the bodies of his mother and sister was not an acceptable alternative. Bartlett connects the story to his own early business life, when having nowhere else to go concentrated his attention and perseverance on Plan A. Research with students solving difficult word puzzles found that groups who first imagined backup ways to get a snack were less motivated and performed worse than groups without a Plan B; later studies with money, prizes, and time reportedly found the same pattern. The law is not a command to ignore dependents, mortgages, safety, or reality: Bartlett explicitly distinguishes focused risk from recklessness and says practicality must remain a priority. Use Plan-A thinking when an important goal needs concentrated effort and the backup would mainly provide an excuse to disengage, while retaining responsible safeguards where the downside is irreversible.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Plan-A thinking**
  - When to use: When a high-effort goal is being weakened by avoidable alternatives and hesitation.
  - How: Commit attention, energy, and creativity to one primary path and treat the absence of an easy escape as a motivational constraint.
- **Focused risk versus recklessness**
  - When to use: When deciding whether commitment has become unsafe.
  - How: Preserve the primary ambition while assessing actual consequences, responsibilities, and the safety net available; do not confuse psychological focus with ignoring material danger.

## Key Concepts

- **Plan A**: The primary path to an important outcome.
- **Plan B**: A backup path that can reduce the felt necessity of Plan A.
- **Motivational burden**: The extra psychological weight carried by an alternative that divides attention.
- **Effort-dependent goal**: A goal whose success is strongly influenced by how much effort is applied.
- **Fear of failure**: A negative consequence that can supply urgency and effort.
- **Focus**: Concentrated allocation of mind, energy, and time.
- **Recklessness**: Risk-taking that ignores responsibilities or consequences.

## Mental Models

- **No-exit concentration** — When every available resource is pointed at one viable path, hesitation and distraction have less room to grow.
- **The only way forward** — A hard constraint can convert a distant aspiration into the next necessary action, but only when the constraint is responsible.

## Anti-patterns

- **Confusing a backup with a safety system**: A Plan B that protects dependents or prevents catastrophe is not the same as a convenient alternative that weakens effort.
- **Romanticizing survival stories**: Parrado’s extreme circumstances are a source of focus, not a template for exposing oneself or others to avoidable danger.

## Worked Example

After the crash, Parrado and a friend climbed a 15,000-foot peak, saw mountains in every direction, and continued across glaciers for ten days despite hunger and failing bodies. A man on horseback eventually found their note, and 72 days after the crash the survivors were rescued. Bartlett says the key psychological fact was that Parrado could not go back; this concentrated his available effort on forward movement. In Bartlett’s own account, having no attractive alternative while broke and disowned helped him stay with his first company long enough to escape that situation.

## Key Takeaways

1. Ask whether an alternative protects against catastrophe or merely provides an escape from effort.
2. Concentrate the resources that the primary goal actually needs.
3. Use urgency as fuel without abandoning practical responsibility.
4. Commit to Plan A when a high-effort outcome is being diluted by unnecessary hesitation.
