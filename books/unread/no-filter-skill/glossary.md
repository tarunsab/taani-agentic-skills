# Glossary

**Algorithmic feed** — A ranked feed that selects and orders posts using predicted relevance or engagement rather than strict chronology (Ch. 9–10).

**Authenticity** — The appearance of personal, effortless, or unfiltered expression; in the book, it can coexist with extensive production and paid promotion (Introduction; Ch. 7–11).

**Cannibalization** — The fear that one product’s growth will take attention, time, or revenue from another product in the same company (Ch. 10; Ch. 12).

**Community curation** — Human selection and relationship work used to model constructive behavior and surface people or stories (Ch. 2; Ch. 7–8).

**Engagement rate** — A creator-performance proxy commonly calculated from likes and comments divided by follower count; useful for comparison but vulnerable to gaming (Ch. 11).

**Explore** — Instagram’s discovery surface that replaced the more openly popular-content orientation and was organized around interests and niches (Ch. 8).

**Finsta** — A private or secondary Instagram account used for less polished, lower-stakes sharing than a main public account (Ch. 9).

**Growth culture** — Facebook’s operating model of instrumenting actions, testing changes, and retaining whatever improves a target metric (Ch. 5–6).

**Influencer** — A person whose audience, taste, or identity can move attention and commercial demand; the role develops from user to publisher and entrepreneur (Introduction; Ch. 7–11).

**Insta-worthy** — A condition in which a person, place, or experience is staged or judged for its potential to perform visually on Instagram (Introduction; Ch. 8–11).

**InstaMeet** — An in-person gathering organized around Instagram photography and community, originally used to build norms and relationships (Ch. 2; Ch. 8).

**Integrity** — Work that protects users and the public from abuse, illegal content, misinformation, manipulation, and related platform harms (Ch. 2; Ch. 10–12).

**Network effect** — A feedback loop in which more users, content, and relationships make a social product more useful and harder to replace (Ch. 3–4; Ch. 6).

**Piggybacking** — Using existing networks such as Facebook, Twitter, Tumblr, or Foursquare for distribution while the new product builds its own value (Ch. 1; Ch. 3).

**Platform incentive** — A reward or constraint embedded in product design, ranking, or business rules that encourages particular user behavior (Introduction; Ch. 9–11).

**Recommended users** — A curated list or recommendation surface that can shape discovery, community norms, careers, and commercial opportunity (Ch. 2; Ch. 4; Ch. 7–8).

**Signal gaming** — Coordinating, buying, automating, or otherwise manipulating a metric so that apparent popularity no longer tracks genuine attention or value (Ch. 8; Ch. 11).

**Stories** — A lower-permanence, more frequent sharing format that Instagram adopted in response to Snapchat and used to reduce the cost of ordinary participation (Ch. 6; Ch. 9).

**Suggested users** — See **Recommended users**; the source uses both language patterns for Instagram’s curated discovery lists (Ch. 2; Ch. 4; Ch. 7).

**Verification** — An identity and impersonation-protection marker that became a status and visibility signal as celebrity and influencer culture grew (Ch. 7; Ch. 11).

**Visibility tax** — The time, money, emotional energy, and behavioral adjustment users spend when being seen affects status, work, or opportunity (Ch. 8; Ch. 11).
