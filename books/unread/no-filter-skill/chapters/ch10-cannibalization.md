# Chapter 10: Cannibalization

## Core Idea

By 2016, Facebook’s concern was no longer whether Instagram would survive but whether it would consume Facebook’s most valuable resource: user attention. Facebook’s news feed was designed around hyperpersonalized distribution, virality, and time, while Instagram still emphasized visual identity, public discovery, and a more restrained relationship with links and news. Political events made the difference consequential: Facebook’s algorithmic feed became part of the public debate over misinformation and influence, whereas Instagram’s human-curation heritage gave it a different form of legitimacy without removing it from the same corporate system. In a meeting about cannibalization, Instagram argued that it competed for the larger pool of entertainment, communication, and leisure, not simply for minutes that Facebook could reclaim. The disagreement reveals a deeper governance problem: a parent company can interpret a subsidiary’s success as a threat even when the subsidiary is growing the overall ecosystem, and can then use navigation, promotion, staffing, or data access to rebalance the pie.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Measure the total job market** — Define competition around the user’s alternatives, including television, sleep, and other apps, not only the parent’s internal products.
- **Interpret data within a product theory** — The same time or engagement data can support different conclusions depending on whether the goal is consumption, creation, trust, or long-term affinity.
- **Protect the public boundary** — A social product’s stance on news, links, comments, and curation determines how much political and informational responsibility it inherits.
- **Watch for internal cannibalization controls** — Cross-links, navigation, promotion, and headcount are levers that can quietly change which product wins.

## Key Concepts

- 2016 feed and election controversy
- Facebook hyperpersonalization and virality

## Mental Models

1. **The attention pie:** product A can reduce product B’s minutes while expanding the set of activities the company captures from television, competitors, or offline leisure.
2. **The governance lever map:** allocation of people + navigation + promotion + measurement can alter product power without changing the public brand.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating engagement time as a universal good regardless of what users are doing or what the product promises.
- Assuming a human-curated feed is automatically safe or neutral because it is less algorithmically viral.
- Solving bullying and abuse only through engagement-preserving tweaks instead of giving users genuine control.
- Evaluating a subsidiary solely by its impact on the parent’s internal dashboard.

## Worked Example

At the cannibalization meeting, Facebook brought senior growth leadership and a large data-science group to compare Instagram’s expanding use with Facebook’s much larger base. Systrom argued that Instagram was competing with the whole attention market and could expand the combined opportunity; Facebook’s interpretation emphasized the value of minutes and advertising revenue moving between its products. The eventual decision to link Instagram more visibly to Facebook while removing Instagram from Facebook’s navigation embodied the conflict. It preserved some cross-product flow but also signaled that integration would be managed according to Facebook’s portfolio priorities.

## Distinctions

Internal cannibalization is different from competition for the user’s total attention: one frames the problem as portfolio allocation, the other as a broader market for leisure and communication. Engagement is also different from public value; a feed can maximize reactions while increasing misinformation or reducing trust. Portfolio integration can share infrastructure without requiring identical identity, editorial, or safety choices.

## Limitations

The book’s election examples show why distribution mechanics matter, but they do not by themselves prove a single platform caused a political outcome. Internal data and executive interpretations are reported in a contested environment, so causal claims should be kept modest. The chapter focuses on Facebook and Instagram and does not provide a complete comparison with every competing source of attention.

## Practical Application

When a parent compares two products, define both the internal transfer and the external alternatives before setting goals. Review navigation, cross-promotion, staffing, and measurement as governance levers, and ask whether they serve users or merely optimize the portfolio. Pair engagement dashboards with quality, trust, and contribution indicators.

## Key Takeaways

- Internal competition can distort product decisions even when the company controls both sides.
- What a platform chooses to measure becomes part of its theory of value.
- Social products carry different informational and political risks depending on their distribution mechanics.
- Integrity work competes for the same resources as growth and therefore needs explicit ownership.
- Product independence is fragile when navigation, staffing, and measurement remain centralized.
