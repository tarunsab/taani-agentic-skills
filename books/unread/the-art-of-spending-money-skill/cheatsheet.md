# Cheatsheet

## First diagnosis

| If you notice… | Ask… | Prefer… |
|---|---|---|
| “More money will fix everything” | What problem is actually missing: health, love, purpose, sleep, or time? | Use money as leverage for an existing good. |
| A purchase must be seen | Who do I want to respect me, and what do they really value? | Internal/eulogy virtues over public display. |
| You envy a lifestyle | What hidden costs and unhappy parts are invisible? | Your own scorecard and chosen social group. |
| A luxury has become normal | What contrast made it feel special? | Keep treats occasional when that preserves joy. |
| Saving feels like deprivation | What independence and future options does this dollar buy today? | Count savings as spent on freedom. |
| A rule feels like “who I am” | Could I change it if facts, age, or goals changed? | Strong beliefs, weakly held; mental liquidity. |

## Purchase decision rules

- If the item would matter on an island, prioritize utility: comfort, function, durability, convenience, view, or connection.
- If its main benefit is strangers’ attention, price in short-lived admiration, envy, privacy loss, and the next upgrade.
- If price is being used as a proxy for quality, test cheaper and less prestigious options; brands primarily promise consistency.
- If you do not know your “thing,” run a small experiment. Keep a wide funnel and a tight filter.
- If the purchase improves time with people you love, ask whether the true target is time and relationship rather than the object.

## Save/spend balance

1. Imagine future regret at several ages and responsibilities.
2. Protect against collapse and buy enough independence to reject bad options.
3. Preserve affordable experiences and memories that compound.
4. Control the big recurring costs first; let small costs compound only after the large shape is sound.

## Social-debt smell test

Before a visible upgrade, check: Will it attract requests? Make others envious? Raise my lifestyle baseline? Make ordinary wear intolerable? Tie me to a job, neighborhood, or audience? If yes, treat those effects as part of the cost.

## Anti-patterns

- Measure net worth as self-worth.
- Compare your inside with other people's outside.
- Risk what you need for what you do not need.
- Treat financial decisions as math-only or emotion-only.
- Let one success prove that your strategy is permanently right.
- Save so fiercely that money becomes an accounting hobby.
- Assume every expensive, popular, or advertised thing is for you.

## North-star defaults

Spend less than you make. Quietly compound. Money serves you. No one is thinking about you as much as you are. Independence is wealth. Health is wealth. Be a good ancestor. Love your family.
