# Chapter 33: Closing the Circle

## Core Idea

German Datex officials report that the route now points to the University of Bremen, while the Ballistic Research Laboratory and White Sands report similar probing, showing that the attacker can reach many military and research systems before anyone notices. Stoll's review of Mitre's bills also identifies TRW and other defense contractors, and TRW confirms that someone has been in its supposedly secure system. The University of Bremen eventually finds a privileged account that had erased its accounting traces, and the Bundespost prepares to watch the account and trace its next use. Stoll simultaneously drafts a profile that separates well-supported behavior from uncertain questions such as whether a program or a person is behind the keyboard.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Cross-site corroboration
- Distributed detection gap
- Profile with uncertainty

## Key Concepts

- University of Bremen
- Ballistic Research Laboratory
- TRW
- Privileged account
- Bundespost

## Mental Models

- A circle of independent witnesses
- The attacker as a gap between guardians

## Anti-patterns

- Assume “secure facility” means no incident occurred
- Merge every related report without checking behavior and timing

## Worked Example

TRW first claims to be secure, then confirms an intrusion after checking its logs, echoing Stoll's experience at LBL. Bremen's finding of a privileged account with erased accounting traces gives the international route a local technical correlate. The evidence closes the circle around the infrastructure while still leaving the individual outside it.

## Key Takeaways

- Ask supposedly secure sites to verify, not merely assert.
- Compare independent reports for shared methods and timing.
- Close the evidentiary circle before closing the system.
