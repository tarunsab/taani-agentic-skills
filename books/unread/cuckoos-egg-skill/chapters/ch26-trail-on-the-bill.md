# Chapter 26: The Trail on the Bill

## Core Idea

Roy asks Stoll for graphics work, and Stoll responds by delivering a telescope visualization program obtained electronically, showing that his normal scientific responsibilities have not disappeared. Meanwhile, Mitre disables its outgoing modems and the hacker vanishes from that route, but Stoll continues checking other leads: a Navy data center, a field-service account, and a Jet Propulsion Laboratory observation. The phone-bill trail suggests Mitre was a stepping-stone rather than the final home, but shutting it down changes the experiment and may simply redirect the attacker. The chapter links ordinary engineering work, evidence review, and the danger of mistaking disappearance for resolution.

## Mental Models

- The disappearing signal
- A case file with loose ends

## Anti-patterns

- Let the investigation erase core duties
- Interpret silence after a shutdown as proof of arrest

## Worked Example

The Caltech visualization helps Stoll demonstrate that he can still contribute to astronomy while running the security investigation. Mitre's modem shutdown removes an observation route, so Stoll checks other systems and accounts before drawing conclusions. The evidence leaves him with a working hypothesis—Mitre as launch point—but no reason to stop monitoring.

## Key Takeaways

- Maintain explicit boundaries around competing responsibilities.
- Before closing a route, record what evidence it provided.
- Review adjacent systems when a stepping-stone disappears.
