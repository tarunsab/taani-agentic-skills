# Epilogue: After the Hunt

## Core Idea

The epilogue follows Stoll and Martha through their wedding and move to Massachusetts, where he returns to astronomy and computing at the Harvard-Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics. The change of address provides some relief from publicity and later break-in attempts, but it does not make computer security disappear from his life. In November 1988, Gene Miya calls from NASA Ames about a new attack, and Stoll finds his Harvard Sun computer overwhelmed by hundreds of network processes that resemble a virus. The book closes with a new unanswered question—what is crawling around his computer—showing that the end of one investigation is not the end of the underlying security problem.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Life after incident
- Recurring-threat awareness
- New-incident triage

## Key Concepts

- Harvard-Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics
- NASA Ames
- Arpanet
- Virus
- Process surge

## Mental Models

- Return to mission with changed awareness
- The next incident as a new observation

## Anti-patterns

- Assume publication or relocation ends exposure
- Carry the old attribution story into a new incident without rechecking evidence

## Worked Example

After the wedding, Stoll builds X-ray astronomy databases and settles into a new academic setting, but a late-night NASA call immediately tests whether the lessons traveled with him. The overloaded machine shows hundreds of outgoing connections, while a similar event at Berkeley suggests a network-spreading process. Stoll recognizes the signature of an attack but does not yet know whether it is a virus or another kind of failure, so the book ends by returning to the first discipline: observe before naming.

## Key Takeaways

- Treat a new event as a fresh case with its own evidence.
- Carry forward methods, not untested conclusions.
- Keep incident response compatible with the system's primary mission.
