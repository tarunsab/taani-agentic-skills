# Chapter 18: The Mirror Notebook

## Core Idea

Stoll disguises his work as ordinary systems administration while the hacker returns through Sventek and probes Anniston. Dan at Stanford has a related hacker, and a newspaper story about a “brilliant hacker” threatens to expose the investigation, but the immediate problem is observation: the intruder finds a privilege bait Stoll left under an inconspicuous name after months of absence. The speed with which the hacker recognizes the path shows methodical experience rather than a one-off prank. Stoll's notebook becomes a mirror of the campaign—each entry records both the attacker's habits and the investigator's own assumptions.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Behavioral baseline
- Discreet decoy
- Notebook as reflective instrument

## Key Concepts

- Sventek
- Anniston
- Stanford hacker
- Privilege bait
- Behavioral signature

## Mental Models

- The attacker reflected in repeated behavior
- The notebook as a second observer

## Anti-patterns

- Assume a quiet period means the campaign ended
- Use a conspicuous trap that reveals the investigator

## Worked Example

After a gap, the hacker returns and finds the deliberately planted privilege path almost immediately. That event is useful because it distinguishes persistence and familiarity from random experimentation. Stoll also learns that publicity can change the behavior of both the hacker and the institutions being asked to help, so operational secrecy is part of the measurement environment.

## Key Takeaways

- Establish a behavioral baseline before interpreting a gap.
- Use decoys that test a specific capability.
- Treat the investigation's visibility as a variable that can change the subject.
