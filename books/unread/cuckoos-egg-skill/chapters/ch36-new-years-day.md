# Chapter 36: New Year's Day

## Core Idea

On New Year's Eve and New Year's Day, the hacker enters the Army's Optimis database and searches for nuclear, chemical, biological, and theater-related material. A printer jam loses part of the session, but the remaining activity is enough for Stoll to alert CIA, FBI, NSA, and AFOSI contacts. The responses again reveal institutional specialization: some agencies care about espionage, some about system security, and some decline because the data is not classified or the case is not theirs. The chapter raises the stakes without claiming that every search term represented a successful retrieval.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Impact-based escalation
- Evidence loss through instrumentation failure
- Institutional specialization

## Key Concepts

- Optimis
- Army database
- Printer jam
- Nuclear/chemical/biological search
- AFOSI

## Mental Models

- A broken sensor at the decisive moment
- Search intent versus data compromise

## Anti-patterns

- Treat keywords as proof that data was copied
- Assume a monitoring failure means the session is unrecoverable

## Worked Example

The partial printout leaves Stoll with enough context to know that a national-security system has been explored, but not with a complete inventory of what was read. He calls the agencies that can judge the significance and continues preserving the evidence that remains. The episode makes monitoring reliability a security concern in its own right.

## Key Takeaways

- Label missing evidence explicitly.
- Escalate when the target's potential consequence changes.
- Design monitoring so one mechanical failure does not erase the case.
