# Chapter 34: Operation Showerhead

## Core Idea

Management threatens to end the investigation, but the FBI's intervention buys Stoll a few more weeks and the German trace becomes fast enough to matter. A live session comes through a public dial-in port in Hannover rather than the University of Bremen, and the technician narrows the telephone origin to one of fifty lines. Stoll and Martha then create Operation Showerhead: an unobtrusive collection of fictitious Strategic Defense Initiative documents, a plausible secretary, and a request form designed to keep the intruder reading long enough for a trace. The bait is deliberately boring and bureaucratic, because a dramatic “top secret” display would expose the trap.

## Mental Models

- Bait as a controlled experiment
- Plausibility is a security property

## Anti-patterns

- Use real sensitive material as bait
- Make a decoy so exciting that it advertises itself

## Worked Example

The decoy combines modified routine documents with invented network descriptions, placing them behind an account that the intruder can reach only after becoming system manager. A form letter offers more information by mail, creating a possible human contact without depending on it. The goal is not to trick every reader; it is to extend one observed session long enough for the German telephone technicians to complete their work.

## Key Takeaways

- Define the observation the decoy is meant to produce.
- Keep bait plausible, bounded, and non-sensitive.
- Obtain explicit internal approval before creating a deceptive environment.
