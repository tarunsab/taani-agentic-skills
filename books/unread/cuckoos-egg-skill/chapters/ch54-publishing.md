# Chapter 54: Publishing

## Core Idea

The FBI asks Stoll to remain quiet, but Stoll argues that research requires a public account of the observation method, the evidence, and the security lessons. Martha helps with the personal decision to marry, while an ACM article goes through review and a May press conference eventually makes the case public. A German tabloid first leaks details from the old logbook, yet the press coverage is more accurate than Stoll feared and helps expose the hacker's identity. The chapter frames publication as a security control with trade-offs: disclosure can warn defenders and create risk, so it should be deliberate, evidence-based, and respectful of information that still needs protection.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Responsible disclosure
- Peer review
- Public-interest publication

## Key Concepts

- ACM article
- May press conference
- German tabloid
- Logbook
- Disclosure trade-off

## Mental Models

- The logbook as a publishable record
- Secrecy versus learning

## Anti-patterns

- Publish raw sensitive material for dramatic effect
- Keep a documented case private forever because publicity is uncomfortable

## Worked Example

Stoll's article turns a personal chase into a method others can inspect, while the later press conference communicates the public lesson. The leak demonstrates that control over timing can be lost, so a prepared, accurate account is safer than improvising after a rumor. Publication does not end the technical work; it changes the audience and the responsibilities attached to the evidence.

## Key Takeaways

- Separate publishable method from sensitive operational detail.
- Use review to test claims before public release.
- Prepare an accurate account when a case may become public unexpectedly.
