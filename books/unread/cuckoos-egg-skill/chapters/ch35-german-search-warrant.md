# Chapter 35: The German Search Warrant

## Core Idea

Wolfgang Hoffman explains that German technicians need legal authority to trace the Hannover telephone line, while the German prosecutor needs an official request from U.S. criminal-justice authorities. The bureaucratic chain runs from LBL to the FBI, the U.S. Legal Attaché in Bonn, the German BKA, and the State Prosecutor, and every handoff risks delay. Stoll's logbook saves DOE from claiming it was never informed and becomes a concrete example of why contemporaneous documentation matters. A false alarm on December 30 then teaches him to verify before waking international partners or weakening their trust.

## Anti-patterns

- Assume technical urgency overrides local law
- Broadcast every beeper signal without confirmation

## Worked Example

The German side is ready to keep technicians available, but it cannot trace a noncomputerized exchange indefinitely without authorization and payment. The FBI says the case is moving while the German officials wait, so Stoll maintains direct technical contact and keeps his records precise. When an electrical event triggers an alarm, he recognizes that a single false escalation could make the real trace harder later.

## Key Takeaways

- Map legal authority as carefully as network topology.
- Keep a dated record of notifications and decisions.
- Treat the credibility of the monitoring team as part of the system.
