# Chapter 42: The Net

## Core Idea

The German technicians work against a deadline and complete a trace from Bremen through Hannover while the hacker reads more of the fake SDINET material. The hacker then probes dozens of military systems and reaches Fort Buckner in Okinawa through an unprotected guest path, showing that the campaign spans geography and security cultures. The Bundespost finally narrows the line to a specific home or business connection, so Stoll feels as if the net is closing around the person. The result is a major attribution step, but still depends on German police and legal action to convert a network trace into an arrest.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Converging trace
- Distributed exposure
- Technical-to-legal handoff

## Key Concepts

- Fort Buckner
- Okinawa
- Bremen
- Hannover
- German trace

## Mental Models

- A net with many openings
- Localization before identification

## Anti-patterns

- Treat a traced telephone line as a completed prosecution
- Assume distant systems have equivalent defenses

## Worked Example

The long SDINET session gives the German technicians time to move from the Datex gateway to the Hannover exchange. The simultaneous Fort Buckner access shows why Stoll has kept LBL open: the intruder remains active while the trace is running. The chapter's endpoint is not certainty but a narrowed, actionable location.

## Key Takeaways

- Keep the monitored host active only for a defined evidentiary purpose.
- Use cross-site activity to assess campaign breadth.
- Hand off the final identification step to the authority that can legally act.
