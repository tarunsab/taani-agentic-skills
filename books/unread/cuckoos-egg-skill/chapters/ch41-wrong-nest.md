# Chapter 41: The Wrong Nest

## Core Idea

Stoll obtains Roy's approval and sends a scientific-style proposal to the FBI, CIA, NSA, DOE, Air Force OSI, and Defense Intelligence Agency, asking them to object if the decoy is unacceptable. No agency takes responsibility, but none blocks the plan, so Stoll treats the silence as permission only within his boss's authority and the stated scope. The first baited session is too short; after Stoll makes the SDINET account more visible, the hacker becomes super-user, reads fake files, and searches for a supposed SDINET portal. The German trace reaches Bremen and then a Hannover dial-in, narrowing the telephone source to one of fifty numbers, but the cuckoo has still not been caught.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Bounded authorization
- Decoy iteration
- Search-window optimization

## Key Concepts

- Scientific proposal
- SDINET account
- One-in-fifty number
- Bremen
- Hannover

## Mental Models

- The experiment with an explicit stop condition
- A bait that must be made visible

## Anti-patterns

- Treat silence from every recipient as unlimited approval
- Assume a failed first bait means the method is invalid

## Worked Example

The first session shows only the list of logged-in users, so Stoll changes the observation setup by leaving the decoy account visibly active. On the next session the hacker follows the path, gains privilege, and spends about forty-five minutes reading invented material. The trace improves, but the source keeps the result bounded: a German network path and a narrowed phone group are not yet a named person.

## Key Takeaways

- Write down who approved the experiment and what they approved.
- Iterate the instrument when the subject does not encounter it.
- Treat partial localization as progress, not completion.
