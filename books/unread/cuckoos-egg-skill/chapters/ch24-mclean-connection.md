# Chapter 24: The McLean Connection

## Core Idea

Dan Stanford identifies a possible student behind his own hacker case, while Stoll's November trace reaches Mitre but cannot expose the path inside the contractor's packet network. Stoll's sister and a Virginia FBI agent help test the McLean hypothesis from different angles: local school records, network behavior, and the computer-fraud statute. The legal and technical evidence converge on a useful location without producing a name. The chapter's mechanism is triangulation—different investigators and evidence types narrow the space of explanations even when none is decisive alone.

## Frameworks Introduced

## Mental Models

- The hypothesis funnel

## Anti-patterns

- Force all clues into one narrative
- Treat a failed internal trace as evidence that Mitre is irrelevant

## Worked Example

The school lead does not identify the attacker, and the internal Mitre trace fails to name a terminal. Both results are still useful because they constrain the search and expose what the current instruments cannot see. Stoll keeps the hypothesis alive only because the network and behavioral evidence continue to point toward it.

## Key Takeaways

- Use multiple evidence types to narrow uncertainty.
- Keep dead ends as part of the case record.
- Require continuing corroboration before promoting a location to an identity.
