# Chapter 30: Germany

## Core Idea

Steve decodes the international network identifier as a German Datex connection, and the German clues reinforce the earlier timing and password observations. The Bundespost can tell that the route passes through West Germany and eventually narrows toward Bremen, but the visible computer or dial-in port is still only a gateway. Stoll considers whether the attacker is a student, a hobbyist, or a spy, and refuses to resolve the question from geography alone. The chapter's central mechanism is progressive localization: combine network codes, time zones, language, and behavior until the hypotheses narrow enough for local investigators to act.

## Worked Example

The German network code answers an important question—where the traffic enters the international system—but not who is sitting at the keyboard. Stoll's atlas work, schedule analysis, and repeated password clue make Germany plausible, while the source continues to preserve alternatives. The next step is to obtain a local trace that can distinguish a computer gateway from a telephone origin.

## Key Takeaways

- Combine independent weak signals.
- Name the level of location each trace actually identifies.
- Keep motive hypotheses subordinate to route and behavior evidence.
