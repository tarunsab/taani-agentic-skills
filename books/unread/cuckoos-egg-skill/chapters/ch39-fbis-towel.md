# Chapter 39: The FBI's Towel

## Core Idea

The FBI tells the Air Force and LBL to shut down the access ports, change passwords, and close the case because it sees no classified compromise, sufficient monetary loss, or practical extradition path. The German Bundespost warns that it will stop tracing without an official U.S. contact, leaving Stoll with a choice between obeying the closure and preserving the only observation channel. Roy decides that LBL will stay open until DOE says otherwise, while the CIA and NSA offer sympathy but not operational ownership. The chapter shows how a distributed incident can be stalled by institutional incentives even when every participant understands part of the risk.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Bailiwick failure
- Institutional escalation
- Observation-channel preservation

## Key Concepts

- FBI closure
- BKA
- CIA
- NSA
- DOE

## Mental Models

- Responsibility passed downstream
- Closing the window versus stopping the threat

## Anti-patterns

- Assume a closed case means the underlying threat ended
- Let agency disagreement erase the technical record

## Worked Example

The FBI's initial withdrawal would make the German trace impossible and leave the hacker free to continue elsewhere. Roy's decision preserves LBL's monitoring, while Teejay quietly pushes the FBI to resume contact. The episode demonstrates that persistence sometimes means keeping a carefully documented local capability alive until the wider coordination catches up.

## Key Takeaways

- Distinguish an agency decision from a threat-state change.
- Keep a safe observation channel when closure would only create blindness.
- Record which organization owns each missing action.
