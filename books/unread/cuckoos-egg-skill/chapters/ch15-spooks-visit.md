# Chapter 15: The Spooks Visit

## Core Idea

Stoll's childhood story about a beer-can incident frames a lesson about agency: when an institution will not solve a problem, he tends to build a way to observe it himself. CIA visitors then explain how trust and compartmentalization work in intelligence systems, and they clarify that the presence of an intrusion does not by itself prove foreign espionage. Stoll's network sits near NSA-related systems, but the agencies still need evidence that distinguishes an outsider, a prankster, and an organized operation. The visit validates the importance of his logbook while leaving the central attribution question open.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Compartmentalization
- Trust boundary
- Agency under institutional delay

## Key Concepts

- CIA visit
- NSA system
- Unclassified computer
- Compartmentalization
- Logbook

## Mental Models

- The logbook as portable credibility
- Trust split into compartments

## Anti-patterns

- Overread institutional proximity as attribution
- Wait for an authority to replace direct observation

## Worked Example

The visitors listen because Stoll can describe dates, accounts, and routes rather than only saying that a hacker exists. They explain that sensitive organizations also limit access internally, so a connection to an unclassified system does not expose the whole institution. Stoll leaves with more context, not a verdict, and keeps the investigation grounded in what his records actually show.

## Key Takeaways

- Document before asking for interpretation.
- Model trust as a set of compartments with different consequences.
- Keep “who,” “what,” and “why” as separate questions.
