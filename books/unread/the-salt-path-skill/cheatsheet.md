# The Salt Path — Decision Cheatsheet

## Decision Rules

| If… | Do… | Because… |
|---|---|---|
| The whole crisis feels impossible | Choose the next landmark, meal, water source, or safe night | A short, shared horizon restores action without pretending the crisis is solved |
| Packs or shelter are too heavy | Remove weight and test the awkward movements before departure | The living-room tent test exposed the help Moth would need |
| A fare competes with food or a safe night | Price the full chain: crossings, food, water, energy, and the next income date | The cheapest single option can create the most dangerous downstream cost |
| A route enters dense, unsafe, or camp-hostile space | Consider a paid shortcut, and record the skipped miles for later | The walkers skipped built-up miles when fear and camping scarcity outweighed pilgrimage pride |
| Someone asks why you are outside | Share the truth when the person has shown capacity to hear it; do not treat disclosure as an obligation | “Sold the house” protected them briefly, while Anna’s response showed the value of truthful connection |
| A person offers food, water, shelter, transport, or work | Accept plainly, clarify the terms, and preserve reciprocal dignity | Concrete help keeps people alive; shelter tied to usefulness can also create control |
| Activity seems to improve an illness | Observe the change, rest, seek clinical advice, and avoid calling correlation a cure | Moth sometimes felt clearer and stronger, but the memoir never proves walking reversed CBD |
| The path feels safer than the town | Use the open place as a temporary home, while still planning for an address and services | Nature offered belonging; it did not remove cold, hunger, fear, or housing insecurity |
| Death or an ending is being avoided | Name the irreversible fact, then choose the small action still available | Moth’s death conversation made room for present love and life |
| A social label narrows the self | Try a role that is true enough to inhabit—reader, student, worker, storyteller—and notice what it returns | Performance and study let Moth act beyond the patient identity |

## Route-and-Resource Defaults from the Memoir

- **Scale**: the guidebook’s route is about 630 miles; the prologue is already 243 miles and 36 nights in, with 387 miles still ahead. Treat such figures as this narrative’s landmarks, not universal targets.
- **Load**: the final packs are about 17.5 lb each; the replacement tent cost £38 and weighed about 6.5 lb. Lighter gear enabled the walk but did not remove risk.
- **Cash**: the departure purse held £115; the pair expected £48 per week in tax credits, while a campsite could cost £80 per week and the bus to Minehead £10 each. The story’s numbers show the scale of the constraint, not a current budget.
- **Food**: cheap, durable calories are repeatedly traded against showers, ferries, and shelter. Hunger makes judgment narrower; if food runs out, seek help rather than treating deprivation as a test of character.
- **Winter**: a friend’s shed and paid farm work create a bridge, but the bridge is unstable. Before leaving a shelter, name rent, work, study funding, credit barriers, and the date the next roof is needed.

## Tells and Smells

- A “simple adventure” story that hides homelessness may be protecting dignity—but may also be preventing useful help.
- A route plan with no place to camp is incomplete, even if the mileage is correct.
- A cheap item that fails in cold, rain, or illness is not cheap in the full chain.
- A person who calls the walkers “tramps,” “old,” or “poets” is revealing the label system around them; do not confuse the label with the person.
- A present moment can be enough for living without being evidence that the future is safe.
