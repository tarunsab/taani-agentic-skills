# Chapter 3: Seismic Shift

**Part**: Part One — Into the Light.

## Core Idea

The title names a collapse in which legal loss, medical uncertainty, lack of income, and the absence of an address arrive as one event. Moth's probable corticobasal degeneration (CBD) changes the meaning of the walk: he has pain, tremor, numbness, and a progressive prognosis, while Raynor has to decide whether time with him matters more than trying to rebuild a conventional life immediately. Housing systems offer little usable help because a campsite is not eligible for the support they need, a council assessment treats them as low priority, and their credit history makes ordinary renting difficult. The couple therefore design a narrow, low-cost plan around a lightweight tent, a guidebook, limited food, and the £48 weekly tax credit still associated with an address they have left. Preparation does not make the journey safe, but it transforms an impossible impulse into a sequence they can attempt together.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Name the combined shock**: A change of home, health, money, and status should be planned as one interacting system rather than as four separate problems.
  - When to use: When one loss is triggering failures in several ordinary systems.
  - How: List the bodily, financial, administrative, and relational consequences before choosing a route.
- **Design for the weaker constraint**: Raynor does not plan around her own capacity; she chooses a lighter tent and slower route because Moth's body sets the margin.
  - When to use: When a shared plan must accommodate illness, disability, or unequal capacity.
  - How: Test the hardest transition, reduce load, and decide what assistance is required each day.
- **Minimum viable mobility**: Keep only what can be carried and used: food, water, clothing, first aid, a penknife, a monocular, a book, and shelter. The point is not asceticism; it is that mobility becomes their only available route to agency.

## Key Concepts

- **Corticobasal degeneration (CBD)**: A rare progressive brain disease given as the likely diagnosis for Moth's symptoms.
- **Diagnostic uncertainty**: There is no definitive test during life; certainty would come only after death.
- **Six-to-eight-year estimate**: The consultant's broad estimate from onset, with Moth already about six years into slow progression.
- **Housing eligibility**: The mismatch between their need for shelter and the rules attached to campsites, councils, and benefits.
- **£320**: The money left after case costs, support, and refunded barn bookings.
- **£48 per week**: The tax credit they expect, even though it is tied to the old address.
- **Lightweight kit**: The replacement packs and £38 used-once Vango tent that make the walk physically possible.

## Mental Models

- **Use a constraint stack**: Put diagnosis, pain, cash, food, shelter, transport, and family obligations on the same page. A route that works for mileage but not for medication or money is not a workable route.
- **Treat preparation as care**: Buying lighter equipment, testing the tent, and deciding how to help Moth with shoulder straps are acts of caregiving, not merely logistics.

## Anti-patterns

- **Call movement a cure**: Moth's later periods of clarity do not establish that walking reverses CBD. The chapter supports observation and adaptation, not medical certainty.
- **Assume official categories match lived need**: A campsite can be real shelter and still fail the benefit or housing test. A low-priority assessment does not mean the household's risk is low.
- **Plan for an imagined healthy traveler**: The old sixty-liter packs and canvas tent are incompatible with the actual bodies and money available.

## Worked Example

The first equipment plan uses old, heavy packs and a canvas tent, but Moth ends up on his knees in pain. The couple cannot afford premium gear, so they buy inexpensive Mountain Warehouse packs and a used Vango tent online for £38; the tent weighs about 6.5 lb, roughly a quarter of the old shelter. At home they pitch it in the living room, where Moth cannot get out without help and Raynor has to drag him in his sleeping bag. They pare both packs to about 17.5 lb, keep only essential food and equipment, and teach themselves how to put Moth's shoulder strap on. The rehearsal reveals the real work of the walk before the first mile begins.

## Key Takeaways

1. Treat a compound crisis as an interacting system of constraints.
2. Make the least-capable body the design boundary for a shared plan.
3. Rehearse the transitions that will consume strength, not only the headline activity.
4. Keep the distinction between observed relief and a medical explanation.
5. Benefits and housing rules can make an address a prerequisite for support even when the address is gone.
