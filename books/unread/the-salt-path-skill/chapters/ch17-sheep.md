# Chapter 17: Sheep

**Part**: Part Five — Choices.

## Core Idea

“Sheep” is the practical middle passage where the couple leave the path, enter a friend's meat-packing shed, and discover that survival now depends on labor, study, and a plan for housing. The shed gives them a roof, stove, and workspace, but it also makes Raynor feel like a guest whose right to stay depends on usefulness, while Moth's condition worsens away from the daily movement of the coast. A consultant says walking may have accelerated his decline and recommends rest and gentler activity; Raynor sees possible benefit in movement, but cannot prove her interpretation. Raynor works through lambing and shearing, eventually earning £1,500 and helping rebuild the shed, yet the arrangement carries tension when rent and control enter the friendship. Moth's acceptance to study environmental skills or teaching offers a longer-term direction, but it requires them to leave the shed so it can be rented, making them voluntarily homeless again with about £200 and a destination still to find.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Separate shelter from tenancy**: A roof offered in friendship is not the same as secure housing when the terms, planning status, or future rent are unclear.
  - When to use: When temporary accommodation depends on a favor, work exchange, or another person's changing need.
  - How: Clarify duration, work, rent, legal status, and the exit plan before treating it as a home.
- **Build the next identity through a real route**: Moth applies for a degree that can give him a new role and student finance, even though his health may not allow completion.
  - When to use: When the former occupation and expected future are gone.
  - How: Choose a concrete institution or role, secure the funding path, and let the attempt have value even if the endpoint is uncertain.
- **Labor as bridge, not redemption**: Raynor's farm work supplies money and competence, but hard work does not erase grief, illness, or unequal control in the arrangement.

## Key Concepts

- **Meat-packing shed**: Temporary winter shelter owned by Polly and later considered for rent.
- **Work exchange**: The arrangement in which Raynor helps with roofing, decorating, laundry, lambing, and farm work.
- **Consultant's warning**: The view that walking may have accelerated Moth's decline and that he should rest.
- **Pregabalin dilemma**: Moth considers returning to the medication because pain is increasing.
- **Shearing**: Seasonal work involving more than 800 ewes and a £1,500 payment.
- **Useful guest**: Raynor's fear that belonging depends on being productive.
- **Environmental degree**: Moth's proposed route into study and a new future.
- **Voluntary homelessness**: Leaving the shed so its rent can support the household and Moth's study.

## Mental Models

- **Use a bridge audit**: For any temporary shelter, list the roof, money, work, health effect, relationship cost, and exit date. A bridge that supports one dimension while damaging another needs redesign.
- **Treat a future attempt as meaningful**: Moth may not live to finish a degree, but applying and being accepted changes the available identity and funding path now.

## Anti-patterns

- **Make work prove worthiness for shelter**: Raynor's productivity helps materially but does not make a person more deserving of a roof.
- **Turn a consultant's caution into an argument to stop listening**: Raynor's hope that movement helps Moth is emotionally understandable, but it remains uncertain against professional advice.
- **Leave a shelter without pricing the next step**: The couple make the choice because the shed can earn rent and study can fund the future, but they still leave with very little cash.

## Worked Example

In the shed, Raynor does manual building and farm work while Moth becomes weaker and needs lifting. Raynor takes a seasonal shearing job, learns its rhythm, and finishes with £1,500—far more than their £11 balance in Bude—but the money sits beside fear about Moth's pain and the future of the friendship. Moth applies for a university degree in environmental skills or teaching and is accepted, with a student loan that might support both of them. Polly needs the shed to generate income, so Raynor tells her the truth about the application and the couple prepare to leave. They choose the uncertain study route, becoming homeless again so the temporary shelter can become paid housing.

## Key Takeaways

1. Temporary shelter must be evaluated by its terms, not only by the fact of a roof.
2. Labor can provide a bridge while leaving grief, illness, and power differences intact.
3. A future role can be worth pursuing even when completion is uncertain.
4. Before leaving shelter, connect the next roof to an actual funding and timing path.
5. Do not confuse productivity with deservingness.
