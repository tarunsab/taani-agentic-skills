# Glossary

**ARPA-E** — A DOE program modeled on DARPA that gives small grants to scientifically plausible, high-risk energy ideas the market may not fund (Ch 2).

**BAFU** — MacWilliams’s shorthand for “Billions and All Fucked Up,” his label for giant projects whose scale can conceal poor management (Ch 2).

**Broken Arrow** — A military term for a nuclear accident that does not lead to nuclear war (Ch 2).

**Career civil servant** — A continuing government employee whose technical knowledge can survive changes in elected leadership (Ch 1, Ch 2).

**Chief data scientist** — The new White House role DJ Patil held to find useful applications for federal data (Ch 4).

**Chief risk officer** — The DOE position created for MacWilliams to examine financial and nonfinancial risks across the department (Ch 2).

**Cone of uncertainty** — A visual range showing where a storm or outcome may go when an ensemble of forecasts disagrees (Ch 4).

**Ensemble forecasting** — Running a model repeatedly with varied initial conditions to represent uncertainty and predictability (Ch 4).

**Fifth risk** — The hidden, systemic, slow-moving danger that is easy to overlook after obvious threats have been listed (Ch 2).

**ForecastAdvisor** — Eric Floehr’s comparison of forecast accuracy using a large archive of predictions (Ch 4).

**Institutional memory** — Knowledge of systems, decisions, risks, and operating details that turnover can erase (Ch 1, Ch 2).

**Landing team** — An incoming administration’s agency-level group meant to learn the existing mission and prepare the changeover (Ch 1, Ch 2, Ch 4).

**Mission-driven** — Sullivan’s description of people motivated by the work and public purpose rather than credit or money (Ch 1, Ch 4).

**Mission versus money** — The book’s lens for distinguishing public purpose from private advantage or capture (Ch 1, Ch 3, Ch 4).

**National laboratories** — DOE research institutions supporting nuclear security, basic science, and advanced technology (Ch 2).

**National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA)** — Commerce Department agency that collects weather, climate, ocean, and environmental data (Ch 4).

**National Weather Service (NWS)** — The public forecasting and warning service whose observations underpin much private weather information (Ch 4).

**People risk** — Exposure created by the competence, motives, loyalties, and blind spots of the people placed in consequential roles (Ch 3).

**Political capital** — The support and attention needed to sustain a public program, especially for dispersed beneficiaries (Ch 3).

**Presidential transition** — The legally supported period for preparing to assume executive power after an election (Ch 1).

**Program management** — The fifth risk in MacWilliams’s list: the danger that large, long-lived projects become badly managed and create future liabilities (Ch 2).

**Public good** — A broadly useful resource, such as weather data, whose social value exceeds one private buyer’s use (Ch 1, Ch 4).

**Public service** — Government work understood as collective safety, opportunity, and stewardship rather than an individual consumer transaction (Ch 1, Ch 3).

**Risk portfolio** — The collection of hazards, obligations, and missed opportunities held by an institution (Ch 1, Ch 2).

**Rural Development** — USDA function that channels low-interest loans and grants to small towns and underserved rural communities (Ch 3).

**Sammies** — Stier’s awards recognizing extraordinary accomplishments by public servants (Ch 1).

**Seven little boxes** — A way to understand USDA by walking through its separate organizational functions and their different vulnerabilities (Ch 3).

**Social science** — Behavioral expertise NOAA added to study how people respond to weather and risk information (Ch 4).

**Stick and carrot** — Zaidi’s distinction between regulation, which compels behavior, and spending, which finances work the government cannot compel (Ch 3).

**Systemic risk** — A danger produced by interdependent, accumulating conditions rather than one isolated incident (Ch 2, Ch 4).

**Tail risk** — A risk with potentially extreme consequences that is often underweighted because it is remote or hard to imagine (Ch 2).

**USDA** — The U.S. Department of Agriculture, a broad department covering farms, forests, science, food inspection, nutrition, and rural finance (Ch 3).

**Weather-Ready Nation** — Sullivan’s effort to make communities more responsive and resilient to weather threats (Ch 4).
