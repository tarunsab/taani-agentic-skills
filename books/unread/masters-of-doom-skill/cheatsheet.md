# Cheatsheet

| Situation | Rule | Tell you are on track | Failure smell |
|---|---|---|---|
| A field feels gated | Make a small artifact with the tools available, publish it, and use feedback to buy access. | Each result unlocks a harder tool or collaborator. | Waiting for permission or credentials before practicing. |
| Two experts disagree | Give each a clear seam and force the disagreement into a playable build. | Engine constraints sharpen design and design exposes useful engine work. | “Technology” or “design” wins the organization. |
| Performance is failing | Protect the player-visible loop; cache, simplify, or omit everything outside it. | The game feels fast even when the implementation is a clever approximation. | Keeping expensive features that make the core feel slow. |
| Distribution is expensive | Give away a bounded, compelling first taste and charge for continuation. | The first slice proves value without requiring explanation. | A free demo that has no reason to continue, or a paid channel that adds overhead. |
| Players want to customize | Separate data from the core, provide tools, and set clear boundaries. | Users become creators and share work with one another. | Mods overwrite originals, create legal confusion, or cannot be supported. |
| Scope is swelling | Run the innovate → optimize → jettison loop; keep only what strengthens the core. | Cuts are explicit and the remaining experience gets clearer. | New promises are announced before the current build is coherent. |
| A team is growing | Add people only for a named ownership gap; budget communication as real work. | Interfaces and decision rights stay visible. | Bonus competition, silent isolation, or many answers to “what are we making?” |
| A founder becomes the face | Separate public claims from schedule ownership and keep progress measurable. | Fans see authentic work and the team can still say no. | Hype, ego, and delivery become the same person’s identity. |
| Controversy arrives | Distinguish make-believe, audience responsibility, evidence, and marketing practice. | Claims are specific and supported; ratings or boundaries are legible. | Correlation is treated as causation, or the audience is treated as children by default. |
| A project is technically brilliant but joyless | Reconnect the engine to player behavior, humor, and social use. | The implementation insight is felt as play. | The team can explain the engine but not why the player should care. |
