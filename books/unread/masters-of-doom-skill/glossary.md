# Glossary

**Adaptive tile refresh** — Carmack’s technique of redrawing only changed screen tiles and caching a strip to make PC side-scrolling smooth (Ch 03).

**Apogee** — Scott Miller’s shareware publisher; its episodic distribution model helped id reach players without a conventional retail launch (Ch 03–04).

**BBS** — Bulletin board system, the dial-up community and distribution infrastructure through which early PC games circulated (Ch 02–04).

**Binary Space Partitioning (BSP)** — A spatial tree that lets Quake determine visible regions and avoid drawing hidden geometry (Ch 09).

**Commander Keen** — The first major id/Apogee series, built around a bounded shareware slice and a distinctive character/world (Ch 03–05).

**Deathmatch** — Competitive multiplayer in which players hunt one another; Doom helped make it a social and eventually spectator format (Ch 09, 13).

**DEU** — Doom Editor Utility, a community-made tool that made building and distributing new Doom levels accessible (Ch 10).

**Design is law** — Romero’s Ion Storm maxim: technology should serve the designer’s intended experience, not dictate it (Ch 11–15).

**Doom Bible** — Tom Hall’s early design document proposing a richer, more story-driven Doom; its rejection signaled the project’s narrowing (Ch 08).

**DWANGO** — Dial Up Wide Area Network Games Operation, a service that turned modem access into a hosted Doom pickup game (Ch 11–13).

**Hacker Ethic** — The book’s ideal of computers as liberating tools for exploration, mastery, sharing, and self-directed creation, associated especially with Carmack (Ch 02, 10, 16).

**Hovertank** — Carmack’s early fast-action first-person experiment using raycasting and scaled sprites (Ch 05).

**Icon of Sin** — Doom II’s final boss and Romero’s hidden self-referential Easter egg (Ch 11).

**id Software** — The company formed by the former Softdisk collaborators on February 1, 1991 (Ch 03–04).

**Ion Storm** — Romero’s large, design-led post-id company, remembered in the narrative as a warning about scale, hype, and weak coordination (Ch 13–15).

**Keen Dreams** — A Commander Keen installment used to experiment with parallax-like depth and layered scrolling (Ch 05).

**QuakeWorld** — Carmack’s later networking work aimed at making Quake play more responsive over the Internet (Ch 13).

**Raycasting** — A rendering shortcut that projects lines from the player to determine wall slices, enabling fast pseudo-3-D on limited hardware (Ch 05–06).

**Shareware** — A low-overhead try-before-pay model; id and Apogee made it effective by giving away a compelling first episode and charging for continuation (Ch 03–04, 07).

**Softdisk** — The Shreveport subscription-disk company that supplied the team, deadlines, equipment, and the conflict from which id emerged (Ch 03–04).

**Spear of Destiny** — A Wolfenstein follow-up that reused the engine while id prepared a larger technical and creative leap (Ch 06–08).

**WAD** — “Where’s All the Data?”: Doom’s separable media/data package, enabling nondestructive mods without replacing the core executable (Ch 10).

**War room** — id’s communal workspace during Quake, intended to increase pressure and communication but experienced as surveillance and stress (Ch 12).

**.plan file** — Carmack’s online work log and Romero’s occasional public announcement channel; it made development unusually visible to fans (Ch 10–12).

**Wolfenstein 3-D** — id’s fast texture-mapped maze shooter, the immediate predecessor that established the company’s action formula (Ch 06–08).
