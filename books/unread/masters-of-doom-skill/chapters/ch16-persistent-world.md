# Chapter 16: Persistent World

## Core Idea

The final chapter returns to the book’s long arc: Carmack and Romero keep pursuing artificial worlds, but their routes now include marriage, family, business failure, mobile games, rockets, and a cautious reconnection. Carmack sees persistent online worlds such as Ultima Online and EverQuest as evidence that players may live through shared systems, and he imagines generalized infrastructure for a Metaverse; when id hesitates, he insists on Doom III as the next project. Romero and Stevie Case leave the wreckage of Ion Storm after Daikatana’s poor reception, while Tom Hall and Romero form Monkeystone for mobile games, showing a smaller and more flexible response to the failed Big Company. Carmack’s Armadillo Aerospace work extends his learning loop into real engineering, where gravity, wind, and hardware are unforgiving tests that simulations cannot fully replace. The closing image of Romero and Carmack helping each other restart a stalled Ferrari is a modest reconciliation, not a return to the old company. The chapter’s thesis is that creative ambition survives when it can change scale, keep learning, and distinguish a shared history from a shared operating model.

## Frameworks Introduced

The chapter revisits the **persistent-world** model: a virtual space gains value when it remembers rules, supports other people, and continues beyond one session or one release. It pairs that with Carmack’s knowledge-building loop—each insight becomes a foundation for the next technical problem—and with a reality-check principle from rockets: physical systems provide feedback that cannot be negotiated away. A final framework is reconciliation by re-entry rather than restoration; the two Johns do not rebuild id, but they can recognize the shared source of their work and help one another move forward. Apply these ideas by preserving the learning and social infrastructure while changing the scale, ownership, or medium that created the failure.

## Key Concepts

- **Persistent world** — a shared system whose rules, objects, and relationships continue across sessions.
- **Doom III** — Carmack’s chosen return to the series after id debates whether to pursue a new direction.
- **Armadillo Aerospace** — Carmack’s rocket project, a real-world extension of his engineering and learning ethic.
- **Reality as test** — wind, gravity, and hardware expose assumptions that a screen simulation can hide.
- **Monkeystone** — Romero and Tom Hall’s smaller mobile-game response after Ion Storm.
- **Reconciliation without merger** — the founders reconnect personally without recovering the old company structure.

## Mental Models

Think of persistence as a memory-and-community problem, not merely a graphics problem. A world lasts when rules, objects, player identity, and access continue to reinforce one another. Think of learning as an asset that compounds even when a product or company fails; Carmack remembers the insights more than the releases, and Romero carries design instincts into a new medium. Think of reconciliation as a new interface between old components: the history is shared, but the implementation can remain separate.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a persistent world as an excuse to expand features without maintaining rules and community trust.
- Repeating a failed scale model because the original ambition still feels emotionally true.
- Assuming simulation removes the need for physical tests, feedback, or humility.
- Mistaking personal reconciliation for proof that the old organizational arrangement should return.

## Worked Example

Carmack’s move from game engines to rockets demonstrates transferable learning without pretending the domains are identical. He starts Armadillo Aerospace with the same preference for understanding mechanisms, building prototypes, and treating barriers as engineering problems, but gravity and wind provide immediate external judgment. The project reveals that simulation is useful yet incomplete: a real vehicle must survive forces that do not care about a programmer’s intention. Romero’s mobile work offers the complementary scale adjustment—smaller teams and devices make a focused experience more practical after the Ion Storm collapse. The two paths meet when Romero helps restart Carmack’s Ferrari, a concrete act that acknowledges shared history without reviving the old power structure. The application is to carry principles forward while allowing the medium, team, and ambition to change.

## Key Takeaways

- Persistent systems depend on rules, access, identity, and community, not novelty alone.
- Learning can compound across failed products, companies, and even domains.
- Reality tests assumptions more harshly and usefully than self-confirming simulations.
- Reconciliation can preserve respect without reconstructing an incompatible organization.
