# Glossary

**Advanced Technologies Group (ATG)** — Uber’s autonomous-vehicle organization, developed amid the Google/Waymo rivalry (Chs. 18, 24).

**Appification** — Moving an offline transaction into a mobile interface that coordinates discovery, payment, and fulfillment (Ch. 4).

**Champion’s Mindset** — Uber’s winning identity: urgency, competitive aggression, and refusal to accept limits (Chs. 9, 14).

**Class B shares** — Super-voting stock carrying ten votes per share in the book’s account, enabling founder control (Chs. 8, 28, 31).

**Contractor classification** — Uber’s position that drivers were independent contractors rather than employees (Ch. 12).

**Founder worship** — Treating a founder as the company’s irreplaceable source of vision, making intervention feel like value destruction (Chs. 8, 23, 28).

**Greyball** — Software that withheld the real Uber service from selected accounts and displayed a simulated service (Prologue, Ch. 25).

**Growth-first operating system** — A management logic that treats every obstacle as a delay to expansion and every metric as a reason to accelerate (Chs. 12, 15).

**Holder report** — Covington & Burling investigation into Uber’s culture and related conduct, with structural recommendations (Chs. 23, 27).

**Homeshow** — Uber’s concentrated investor process, designed around scarcity, founder access, and a tightly controlled schedule (Ch. 10).

**Marketplace network effect** — The reinforcing relationship in which more drivers improve rider service and more riders attract drivers (Chs. 7, 9).

**Negative churn** — Uber’s fundraising claim that repeat use and referrals made existing customers increasingly valuable (Ch. 10).

**Principled Confrontation** — Uber value that framed direct conflict and challenging constraints as virtuous (Prologue, Ch. 1).

**Safe Rides Fee** — A $1 customer charge presented in a safety context but described as margin rather than a dedicated safety fund (Ch. 14).

**SoftBank tender offer** — The 2017 secondary purchase of roughly 17.5 percent of Uber at a steep discount, paired with governance changes (Ch. 31).

**Super pumpedness** — Relentless urgency and stamina treated as a defining personal and organizational virtue (Ch. 1).

**UberX** — Peer-to-peer ride service that broadened Uber beyond premium black cars (Ch. 9).

**Venture power law** — The fund logic that many failures can be offset by one or two extraordinary investments (Ch. 8).

**VTOS playbook** — Uber operating material that helped spread Greyball-related tactics across locations (Ch. 25).

**Waymo** — Google’s self-driving subsidiary and plaintiff in the trade-secret dispute over Uber’s Otto acquisition (Chs. 11, 18, 24).

**Workation** — Proposed use of phone motion data to detect Lyft-driver activity; abandoned because of FTC concerns (Ch. 19).

**Xchange** — Uber-linked vehicle lease program associated with driver losses, unsafe vehicles, and moral hazard (Ch. 14, 22).
