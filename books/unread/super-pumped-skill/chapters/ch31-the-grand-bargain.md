# Chapter 31: The Grand Bargain

## Core Idea

Chapter 31 describes the search for a new CEO and the financing deal that finally made Kalanick’s removal durable. Jeff Immelt was Kalanick’s preferred candidate because he seemed more open to the founder’s continued influence, while Dara Khosrowshahi understood marketplaces, logistics, budgets, engineering, and brand repair; Meg Whitman was the strongest governance candidate but withdrew after leaks and demanded a board without unilateral actions. The board’s vote settled five to three in favor of Khosrowshahi after Benchmark’s ultimatum over its lawsuit damaged Whitman’s prospects. In December, SoftBank purchased roughly 17.5 percent of Uber’s shares through a tender offer at $33 per share, valuing the secondary market at about $48 billion, while a separate $1.25 billion primary investment preserved the paper valuation of $68.5 billion. The bargain added six board seats and enforced one share, one vote, sharply reducing Kalanick’s super-voting power. The distinction is between changing the CEO and changing the constitution: Khosrowshahi could only govern if capital, board composition, and voting rights prevented a second founder comeback.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Grown-up succession:** Choose a leader for operational judgment, governance, and stakeholder repair rather than charisma alone.
- **Governance recapitalization:** Use a financing transaction to reset board seats, voting rights, and shareholder coalitions.
- **Dual-track valuation:** Distinguish the discounted price investors actually pay for existing shares from a primary financing price that preserves a headline valuation.

## Key Concepts

- **Dara Khosrowshahi:** Expedia chief selected as Uber’s next CEO because of marketplace, logistics, and operational experience.
- **SoftBank tender offer:** Secondary purchase of approximately 17.5 percent of Uber at a steep discount.
- **One share, one vote:** Rule that ended the practical force of Kalanick’s ten-to-one voting rights.

## Mental Models

- **Leadership change requires constitutional change:** If control rights stay intact, the old system can return through formal or informal channels.
- **Price and valuation can diverge:** Analyze primary capital, secondary liquidity, and governance concessions separately.

## Anti-patterns

- **Choosing a candidate for founder compatibility:** A CEO who leaves a second center of power in place cannot repair governance.
- **Pretending a divided board is unified:** Managed unanimity may calm the announcement while preserving the conflict underneath.

## Worked Example

Khosrowshahi’s presentation included a clear message that Uber could not have two CEOs, while Whitman demanded an end to leaks and Kalanick’s operational role. After Khosrowshahi won, SoftBank’s deal added independent capacity to the board and removed the super-vote. The financial transaction therefore functioned as a governance settlement, not merely a new investment.

## Key Takeaways

- The right successor must match the company’s next operating problem, not its previous mythology.
- Capital can repair a control structure when ordinary board action is blocked.
- A discounted financing round can reveal how far private-market confidence has fallen beneath the headline valuation.
