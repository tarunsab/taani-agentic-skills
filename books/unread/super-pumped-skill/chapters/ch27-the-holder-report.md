# Chapter 27: The Holder Report

## Core Idea

Chapter 27 describes the Holder report as both an investigation and a referendum on whether Uber could change. Eric Holder and Tammy Albarrán reviewed millions of documents, interviewed more than two hundred people, and collected many more anonymous accounts; the final report described misconduct across global offices, including sexual assault and physical violence. Its top recommendations were structural: Kalanick should take a leave and relinquish day-to-day control, Emil Michael should be fired, and Uber should appoint an independent board chair. The seven-person board accepted the recommendations unanimously, but the rollout immediately exposed the old culture when David Bonderman made a sexist comment during the all-hands presentation and then resigned. The distinction is between adopting reforms and embodying them: a company can publish a “culture” plan while its leaders reproduce the behavior the plan condemns. The chapter also shows how Kalanick’s last-minute leave email kept open the possibility of return, so the report reduced his formal role without fully resolving the power struggle.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Structural remediation:** Change authority, reporting lines, and board composition rather than relying on individual discipline.
- **Independent oversight:** Put a chair and operating structure in place that can challenge the founder.
- **Reform under observation:** Treat every reform announcement as a live demonstration of the culture it claims to change.

## Key Concepts

- **Holder report:** Covington & Burling investigation into Uber’s workplace culture and related conduct.
- **Uber 2.0/Travis 2.0:** Kalanick’s language for rebuilding the company and himself after the crisis.
- **Board chair:** Independent role recommended to provide perspective and balance executive deliberations.

## Mental Models

- **Put the highest-leverage actions first:** Control, leadership, and oversight determine whether policies can survive pressure.
- **Public rituals are tests:** A single comment at a reform meeting can reveal whether the underlying norms changed.

## Anti-patterns

- **Treating a report as the remediation:** Findings do not change behavior without implementation, monitoring, and consequences.
- **Symbolic diversity without power:** Adding a director matters less if the board still permits founder domination or sexist norms.

## Worked Example

The board accepted Holder’s recommendations, and Kalanick announced a leave in an email that still said “see you soon.” During the same all-hands, Bonderman joked that a board with women would mean more talking; employees and reporters saw the contradiction immediately. Bonderman resigned, giving Kalanick one fewer board opponent while also demonstrating why reform needed independent governance rather than only new language.

## Key Takeaways

- Structural recommendations are stronger than cultural slogans because they alter who can act.
- Reform credibility can be lost in minutes when leaders are not held to the new standard.
- A leave of absence is not a succession plan if the founder retains operational access and influence.
