# Chapter 20: Three Months Prior

## Core Idea

Chapter 20 marks the political turning point before Uber’s public crisis fully broke. The 2016 election challenged Silicon Valley’s assumption that technology and social progress moved together; after expecting a Clinton administration to bring labor and regulatory pressure, Kalanick saw a possible advantage in Donald Trump’s victory. When Uber was asked to leave China, the company suspended operations and received a 17.7 percent stake in DiDi, a result that made Kalanick feel that the global war had been lost even as the deal preserved value. He accepted a seat on Trump’s advisory council and defended it as a chance to have a seat at the table, but employees saw the decision as a moral and reputational commitment rather than a neutral policy channel. The distinction is between access and alignment: proximity to power may improve information, but it can also signal endorsement and contradict a workforce’s values. Political context therefore became part of the product’s trust relationship with riders and employees.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Access-over-symbolism defense:** Justify participation in a controversial institution by emphasizing influence from inside.
- **Political-risk inflection point:** A change in government can alter the meaning of a company’s partnerships and public stance.
- **Strategic retreat reframed as portfolio value:** Convert a lost market into an investment position while absorbing the strategic defeat.

## Key Concepts

- **Trump advisory council:** The forum Kalanick joined and later left under employee pressure.
- **DiDi stake:** The equity Uber received after suspending China operations.
- **Seat at the table:** Kalanick’s argument that engagement was preferable to disengagement.

## Mental Models

- **Stakeholder interpretation matters:** A decision’s meaning is shaped by the people who must trust the company, not only by the executive’s intent.
- **Exit value is not operating presence:** Owning part of a market leader does not equal serving the market or learning from it directly.

## Anti-patterns

- **Treating political access as morally neutral:** Employees and customers may reasonably view participation as endorsement.
- **Defending a decision only through intent:** “I want influence” does not answer who is empowered or harmed by the association.

## Worked Example

Kalanick joined Trump’s advisory group and told employees that engagement could protect Uber’s interests. The defense failed to account for the symbolic effect of appearing beside a president whose policies many Uber employees opposed. The choice made later operational controversies easier to interpret as evidence of a company willing to trade values for access.

## Key Takeaways

- Corporate strategy includes political relationships and the meaning employees attach to them.
- A founder’s personal participation can become an organizational identity decision.
- Market exits can preserve financial value while still reducing strategic capability.
