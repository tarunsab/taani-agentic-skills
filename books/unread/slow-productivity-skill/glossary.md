# Glossary

**Active list** — The small set of projects currently receiving focused effort; Newport recommends no more than three in a simulated pull system (Ch 3).

**Appealing alternative** — A positive, enjoyable replacement that makes a failed status quo less attractive by comparison (Ch 2).

**Autopilot schedule** — Fixed times, places, and sometimes rituals for recurring task categories, reducing the thought needed to start them (Ch 3).

**Core activity** — The output that most determines a role’s impact or evaluation after surrounding busyness is stripped away (Ch 5).

**Docket-clearing meeting** — A recurring team session that resolves pending questions and assigns collaborative work in one concentrated conversation (Ch 3).

**Holding tank** — The nonactive queue for accepted projects waiting until capacity opens on the active list (Ch 3).

**Knowledge work** — Economic activity that transforms knowledge into a market-valued artifact through cognitive effort (Ch 2).

**Mission** — An ongoing goal or service that directs professional attention (Ch 3).

**Natural pace** — A sustainable, varied rhythm of effort rather than continuous maximum intensity (Ch 4).

**Office hours** — A recurring window in which colleagues or clients can resolve many small questions in real time (Ch 3).

**Overhead tax** — The coordination, memory, messaging, meeting, and switching burden created by an active commitment (Ch 1, Ch 3).

**Perfectionism** — Quality-seeking extended until completion, immediacy, or forward movement is blocked (Ch 5).

**Pull workflow** — A capacity-limited process in which a stage admits new work only when it is ready for it (Ch 3).

**Pseudo-productivity** — The use of visible activity as the primary means of approximating actual productive effort (Ch 1).

**Quality** — The excellence and value of the core artifact, rather than the volume of activity surrounding it (Ch 5).

**Reverse task list** — A public list where requesters enter complete task details and can see current status (Ch 3).

**Rest project** — Leisure deliberately scheduled after a demanding work project to create a peak-and-trough rhythm (Ch 4).

**Seasonality** — Variation in work intensity across a year or recurring cycle (Ch 4).

**Simulated pull** — An individual workaround for a push environment using a holding tank, a capped active list, transparent intake, and weekly cleaning (Ch 3).

**Slow productivity** — A philosophy for sustainable, meaningful knowledge work: do fewer things, work at a natural pace, and obsess over quality (Ch 0, Ch 2).

**Small seasonality** — Modest recurring respites, such as a meeting-free day, monthly weekday escape, rest project, or cooldown (Ch 4).

**Social capital** — Trust and esteem placed at risk when a public promise is made (Ch 5).

**Stress heuristic** — Waiting until personal distress feels high enough to justify saying no to incoming work (Ch 3).

**Striking ritual** — A memorable sequence of actions that shifts the mind into a more useful state for work (Ch 4).

**Task** — A smaller commitment that can usually be handled without the multiple sessions required by a project (Ch 3).

**Task engine** — A project selected or designed in a way that generates many urgent questions, requests, and coordination chores (Ch 3).

**Taste** — The developed ability to recognize what is effective, promising, or excellent (Ch 5).

**Tipping point** — The workload threshold beyond which overhead consumes enough time to prevent existing work from finishing (Ch 1, Ch 3).

**Through-line** — A central thread that gives a complex body of material a workable structure (Ch 0).

**Time-tested wisdom** — Practices refined through long cultural trial and error, used as a source of adaptable principles (Ch 2).

**Work poetically** — Shape spaces and rituals so the context of work supports attention, meaning, and a natural pace (Ch 4).
