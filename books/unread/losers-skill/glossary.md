# Glossary

**Active citizen** — A person who treats civic participation as a meaningful way of life despite ridicule, inconvenience, or low odds of immediate success (Ch 14, Ch 26).

**Content-free politics** — Campaign activity that supplies appearances, emotion, and repetition while avoiding consequential issues or clear commitments (Ch 14, Ch 26).

**Culture/politics question** — Lewis's unresolved question of whether culture primarily constrains politics or policy primarily reshapes culture; in quiet times he leans toward culture's constraint (Ch 16, Ch 23, Ch 26).

**Insider** — A discreet, stable, risk-averse political actor who works within party and institutional incentives and tends to compete through tactics (Ch 14, Ch 26).

**Loser** — A failed or marginal candidate whose ideas, passion, or conduct may reveal and reshape politics even without winning office (Ch 1, Ch 11, Ch 26).

**Media bubble** — The managed layer of cameras, handlers, sound bites, and staged backdrops that makes a candidate appear exposed while controlling what can be seen (Ch 3, Ch 5, Ch 22, Ch 27).

**Political food chain** — The movement of ideas and attacks through a campaign field: candidates poach from those below and attack those above (Ch 1, Ch 4, Ch 11).

**Protective imitation** — Nader's term for the two-party habit of copying positions so the parties remain electorally distinct in style but similar in substance (Ch 14, Ch 19, Ch 26).

**Rented stranger** — Lewis's term for a paid political professional who manages a candidate's image, access, messaging, and tactics (Ch 3, Ch 9, Ch 15, Ch 27).

**Symbolic politics** — A visible gesture or object that reassures a constituency or signals allegiance without necessarily solving the underlying problem (Ch 16, Ch 23, Ch 26).

**Outsider** — An agitator or champion of a lost cause whose temperament resists the system and whose target is often structural rather than partisan (Ch 14, Ch 18).

**Voice displacement** — The movement of a group's political expression into another cultural or informal channel when official politics stops speaking to it, as Lewis sees with rap and poor young Black men (Ch 20).
