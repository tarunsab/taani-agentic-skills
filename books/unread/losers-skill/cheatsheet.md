# Cheatsheet

## Decision rules

| If you see… | Ask / do… | Because… |
|---|---|---|
| Every major candidate sounds alike | Follow the most committed marginal voice | The bottom of the food chain may be framing tomorrow's issue |
| A “spontaneous” event has perfect timing, backdrop, and answers | Visit the setting without the campaign | The stage may be hiding the actual conditions |
| A campaign claims to be honest while buying distortion | Compare stated ends with the means used to win | Winning mechanics can erase the reason for office |
| A crowd is large but the candidate is ignored | Separate people, cameras, votes, and policy movement | Attention is not the same as influence |
| A politician uses a symbol as a solution | Identify the material problem the symbol leaves untouched | Symbolic reassurance can substitute for action |
| A movement is passionately unified | Ask what it excludes and what it cannot govern | Passion can reveal truth and magnify blind spots |
| A loser is being mocked as irrelevant | Track which ideas, language, or constituencies survive | Electoral defeat can still change the field |
| You feel certain about a public character | Seek an off-camera behavior that could falsify the portrait | Proximity and contradiction beat caricature |

## Insider / Outsider triage

1. **Temperament**: discreet and risk-averse → likely Insider; indiscreet and risk-loving → possible Outsider.
2. **Target**: rival party → conventional contest; system/institution → Outsider pressure.
3. **Issue**: safe position → protective imitation; structural issue → potential agenda change.
4. **Test**: Would the actor keep the position if it reduced the chance of winning? If no, it is tactics; if yes, it may be witness or conviction.

## Evidence ladder

- **Best**: repeated off-script behavior, direct observation, corroborated local facts.
- **Useful**: private conversation, campaign documents, contrasting public/private statements.
- **Weak**: a single sound bite, a staged backdrop, a poll without context, rumor repeated as fact.

## Tells and smells

- “Typical” people who were prepped beforehand.
- A campaign that hides from the press while claiming to be transparent.
- A symbol that is easy to photograph and hard to enforce.
- A platform too long for its candidate to read.
- Journalists chasing a disaster because the ordinary story has become boring.
- A movement whose followers know the lines but not the policy.

## Questions before acting

- What issue is being avoided because it is hard to frame?
- Who benefits from public boredom or private secrecy?
- What would this choice look like without cameras, polls, or donors?
- Is the proposed reform changing incentives, or only changing the sign above the problem?
- What does the loss preserve, and what does it fail to accomplish?
