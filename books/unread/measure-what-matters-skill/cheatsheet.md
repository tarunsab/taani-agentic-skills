# OKR Decision Cheatsheet

## Choose the goal

| If… | Do… | Because… |
|---|---|---|
| Everything seems important | Keep 3–5 objectives and name what will not be done | Finite capacity requires explicit tradeoffs |
| The goal is a mission or slogan | Turn it into a concrete objective with time-bound KRs | Direction alone cannot guide execution |
| The KR says “launch,” “analyze,” or “help” | Rewrite it as an outcome with evidence | Activities do not prove value |
| More output can damage quality | Pair the quantity KR with a quality/counter-effect KR | One-dimensional targets invite harmful optimization |
| A KR needs unusual attention | Elevate it to an objective temporarily | Altitude should follow importance |

## Set the operating mode

- Must be delivered at 1.0, with resources and schedule adjusted → **Committed OKR**.
- Valuable future state with uncertain path/resources → **Aspirational OKR**; expect roughly 0.7, not perfection.
- Need breakthrough rather than optimization → ask what 10x would require, then test it with smaller KRs.
- Culture is distrustful or accountability is unsafe → repair the environment and leadership behavior before scaling the tool.

## Align the system

- Publish company and team OKRs; explain the why repeatedly.
- Let contributors negotiate the hows and create roughly half of goals when context allows.
- Make every material cross-team dependency explicit; keep one accountable owner per OKR.
- When a new request arrives, ask: “What existing commitment will this displace?”

## Track and respond

| Signal | Move |
|---|---|
| Green / on track | Continue; do not add work casually |
| Yellow / needs attention | Update timing, scope, resources, or competing priorities |
| New need mid-cycle | Start an OKR and tell affected teams |
| Red / obsolete | Stop it, notify dependents, and record the lesson |

- End a cycle with three lenses: objective score, contextual self-assessment, reflection.
- Google-style bands: 0.0–0.3 red; 0.4–0.6 yellow; 0.7–1.0 green.
- If every aspirational OKR is green, the portfolio may be timid; if a committed OKR is at risk, escalate early.

## Keep performance human

- Use one-on-ones for goal setting, progress, coaching, career growth, and lightweight review.
- Make feedback specific, timely, two-way, and multidirectional.
- Recognize small and large contributions publicly, frequently, and against values or OKRs.
- Keep forward-looking goals separate from compensation decisions; otherwise people defend, sandbag, and stop stretching.
