# Chapter 11: Track: The Gates Foundation Story

## Core Idea

The Gates Foundation story applies OKR tracking to a mission so large and fluid that moral ambition alone cannot show whether capital is helping. Bill and Melinda Gates created a $20 billion foundation with a mission that everyone should have a healthy and productive life, and Patty Stonesifer needed a disciplined way to make choices while Bill’s time remained scarce. The foundation used the Disability-Adjusted Life Year (DALY) for a period to compare the potential effect of investments such as micronutrients, river-blindness work, and vaccines. A clear metric helped turn a mission into quarterly and annual beats, but the team also learned that data sets can be wrong or become obsolete; the yam seed example shows why adoption and user reality matter more than an impressive production number. Bill distinguishes a directional mission from an objective with concrete steps and an intended measurement, warning that an over-aspirational eradication date can damage credibility. Tracking therefore supports reallocation: if a key result shows that a path or investment is not advancing the larger aim, leaders can change the lever rather than defend the original plan. The malaria objective—global eradication by 2040—illustrates how a huge goal can be broken into regional elimination, scale-up tools, and sustaining current progress. The chapter’s limit is explicit: philanthropy lacks a market signal, so it must question continuously whether its measures are actually connected to ultimate impact.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Mission → objective → key results**
  - **When to use**: When a mission is morally broad or long term.
  - **How**: Define a concrete objective with a time horizon, then choose measurable levers that show progress toward it.
- **Metric as capital discipline**
  - **When to use**: When leaders allocate scarce funds across uncertain interventions.
  - **How**: Compare credible outcomes, set quarterly/annual beats, and reallocate when the evidence changes.
- **Big goal, practical lever**
  - **When to use**: When the desired end state is enormous.
  - **How**: Keep the ambition visible while defining the tools, coverage, and intermediate milestones needed to approach it.

## Key Concepts

- **Mission**: Directional statement of the change an organization exists to pursue.
- **Objective**: Concrete, time-bound result the organization is actively trying to achieve.
- **DALY**: Disability-Adjusted Life Year, used for a period as a comparative global-health impact measure.
- **80/90 rule**: A vaccine-coverage example in which 80 percent of districts reach at least 90 percent coverage.
- **Reallocation**: Moving capital or effort when tracking shows that an intervention or metric is not producing progress.

## Mental Models

- **Blank sheet with weight**: Unlimited ambition creates responsibility to prove that choices are making progress.
- **Rocks and levers**: A huge objective becomes actionable when the team identifies the few levers that can move it.
- **Metric humility**: A measure is a tool for learning, not a guarantee that the organization is measuring the right thing.

## Anti-patterns

- **Mission-objective confusion**: “Everyone deserves a healthy life” does not specify what will be done this cycle or how success will be judged.
- **Credibility-breaking aspiration**: A malaria-eradication date that is not realistic weakens trust in the whole system.
- **Metric worship**: Doubling production is not impact if the user will not adopt the intervention, as the yam-seed example demonstrates.

## Worked Example

The foundation’s malaria objective was **Global eradication of malaria by 2040**. Its KRs were to prove that a radical cure-based approach could lead to regional elimination, prepare a scale-up tool through the SERCAP diagnostic, and sustain current global progress so conditions remained favorable for eradication. The objective stays bold; the KRs make it possible to inspect whether the strategy, tools, and enabling conditions are moving. The same logic applies to vaccine coverage: a broad goal becomes operational when districts and percentages specify the next lever.

## Key Takeaways

1. Keep the mission directional but make each active objective concrete and measurable.
2. Use data to decide which levers deserve capital, while remaining willing to change the data.
3. Break a moonshot into meaningful quarterly and annual beats without mistaking them for the mission itself.
4. Treat an unclear or obsolete metric as a reason to learn and reallocate, not to defend the plan.
