# Chapter 9: Connect: The Intuit Story

## Core Idea

Intuit demonstrates how public OKRs can connect a mature, global technology organization that is simultaneously moving from desktop software to the cloud, from closed products to an open ecosystem, and from a North American company to a global one. CIO Atticus Tysen began with direct reports, then the director level, and finally all 600 IT employees, allowing a pilot to build enthusiastic rather than bureaucratic compliance. The department’s thousands of visible objectives let contributors see links between their daily work, colleagues’ priorities, and Intuit’s True North; about half aligned to higher-level or department goals. Transparency also gave remote teams in Bangalore a shared vocabulary with headquarters and made the constraints of IT legible to internal partners. Live, cloud-based data changed the required operating rhythm: leaders needed current funnel and subscription signals rather than a rearview-mirror report. When a key result needed extra attention—such as workforce technology—Intuit elevated it to a top-level objective, deployed Slack, Google Docs, Box, and BlueJeans, and later let the goal drift back to its normal altitude. Horizontal links eventually connected ecommerce and billing engineers directly, replacing senior committees with peer coordination. The lesson is to pilot, publish, connect, and adjust; visibility is valuable only when it changes how people work together.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Pilot before scale**
  - **When to use**: In a mature organization uncertain about the process.
  - **How**: Start with a few hundred users or one department, learn the failure modes, and expand when the system earns trust.
- **Altitude adjustment**
  - **When to use**: When a KR suddenly needs organization-wide attention.
  - **How**: Elevate it to a top-level objective with an explicit explanation, then return it to a KR when the special focus is no longer needed.
- **Live-data connection**
  - **When to use**: In a cloud or real-time operating environment.
  - **How**: Make current data accessible, link teams that depend on it, and encode the work in measurable objectives.

## Key Concepts

- **Enthusiastic compliance**: Adoption driven by usefulness and ownership rather than forced paperwork.
- **True North**: Intuit’s enduring mission-level orientation that gives local goals meaning.
- **Live data**: Current operational information used to make decisions in a fast-moving cloud business.
- **Horizontal connection**: Direct coordination among peer teams rather than relying on managers above them.
- **Rationalize, modernize, secure**: Intuit’s shared verbs for its technology transformation.

## Mental Models

- **Lasik for the organization**: Visibility can correct the blurry links between one person’s task and the company’s purpose.
- **Rearview mirror versus windshield**: Historical reports explain what happened; live data helps leaders steer what is happening now.
- **Connected house**: Public goals turn remote locations and formerly separate systems into one legible operation.

## Anti-patterns

- **Opaque IT activity**: Ticket volume and firefighting can hide whether technology is adding business value.
- **Forced rollout**: Mandating a process everywhere before proving its usefulness creates compliance without commitment.
- **Top-level goal hoarding**: If the CIO’s goals never change while everyone else’s do, leadership itself looks disconnected from the work.
- **Manager-only integration**: Expecting program managers to connect independent teams leaves the people doing the work without direct coordination.

## Worked Example

One EBS objective was to **Modernize, rationalize, and secure the technology used to run Intuit**, measured by migrating Oracle eBusiness Suite, delivering wholesale billing as a platform, onboarding small-business agents to Salesforce, planning legacy retirement, and aligning workforce-technology strategies. A separate top-level objective, **Enable every Intuit worker to make decisions based on live data**, used KRs for data marts, a real-time enterprise warehouse, unified visualization ownership, and a teaching module. The examples show how infrastructure work can be phrased in business terms and how an OKR can connect technical, geographic, and organizational boundaries.

## Key Takeaways

1. Use a bounded pilot to discover how the method fits an existing culture.
2. Publish priorities and constraints so remote and partner teams can coordinate realistically.
3. Elevate a KR when it needs special attention, but explain the altitude change.
4. Build horizontal links among the people whose work must actually fit together.
