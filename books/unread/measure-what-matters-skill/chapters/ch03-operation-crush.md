# Chapter 3: Operation Crush: An Intel Story

## Core Idea

Operation Crush shows how a coordinated OKR system can move a large organization rapidly against an existential competitive threat. In late 1979, Intel’s 8086 microprocessor was losing to Motorola’s 68000 and Zilog’s Z8000; a field sales warning reached Andy Grove, who helped redirect company priorities within weeks. Intel did not redesign the product. Instead, it changed the story and the system around the product: marketing emphasized Intel’s broader product family, technical support, system-level performance, and lower total cost of ownership, while sales shifted from persuading programmers to persuading CEOs. The phrase “as measured by” made the link between a broad objective and its concrete KRs explicit. Operation Crush cascaded from leadership but allowed lower levels to determine many of the mechanics, and its shared goals pulled sales, engineering, manufacturing, marketing, communications, and geographies together. The campaign created urgency without requiring every participant to know the whole strategy, because each group could see its part and report against black-and-white criteria. The story also depends on culture: field engineers could contradict senior leaders without retribution, and managers sacrificed local fiefdoms for the company’s survival. By 1986, the 8086 had recaptured 85 percent of the 16-bit market, illustrating the power of focus, alignment, tracking, and stretch in one case.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **“As measured by” (a.m.b.)**
  - **When to use**: When a broad corporate objective could otherwise remain a slogan.
  - **How**: Attach a short list of explicit, time-bound KRs that make the intended result assessable.
- **Crisis cascade with local hows**
  - **When to use**: When a rapid, company-wide response is required.
  - **How**: Leadership declares the priority and why; divisions adopt relevant KRs and teams determine the practical route.
- **Vector alignment**
  - **When to use**: When many groups are expending effort toward a common result.
  - **How**: Point their measurable goals in the same direction so effort compounds instead of canceling out.

## Key Concepts

- **Design win**: A customer agreement to put Intel’s microprocessor into a product or appliance.
- **Urgency**: A shared understanding that a threat requires immediate, coordinated action.
- **Cross-functional OKR**: A goal requiring material contributions from multiple departments.
- **Resource redirection**: Moving people, budget, or attention toward the priority as evidence changes.
- **Apolitical execution**: Sacrificing departmental fiefdoms for the greater organizational result.

## Mental Models

- **Rallying point**: A very large number can unify a demoralized group when it gives the effort a clear finish line.
- **Broom, not mess**: Leaders can state the mess to clean without dictating which broom every local team must use.
- **Crisis as diagnostic**: A crisis exposes whether a company can speak honestly, decide quickly, and coordinate across silos.

## Anti-patterns

- **Inspiring speech without a system**: Enthusiasm that produces no concrete KRs leaves people unsure what to do three months later.
- **Top-down mechanics**: Senior leaders cannot know every implementation detail; dictating the how would waste local knowledge.
- **Retaliating against inconvenient data**: Without psychological safety, the sales warning that triggered Crush would never have reached leadership.

## Worked Example

Intel’s corporate objective was to establish the 8086 as the highest-performance 16-bit microprocessor family. Its KRs included publishing five superior-performance benchmarks, repackaging the product family, putting the 8MHz part into production, and sampling the arithmetic coprocessor by a fixed date. An engineering objective then translated that direction into delivering 500 8MHz parts to CGW by May 30, with KRs for final art, masks, test tapes, and fab start dates. The chain preserved the corporate what while giving engineering a concrete how. A January kickoff spread the message through two management levels immediately and a third within a day; the campaign ultimately produced more than 2,300 design wins against the 2,000 target.

## Key Takeaways

1. In a crisis, state the priority and the reason, then attach explicit KRs immediately.
2. Cascade the outcome, not every local method; let expertise flow upward from the trenches.
3. Make every material department’s contribution visible and measurable.
4. Protect dissent and redirect resources without political retaliation.
