# Chapter 10: Benchmarks, Recovery Bets, and Moral Drift

## Source sections retained

Part Four §10; Part Five §10. Parts One, Two, and Three have no §10. The source number resets at each part boundary; both available sections are retained here.

## Core Idea

These sections move from an explicit decision to leave London to the confrontation that turns leaving into a fight. Gary's partner says she will follow him to Tokyo, but the move does not remove his dependence on the bank; it relocates it. In the Tokyo ramen scene, Caleb repeats the story of a trader whose attempted exit brought legal and financial trouble, and Gary hears it as both a warning and an invitation to submit. He responds by refusing sleep, becoming physically ill, and seeking a medical boundary rather than negotiating the bank's story on its terms. Kousuke's suggestion to record meetings introduces evidence as a counterweight to institutional memory and unequal power. The mechanism is a benchmark reversal: Caleb once benchmarked Gary's entry into the desk, but now the same sponsor voices the system's demand that he remain. The distinction is between advice from a trusted person and authority borrowed from the institution they represent. Practical application is to slow down high-pressure decisions, obtain independent advice, and preserve an accurate record of commitments and threats. The limitation is that the source intentionally withholds parts of the final conversation, so the reader cannot independently reconstruct every claim.

## Part Four: The move toward Tokyo

Frog refuses Gary's request for a sabbatical and says Caleb wants him in Tokyo. Gary plans to break up with Wizard to avoid dragging her into the move, but she says she will go too, and they leave London in September 2012. This is a practical act of loyalty that contrasts with the desk's transactional relationships. Yet the decision also carries a hidden assumption: that moving geographically will solve the problem of being trapped financially and psychologically. The source begins the Tokyo arc with that assumption already under strain.

## Part Five: The ramen warning and a medical boundary

In February 2013, Caleb takes Gary to ramen and asks whether he knows what happened to the Deutsche trader who tried to leave. The story of legal trouble, a broken career, and the maxim that bad things happen to good people is delivered by someone Gary once trusted as a sponsor. Gary recognizes that the warning is not neutral and decides to resist rather than accept it. He stops sleeping, vomits, and visits a doctor, who prescribes short-term anti-anxiety medication and a month of sick leave. Kousuke calls the situation potentially illegal and buys Gary a recorder, turning the conflict from a private emotional struggle into a question of evidence and process. Gary records a meeting with Caleb and tries to provoke clarity, but Caleb redirects authority to the company doctor.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Sponsor reversal:** the person who opened the game can later carry the institution's pressure.
- **Geographic fallacy:** moving location does not remove financial, contractual, or identity dependence.
- **Health as a boundary:** medical leave can be an enforceable limit when ordinary negotiation is captured by the employer.
- **Record as counter-power:** contemporaneous evidence reduces the institution's ability to define the meeting after the fact.

## Key Concepts

- Sabbatical refusal.
- Tokyo relocation.
- Ramen warning.
- Sick leave and company doctor.
- Recording and evidence.

## Mental Models

- **Advice provenance:** label each piece of advice as personal care, institutional policy, or strategic pressure.
- **Boundary ladder:** conversation → independent advice → medical limit → formal process; escalate when the lower rung is not respected.

## Anti-patterns

- Assuming a sponsor's loyalty survives a change in incentives.
- Treating relocation as an exit plan.
- Entering a high-stakes meeting without a clear record of what was said and what remains uncertain.

## Worked Example

Gary's doctor gives him time away when the bank's informal negotiation has become physically destabilizing. A practical version of this pattern is to recognize stress symptoms as decision-quality data, consult an independent professional, and make the boundary explicit. The recorder does not solve the conflict by itself; it improves the factual basis for the next step. The institution still may contest the boundary, so the plan needs follow-through rather than one dramatic meeting.

## Key Takeaways

- Trust and institutional authority can point in opposite directions.
- Exit decisions need a process, not only a declaration.
- Health and evidence are forms of agency when status and money are being used against the individual.
