# Chapter 7: Caleb Leaves; the System Promotes

## Source sections retained

Part Two §7; Part Four §7; Part Five §7. Parts One and Three have no §7. The source number resets at each part boundary; all three available sections are retained here.

## Core Idea

Caleb's departure reveals how much Gary's early career depended on a sponsor who could bend normal rules, and it forces the desk to redistribute both authority and risk. Gary temporarily becomes the Swiss-franc trader, executes the first trade from his private target sheet, and proves that a junior can turn a plan into twelve million dollars when a book becomes available. The celebration of Caleb's exit contrasts with Gary's later house party, where Harry's injury and the collapse of their friendship show that professional belonging has crowded out personal care. In Tokyo, Gary's communication with Kousuke improves only after he learns enough language to share ordinary food and fears, while Wizard's physical distress in London makes the distance between them tangible. The mechanism is substitution: the institution replaces a departing leader with a new boss and replaces damaged relationships with tasks, while Gary tries to replace dependence with control. The distinction is between promotion as opportunity and promotion as proof that the system can continue without the person who made it meaningful. Practical application is to identify which parts of a role are portable skills and which are borrowed authority. The limitation is that the book's comic portrait of Chuck and its compressed relationship scenes are deliberately subjective.

## Part Two: Caleb's exit creates Gary's opening

Caleb leaves at twenty-nine to build a house in California, fulfilling the fantasy that every trader discusses but few enact. Bill refuses the management job, Chuck becomes the new desk head, and Gary is left temporarily running the Swiss-franc book because Caleb's seat is empty. He uses the opportunity to place the first billion-dollar Swiss-franc trade from the two-page target sheet he made after asking Caleb how to earn a £100,000 bonus. The trade makes just over twelve million dollars by year-end, exactly matching the written plan. The source evidence supports both preparation and luck: Gary had a target and a mechanism, but he could execute only because a senior person's departure opened the book.

## Part Four: The farewell that does not feel like freedom

Gary's own farewell house party is supposed to mark his rising independence, but Harry arrives injured, bleeding from a glass in his hand. Gary leaves with Wizard and then does not speak to Harry for eight years. In the empty apartment afterward, Wizard finds a sofa through Freecycle while Gary chooses a work meeting over a school speech, sees holes in his shoes, and finally understands that he wants to quit. The scene demonstrates that material success has not produced a stable home or a stable self. It also shows a painful asymmetry: Gary can leave a physical party, but not yet the emotional obligations he has neglected.

## Part Five: Language makes a small human bridge

Kousuke remains in Tokyo after Arthur's departure, and Gary discovers that learning Katakana lets him communicate beyond the desk's English-speaking hierarchy. They eat okonomiyaki, exchange fears about work and quitting, and create a modest relationship inside an otherwise isolating environment. Gary cycles through Tokyo alone while Wizard waits in a London hotel and feels her legs shake when he holds her. The language lesson is practical and symbolic: a small investment in another person's world can restore agency that money and status had not supplied. The source does not make the bridge solve the resignation problem; it makes the cost of distance emotionally legible.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Sponsor dependence:** early access can be real and generous while still making a career vulnerable to one person's departure.
- **Opportunity through vacancy:** a role opens when authority leaves, but the new holder inherits its risks as well as its upside.
- **Portable versus borrowed power:** distinguish what one can do alone from what one could do only under a sponsor's name.
- **Language as agency:** learning the other person's medium can convert isolation into mutual understanding.

## Key Concepts

- Sponsorship and desk succession.
- Temporary book ownership.
- Target sheet and execution window.
- Empty apartment and broken friendship.
- Katakana and relational access.

## Mental Models

- **Departure audit:** when a mentor leaves, list the permissions, knowledge, and political cover that leave with them.
- **Bridge action:** choose one small, reciprocal act—language, meal, or honest conversation—that is not measured by PnL.

## Anti-patterns

- Confusing a vacant seat with a durable promotion.
- Treating an exact outcome as proof that the plan alone caused it.
- Using work continuity to avoid repairing a relationship.

## Worked Example

Gary's target sheet contains five trades, but the first becomes possible only when Caleb's Swiss-franc book is unoccupied. This is a useful distinction for any ambitious plan: specify the action, the capability, and the external permission each require. When the permission arrives, execute with defined risk; when it does not, do not pretend the plan was independently controllable. The same audit should be applied to a mentor's departure.

## Key Takeaways

- Promotions redistribute dependency; they do not eliminate it.
- A written target is useful only when the execution conditions are visible.
- Small acts of communication can recover agency that status cannot provide.
