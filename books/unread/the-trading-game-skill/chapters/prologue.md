# Prologue: The Warning in Tokyo

## Source section retained

Opening Prologue.

## Core Idea

The book opens in a Tokyo ramen restaurant, where Caleb's warning about a Deutsche Bank trader who tried to leave turns an apparently private resignation into a story about institutional retaliation. The story's mechanism is simple: a bank can use contracts, reputation, legal pressure, and deferred money to make an employee regret choosing freedom. Gary hears the warning through the lens of his own path from East London poverty to a trading floor, so the threat is also a reminder that the system he conquered may still define the terms of his life. The scene distinguishes formal legality from practical power; nothing has to be openly criminal for a well-resourced institution to make departure frightening. The prologue then folds into childhood memories of violence, exclusion, and the promise to become someone, which explains why losing the game feels more dangerous than continuing to play it. Its practical lesson is to treat an exit as a risk problem with dependencies, not as a single brave conversation. The scene also establishes the memoir's limit: it is a retrospective confession shaped by fear, loyalty, and the narrator's need to explain what winning cost.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **The institution can turn money into a leash:** deferred compensation and professional status change the payoff of leaving.
- **Threat is often indirect:** contract, reputation, access, and uncertainty can discipline behavior without a visible order.
- **The childhood scoreboard persists:** later wealth does not automatically erase the older need to prove that one belongs.

## Key Concepts

- Tokyo as distance from London but not escape from the desk.
- Exit risk versus employment risk.
- “Bad things happen to good people” as a warning about systems, not fate.
- Memory as an explanation for ambition.

## Mental Models

- **Ramen-shop stress test:** before leaving a powerful system, list the ways it can make the choice costly even if the choice is lawful.
- **Two games:** the visible game is PnL; the hidden game is who controls the terms of participation and exit.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a resignation as a purely emotional act.
- Assuming a successful career has removed the class and identity pressures that started it.
- Mistaking a warning from a senior insider for either a complete truth or harmless office folklore.

## Worked Example

Gary's decision is not just “I dislike this job, so I will quit.” He has deferred pay, a bank that may contest his route out, a partner in another country, and a body already showing strain. A robust exit plan therefore needs a health boundary, independent advice, a map of compensation clauses, and a fallback if the employer refuses cooperation. The prologue's evidence suggests that courage without preparation can leave the departing person negotiating on the institution's terms.

## Key Takeaways

- Institutional power is strongest where money, identity, and uncertainty overlap.
- The price of exit should be modeled before the exit conversation.
- The memoir's later trading lessons are inseparable from its class and belonging story.
