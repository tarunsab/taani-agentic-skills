# The Trading Game: A Confession

## Purpose

This is a private, study-depth skill built from the supplied full text of Gary Stevenson's *The Trading Game: A Confession*. It treats the memoir as both a trading education and an account of class mobility, institutional incentives, economic inequality, addiction to winning, and the difficulty of exiting a system that rewards profitable harm.

## Core frameworks

1. **Edge is disagreement made actionable.** A trader earns when a price is wrong relative to what the trader knows, observes, or infers; the edge is strongest when the market's consensus is wrong. The Trading Game makes this concrete through arbitrage, central-bank pricing, and bets built from ordinary economic facts.
2. **A good trade must survive its risks.** Being directionally right is insufficient if the position is too large, the funding can disappear, or the institution can fail first. Gary's Swiss-franc loss turns “right eventually” into a survival test: size the worst case, know the mechanism, and remain alive at the end.
3. **“Easy and allowed” is an institutional-risk signal.** The book's most profitable early trades were easy because everyone was doing them and allowed because senior leaders had authorized them. That combination can mean the system is concentrating one trade, one incentive, and one failure mode rather than proving the trade is safe.
4. **Read economies as lived systems.** Interest rates, debt, spending, and asset prices are downstream of households, businesses, governments, and unequal balance sheets. The narrative repeatedly contrasts models and market consensus with what Gary can see in shops, homes, streets, and conversations.
5. **Incentives shape both prices and ethics.** PnL, ownership of books, bonuses, sponsorship, social access, and deferred compensation decide what people notice and what they ignore. The same structures that create belonging and exceptional performance can normalize customer exploitation, silence, coercion, and moral evasion.
6. **Exit is a risk-management problem.** Leaving requires liquidity, legal and contractual options, a credible health boundary, allies, documentation, and willingness to absorb status loss. The final part is not a simple “quit your job” lesson: it is a study of how an institution can make money feel like a leash.

## Scope & limits

This skill is an interpretive study aid, not investment, legal, medical, or career advice. It reports the memoir's mechanisms and examples; it does not treat Gary's claims about markets, employers, or people as independently verified facts. The narrative is retrospective, stylized, and ethically self-accusing, so dramatic scenes should be used to generate questions and models rather than as universal evidence. The book's trading examples are historical and product-specific; current regulations, market conventions, and risk controls require current primary sources. The source sections are grouped only to meet the requested 20-file structure; use the “Source sections retained” line in each note when a part-specific passage matters.
