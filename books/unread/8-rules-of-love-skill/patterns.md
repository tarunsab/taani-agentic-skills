# Patterns — 8 Rules of Love

## Solo Audit
**When to use**: Alone time feels threatening, empty, or invisible.
**How**: Track one week of active unaccompanied time; record activity, comfort, and reason. Add one deliberately chosen solo activity each week for a month, then reflect on distraction, preference, and self-trust.
**Trade-offs**: It builds self-knowledge but does not replace friendship, partnership, or relationship practice. Choose activities that are safe and genuinely informative rather than using solitude as avoidance.

## Karma Cycle Review
**When to use**: The same relationship result or reaction keeps recurring.
**How**: Name the inherited impression; identify the adult choice it steered; record the consequence; decide what the result teaches; form a new impression and test a different choice.
**Trade-offs**: The model creates agency without requiring blame, but it should not be used to explain every misfortune or excuse another person’s harm.

## Gifts-and-Gaps Reset
**When to use**: A partner’s ordinary behavior triggers an outsized expectation.
**How**: Recognize the trigger; write a reminder of the response you want; repeat it as a mantra; reduce the old reaction; remove the habit through repetition. Fill the underlying need directly where possible.
**Trade-offs**: Reflection can reveal useful patterns, but deeply buried wounds may require therapy or other specialized support.

## Three-Date / Disclosure Lens
**When to use**: Attraction is strong but evidence about compatibility is thin.
**How**: Explore personality, then values, then goals through reciprocal questions and gradually increasing vulnerability. Observe curiosity, respect for hesitation, and interest in the other person’s actual life.
**Trade-offs**: The sequence slows fantasy and premature commitment, but it is not a rigid number of dates or a demand for identical values and goals.

## Rhythms, Routines, and Boundaries
**When to use**: Early dating is dominated by guessing, waiting, or over-merging.
**How**: Discuss contact, exclusivity, physical pace, and free time. Build a workable mix of nights alone, together, with mutual people, and with separate friends; revisit the agreement as reality changes.
**Trade-offs**: Structure reduces uncertainty and protects autonomy, but it cannot make two incompatible commitments align.

## Mutual Guru Practice
**When to use**: A partner’s growth or feedback is becoming a control contest.
**How**: Ask what matters, what is needed, and whether help is wanted. Support the partner’s goal, model the habit, match their learning style, translate before defending, and name specific strengths.
**Trade-offs**: Service can deepen trust, but an equal partner is not a parent, therapist, business mentor, or authority entitled to obedience.

## Pyramid of Purpose
**When to use**: A purpose feels vague, intimidating, or stalled.
**How**: Move through Learn, Experiment, Thrive, Struggle, and Win. Use mentors, small tests, routines, measurable actions, and a return to earlier steps when evidence shows a gap.
**Trade-offs**: The sequence makes purpose actionable without requiring immediate career change; financial, family, health, and time constraints still govern the plan.

## PEACE Conflict Agreement
**When to use**: A recurring argument escalates before either person can think.
**How**: Pre-agree on a place and time, shared intention, neutral issue statement, listening rules, possible solutions, and a return time. During conflict use specific “I feel” and “we” language; finish with acceptance, articulation, and action.
**Trade-offs**: Preparation improves the odds of repair, but both partners must participate. Abuse, coercion, threats, and control require safety support, not a better script.

## Elevate-or-Separate Ladder
**When to use**: A difference feels intolerable and the future of the relationship is uncertain.
**How**: Test safety and willingness first; move from intolerable to tolerable, understanding, acceptance, and appreciation only where mutual effort remains possible. If the issue remains unsafe or structurally incompatible, separate.
**Trade-offs**: Understanding may reveal compassion and options, but acceptance is not consent to permanent harm and separation is a valid outcome.

## Closure and Re-entry
**When to use**: A breakup leaves rumination, revenge urges, or fear of dating again.
**How**: Make an honest inventory of pain, responsibility, gains, losses, and lessons; construct a usable story; rebuild friendships, solitude, purpose, and boundaries; date again only when choice feels stronger than rescue.
**Trade-offs**: A healing story is useful but revisable, and clean distance often helps recovery; co-parenting or safety needs may require carefully bounded contact.

## Radius Expansion
**When to use**: Love has narrowed to one partner, family, or reciprocal approval.
**How**: Start with understanding, belief, acceptance, and appreciation for close people; extend service to colleagues, community, strangers, organizations, Earth, and the wider world. Adjust distance and disclosure to risk.
**Trade-offs**: Service can create connection and purpose, but it must not become people-pleasing, unsafe vulnerability, or a substitute for structural action.
