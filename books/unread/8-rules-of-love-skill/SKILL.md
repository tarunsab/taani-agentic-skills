## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**Love as practice and the four ashrams.** Love begins with attraction but survives through daily care, attention, and learning. Shetty uses the Vedic ashrams as classrooms: Brahmacharya prepares through solitude and past reflection; Grhastha practices love with another mind; Vanaprastha protects love through struggle, healing, and discernment; Sannyasa expands love through service. The eight rules are cumulative but can be revisited at any age or relationship stage. Treat the map as a developmental lens, not a promise that every relationship should continue.

**Rule 1 — Let Yourself Be Alone.** Use solitude when loneliness, chemistry, or dependency is distorting a relationship choice. Move from presence to discomfort to confidence: notice values, stay with unaccompanied experience, and act on growth goals with coaching, consistency, and community. Solitude creates the space between attraction and reaction and helps a person enter love as a whole self. It is preparation, not a substitute for connection or the entire education of love.

**Rule 2 — Don’t Ignore Your Karma.** Treat karma as the consequences that teach, not as a cosmic scorecard. Trace the cycle from absorbed samskara to adult choice to result, then revisit the impression and choose a new response. Examine parental gifts and gaps, media scripts, first loves, role patterns, and the opulences that attract you. Present the self you want loved, fill your own emotional gaps, and use past relationships as data rather than as proof of bad luck.

**Rule 3 — Define Love.** Do not treat “I love you” as a universal contract; ask what the phrase means and what behavior supports it. Use the four phases—attraction, dreams, struggle and growth, trust—to distinguish chemistry from a durable bond. The Three-Date Rule focuses attention on liking personality, respecting values, and wanting to help with goals; it is a lens, not a rigid schedule. Build trust through reciprocal disclosure, realistic rhythms, boundaries, follow-through, and repair.

**Rule 4 — Your Partner Is Your Guru.** A partner can teach with you, from their skills, and through the reactions the relationship evokes. Be a guru by serving rather than controlling, modeling good habits, supporting the other person’s goals, adapting to their learning style, and offering feedback without criticism or abuse. Be a student through beginner’s mind, humility, translation, and gratitude. Learn without making the partner your god: shared karma and life do not require surrendering identity, friends, interests, or judgment.

**Rule 5 — Purpose Comes First.** Dharma is purpose at the intersection of passion, capability, and service; it need not be a job. Purpose gives meaning to work, money, pleasure, and relationship, and protects self-esteem from external approval. Follow the Pyramid of Purpose—Learn, Experiment, Thrive, Struggle, Win—and treat the peak as a small by-product of the process. In a couple, protect both purposes with explicit agreements about time, money, childcare, household work, risk, and review dates.

**Rule 6 — Win or Lose Together.** Conflict is unavoidable; avoidance hides information and leaves partners on the surface. Reframe the issue as a shared goal, purify the ego, find neutrality, identify whether the energy is pointless, power-seeking, or productive, and peel the argument down to its inner source. Use PEACE—Place and time, Expression, Anger management, Commitment, Evolution—to turn disagreement into a concrete change and a real apology. The partner is never the enemy, and abuse is never a communication technique.

**Rule 7 — You Don’t Break in a Breakup.** Distinguish scratches from cracks and structural ruptures; do not catastrophize every flaw or minimize danger. If safety, trust, interest, or willingness to grow is gone, elevate only when mutual effort remains possible, otherwise separate deliberately. The path from intolerable to tolerable, understanding, acceptance, and appreciation can reveal compassion without requiring permanent tolerance. Closure is self-given through grief, an honest inventory, a usable story, restored value, solitude, purpose, community, and careful re-entry into dating.

**Rule 8 — Love Again and Again.** Expand the radius of love from close people to colleagues, community, strangers, organizations, Earth, and humanity. Give understanding, belief, acceptance, and appreciation to those closest; offer difficult people a radius of respect that protects safety and values. Serve without needing praise, debt, or reciprocity, while keeping wise boundaries around vulnerability. Small acts matter even when the larger problem is impossible to solve, and service joins connection with purpose.
