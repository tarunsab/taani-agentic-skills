# Cheatsheet — Four Thousand Weeks

## Fast decision rules

| If you notice… | Do this | Because… |
|---|---|---|
| The list keeps refilling | Keep an open list, but cap a closed list at 10; add only after completion | Capacity is finite; “everything” will not empty |
| Many projects are half-started | Serialize: one big project, or one work + one nonwork project | Switching preserves control-feeling and prevents depth |
| A valued activity waits for free time | Pay yourself first: reserve a small recurring block now | Free time will not magically appear after the backlog |
| Everything feels somewhat important | Say no to middling priorities; choose the few that define the season | A finite life must sacrifice even attractive options |
| You are avoiding a project | Ask what discomfort or loss starting would expose; begin anyway | Bad procrastination protects fantasy from reality |
| A device is one swipe away from escape | Remove optional apps, use grayscale, choose single-purpose tools | Environment beats a willpower contest |
| The future is demanding reassurance | Make a plan as present intent, then release the demand for certainty | Future time is expected, never possessed |
| A process will not hurry | Let it take its Eigenzeit; work in small sessions and stop on time | Speed can create errors and teach avoidance |
| Leisure feels like another assignment | Do something atelic, “wasteful,” or pleasantly mediocre | Rest has value without future productivity |
| You feel lonely despite flexible hours | Add a recurring shared rhythm | Time is a network good; coordination enables relationship |
| Your meaning standard requires a legacy | Apply cosmic insignificance therapy | Ordinary care need not be remarkable to matter |
| The whole path is unclear | Do the next and most necessary thing | The present is the only time available for action |

## Boundaries and defaults

- **Fixed volume**: closed list ≤10; no new entry until one leaves.
- **Work in progress**: default to ≤3 active items; break “write a book” into the next achievable step.
- **Workday**: when possible, set a finish time first and fit work inside it.
- **Rest**: schedule and socially reinforce it; expect discomfort before pleasure.
- **Attention**: distinguish involuntary capture from voluntary direction; do not aim for total control.
- **Generosity**: act on the impulse before perfectionism turns it into a deferred project.

## Diagnostic flow

1. **Is the demand genuinely impossible?** If yes, stop optimizing and choose what to neglect.
2. **Is the problem quantity, quality, or meaning?** Reduce commitments; protect consequential work; restore present value.
3. **Are you trying to control a person, process, or future outcome?** Convert the plan to an intention and allow uncertainty.
4. **Are you fleeing discomfort?** Name the intimate interrupter, stay with the present for one small interval, and return.
5. **Is individual freedom isolating you?** Trade some schedule sovereignty for a shared rhythm.

## Tells and smells

- “Once I finally get X under control, life can begin” → provisional life.
- Faster completion followed by more incoming work → efficiency trap.
- A good option that distracts from the few best ones → middling priority.
- A hobby valued mainly for profit or performance → instrumentalized leisure.
- “I can’t read anymore” when time exists → impatience with the pace reading requires.
- Crisis feed makes every crisis equally urgent → consolidate caring.
