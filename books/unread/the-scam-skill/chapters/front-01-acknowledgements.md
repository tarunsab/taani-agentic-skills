# Acknowledgements

## Core Idea

The acknowledgements explain the book's evidentiary posture: it was assembled through sustained investigative reporting, interviews, and anonymous source relationships rather than from a single official record. The authors say they followed the securities scam while working at *The Times of India* and *Business Today*, and spoke with brokers, bank officials, and other participants. Because securities transactions were opaque and many insiders feared exposure, anonymity was part of the reporting method. A particularly important anonymous source, nicknamed “Deep Throat,” is described as an insider in the securities business who supplied transaction-level detail. The authors also acknowledge that the book began in 1993, was revised after the Ketan Parekh episode, and was expanded across later editions.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Investigative triangulation:** official records, interviews, transaction details, and repeated source checks are combined.
- **Source protection:** anonymity can open access to evidence that institutions will not release, but it also requires readers to distinguish reported information from adjudicated fact.
- **Edition as update:** a financial scandal is treated as a continuing system rather than a finished event.

## Key Concepts

The reporting team and its source network are part of the book's method, not just background. The acknowledgements name interviews with senior market and banking figures, but also emphasize that some of the most useful information came from people whose identities could not safely be disclosed. The later editions signal that the authors regard the 1992 scandal, the 2001 scandal, and the subsequent institutional response as one connected story. Editing, research, and production acknowledgements also show the work as collaborative rather than the product of one narrator's access.

## Mental Models

Read the book as an investigative map with uneven evidentiary visibility. A named report, an admission, a conviction, a confidential source's account, and the authors' interpretation are different evidence classes even when they appear in the same narrative. The source list is therefore a reminder to ask “what supports this claim?” before treating a vivid episode as settled fact.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating anonymity as proof, or treating lack of public documentation as proof that an event did not happen.
- Reading the later edition as if every 1992 claim had acquired a final legal resolution.
- Separating the reporting process from the book's conclusions about institutional accountability.

## Worked Example

The “Deep Throat” example illustrates both the value and the limit of protected sources. An insider could explain how a bank receipt, broker, and settlement entry fitted together when a public reader could see only fragments. That access helps the authors reconstruct a mechanism. It does not, by itself, replace corroboration or tell the reader whether a court later accepted the interpretation.

## Key Takeaways

1. The book's source base is investigative and multi-layered.
2. Anonymous transaction detail is central to how the system becomes legible.
3. Editions and updates matter because the authors connect later scandals to unresolved institutional patterns.
