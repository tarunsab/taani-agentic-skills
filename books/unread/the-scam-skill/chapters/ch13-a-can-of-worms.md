# Chapter 13: A Can of Worms

## Core Idea

Canfina and CanMutual were meant to channel institutional and household savings, yet their links to brokers and parent-bank relationships made them major sources of liquidity for the scam network. Canfina collected public-sector money and traded aggressively; CanMutual collected small-saver money but was managed through broker relationships, cross-scheme transfers, and inflated performance. The chapter describes BRs and SGLs worth hundreds of crores, weak contract-note and exposure controls, and brokers who could influence or milk the funds while remaining close to senior staff. Canfina's PMS book grew to roughly Rs 3,162 crore and reportedly declared profits without booking the full losses. The central fiduciary question is whether a fund can claim professional management when its decisions protect a market ecosystem rather than its unit-holders.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Fiduciary inversion:** an institution serving savers acts to stabilize brokers or issuers instead.
- **Distribution-to-risk pipeline:** the parent bank's fund-raising capacity becomes market leverage.
- **Cross-scheme camouflage:** transfers and relationships can make performance look better while moving losses.

## Key Concepts

### Canfina's institutional cash

Canfina held about Rs 374.35 crore of Karad BRs/SGLs through Hiten Dalal, while CanMutual held about Rs 103.82 crore. Money moved through Abhay Narottam and other brokers. Canfina's aggressive launch of public-sector issues and IRFC bonds increased its role as a buyer and financier. When institutions were desperate to place or refinance securities, Canfina's relationship network made it easier to turn savings into trading liquidity.

### CanMutual and the small-saver promise

CanMutual was created in 1987 and marketed as a mutual fund for ordinary savers, but the initial capital and later scheme support depended heavily on Canara Bank and industrial relationships. Canshare's public subscription brought only about Rs 4 crore before the parent and industrialists increased the capital to roughly Rs 23 crore; Canstar grew to around Rs 1,000 crore. SEBI found high brokerage to Canfina, transfers between schemes, and broker collusion that inflated reported performance. Hiten's presence around CanMutual personnel illustrated how informal access could displace arm's-length oversight.

### Controls that did not control

Canfina lacked complete contract notes and BR records, exposure limits, and reliable evidence of who held the underlying securities. Brokers could take positions for themselves while appearing to act for the institution. The Reliance G-series episode showed how a large relationship could become an institutional safety net. The chapter says Canfina failed to book losses and instead reported roughly Rs 100 crore in profit while running a large PMS operation. Executives denied wrongdoing, but the pattern demonstrates why related-party and counterparty controls matter.

## Mental Models

Apply the **saver-to-risk trace**: saver contribution → fund mandate → manager decision → broker → security/counterparty → beneficiary → realized result. Check whether the fund's fees, brokerage, and parent-company incentives point in the same direction as the saver. If the fund's success requires supporting a connected broker's inventory, its fiduciary role has inverted.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a mutual-fund label as proof of arm's-length management.
- Measuring performance before accounting for missing securities and unbooked losses.
- Allowing a parent bank's distribution power to substitute for independent governance.

## Worked Example

The Karad BR holdings show why the “can” opens into many institutions. Canfina and CanMutual held large documents tied to a tiny bank and to the same broker network; replacing a bounced SGL or accepting a BR kept the position alive. A fund-level review that ignored the parent, broker, and bank links would see separate accounts rather than one liquidity chain.

## Key Takeaways

1. Savings institutions can become funding engines for market operators.
2. Related-party access and missing documents are a fiduciary risk, not just an audit issue.
3. Loss recognition is the dividing line between performance reporting and concealment.
