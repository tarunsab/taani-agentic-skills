# Glossary

Alphabetized working vocabulary from *The Scam*. Meanings are the book's period-specific usage; current law or market practice may differ.

- **Accommodation transaction:** A trade or loan arranged to provide liquidity or hide an exposure rather than to transfer a genuine investment risk.
- **Badla:** A carry-forward arrangement in which a position is rolled to a later settlement for a financing charge; the book distinguishes official and Kolkata “unofficial” badla.
- **Bank Receipt (BR):** A bank's promise that securities were sold and payment received, with delivery to follow. In the book, BRs sometimes replaced SGLs without underlying securities.
- **Beneficial owner:** The person or group that economically controls or benefits from an account, security, or trade, even when another legal entity appears on the record.
- **Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR):** The required cash reserve against deposits; the book pairs it with SLR as a constraint on deployable bank funds.
- **Carry-forward position:** A market position rolled beyond a settlement date, creating financing, margin, and delivery exposure.
- **Circular trading:** Repeated buy/sell transactions within a connected group that manufacture volume, support price, or move funds without independent demand.
- **Custodian:** The authority that attached notified persons' assets after the 1992 scam and was supposed to preserve and dispose of them for recovery.
- **FII:** Foreign Institutional Investor, a route through which overseas investment accounts entered Indian securities; the book examines opaque sub-accounts.
- **JPC:** Joint Parliamentary Committee. The 1992 and 2001 committees investigated the scandals, but the book criticizes closed procedures and weak follow-through.
- **K-10:** Ketan Parekh's group of ten favored stocks: Silverline, Global Telesystems, Satyam Computers, Zee, HFCL, SSI, Aftek Infosystems, DSQ Software, Ranbaxy, and Pentamedia.
- **Market-maker/jobber:** A trader who quotes or takes positions to provide liquidity in the exchange ring; Mehta learned this role before becoming a principal.
- **MMCB:** Madhavpura Mercantile Cooperative Bank, whose pay orders and lending exposed Ketan Parekh's 2001 funding chain.
- **NHB:** National Housing Bank, an RBI subsidiary that lent through bank channels and became central to the 1992 Mehta funding dispute.
- **OCB:** Overseas Corporate Body, often a lightly capitalized overseas vehicle used to invest or route money; the book repeatedly asks which regulator owned the perimeter.
- **PDO:** Public Debt Office, whose ledger of government securities helped SBI detect its mismatch with internal books.
- **PMS:** Portfolio Management Services. Rules required market-linked, documented, one-year arrangements without guaranteed returns; banks often used “assured,” “benchmark,” or “cash management” labels instead.
- **Pay order:** A bank-issued payment instrument. In 2001, Bank of India discounted Madhavpura pay orders before they were dishonoured.
- **Principal:** The economic risk-taker in a trade, as opposed to an agent executing for a client; Mehta's shift to principal trading magnified exposure.
- **Ready forward:** A linked sale and repurchase of securities that functions like secured financing when the second leg is genuine; the book describes versions with missing delivery or financing hidden in pricing.
- **Regulatory arbitrage:** Shifting an economically similar activity into the gap between formal rules, labels, or agencies.
- **SGL:** Securities General Ledger entry used to transfer government securities through the PDO. A bounced SGL indicates that the selling bank lacked the required balance.
- **Settlement Guarantee Fund:** Exchange-level protection that can secure payment when a member defaults; the book says it could also make synchronized financing trades look safer.
- **SLR:** Statutory Liquidity Ratio, the required share of deposits held in approved liquid assets; together with CRR it limited banks' free funds.
- **Sub-broker:** A broker or entity operating through a member's card or registration; multiple cards can multiply exposure and obscure the operator.
- **TMT:** Telecom, media, and technology stocks, the bubble sector at the center of the 2000–01 Parekh positions.
- **Unit-64/US-64:** UTI's Unit-64 scheme, a major mutual-fund vehicle whose holdings and bailouts affected small savers.
- **UTI:** Unit Trust of India, the dominant mutual-fund institution in the book, involved in both 1992 funding questions and the 2001 CSE/DSQ bailout.
- **Yield-to-maturity (YTM):** The effective return implied by a bond's price, coupon, and maturity; the book uses the distinction from nominal coupon to expose “rape deals.”
