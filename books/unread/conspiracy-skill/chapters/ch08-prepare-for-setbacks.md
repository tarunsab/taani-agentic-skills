# Chapter 8: Prepare for Setbacks

## Core Idea

The doing phase begins with almost no visible momentum: Hogan’s temporary restraining order and preliminary injunction are denied, a copyright theory requires obtaining the recording rights from Bubba Clem, and the FBI sting over further tapes ends without charges. Holiday treats these reversals as the normal friction of litigation and conspiracy, where progress is measured over years rather than headlines. The team responds by moving the case from federal to Florida state court, a procedural retreat that unexpectedly exposes Gawker to a supersedeas bond tied to the verdict, capped at $50 million—an amount the company may not be able to post. This illustrates the difference between a plan and a route through reality: a good case can be delayed, displaced, or nearly killed while still accumulating strategic position. The chapter links conspiracy to Paul Graham’s “trough of sorrow” and Clausewitz’s friction: after initial excitement, a project meets delays, mistakes, bad data, and the distance between a clear view and a short distance. The required habit is to expect difficulty, build redundancy, and treat obstacles as information rather than as proof that the project is impossible. Thiel’s team also preserves perspective by treating Hogan’s case as one possible vehicle among several, even while using it as the main effort. Setbacks do not guarantee success; they test whether the team can continue long enough to discover the opportunity hidden inside them.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Friction**: The delays, procedural surprises, mistakes, and environmental complications that distort a clean plan.
  - When to use: In forecasting any long campaign; assume the plan will meet resistance that is neither strategic nor fair.
  - How: Add time, money, and procedural redundancy; update the plan as friction reveals the real terrain.
- **Trough of sorrow**: The post-launch descent from excitement into hard, uncertain work.
  - When to use: To normalize the period when early attention fades and execution becomes repetitive and discouraging.
  - Limit: A trough is not evidence that the project is sound; it still needs learning and a viable path.
- **Two steps forward, one step back**: Judge progress by net position, not by the emotional meaning of each ruling.
- **Supersedeas-bond leverage**: A procedural requirement can turn a verdict into an existential financial threat.

## Key Concepts

- **Temporary restraining order**: Short-term relief sought before a full hearing.
- **Preliminary injunction**: A temporary order preserving a position before trial.
- **Prior restraint**: Pretrial removal of speech or material.
- **Forum shift**: Moving a case into a different court with different strategic consequences.
- **Supersedeas bond**: Security required to pause enforcement while appealing.
- **Rube Goldberg contraption**: Thiel’s image for a plan with too many probabilistic failure points.
- **Redundancy**: Prepared alternatives that absorb expected loss.
- **Entropy**: The tendency for an extended campaign to accumulate disorder and decay.

## Mental Models

- Use **the setback-as-data rule**: ask what a denial reveals about venue, doctrine, timing, or the opponent’s assumptions.
- Think of **procedural retreat as terrain selection**, not automatically as defeat.
- Use **the expectation buffer**: if a plan requires a particular ruling at every step, it is too fragile.

## Anti-patterns

- **Treating early momentum as the plan**: Courtroom publicity cannot substitute for a durable legal path.
- **Emotional overreaction to each setback**: It wastes energy and obscures cumulative progress.
- **Ignoring second-order procedural effects**: Moving a case may alter appeal, bonding, venue, and financial leverage.
- **Relying on one scenario**: A single plaintiff or ruling can fail for reasons unrelated to the underlying objective.

## Worked Example

The team loses in federal court repeatedly and eventually amends the Florida state complaint to include Gawker. Gawker calls the move forum shopping and expects another victory, but the state venue is where a large verdict would require a bond Gawker may not afford. The FBI sting also appears to fail: the government declines prosecution, and a participant retracts a recorded statement. Yet the sting produces evidence that Hogan did not leak the tape and helps establish the emotional stakes, while the state-court move keeps the case alive. The same sequence is therefore a legal setback, an evidentiary complication, and a strategic improvement depending on the time horizon.

## Key Takeaways

1. Plan for friction as a default condition, not a surprise.
2. Measure the campaign’s position after setbacks instead of judging one ruling in isolation.
3. Preserve alternate paths while concentrating resources on the strongest one.
4. Look for procedural consequences that the opponent’s immediate victory may conceal.
