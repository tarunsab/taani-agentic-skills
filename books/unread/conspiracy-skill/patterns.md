# Reusable Patterns

These patterns abstract questions from Holiday's narrative. They are for lawful, accountable strategy and analysis, not covert harm, harassment, or evasion.

## Define the Objective and Phase

**When to use:** When a dispute feels emotionally urgent or the desired outcome is still vague.  
**How:** Name the end state, the current phase—planning, doing, or aftermath—and the cost of inaction. Separate the direct objective from status, revenge, publicity, and other motives.  
**Trade-offs:** A narrow objective improves discipline but may omit legitimate wider effects; a broad objective can rationalize endless action.  
**Explanation:** Holiday's three-part structure shows that the same tactic changes meaning across phases. A plan that is appropriate before filing can be reckless after a verdict. Naming the phase exposes whether a proposed action advances the objective or merely relieves emotion.

## Build a Prepared “Briefcase”

**When to use:** When a principal has resources but lacks time, specialized skill, or a trusted execution path.  
**How:** Choose a small team with complementary competence, give clear authority, define roles and reporting, and prepare for likely objections before the public move. Keep access proportionate to responsibility, but preserve ethical oversight and a record of decisions.  
**Trade-offs:** Delegation increases speed and focus; compartmentalization can create blind spots, leaks through human error, or moral diffusion.  
**Explanation:** The book's professional-son and plenipotentiary images emphasize fit, hunger, and authority over headcount. The point is not secrecy for its own sake; it is reducing coordination cost while retaining responsibility at the principal level.

## Select the Back Door

**When to use:** When the opponent dominates the obvious public contest.  
**How:** Work backward from a lawful, defensible end state; map the opponent's strongest defense, neglected obligations, and institutional venues; then test whether the alternative route has factual merit.  
**Trade-offs:** Indirect routes can bypass entrenched power, but they are slower to explain and can look manipulative if the claim is weak.  
**Explanation:** Holiday's “back door” is a change of terrain, not a loophole license. The Hogan case matters because the alternate route rested on a direct privacy injury and a sympathetic fact pattern. If the underlying claim would not survive scrutiny, the back door is only concealment.

## Run a Reconnaissance Pass

**When to use:** Before committing to a costly action or assuming the opponent is predictable.  
**How:** Study incentives, identity, money, time horizon, allies, public statements, institutional habits, and likely reactions. Look for an opponent's unforced errors, but distinguish evidence from a story you want to believe.  
**Trade-offs:** Better modeling creates leverage; obsessive research can delay action, invade privacy, or turn people into caricatures.  
**Explanation:** Gawker's failure to understand Thiel's patience and resources is a central contrast. Opponent knowledge is most valuable when it changes the choice of terrain or timing, not when it merely supplies embarrassing trivia.

## Use Compartmentalized Roles and a Main Thrust

**When to use:** When several lawful avenues can create pressure but attention and trust are scarce.  
**How:** Label the decisive objective, supporting probes, and stop conditions. Give each specialist a bounded remit, avoid unnecessary duplication, and return resources to the main thrust when a side action no longer changes the opponent's choices.  
**Trade-offs:** Multiple fronts create uncertainty and consume capacity; too much dispersion obscures accountability and weakens the decisive case.  
**Explanation:** Holiday presents side lawsuits and public confusion as pressure around the Hogan case, not as substitutes for it. The pattern is useful only while each front contributes information, leverage, or protection to the endgame.

## Test the Real Audience

**When to use:** When success depends on a jury, customer, voter, employee, or community rather than on a technical expert alone.  
**How:** Identify what the audience must remember, match the messenger to its social context, make the concrete facts visible, and test whether the legal or abstract rule follows naturally from them.  
**Trade-offs:** Humanization increases moral clarity but can oversimplify; local fit helps persuasion but may reward bias.  
**Explanation:** The trial chapters distinguish arguing the law from winning hearts and minds. Local counsel and Hogan's testimony gave the jury a person and an act to judge, while Gawker's lecture on principle felt detached from the harm.

## Offer a Paved Retreat

**When to use:** When the objective is secured but the opponent can still retaliate or destroy remaining value.  
**How:** Define what must be deterred, what can be conceded, and what exit preserves the opponent's dignity without restoring the harmful status quo. Negotiate the exit as a way to close the conflict, not as a denial of what happened.  
**Trade-offs:** Mercy can shorten conflict and preserve value; excessive concession can erase deterrence or reward misconduct.  
**Explanation:** Holiday's examples of Grant and Lee, Scipio, and the Thiel-Denton settlement show that annihilation is rarely the practical goal. A retreat is part of victory when it makes the result durable.

## Audit Second- and Third-Order Effects

**When to use:** Before declaring success, especially when a tactic creates a new capability or precedent.  
**How:** List intended outcomes, foreseeable side effects, who gains or loses, how the method may be copied, and what happens if the secret becomes public. Set a review date and a stopping rule.  
**Trade-offs:** Forecasting improves responsibility but can never eliminate uncertainty; excessive caution can preserve the original harm.  
**Explanation:** The book's final chapter tracks privacy gains, chilling-effect concerns, political blowback, imitation by less restrained actors, and power's effect on its users. The correct response is neither triumphalism nor paralysis, but explicit ownership of the full consequence set.
