# Cheatsheet

## Fast decision rules

| If you notice… | Ask / do… | Book logic |
|---|---|---|
| “Nothing can be done.” | Which constraint is real, and which is only the opponent's preferred terrain? | High agency begins by testing the frame. |
| A public fight favors the opponent. | Is there a lawful, meritorious back door? | Change terrain; do not weaken the claim. |
| A promising plaintiff or ally appears. | Is the fit strong enough to carry the endgame? | Opportunity matters when facts, person, venue, and objective align. |
| The plan needs everyone to know everything. | Which roles actually require full context? | Small teams and bounded knowledge reduce noise and leaks. |
| The first move fails. | What did the obstacle reveal; what redundancy or alternate route exists? | Friction is information, not a verdict. |
| The opponent seems carefree. | What are they failing to model about us, the audience, or the time horizon? | Unforced errors can create leverage. |
| Several fronts are available. | Which is the main thrust, and what is each side action for? | Pressure is useful only when it serves the endgame. |
| You are about to win. | What must be enforced, explained, settled, or stopped next? | Victory creates a new game. |
| The opponent is cornered. | What dignified exit ends the conflict without restoring the harm? | A paved retreat makes deterrence durable. |
| A tactic is illegal, gray, or meritless. | Reject it; return to a defensible claim. | Secrecy cannot make a bad method legitimate. |
| Moral certainty is rising. | Where could we become like the enemy, and who bears collateral cost? | Agency needs compassion and limits. |

## Three-phase map

1. **Planning:** define the objective, decide to act, select terrain, assemble a capable team, and locate the back door.
2. **Doing:** expect friction, model the opponent, protect necessary secrets, use pressure deliberately, and maintain coalition cohesion.
3. **Aftermath:** win the audience, enforce the result, manage disclosure, negotiate an exit, and audit unintended effects.

## A compact endgame test

Before the next move, answer:

- What exact change counts as success?
- What is the strongest lawful evidence for it?
- Who must be persuaded, and what will they remember?
- What can the opponent still do after losing?
- What is the stopping point?

## Smells and safeguards

- **Revenge replacing objective:** write the objective in one sentence and remove actions that do not advance it.
- **Coalition held only by shared hatred:** test whether members still cooperate when the target is gone.
- **Secret that cannot survive exposure:** reconsider legality, proportionality, and explanation before proceeding.
- **Public victory with no narrative:** prepare a truthful account of purpose, process, and limits.
- **Power becoming addictive:** schedule an outside review and ask whether the next action protects value or merely extends control.
