# Glossary

**Abatis** — A defensive obstacle; in the book, Gawker's public shamelessness makes criticism harder to land (Chapters 3, 9).

**Actual malice** — The public-figure defamation standard discussed as a limit on an easier legal route (Chapter 5).

**Agency** — The capacity to shape events intentionally rather than treating existing power as fate (A Word, Chapters 2–3, Conclusion).

**Aftermath** — The phase after action when enforcement, narrative, retaliation, settlement, and consequences become the live problem (Introduction, Chapters 16–18).

**Amor fati** — Stoic acceptance of what has happened, used as a way to face setbacks and consequences without denial (Chapters 12, 18).

**Back door** — An indirect route around the opponent's strongest defense, found by working backward from the desired end (Chapter 5).

**Blowback** — Harm that returns from a tactic or capability after its initial target has been defeated (Chapter 18).

**Casus belli** — The incident that supplies a conflict's immediate cause; the Hogan tape becomes the campaign's flash point (Chapters 1, 7).

**Champerty** — Funding litigation without being the direct claimant, raised in the discussion of Thiel's role and its public meaning (Chapter 18).

**Compartmentalization** — Separating knowledge and roles so that few participants hold the entire plan (Chapters 4, 10).

**Conspiracy** — Holiday's broad term for determined people coordinating, often secretly, to disrupt an established order or accomplish a difficult aim (A Word).

**Culture eats strategy** — The idea that norms, incentives, and routines can defeat an organization's formal plan (Chapter 12).

**Culminating point** — The point at which further advance begins to endanger the victory already secured (Chapters 16–17).

**Discovery** — The pretrial process of exchanging evidence and questioning parties and witnesses (Chapters 8–13).

**Friction** — Delays, mistakes, procedural obstacles, uncertainty, and emotional drag that accumulate during execution (Chapters 8, 13).

**High agency** — Refusing to accept “nothing can be done” as a final description of the situation (Chapter 3).

**Hofstadter's Law** — The observation that a project takes longer than expected even after accounting for that tendency (Chapter 13).

**Intrigue** — A coordinated secret effort to alter an existing order; the book uses it as a morally open category (A Word).

**Kayfabe** — Wrestling's staged public fiction, used as a metaphor for routine legal posturing until the dispute becomes a real fight (Chapters 7, 15).

**Maskirovka** — Active deception or confusion intended to make the opponent misread the main action (Chapter 11).

**Mimetic desire** — René Girard's idea that people want what others want, making imitation a source of rivalry (Chapters 3, 17).

**Open secret** — A fact known privately or by insiders but not publicly acknowledged (Chapter 1).

**Plenipotentiary model** — A principal delegates broad authority to a trusted representative who can act without constant permission (Chapter 4).

**Prior restraint** — A court order sought before publication to suppress speech; the first injunction attempt fails (Chapter 8).

**Professional son** — The book's image for an ambitious younger operator seeking a demanding patron, competence, and a legacy (Chapter 4).

**Ressentiment** — Resentment that feeds on status injury and reproduces more resentment (Chapter 1).

**Shoot** — A real fight that breaks the expected script, contrasted with wrestling's kayfabe (Chapter 15).

**Specific deterrence** — Making a particular actor less likely to repeat the conduct, distinct from destroying every possible adversary (Chapters 16–17).

**Streisand Effect** — The risk that an attempt to suppress information increases attention to it (Chapter 3).

**Supersedeas bond** — Security required to pause enforcement of a judgment during appeal (Chapters 8, 16).

**Trough of sorrow** — The long, discouraging work period after an exciting beginning or apparent breakthrough (Chapters 8, 13).

**Unforced error** — An opponent's self-damaging act that the other side did not need to create (Chapter 9).

**Winchell Jitters** — The inhibiting fear that a gossip columnist's attention can create for public figures (Chapter 2).

**Working backward / endgame** — Starting from the desired final position and reasoning toward a feasible next move (Chapter 5).
