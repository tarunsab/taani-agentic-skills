# Chapter 10: How to Make Comparison Your Teacher

## Core Idea

This chapter turns comparison from self-punishment into data. Comparison is useful when it points to a changeable result—better work, health, relationships, money, habits, or creative output—that could be built through time and consistent effort. Robbins insists that another person’s success is not evidence that the opportunity is gone; it can be the formula showing what is possible and what work has been done. The story of interior designer Molly makes jealousy diagnostic: a disliked neighbor’s polished website and successful social posts expose the marketing work Molly has postponed. Robbins calls jealousy an invitation from a future self and anger a possible fuel, but only if the feeling is converted into action and “putting in the reps.” A second story about a friend’s renovated house reveals a deeper truth: the jealousy was not really about the house but about Robbins having abandoned her own ambition and expected someone else’s success to fund the life she wanted. The book’s practical distinction is therefore between noticing possibility and merely consuming someone else’s success; the teacher is useful only when Let Me turns the lesson into repeated work.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Comparison as teacher**: Use another person’s changeable result as a model or formula.
  - When to use: You want something the example has achieved and could create through sustained effort.
  - How: Extract the behaviors, skills, and repetitions behind the result, then start doing them.
- **Jealousy as an invitation**: Treat envy as a clue about a desired future rather than proof of inferiority.
  - When to use: Someone’s success provokes anger, shame, or a painful sense of being behind.
  - How: Ask what the reaction is showing you and choose the first rep.
- **Putting in the reps**: Build skill and results through consistent, sometimes boring practice.

## Key Concepts

- **changeable result**: An outcome that can plausibly be influenced by learning, effort, consistency, or time.
- **formula**: The observable sequence or structure behind another person’s result.
- **jealousy data**: Information about a desire, neglected responsibility, or possible path.
- **anger as fuel**: Energy that can galvanize action when it is not spent attacking the other person.
- **reps**: Repeated practice or work that compounds into capability and results.
- **abundance frame**: The book’s assertion that another person’s happiness, success, or money need not reduce your possibility.

## Mental Models

- Ask, “What is this person showing me that I could learn or build?”
- Compare behavior and process, not only the polished outcome.
- Let the emotional signal wake you up, then stop feeding it and get to work.

## Anti-patterns

- **Treating wins as theft**: Another person’s success does not prevent you from building a similar result.
- **Admiring the result while avoiding the reps**: The polished website or house hides months or years of work, not a shortcut.
- **Using inspiration as a substitute for consistency**: A brief emotional lift does not create the desired change.

## Worked Example

Molly sees a neighbor with no interior-design background attract attention online and becomes consumed by the neighbor’s website, posts, and comments. Robbins reframes the neighbor as a teacher: the website, daily content, and professional presentation reveal work Molly has known she should do. Robbins’s own renovated-house story then reveals that envy was also anger at having postponed her ambition. The Let Me response is to update the site, learn the platform, create content, and keep doing the work until the life she wants becomes possible.

## Key Takeaways

1. Let others lead the way when their results are changeable and relevant.
2. Use jealousy to name a desire or neglected action.
3. Study the process behind the result and put in the reps.
4. Do not turn anger into gossip or self-attack; turn it into a first move.
