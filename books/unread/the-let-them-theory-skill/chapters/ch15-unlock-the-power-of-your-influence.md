# Chapter 15: Unlock the Power of Your Influence

## Core Idea

Once pressure is removed, the chapter redirects energy toward influence through example. People are social creatures, and the book’s account of Dr. Tali Sharot’s research calls behavior spreading through observation **social contagion**. A co-worker who takes an enjoyable lunch walk can make the action look easy, pleasurable, and effective until another person feels that the idea is their own. The practical sequence is to Let Them be, model the desired change for yourself, and wait without expecting the other person to follow; Robbins says this may take six months or more. For a more deliberate attempt, she creates the **ABC Loop**: Apologize for judging or pressuring, Ask open-ended questions, Back off and observe Behavior, then Celebrate progress while continuing to model Change. The preparatory **5 Whys** exercise helps uncover whether the supporter’s anger is really fear, shame, judgment, or loss of control. Open questions from motivational interviewing are meant to help the other person hear the gap between what they want and what they do, not to make them confess or accept your opinion. Immediate, genuine positive acknowledgment pairs a difficult behavior with pleasure and can strengthen intrinsic motivation, while the person may still choose not to change.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Social contagion**: Model a behavior so others observe its benefits and become more likely to approach it.
  - When to use: You want to influence a habit without demanding compliance.
  - How: Practice consistently, make the change visibly enjoyable, and remove expectation.
- **5 Whys**: Ask why the situation bothers you, then ask why four more times to reach the deeper source.
  - When to use: You are angry, ashamed, or stuck in a pressure cycle.
  - How: Write each answer honestly; use the result to own your part and prepare an apology.
- **ABC Loop**:
  - **A — Apologize and Ask**: Apologize for pressure, then ask open questions and reflect answers.
  - **B — Back off and observe Behavior**: Stop intervening and give space for tension to become motivation.
  - **C — Celebrate and model Change**: Acknowledge small progress immediately while continuing your example.

## Key Concepts

- **positive influence**: Change pressure into an observable example that another person can choose.
- **open-ended question**: A question that invites the other person to describe their own experience.
- **motivational interviewing**: The research-backed conversational approach the chapter uses to evoke self-reflection.
- **behavioral discrepancy**: The tension between what someone says they want and what their current behavior produces.
- **immediate positive reward**: Encouragement after a hard action that makes repeating it more attractive.
- **expectation-free modeling**: Demonstrating a change because it helps you, not as a covert demand.

## Mental Models

- Be the evidence of the change you want to see.
- Your role in the ABC Loop is to evoke reflection, not to deliver the correct answer.
- Space and time are active ingredients: they let the other person own the idea.

## Anti-patterns

- **Modeling as a hidden demand**: If every healthy action is followed by “now you should,” influence becomes pressure.
- **Turning open questions into a cross-examination**: An opinion or argument ends the curiosity the method needs.
- **Demanding immediate results**: Motivation may take months, and the person may never choose the change.
- **Celebrating only perfect outcomes**: The chapter emphasizes small progress as the behavior to reinforce.

## Worked Example

Before speaking with the husband about health, the supporter asks five whys. The surface complaint becomes concern about the children’s model, resentment about his choices, fear of losing him, and finally fear of losing the love of her life. She apologizes for judging, asks how he feels about his health, reflects his answers without inserting her view, and then backs off. If he exercises, she offers immediate affection and praise; if he does not, she keeps modeling her own change and later decides what the unchanged pattern means for her.

## Key Takeaways

1. Let Them be before trying to influence.
2. Use 5 Whys to discover the fear or control need underneath your pressure.
3. Apologize, ask, listen, back off, model, and celebrate small progress.
4. Give influence time and release the expectation that the other person must change.
