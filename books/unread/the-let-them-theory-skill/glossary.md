# Glossary

**ABC(DE) Loop** — An extension of the ABC Loop for relationships: after Apologize/Ask, Back off/observe Behavior, and Celebrate/model Change, Decide whether the issue is a Deal Breaker and End the bitching or End the relationship (Ch 15, Ch 20).

**ABC Loop** — A change-influence sequence: Apologize and Ask open-ended questions; Back off and observe Behavior; Celebrate progress while modeling Change (Ch 15).

**agency** — The felt control a person has over their own decisions and actions; pressure threatens it and can produce resistance (Ch 14–15).

**adult childlike behavior** — The book’s therapist-derived metaphor for adult reactions such as sulking, silent treatment, rage, avoidance, or victim-playing when emotions are not regulated (Ch 07).

**comparison as teacher** — Using another person’s changeable result as evidence, a formula, or a prompt to work, rather than as proof of personal inferiority (Ch 10).

**comparison as torture** — Repeatedly judging yourself against fixed traits, histories, or circumstances you cannot change (Ch 09).

**conditional support** — Help offered with explicit conditions tied to safety, treatment, sobriety, school, or another agreed responsibility; unlike unconditional financial enabling (Ch 17).

**control** — The human desire to feel safe by directing life’s conditions; the book’s central distinction is between control of self and attempted control of other adults (Ch 01–02).

**emotional wave** — The rise and fall of an emotion; the book describes emotions as automatic chemical events that can pass without impulsive action (Ch 07–08).

**enabling** — Using money, excuses, or actions to shield another adult from the consequences of problematic behavior, thereby prolonging dependence or harm (Ch 16–17).

**Frame of Reference** — The lived-experience lens through which a person interprets a choice or relationship; understanding it can make room for two perspectives without surrendering agency (Ch 06).

**Great Scattering** — The recurring adult-life dispersal in which friends move, change schedules, form families, or enter new life stages, removing childhood friendship structure (Ch 11).

**intrinsic motivation** — The other person’s own felt desire to act; the book argues that durable adult change must be internally chosen (Ch 14–15).

**Let Me** — The second half of the theory: own your attitude, behavior, values, needs, boundaries, and next action (Ch 02).

**Let Them** — A deliberate release of the attempt to control another adult’s thoughts, feelings, choices, or behavior; it is paired with Let Me (Ch 01–02).

**motivational interviewing** — The open-question-and-reflection approach the book uses in the ABC Loop to help a person notice the gap between current behavior and desired outcomes (Ch 15).

**natural consequences** — Real-world results of a person’s choices that supporters should not automatically remove or conceal (Ch 16–17).

**negative opinion** — A thought or judgment another person may have; the book treats it as unavoidable and outside the reader’s control (Ch 05–06).

**proximity, timing, and energy** — The three pillars of adult friendship: physical access, shared life stage, and the felt click or fit that can shift over time (Ch 11–12).

**self-rejection** — Editing, hiding, deleting, or staying silent because of anticipated judgment, thereby withholding one’s own expression before others reject it (Ch 05).

**social contagion** — The book’s term for behavior spreading through observation: people are more likely to approach an action that looks pleasurable, easy, or effective for someone nearby (Ch 15).

**stress response** — The automatic fight, flight, or freeze state associated in the book with amygdala activation and reduced deliberate control (Ch 03–04).

**weak ties** — Familiar people in everyday settings who are not close friends but can create belonging and social scaffolding when you initiate contact (Ch 13).

**5 Second Rule** — Mel Robbins’s separate action tool: count backward 5-4-3-2-1 and move before overthinking talks you out of a needed step (Introduction).
