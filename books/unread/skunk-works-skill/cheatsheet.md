# Cheatsheet

## The Skunk Works test

1. What exact mission is being solved?
2. What is the decisive technical uncertainty?
3. What is the smallest test that can answer it?
4. Who has end-to-end decision authority?
5. Are the customer and operators in the build loop?
6. Which components can be reused?
7. Who can stop work for a quality problem?
8. What is the budget and schedule boundary?
9. What evidence would trigger redesign, pause, or cancellation?
10. What must remain independent if the project scales?

## Rules in compressed form

Small team; one accountable manager; limited access; simple drawings; minimal useful reports; monthly cost review; competitive suppliers; independent inspection; contractor-led testing; agreed requirements; timely funding; direct trust with the customer; controlled visitors; reward results.

## Decision heuristics

- Test the mission advantage before polishing the rest.
- Use proven hardware around the novel feature.
- Prefer a fast, reversible decision over a delayed “perfect” one.
- Make quality everyone’s job, with an independent stop authority.
- Keep prototype learning ahead of production scale.
- Treat the memoir's numbers as situated claims and verify before using them for a consequential decision.
