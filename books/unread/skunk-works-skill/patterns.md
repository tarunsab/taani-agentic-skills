# Patterns

## 1. Put one accountable leader close to the work

Give a program manager authority over design, manufacturing, test, suppliers, and tradeoffs. Keep the leader close enough to see the real state of the work and senior enough to absorb organizational heat.

## 2. Make the decisive uncertainty cheap

Identify the one question that can kill the concept, then build the smallest credible test: a wooden model for radar return, a half-scale thermal model, or a chamber test. Do not let a polished full-scale design substitute for evidence.

## 3. Use customer operators as design input

Bring pilots, maintainers, or end users into the build. Their constraints expose failure modes that a specification or committee may miss, while their early involvement improves acceptance and operational learning.

## 4. Keep design and production in the same conversation

Put engineers, shop workers, inspection, and flight test in physical and social proximity. Short feedback loops make a design change cheaper and make quality a shared responsibility.

## 5. Reuse mature components around the novel core

Use available engines, computers, actuators, seats, displays, and navigation equipment when they are adequate. Spend bespoke effort on the feature that creates the mission advantage.

## 6. Treat security as an operating design problem

Restrict access, but also design storage, communication, staffing, and interfaces so that security does not silently destroy speed. Periodically review whether restrictions are still justified.

## 7. Automate recovery from known failure modes

If a failure is predictable but the human response is too fast or too stressful, detect it and put the system into a safer state automatically. The Blackbird's coupled inlet recovery is the memoir's clearest example.

## 8. Stop honestly when evidence is negative

Cancel a concept whose measured performance misses the mission. Returning unused funds can strengthen the sponsor relationship and preserve resources for better ideas.

## 9. Protect the technical objective through handoffs

A promising design can fail when responsibility moves to a larger organization with different incentives. Before handoff, preserve decision rights, field expertise, interfaces, and the feedback loop that produced the result.

## 10. Prototype before institutional scale

Demonstrate a small operational capability, learn tactics and support needs, then decide whether mass production is justified. This prevents large budgets from being committed before the real use case is understood.

## Anti-patterns to diagnose

Large attendance replacing decisions; progress measured by paperwork; late user involvement; treating every part as custom; funding that arrives after the schedule; quality separated from production; security with no sunset; political allocation overriding mission needs; and spending the final money on marginal perfection.
