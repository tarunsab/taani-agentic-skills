# Glossary

**5 Whys** — Repeatedly ask why to investigate the root cause of a problem or disappointing day (Ch 4, Ch 7).
**80/15/5 communication ladder** — Use email for most non-urgent questions, chat/IM for near-term needs, and the phone for genuine crises (Ch 2).
**Asynchronous collaboration** — Working together without requiring everyone to be online at the same time (Introduction, Ch 1, Ch 3).
**Cabin fever** — The strain caused by too little live human interaction while working remotely (Ch 4).
**Change of scenery** — Deliberately varying work locations to introduce new stimuli and perspectives (Ch 7).
**Check-in/check-out** — A boundary practice for starting and ending work and judging whether the day was productive (Ch 4).
**Contractor** — An independent worker engaged through an invoicing arrangement rather than local-employee benefits (Ch 4, Ch 5).
**Culture** — The spoken and unspoken values expressed in how an organization decides, communicates, serves customers, handles quality, and treats workload (Ch 2).
**Distributed workforce** — A team whose members work from different places and often different schedules (Introduction, Ch 1).
**Equal pay for equal work** — Paying for contribution rather than reducing compensation because a worker lives in a cheaper location (Ch 5).
**Four-hour overlap** — A 37signals rule of thumb for enough shared time to avoid excessive collaboration delay (Ch 3, Ch 5).
**Good day’s work** — A worker’s end-of-day judgment about whether meaningful progress was made, independent of endless hours (Ch 4).
**Interruption factory** — An office whose meetings and drop-ins fragment the day into intervals too short for deep work (Ch 1).
**Level playing field** — Equal ability for office and remote workers to hear, contribute, decide, and be recognized (Ch 6).
**Managers-of-one** — Trusted people who can make good decisions and work consistently with company values without constant supervision (Ch 2, Ch 6).
**M&Ms** — The authors’ shorthand for meetings and managers when overused as interruptions and busywork (Ch 3, Ch 6).
**Motivation barometer** — The idea that motivation reveals the quality of the work, environment, team, or personal condition (Ch 7).
**No Broken Windows** — Addressing small snippy or poisonous behaviors before they normalize and spread (Ch 5).
**Nexus** — A taxable presence that can create additional state tax or sales-tax obligations (Ch 4).
**One-on-one** — A recurring, casual manager–worker conversation that surfaces concerns early (Ch 6).
**Output yardstick** — Measuring performance by work produced rather than attendance, hours, or visible busyness (Ch 2, Ch 3, Ch 5).
**Pre-hiring** — A paid one- or two-week mini-project used to evaluate a candidate before making a full hire (Ch 5).
**Remote work** — Work performed without a required office location or fixed office schedule; it may happen at home, elsewhere, or in a hybrid pattern (Introduction, Ch 1).
**Screencast** — A narrated recording of a computer screen for asynchronous demonstration or review (Ch 3).
**Single Point of Failure (SPoF)** — A dependency whose failure stops a system from functioning, such as an office that everyone must use (Ch 3, Ch 4).
**Sunk cost** — Money already spent that should not decide whether a future work arrangement is productive (Ch 1, Ch 2).
**Test project** — A meaningful, paid piece of role-specific work used to see how a candidate performs in practice (Ch 5).
**Virtual water cooler** — A persistent group chat for informal social cohesion, breaks, and lightweight questions (Ch 3).
**Work over place** — The principle that the quality and result of work matter more than where the worker is visible (Ch 1, Ch 2, Ch 3).
