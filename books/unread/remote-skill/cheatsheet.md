# Cheatsheet

## Decide whether remote fits

| If you see… | Prefer… | Because… |
|---|---|---|
| Long uninterrupted work is hard to find | Remote or protected focus blocks | The office may be an interruption factory |
| A role needs physical inventory or equipment | Office or hybrid for that role | Fairness is role-specific, not uniform |
| A time-zone gap is small | Staggered schedules with about four hours overlap | Shared time prevents slow handoffs |
| A time-zone gap is extreme | Async work only if talent is exceptional | Labor savings do not repay constant delay |
| The office is unavailable | Practiced remote capability | The office is an organizational SPoF |

## Route the interaction

1. Can the question wait hours? Put it in email.
2. Is an answer needed within minutes? Use concise chat/IM.
3. Is it a genuine crisis or unusually complex human conversation? Call.
4. Do not demand an instant reply just because the message was sent.

## Run a credible pilot

| Step | Default |
|---|---|
| Scope | A whole team, including project management and stakeholders |
| Start | Trusted employees; begin with a couple of remote days |
| Duration | Long enough to pass the adjustment period; the book recommends at least three months for local conditioning |
| Evidence | Work quality, pace, morale, access, client response, and failure points |
| Next move | Iterate, increase remote time, or preserve a role-specific hybrid pattern |

## Hire and retain

- Search beyond the hub once overlap, language, and legal constraints are understood.
- Keep proven employees when life moves them; knowledge and trust already exist.
- Filter written communication early.
- Ask for work, not riddles.
- Pay for a meaningful test project; never ask for free labor.
- Meet finalists for character and team fit.
- Look for both Smart and Gets Things Done.
- Pay for contribution, not geography.

## Manage the remote system

- Judge output, not chairs, hours, or browser visibility.
- Put files, decisions, tasks, code, and schedules in shared systems.
- Use one-on-ones to surface small issues before annual reviews.
- Give reasonable people default access and decision authority.
- Make remote workers equal in audio, discussion, status, and recognition.
- Watch for overwork before underwork; model roughly forty hours on average.
- Make face time scarce enough that it is used well.

## Protect the person

- Create a start/stop routine and a dedicated work setting.
- Separate work and leisure devices or accounts.
- Move when the commute no longer supplies movement.
- Use a café, library, or coworking space when isolation or home space is the problem.
- Treat a motivation slump as diagnostic information before blaming yourself.
