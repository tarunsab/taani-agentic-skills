# Glossary

**AI Disappoints, Deficits Drag** — Scenario in which AI remains incremental, structural deficits raise borrowing costs, and growth weakens while inflation stays persistent (Ch 5, Ch 7, Ch 9).

**AI Transforms, Productivity Surges** — Scenario in which AI becomes a general-purpose technology, lifts productivity, and creates new products, services, and tasks (Ch 5, Ch 7, Ch 9).

**Augmentation** — Technology raises the quality, scale, or insight of human work without eliminating the underlying task (Preface, Ch 2).

**Big Four** — Real GDP growth, inflation, interest rates, and stock earnings yield/valuation, the main outcomes linked to megatrends (Preface, Ch 1, Appendix).

**Demographics** — Population growth and age composition in the book’s focused analysis; a force that matters but does not determine outcomes alone (Ch 4).

**Idea Multiplier** — Measure of how many subsequent good ideas are sparked by one good idea; used as a leading indicator of innovation (Ch 3, Ch 6).

**Innovative Technology** — Technology that automates or augments existing work, such as power tools or an assembly line (Ch 1).

**J-curve** — Adoption pattern in which investment and disruption precede a later productivity payoff once complementary systems diffuse (Preface, Ch 1, Ch 6).

**Megatrends** — Long-run, time-varying supply forces including technology, demographics, globalization, and fiscal deficits; the model also considers climate/temperature and geopolitical risk (Preface, Ch 1).

**Megatrends-Aware Portfolio** — Book’s diversified, scenario-weighted portfolio that modestly favors value, international equities, and bonds while retaining broad-market core exposure (Ch 9, Ch 10).

**Megatrends Model** — Integrated empirical framework connecting structural megatrends, cyclical variables, and financial returns through the Big Four (Preface, Ch 1, Appendix).

**General-purpose technology (GPT)** — Rare technology that changes work and broader economic and social life for an extended period; electricity and the personal computer are examples (Ch 1, Ch 7).

**Structural fiscal deficit** — Persistent spending–revenue imbalance after temporary recession and war effects are separated from the budget (Ch 5, Appendix).

**Task automation** — Technology performs particular tasks formerly performed by people; it does not imply that an entire job disappears (Ch 2).

**Trade in ideas** — Cross-border exchange of knowledge, research, patents, and concepts that lets innovation build on distributed building blocks (Ch 3).

**Transformation** — Technology enables new industries, services, and social outcomes beyond efficiency gains in existing work (Preface, Ch 2, Ch 7).

**Triangle of Transformation** — Test requiring meaningful effects across technology, work, and society’s unmet or unrecognized needs (Preface, Ch 7).

**Value stocks** — Broadly, companies with lower relative valuations/greater earnings yield; the book argues they may benefit as general-purpose technologies diffuse beyond technology leaders (Ch 1, Ch 9).
