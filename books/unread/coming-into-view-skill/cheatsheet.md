# Coming into View — Decision Cheatsheet

## Start with the decision rule

| If you hear… | Ask… | Davis’s move |
|---|---|---|
| “The consensus is 2% forever.” | What assumptions make all megatrends cancel? | Build probabilities for more than one regime. |
| “AI will eliminate jobs.” | Which tasks are automatable, augmentable, physical, or relational? | Analyze tasks, not job titles. |
| “Debt/GDP is above a threshold.” | Is the deficit temporary or structural? | Inspect recurring spending, taxes, aging, rates, and growth. |
| “Globalization is retreating.” | Are ideas and knowledge still crossing borders? | Track the Idea Multiplier separately from goods trade. |
| “The population is aging.” | Is technology, immigration, participation, or spending mix changing? | Treat demographics as a factor, not destiny. |

## Scenario fork

- **Status quo (15–20%)**: ~2% growth, ~2% inflation, low rates; a narrow historical bet.
- **AI Disappoints, Deficits Drag (30–40%)**: ~1% growth, persistent inflation pressure, Treasury yields above 5% in the book’s 2034 vignette, weaker stocks.
- **AI Transforms, Productivity Surges (45–55%)**: ~3% growth in 2028–35, low inflation, productivity-led earnings and new services.

Plan for the last two together; their combined probability is above 75% in the book. The field where transformation occurs is uncertain, so test current AI capability, complementary technology/timeline, replacement versus new demand, and the size of unmet needs.

## Portfolio defaults from the book’s moderate-risk example

- Keep a low-cost broad U.S. equity core.
- For the all-passive Megatrends-Aware example: U.S. broad stocks 25–30%; U.S. value 15–20%; international 10–20%; government bonds 15–20%; corporate bonds 20–30%; broad taxable bonds 0–10%.
- Target totals: 50–60% equities, 40–50% bonds, 0% active.
- If adding active strategies, preserve 55–65% indexed; the illustrative active share is 35–45%.

## Fast tells and trade-offs

- Widespread adoption ≠ transformation; look for complementary investment and new uses.
- Higher debt ≠ automatic stagnation; persistent structural deficits are the danger signal.
- Higher rates can improve bond income; they can also pressure stock valuations.
- Value and non-U.S. exposure may help if AI disappoints, but can lag if U.S. technology leadership dominates.
- More bonds can narrow downside, but reduce upside if AI transforms.
- “Stay the course” means goals, balance, low cost, and discipline—not never reassessing risk.
