# Chapter 7: The Tug-of-War: AI vs. Fiscal Deficits

## Core Idea

Chapter 7 turns the book’s history and megatrend analysis into a probabilistic outlook for the U.S. economy and markets. Electricity’s role in creating Hollywood’s first red-carpet premiere illustrates the difference between an innovation that improves efficiency and a general-purpose technology that enables new cultural and economic possibilities. The two dominant forces ahead are AI-driven transformation and slowly accumulating age-driven deficits, and the outcome determines growth, inflation, rates, stocks, and daily life. Davis models three paths: a low-probability status quo, `AI Disappoints, Deficits Drag`, and the more likely `AI Transforms, Productivity Surges`; the two non-consensus paths together have more than 75% probability in his framing. In the downside path, AI’s J-curve flattens, productivity gains remain around 5–10%, structural deficits lift yields, and living standards stagnate. In the upside path, AI saves about 20% of work hours in 70% of tracked jobs, productivity and growth rise, inflation remains contained, and new products and treatments improve well-being. Transformation is not inferred from better software alone: it must create complementary applications and meaningful effects on unmet needs, with healthcare offered as a candidate field. The investment lesson is to prepare for both paths because the verdict is uncertain and the status quo is the riskiest assumption.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Three-scenario fork**: **When to use**: a consensus path competes with materially different technology and fiscal outcomes. **How**: specify the status quo, the AI-disappointment/fiscal-drag path, and the AI-transformation/productivity path, then assign probabilities and link them to the Big Four.
- **AI Disappoints, Deficits Drag**: **When to use**: AI capability plateaus while age-related deficits persist. **How**: model low productivity, higher inflation expectations, rising yields, weaker earnings, and slower living-standard gains.
- **AI Transforms, Productivity Surges**: **When to use**: AI becomes a general-purpose technology. **How**: model task automation, augmentation, new industries, productivity growth, higher tax receipts, and positive social spillovers.
- **Transformation criteria**: **When to use**: deciding whether a promising application can change the economy. **How**: assess current AI capability, complementary technologies and deployment time, replacement versus genuinely new activity, and the scale of unmet needs.

## Key Concepts

- **Innovation**: improvement in an existing process or technology that may raise efficiency without reshaping society.
- **Transformation**: new industries, services, products, or social outcomes enabled by a technology.
- **Status Quo scenario**: roughly 2% growth, 2% inflation, and low rates continuing from the pre-COVID world.
- **AI Disappoints, Deficits Drag**: incremental AI combined with rising structural deficits and weaker growth.
- **AI Transforms, Productivity Surges**: general-purpose AI combined with faster productivity and new applications.
- **Feedback loop**: one megatrend’s change alters another driver and feeds back into the system.
- **Years of potential life lost**: measure used to frame the economic and human value of reducing premature deaths.
- **Goldilocks outcome**: higher growth with restrained inflation, as in the optimistic path.
- **Bond vigilantes**: investors demanding higher yields when chronic deficits threaten fiscal sustainability.
- **Probability-weighted outlook**: a decision framework that uses odds rather than selecting one certain future.

## Mental Models

- **Hollywood test**: ask whether a technology creates new ways of living and consuming, not only faster existing work.
- **Fork in the road**: the economy will realize one path, so portfolios need exposure to the implications of both.
- **Tug-of-war**: AI is a potentially powerful tailwind; age-related deficits are a persistent headwind.
- **One percentage point compounds**: apparently small growth differences become large over 15 years.

## Anti-patterns

- **Assume the median is the safe forecast**: the chapter says the status quo is historically narrow and receives only 15–20% probability.
- **Confuse better task efficiency with transformation**: sustained ~3% growth requires new products, services, or social effects comparable to earlier GPTs.
- **Treat the upside as certain**: AI may plateau, and the optimistic probabilities are only roughly 45–55%.
- **Use a single technology sector as the transformation proxy**: healthcare, transportation, energy storage, robotics, and medicine may matter more for broad well-being.

## Scenario Mechanics

### AI Disappoints, Deficits Drag (30–40%)

In the book’s 2034 vignette, real GDP grows around 1% for a third straight year, China’s GDP level has passed the United States, Treasury yields are above 5.5%, and stocks have lagged while fixed-income income is higher. AI plateaus in the late 2020s, produces only 5–10% gains in selected fields, and resembles social media more than the personal computer. Work still uses keyboards, spreadsheets, email, and largely unchanged clinical routines because no general-purpose knock-on effects arrive. Higher structural deficits lift inflation expectations and borrowing costs, while the Federal Reserve uses higher short-term rates to preserve credibility and avoid a repeat of 1970s inflation. Material life improves modestly, but education and healthcare costs remain heavy, a typical 30-year-old is unlikely to exceed their parents’ standard of living, and household living standards take about 70 years to double.

### AI Transforms, Productivity Surges (45–55%)

In the contrasting 2034 vignette, an autonomous vehicle handles the commute, U.S. growth averages nearly 3% between 2028 and 2035, and AI-related electricity and data-storage investment exceeds $100 billion over seven years. For 70% of tracked jobs, automating about 20% of work hours frees time for new and more valuable tasks, producing the J-curve productivity pickup by around 2030. Nurses shift time from records to care, advisors scale customized planning, and programmers move from routine coding toward data science and AI-enabled insight. Higher output raises tax revenues and keeps deficits from climbing as quickly, while technology helps hold inflation down; median household income could reach $127,000 in 2040 dollars versus $80,600 in 2024, a 20% improvement over status-quo growth. The path still includes meaningful job displacement and disruption, so productivity does not mean a frictionless utopia.

## Potential Transformation: Healthcare

Davis explicitly says healthcare is a candidate area he is identifying, not predicting. Roughly 12 million working-age Americans cannot work because of a disability and another 4 million report illness or disability limiting their preferred work; in 2022, deaths under age 65 represented about 25.6 million years of potential life lost. AI-enabled prevention, medical diagnostics, safer transport, drug discovery, GLP-1 therapies, and other biomedical tools could reduce part of that burden. The chapter estimates that even a 10–20% reduction in the listed causes of premature death could modestly improve longevity, well-being, and labor-force participation. Earlier electrification offers a precedent: safer, better-lit manufacturing reduced hazards in unexpected ways alongside later unions and regulation. The field qualifies as transformative only if capabilities move from plausible technology to widespread, complementary, unmet-need-reducing applications.

## Worked Example: What Daily Life Could Feel Like

| Dimension in 2035–2040 | AI Disappoints, Deficits Drag | AI Transforms, Productivity Surges |
|---|---|---|
| Commute | Human drivers dominate | Autonomous vehicles are the majority of ridership |
| Office | Keyboard, mouse, spreadsheets, and email | Voice-enabled AI with little manual operation |
| Markets | Bond yields above 5% in the vignette; stocks lag | Bond income near 4%; stocks outpace bonds on average |
| Cost of living | Healthcare and education outpace inflation | Technology bends those cost curves moderately |
| Health | Life expectancy around 80 | Nearly 83 by 2040 in the vignette |
| Prospects | Living standards double in about 70 years | Living standards double in about 25 years |

The table is a scenario artifact, not a prediction of specific products. It makes the economic model human-readable by translating growth, inflation, and productivity into commuting, work, costs, health, and intergenerational mobility. The author uses a “Rip Van Winkle” comparison to emphasize that the disappointment path would look technologically polished but socially familiar. The transformation path is more exciting and more disruptive because new capabilities alter the value of work and create new risks of displacement. Both paths remain livable; they differ in opportunity, affordability, and resilience.

## Key Takeaways

1. Use probabilities to prepare for both major paths.
2. Require broad productivity, new applications, and unmet-need effects before calling AI transformative.
3. Expect the downside to be stagnation and fiscal drag rather than necessarily mass unemployment.
4. Expect the upside to combine task change, new industries, better health, and disruption.
5. Small growth differences compound into large living-standard differences.
6. Use the scenario fork as the bridge from economic analysis to portfolio design.
