# Glossary

**Aerobic respiration** — Oxygen-dependent energy production described as efficient and sustainable (Ch 2, Ch 5).

**Air hunger** — The mounting need to breathe as carbon dioxide rises or breathing is restricted (Ch 6, Ch 9).

**Amygdala** — Brain structure involved in threat learning, emotion, and fear processing (Ch 9).

**Anaerobic respiration** — Faster, less efficient energy production used when oxygen supply is insufficient (Ch 2).

**Bandhas** — Muscular locks used in the book’s account of redirecting and holding prana (Ch 10).

**Bohr effect** — The release of oxygen from hemoglobin as carbon dioxide and acidity increase (Ch 5).

**Breathing Coordination** — Carl Stough’s coordinated diaphragm, exhalation, posture, and circulation practice (Ch 4, Ch 12).

**Breathing+** — Nestor’s label for deliberate intense breathing, holds, and related stress practices (Ch 8, Ch 11).

**Central chemoreceptors** — Brain-stem sensors that use carbon dioxide changes to regulate breathing (Ch 9).

**Cilia** — Hair-like structures that move nasal mucus and inhaled debris toward the throat (Ch 3).

**Coherence** — Coordinated heart, circulation, and nervous-system function during resonant breathing (Ch 5).

**Control Pause** — Buteyko timing measure taken after a soft exhale until the first strong urge to breathe (Ch 6, Ch 12).

**Diaphragm** — Dome-shaped respiratory muscle whose range affects lung expansion and the thoracic pump (Ch 4).

**Dysevolution** — Harmful biological change caused by modern environmental and behavioral inputs (Ch 1, Ch 7, Ch 11).

**Email apnea** — Unconscious breathing pauses and shallow breathing during concentrated screen work (Ch 9).

**Hypocapnia** — Low carbon dioxide associated in the book with overbreathing, dizziness, and confusion (Ch 2, Ch 6).

**Hypoventilation** — Reduced ventilation used in Buteyko and athletic training (Ch 6).

**Malocclusion** — Misaligned teeth or jaws, including crowding, overbites, and underbites (Ch 1, Ch 7).

**Masseter** — Powerful chewing muscle below the ears (Ch 7).

**Maxilla** — Plastic membrane bone forming the center of the face and upper jaw (Ch 7).

**Mewing** — Tongue-posture exercise popularized by Mike Mew (Ch 7).

**Nasal cycle** — Alternating opening and congestion of the two nostrils (Ch 3).

**Nasal concha/turbinate** — Coiled nasal bones that shape, filter, warm, and pressurize air (Ch 3).

**Nitric oxide** — Sinus-produced molecule linked in the book to vessel dilation and oxygen delivery (Ch 3, Ch 12).

**Oral posture** — Lips together, teeth lightly touching, tongue on the palate, head and spine aligned (Ch 7).

**Overbreathing** — Ventilating more air than current metabolism needs (Ch 6, Ch 8).

**Prana** — Traditional Indian term for life force or vital energy carried by breath (Ch 10).

**Pranayama** — Yogic regulation of breath (Ch 3, Ch 10).

**Resonant/Coherent Breathing** — Approximately 5.5-second inhale and exhale rhythm (Ch 5, Ch 12).

**Sudarshan Kriya** — Multi-phase practice combining chant, restriction, pacing, and heavy breathing (Ch 0, Ch 10, Ch 12).

**Tummo** — Tibetan “inner fire” practice using intense breathing, holds, and sometimes cold (Ch 8, Ch 12).

**Vagus nerve** — Network connecting brain and organs in autonomic regulation (Ch 8).

**VO2 max** — Maximum oxygen consumption, used as a cardiorespiratory fitness measure (Ch 6).
