# Cheatsheet

## Start here

| If you notice… | First decision | Book’s reason |
|---|---|---|
| Resting mouthbreathing, dry mouth, snoring | Favor a nasal baseline and investigate obstruction | Nose filters, humidifies, pressurizes, and supports airway tone |
| Shallow, chest-high breathing | Add a soft, complete exhale | Diaphragm range and thoracic-pump efficiency |
| Stress or poor sleep | Try 5.5 seconds in / 5.5 seconds out for ten cycles | Resonant breathing supports parasympathetic coherence |
| Large, noisy, frequent breaths | Reduce volume before chasing a slower rate | “Slow” and “less” are separate levers |
| Crowding, narrow palate, tongue falling back | Seek airway-aware dental/medical assessment | Structure, posture, and chewing affect airway room |

## Choose by goal

- Calm and regulation → Resonant/Coherent Breathing.
- Diaphragm and exhalation → Breathing Coordination.
- Gentle nasal balance → Alternate Nostril Breathing.
- Overbreathing/asthma or panic pattern → Buteyko-style coaching; do not replace prescribed care.
- Endurance training → Nasal, aerobic-zone breathing first; specialized hypoventilation only with expertise.
- Intentional autonomic stress → Tummo only in a safe, instructed setting.
- Psychological exploration → Facilitated Holotropic Breathwork, not solo hyperventilation.
- Jaw and airway structure → Appropriate chewing, oral posture, and qualified functional dental care.

## Defaults and thresholds

- Book’s default: about 5.5-second inhale + 5.5-second exhale, about 5.5 breaths/minute, about 5.5 liters/minute at rest.
- Maffetone heuristic in the book: an aerobic ceiling of roughly 180 minus age; treat it as a heuristic, not a medical rule.
- Resonant practice: at least ten cycles.
- Alternate nostril: five to ten cycles.
- Breathing Coordination: ten to thirty cycles.
- Tummo: about thirty fast breaths, recovery hold, at least three rounds; it is not a beginner default.

## Fast tells

- More air but more air hunger → possible hypocapnia/overbreathing loop.
- Snoring, choking, or repeated waking → airway problem, not “normal sleep.”
- Dizziness, visual changes, tingling, tetany, or panic → stop the intense method and recover.
- A dramatic vision or heat → an effect to investigate, not proof of cure.
- A device, gas tank, or online protocol promising certainty → check the evidence, dose, and supervision.

## Hard boundary

Never practice extreme overbreathing, long holds, or Tummo near water, while driving or walking, or where fainting could injure you. Pregnancy, heart disease, serious respiratory disease, severe psychiatric symptoms, and prescribed medication require professional guidance. Breathing cannot remove an embolism, cure advanced cancer, reverse a genetic neuromuscular disease, or replace urgent medical treatment.
