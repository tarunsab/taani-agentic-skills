# Chaos Monkeys — Recurring Patterns

These patterns are syntheses of the memoir's repeated mechanisms. Use them as
diagnostic lenses, not as universal laws.

## 1. Attention becomes an auction

**Situation:** A scarce audience can be reached by many advertisers.

**Mechanism:** Data estimates intent, an exchange compares bids, and an ad
server records delivery and outcomes. Whoever controls identity, inventory, or
the accounting standard can capture a spread or shape the market.

**Failure mode:** Optimize clicks or impressions while ignoring intent,
incrementality, latency, fraud, or the real customer outcome.

**Ask:** What is the scarce unit, who owns its signal, and what event proves
value?

## 2. More data is not more signal

**Situation:** A platform has a huge, intimate behavioral dataset.

**Mechanism:** Data becomes useful only when it predicts a decision or joins to
an outcome such as a purchase. Social conversation can be rich in context yet
weak in commercial intent.

**Failure mode:** Retain or expose sensitive data because it is available,
without an incremental lift that clears the cost and risk.

**Ask:** What baseline does the new field beat, by how much, and at what privacy
and operational cost?

## 3. Dashboard revenue can lie by omission

**Situation:** Two products appear to generate similar revenue.

**Mechanism:** One may redirect spend that would have occurred anyway; the other
may win new budget from a competing channel. The dashboard records buckets, not
the counterfactual.

**Failure mode:** Reward the easier internal reallocation and starve the harder
product that creates genuinely incremental demand.

**Ask:** What would have happened without the product, and how certain is that
counterfactual?

## 4. A startup needs multiple miracles

**Situation:** A clever product depends on many outside behaviors or systems.

**Mechanism:** Each dependency can be plausible while their joint probability
  is poor; founders mistake a polished concept for a tested market.

**Failure mode:** Build every component before testing the riskiest adoption,
  data, distribution, or payment assumption.

**Ask:** Which single dependency can kill the thesis, and what is the cheapest
experiment that tests it?

## 5. Launch attention decays

**Situation:** A product receives a press or viral spike.

**Mechanism:** Attention creates signups and social proof, then falls away;
retention, service cost, and acquisition economics determine survival.

**Failure mode:** Treat traffic, followers, or first-day enthusiasm as fit.

**Ask:** What do retained users do after the spike, and what channel acquires
the next cohort profitably?

## 6. Network leverage counters balance-sheet leverage

**Situation:** A small company faces a larger incumbent with more money.

**Mechanism:** The incumbent depends on investors, customers, partners, talent,
or reputation. Accurate communication to those dependency nodes can change the
incumbent's cost of waiting.

**Failure mode:** Use unsupported public accusations or coercion instead of
factual, proportionate stakeholder communication.

**Ask:** Which relationship supplies the opponent's next unit of oxygen?

## 7. Acquisition price is a waterfall

**Situation:** A buyer announces a headline number.

**Mechanism:** The number is allocated among company consideration, investors,
founders, employees, retention, escrow, taxes, and vesting.

**Failure mode:** Compare bids without modeling who receives what, when, and
under which risk.

**Ask:** What is the after-tax, liquidity-adjusted outcome for each stakeholder?

## 8. Culture is an incentive system

**Situation:** A company says it values speed, ownership, or truth.

**Mechanism:** Rituals, tools, access rules, review practices, status, and
consequences teach employees what actually matters.

**Failure mode:** Mistake slogans, perks, or long hours for commitment while
ignoring information boundaries, dissent, burnout, or inclusion.

**Ask:** What behavior is rewarded, punished, made visible, or made impossible?

## 9. Open systems threaten incumbents

**Situation:** A platform can connect to outside data, bidders, or tools.

**Mechanism:** Openness improves adoption, interoperability, and price
discovery, but it can reduce the platform's information asymmetry and control.

**Failure mode:** Build a closed substitute slowly while claiming to serve the
ecosystem, or open data without adequate privacy and security boundaries.

**Ask:** Which value comes from unique platform data, and which comes from
standardized external plumbing?

## 10. Speed needs a budget

**Situation:** A team must ship under a deadline.

**Mechanism:** A narrow, reversible feature and a measured feedback loop turn
time pressure into learning; unbounded haste creates technical, legal, and
relationship debt.

**Failure mode:** Celebrate the deadline while underfunding reliability,
moderation, documentation, or recovery.

**Ask:** What can be reversed, what cannot, and what failure budget is acceptable?

## 11. Identity is a bridge, not a fact

**Situation:** Data from devices, browsers, CRM records, and stores must be
joined.

**Mechanism:** Identifiers and probabilistic matches connect events to an
individual or household, enabling targeting and attribution.

**Failure mode:** Treat a cookie, device ID, or high match rate as proof of a
person, intent, consent, or causation.

**Ask:** What evidence supports the edge, what is the confidence, and how can it
be deleted or audited?

## 12. The winner rewrites the story

**Situation:** One product succeeds after several bets.

**Mechanism:** The press and company narrate the outcome as inevitable and
visionary, hiding randomness, failed paths, timing shocks, and political work.

**Failure mode:** Copy the winner's visible behavior without reproducing the
market conditions that made it work.

**Ask:** Which assumptions were actually validated, and which were merely
explained after the fact?
