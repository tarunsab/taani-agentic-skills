# Chaos Monkeys

## Purpose and scope

This private skill is a study guide to Antonio García Martínez's *Chaos
Monkeys*. It follows the book's actual body structure: a prologue, three
parts, forty-seven narrative chapters, and an epilogue. The supplied EPUB
extractor reported one numeric chapter, so the chapter boundaries here were
mapped manually against the Contents and the body headings. Acknowledgments
and the index are not treated as chapters.

**Conversion profile:** full conversion; `BOOK_TYPE=text`; `DEPTH=study`; no
Anki deck; no GitHub publication. The source is the supplied extracted text,
not an external edition.

Use the skill to reason about the mechanisms described in the memoir, not to
turn the author's judgments into universal laws. The chapters preserve the
book's important distinctions—attention versus intent, incremental versus
reallocated revenue, open versus closed systems, founder versus investor
payoffs, growth versus monetization, and cultural conviction versus
organizational status.

## Core mental model

The book's recurring model is a conversion chain: scarce human attention is
observed, priced, routed, and converted into money. Online advertising makes
that chain explicit through auctions, identifiers, targeting data, ad
servers, and attribution. A startup is another kind of market mechanism: it
converts uncertainty, labor, capital, narrative, and social leverage into a
chance at product-market fit. Large companies retain power when they control
the measurement layer, the distribution channel, or the information
asymmetry; insurgents gain leverage by making themselves difficult to ignore,
by exploiting dependency, or by shipping a credible alternative quickly.

The memoir also supplies a counter-model to clean innovation stories. Product
outcomes emerge from many attempts, political choices, timing shocks, and
capability differences; the successful attempt is often explained only after
the fact. Speed and intensity help exploration, but they can create technical
debt, legal exposure, brittle relationships, and personal damage. Culture can
act as a competitive moat when it produces true commitment, yet the same
culture can become zealotry, secrecy, homogeneity, or a way to excuse abuse.

## How to apply the chapters

1. Start with the chapter whose mechanism matches the problem, then read its
   Connections links in the hub or adjacent chapter notes.
2. Identify the scarce resource: attention, runway, engineering capacity,
   data, distribution, decision authority, or social capital.
3. Separate the observable dashboard metric from the economic reality behind
   it. Ask whether the metric is incremental, attributable, and decision-useful.
4. Map incentives across founders, employees, investors, acquirers, platforms,
   advertisers, and users before accepting a stated narrative.
5. Use the limitations section to test transferability. The book is a memoir,
   written with deliberate satire and hindsight; examples are historically
   situated and not a neutral survey of technology or labor.

## Limits and reading stance

The source is a first-person memoir with reconstructed conversations,
selective disclosure, satire, strong language, and retrospective causal
claims. Figures, company states, legal descriptions, compensation examples,
and product histories are summarized as presented in the supplied text, not
independently verified. The EPUB extraction dropped four images and included
front matter, pagination artifacts, and back matter; the study notes use the
substantive narrative body only. Practical applications are analytical
prompts, not legal, financial, privacy, employment, or investment advice.
