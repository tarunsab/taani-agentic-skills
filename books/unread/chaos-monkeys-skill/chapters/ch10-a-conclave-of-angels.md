# Chapter 10 — A Conclave of Angels

## Thesis

Seed financing is a negotiation over risk, future dilution, and network access,
with a convertible note translating an uncertain early company into an option
on a later valuation.

## Core Idea

The first investor meeting nearly fails because the founders arrive late, but
Russell Siegelman evaluates them as an experienced angel with personal capital,
skin in the game, and a useful network. A convertible note begins as debt and
converts into equity when a later financing sets a price; its valuation cap
protects the early investor by limiting the price used for conversion. The
chapter's example shows why a $100,000 note at a $10 million cap implies about
1%, while a $3 million cap implies roughly 3.3% before other terms and
financing effects. Founders want a higher cap because it means less early
ownership transferred, while investors want protection against a cheap future
round. Chris Sacca's quick commitment demonstrates a different form of value:
his name and ability to attract others can matter nearly as much as his check.

## Frameworks

- **Convertible-note option:** postpone exact pricing while setting a ceiling on
  the future conversion price.
- **Capital plus signaling:** evaluate an investor by cash, terms, diligence,
  network, reputation, and future fundraising influence.

## Key Concepts

- Convertible note
- Valuation cap
- Priced equity
- Dilution
- Angel vs. micro-VC

## Source Examples / Evidence

- The chapter compares a $10 million cap with a $3 million cap to show how
  investor ownership changes on the same $100,000 check.
- In the 2010 YC environment, a $3–4 million cap is treated as a realistic
  middle range, and the author tests whether Russ will accept a higher one.

## Distinctions

- **Cap vs. company valuation:** a cap is a conversion ceiling, not necessarily
  the company's present fair-market value.
- **Ownership percentage vs. economic outcome:** later dilution and exit value
  determine the eventual result.
- **Investor enthusiasm vs. diligence:** a fast verbal yes is not the same as a
  fully documented commitment.

## Limitations

The numerical examples simplify interest, discounts, option pools, multiple
notes, and later-round mechanics. Financing law and customary terms vary by
jurisdiction and time; the chapter is not a substitute for counsel.

## Practical Application

Model at least three financing paths: a strong priced round, a down round, and
an acquisition before the next round. Show founders and investors the cap-table
effect, then compare the investor's non-cash value without letting prestige
hide unfavorable terms.

## Worked Example

A startup raises $200,000 on a $4 million cap, then later raises at $12 million.
Before modeling interest and discounts, the note converts near the cap rather
than the later price, giving the early investor a larger share than the same
cash would have bought in the later round. The founder should compare this
ownership cost with the runway and introductions the investor provides.

## Key Takeaways

- Early capital prices future uncertainty, not just present assets.
- A lower cap protects investors but materially increases founder dilution.
- The right investor can contribute distribution and credibility as well as money.
