# Chapter 22 — Retweets Are Not Endorsements

## Thesis

Signals of support, formal offers, and personal loyalty are different things;
confusing them makes acquisition communication brittle.

## Core Idea

The author continues helping the AdGrok engineers interview at Twitter even
while intending to join Facebook, and he interprets Twitter's work culture as
less attractive from its refusal to make the workplace a total life. When
Twitter's team calls with a real acquisition path, the author finally tells his
cofounders that Facebook did not reject everyone and that he will take its
offer. Their reaction is hurt and anger, but the company has reached a point at
which the author's choice is difficult to reverse. The chapter's title captures
the difference between visible social activity and actual endorsement: a
retweet, a friendly interview, or an email can be ambiguous, whereas a phone
call carrying a concrete term sheet is an expensive signal. It is also a moral
distinction—the author believes his deception served the company's outcome,
while the cofounders experience it as a breach of trust.

## Frameworks

- **Signal hierarchy:** public interaction < exploratory conversation < direct
  call < written terms < signed agreement.
- **Trust-debt accounting:** every delay or omission may preserve bargaining
  leverage while creating a relational liability that must eventually be paid.

## Key Concepts

- Term-sheet signal
- Founder disclosure
- Work-life culture
- Trust debt
- Fait accompli

## Source Examples / Evidence

- Twitter's actual call and forthcoming term sheet mark a change from months of
  slower, less decisive interaction.
- The author tells the other founders only after choosing Facebook, leaving them
  to accept a decision already made.

## Distinctions

- **Attention vs. endorsement:** engagement metrics do not establish agreement.
- **Confidentiality vs. concealment:** protecting a negotiation can be valid;
  hiding material information from cofounders is a separate choice.
- **Decision authority vs. relational legitimacy:** a founder may be able to act
  without making the action fair.

## Limitations

The author controls the narrative and does not fully reproduce the cofounders'
internal reasoning. Different shareholder agreements, boards, or employment
contracts would change what disclosure is required.

## Practical Application

Agree in advance on which deal milestones must be disclosed to cofounders and
investors, who can negotiate separately, and when confidentiality expires. Mark
every external signal by its cost and reversibility instead of treating social
enthusiasm as commitment.

## Worked Example

A cofounder receives a private recruiting call from a buyer while the company
is negotiating a sale. The team agreement can require disclosure of any offer
that would materially affect the shared process, while allowing reasonable
confidentiality on price until a defined stage. This protects both negotiation
optionality and informed consent.

## Key Takeaways

- Social signals vary greatly in evidentiary value.
- Confidentiality can protect a deal without justifying indefinite concealment.
- A technically authorized decision can still create lasting trust debt.
