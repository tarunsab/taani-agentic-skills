# Chapter 38 — Monetizing the Tumor

## Thesis

Growth and monetization can pull in opposite directions: the placement that
earns money today may reduce the future user base that makes the platform more
valuable.

## Core Idea

Facebook's Growth team turns money into users, while Ads turns users into money,
creating an internal exchange between two kinds of performance. Growth uses
direct-response tactics, retargeting, reminders, carrier partnerships, and
global distribution to expand the network. Ads seeks inventory and pricing, but
an aggressive monetization placement can occupy a surface that Growth needs for
activation or retention. The Logout Experience illustrates the conflict: selling
the logout screen to advertisers is attractive in mature markets, yet the same
gateway may be essential for user growth in developing countries. Facebook
therefore restricts the placement by market rather than treating one global
revenue metric as sufficient. The chapter's “tumor” metaphor is a warning that
monetization can behave like a growth attached to the product, consuming the
host if it is not constrained.

## Frameworks

- **Growth–monetization frontier:** evaluate revenue per user against acquisition,
  engagement, retention, and future addressable users.
- **Surface allocation:** every screen or notification has competing uses; assign
  it by market maturity and strategic value.

## Key Concepts

- Growth team
- Direct response
- Logout Experience
- Ad inventory
- Market maturity

## Source Examples / Evidence

- Growth tactics include retargeting, reminders, carrier deals, and campaigns
  aimed at expanding Internet access.
- Logout monetization is allowed in mature markets but withheld where the screen
  is more important for growth, with Brazil used as an example.

## Distinctions

- **Revenue optimization vs. company optimization:** maximizing today's CPM can
  harm future users or strategic reach.
- **Global product vs. market-specific economics:** one surface can have
  different opportunity costs by country.
- **Acquisition vs. activation:** buying a user is not the same as creating a
  retained, engaged user.

## Limitations

The chapter provides a few examples rather than a complete causal model of
growth. Market classification and user harm are complex; the source's business
logic should not be read as permission to monetize vulnerable populations.

## Practical Application

For each monetized surface, track short-term revenue, user activation,
retention, downstream lifetime value, and market-specific alternatives. Make
Growth and Ads sign off jointly when the same inventory affects both goals.

## Worked Example

A social app can sell a welcome-screen ad for $0.04 per new user, but the same
screen doubles the chance that a new user completes onboarding. The company
should compare immediate ad revenue with retained-user lifetime value and test
the choice separately in markets where acquisition is cheap or scarce.

## Key Takeaways

- Monetization consumes product real estate.
- Growth and Ads need shared metrics and market-specific rules.
- The best revenue placement may be the one the company refuses to sell.
