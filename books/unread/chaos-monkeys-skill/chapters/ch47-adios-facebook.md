# Chapter 47 — Adiós, Facebook

## Thesis

Leaving a powerful company is a timing and information-security operation as
well as an emotional decision, because vesting schedules, reputation, and
institutional retaliation can make a single day economically significant.

## Core Idea

The author waits until his quarterly vesting date passes, cleans out his desk,
and prepares to resign while protecting the value he believes he has earned.
The final exit becomes an HR ambush: a severance offer includes a
non-disparagement payment, and the author interprets the package as an attempt
to buy silence and strengthen the company's position. He has already wiped
personal files, distrusts management, and leaves with a mixture of anger,
triumph, and suspicion. The episode shows how a relationship that once felt
like a mission can end as a contract-enforcement interaction. It also shows
the danger of the author's own combative style: the same public voice that
created leverage and attention makes the employer anticipate reputational risk.

## Frameworks

- **Vesting-timing discipline:** know the exact vesting trigger, employment
  status, tax effect, and notice rules before announcing departure.
- **Exit hygiene:** separate personal from company data, return assets, preserve
  lawful records, and obtain counsel before signing a severance or silence term.

## Key Concepts

- Quarterly vesting
- Non-disparagement
- Severance
- Data hygiene
- Non-solicitation
- Exit retaliation risk

## Source Examples / Evidence

- The author waits through April 15 so the quarterly stock grant appears in his
  account before quitting.
- HR offers $30,000 tied to a one-year non-disparagement clause and requests
  return of hardware, IP assignment, and non-solicitation.

## Distinctions

- **Data deletion vs. evidence destruction:** personal cleanup must not erase
  records subject to legal hold or company policy.
- **Silence payment vs. ordinary severance:** confidentiality and
  non-disparagement terms have different legal and practical effects.
- **Exit timing vs. retaliation:** timing may protect earned value, but covert
  preparation can itself breach policy.

## Limitations

The chapter is an adversarial personal account and should not be used to infer
Facebook's motives or legal obligations. Employment, tax, IP, and data-retention
rules vary; counsel should review any exit plan.

## Practical Application

Review equity documents well before a vesting date. Make a checklist for
personal data, lawful record preservation, device return, references, benefits,
confidentiality, and post-employment restrictions; then negotiate with a lawyer.

## Worked Example

An employee plans to resign two days before a large vesting event. She checks
the plan document, asks counsel about the employment-date definition, preserves
work records under policy, removes personal data lawfully, and does not solicit
colleagues before permitted. A short delay may preserve value; a careless one
may forfeit it or create a new dispute.

## Key Takeaways

- Exit timing can materially change compensation.
- Treat severance and silence clauses as legal terms, not gifts.
- Leave with clean boundaries even when the relationship ends badly.
