# Chapter 11 — The Hill of Sand

## Thesis

Venture fundraising is an information and power network, so the decisive
question is often not whether a firm likes the company but whether the person
in the room can actually make the investment decision.

## Core Idea

Sand Hill Road represents the concentrated geography and status hierarchy of
Silicon Valley venture capital, where logos, tombstones, and introductions act
as market signals. AdGrok's Sequoia meeting nearly fails when a last-minute
code change breaks the demo, but a screenshot preserves enough credibility to
continue the conversation. Sequoia declines because it sees strategic overlap
with an incumbent, while remaining helpful; the chapter treats that as a
reminder that a polite rejection can still produce network value. Later social
events with investors show that access and informal context shape who gets a
real hearing, while Ben Narasin's decision turns on authority rather than the
prestige of the fund. The practical map is simple: identify decision power,
understand the firm's process, and read response speed as a signal of internal
conviction.

## Frameworks

- **Decision-power test:** ask whether the contact can approve the check or
  must sell the deal upward.
- **Signal-speed heuristic:** a fast yes suggests a live internal champion; a
  slow, vague response often means the deal is not winning priority.

## Source Examples / Evidence

- A changed build threatens the Sequoia demo, so a prepared screenshot becomes
  the difference between a recoverable glitch and a failed presentation.
- The author asks Narasin directly whether he has decision-making power and gets
  a quicker path to a commitment.

## Distinctions

- **Prestige vs. authority:** a famous brand on a business card may not control
  the investment decision.
- **Rejection vs. hostility:** a firm can decline and still be a valuable
  future connector.
- **Warm introduction vs. investment:** social access creates an opportunity,
  not a term sheet.

## Limitations

The author's network, technical background, and YC affiliation give him access
that many founders do not have. Speed of response is a useful heuristic, not a
reliable substitute for understanding a fund's strategy or decision calendar.

## Practical Application

Before each investor meeting, map the fund's portfolio conflicts, check size,
partner ownership, and next decision point. Bring a demo fallback and end with
an explicit ask: what evidence is missing, who decides, and by when?

## Worked Example

A founder meets an associate at a large fund who is enthusiastic but cannot
approve a seed check. Rather than treating the meeting as a yes, the founder
asks for the partner who owns the category, clarifies the internal meeting
date, and sends the one metric that partner needs. The process becomes legible
instead of a vague prestige encounter.

## Key Takeaways

- Find the person with decision rights.
- A resilient demo protects the narrative when reality misbehaves.
- Social capital compounds, but it must be converted into a concrete next step.
