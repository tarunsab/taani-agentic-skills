# Chapter 35 — The Barbaric Yawn

## Thesis

Speed is relative to organizational context: a company can preserve startup
intensity while growing into a bureaucracy whose vesting, routines, and scale
change what urgency feels like.

## Core Idea

The author compares a startup racetrack with Facebook's autobahn, where the
company is still fast by ordinary standards but no longer feels like a tiny
team. His four-year vesting schedule, with quarterly vesting after the first
year, acts as a golden handcuff and makes leaving economically expensive. The
company's Friday rituals and social scene blur work and personal life, while
acqui-hires bring people who are used to shorter founder timelines into a longer
corporate one. The result is not simple slowness: Facebook can still move
quickly, but decisions, incentives, and status operate through a larger machine.
The chapter warns that “move fast” slogans can hide the fact that compensation
and institutional comfort are also mechanisms of control.

## Frameworks

- **Relative-speed model:** compare execution speed with the relevant
  competitors, decision latency, and the cost of coordination—not with a
  startup's memory.
- **Golden-handcuff analysis:** calculate how vesting, refresh grants, and
  lifestyle costs affect a person's ability to leave.

## Key Concepts

- Startup racetrack
- Corporate scale
- Quarterly vesting
- Golden handcuffs
- Acqui-hire culture

## Source Examples / Evidence

- The author describes a four-year vesting schedule with a one-year cliff and
  subsequent quarterly vesting.
- Facebook's social rituals and campus life create a dense environment that is
  energetic yet far from a small startup's operating rhythm.

## Distinctions

- **Fast execution vs. fast decision:** a team can ship quickly after a slow
  political decision.
- **Retention vs. commitment:** staying because unvested equity is valuable is
  not the same as believing in the mission.
- **Scale efficiency vs. scale drag:** shared infrastructure can accelerate
  work while coordination and status slow it down.

## Limitations

The chapter's social observations are personal and intentionally bawdy. Vesting
terms differ widely, and a four-year grant can be reasonable compensation when
the role, risk, and refresh policy are understood.

## Practical Application

Measure the time from idea to decision to launch, then locate the queue causing
delay. Show employees the real value and forfeiture risk of their equity so
retention is informed rather than mysterious.

## Worked Example

A platform claims to move fast, but a product decision takes six weeks and the
implementation takes three days. The bottleneck is governance, not engineering;
the fix may be a decision owner and review threshold rather than more coders.

## Key Takeaways

- Speed must be measured against the whole decision system.
- Vesting can retain people without creating belief.
- Scale changes the meaning of startup culture.
