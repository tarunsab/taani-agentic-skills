# Chapter 20 — Getting Poked

## Thesis

An acqui-hire evaluates people as the product, so interviews are simultaneously
technical diligence, cultural selection, and a source of leverage for a small
team that may otherwise be treated as one bundle.

## Core Idea

Facebook screens the AdGrok founders as if they were ordinary candidates even
though it is considering acquiring the company, requiring the team to pass
several specialized interviews. The author recommends learning who will
interview each person and researching their incentives, because each session
tests a different kind of truth: quantitative reasoning, system design,
technical skill, product judgment, or cultural compatibility. Culture fit can
be a proxy for homogeneity, credential filtering, and comfort with the
company's ego and work norms rather than an objective measure of collaboration.
The product interview asks for a first change to Facebook Ads, and the author
answers with conversion tracking, an answer that signals an understanding of
advertiser value rather than surface feature polish. The founders leave with a
stronger impression of Facebook's scale and a weaker impression of its
organizational atmosphere.

## Frameworks

- **Interview-stack model:** assess people across reasoning, execution, domain
  judgment, collaboration, and learning rather than using one proxy.
- **Acqui-hire leverage:** a coordinated team can negotiate as a unit until the
  buyer has evaluated each person's independent value.

## Key Concepts

- Acqui-hire
- Technical diligence
- Culture fit
- Conversion tracking
- Team-bundle economics

## Source Examples / Evidence

- Facebook conducts estimation, design, technical, product, and culture
  interviews, resembling a normal hiring loop despite the acquisition context.
- Conversion tracking is treated as the high-value product answer because it
  links ad spend to business outcomes.

## Distinctions

- **Skill fit vs. culture conformity:** collaboration can be tested without
  demanding sameness of background or personality.
- **Team value vs. individual value:** an acquirer may want the system of people,
  then price each retention risk separately.
- **Feature knowledge vs. economic understanding:** conversion measurement is
  valuable because it changes advertiser willingness to spend.

## Limitations

The author's description of Facebook culture is highly subjective and uses
deliberate provocation. Interview signals are noisy; a well-designed process
should not infer character or future performance from one answer.

## Practical Application

For an acqui-hire, document each person's must-have role, vesting or retention
terms, reporting line, and team-dependency risk before interviews. For hiring,
replace vague culture-fit questions with observable behaviors and structured
scoring.

## Worked Example

A buyer wants a four-person startup but offers separate packages. The founders
should identify which workflows require all four, negotiate a team-level
closing condition, and compare cash, equity, vesting, and autonomy. Otherwise
the buyer can capture the most valuable individual while leaving the rest to
absorb the uncertainty.

## Key Takeaways

- Acqui-hire interviews are diligence disguised as hiring.
- Conversion and attribution can be more valuable than another targeting feature.
- Culture evaluation needs behavioral evidence, not a sameness filter.
