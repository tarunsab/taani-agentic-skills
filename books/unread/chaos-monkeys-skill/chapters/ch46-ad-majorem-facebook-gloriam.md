# Chapter 46 — Ad Majorem Facebook Gloriam

## Thesis

In a large company, the fate of a product depends on institutional proximity and
political legitimacy as much as on revenue, technical merit, or external demand.

## Core Idea

Boz takes over Facebook Ads and quickly becomes the authority around which
resources and status orbit. The open-versus-closed debate then becomes a contest
between FBX's genuinely incremental outside spend and Custom Audiences' easier,
partly reallocated Facebook spend. FBX is technically harder and requires new
advertiser behavior, while Custom Audiences fits existing interfaces and
budgets, so dashboard revenue makes the less innovative path look stronger. The
author argues that incremental revenue is the correct comparison, but his
insubordination and lack of coalition support weaken his internal position. In
the final meeting, Boz recommends keeping identity matching and mobile inventory
out of FBX, Sheryl accepts the recommendation, and the author's product is put
on life support. The chapter's tragedy is that a product can be strategically
right, operationally promising, and still lose when its advocates lack the
authority and institutional capital to carry it.

## Frameworks

- **Incrementality test:** discount revenue that would have been spent through
  the platform anyway; compare new dollars, not bucket size.
- **Political-capital ledger:** track allies, detractors, executive access,
  resource dependencies, and the cost of public dissent.

## Key Concepts

- Custom Audiences vs. FBX
- Incremental revenue
- Institutional proximity
- Middle management
- Product life support

## Source Examples / Evidence

- FBX attracts budgets formerly spent on Google, while Custom Audiences can
  redirect money already earmarked for Facebook.
- After months of debate, Sheryl accepts the recommendation not to add identity
  matching or mobile inventory to FBX.

## Distinctions

- **Dashboard revenue vs. incremental revenue:** accounting buckets can reward
  reallocation rather than growth.
- **Being right vs. being effective:** a good argument needs sponsorship,
  resources, and a coalition.
- **Decision closure vs. strategic proof:** a leadership decision can end a
  project without proving the rejected strategy wrong.

## Limitations

The author's calculation of incrementality and account of internal politics are
contested perspectives. Open systems can impose governance and privacy costs,
and closed systems can create value when a platform truly offers unique data or
performance.

## Practical Application

Present both the dashboard metric and a counterfactual estimate of what spend
would have occurred anyway. Before a high-stakes review, secure allies across
engineering, sales, finance, and the executive chain; do not rely on one
champion's technical explanation.

## Worked Example

A new payment feature produces $5 million in processed volume, but interviews
show that 80% would have used the old flow. The business case should report
$1 million incremental volume, then compare it with the cost and strategic
value of the feature. That framing may change which roadmap wins.

## Key Takeaways

- Internal metrics can reward the easiest reallocation.
- Political capital is a real product resource.
- Strategic correctness does not guarantee institutional survival.
