# Chapter 17 — Dates @Twitter

## Thesis

Acquisition courtship is a sequence of social signals, technical proof, and
formal commitment; the gap between a pleasant conversation and a real deal is
usually visible in who invests time, legal work, and reputational capital.

## Core Idea

After reading the author's public writing, Twitter's Jessica Verrilli invites
him to lunch, and the author applies a due-diligence habit: research the people,
their incentives, their networks, and the public signals around them. A hosted
remote demo rescues the meeting when he forgets his laptop, and Twitter's
leaders see AdGrok as a way to build an advertising API before Twitter has one.
The API would let external software and advertisers interact with Twitter's
inventory, making it strategic infrastructure rather than a cosmetic feature.
The chapter distinguishes real deal movement from performative interest: phone
calls, in-person meetings, an NDA, counsel, and an explicit term sheet carry
more weight than a chain of enthusiastic emails. It also introduces the
ecosystem's translators—insiders who selectively interpret signals, leak
information, and help each side understand the other—showing that acquisition
processes are as political as they are technical.

## Frameworks

- **Courtship ladder:** public signal → exploratory meeting → technical fit →
  confidentiality → legal work → priced commitment.
- **Counterparty diligence:** research the decision-makers' histories and
  incentives before assuming their interest is aligned with yours.

## Distinctions

- **Interest vs. commitment:** attention can be free; legal and organizational
  effort is costly and therefore more informative.
- **Technical fit vs. strategic fit:** a product can solve a technical gap yet
  still be unimportant to the acquirer's long-term plan.
- **Information sharing vs. confidentiality:** an adviser can explain a market
  without transferring protected company knowledge.

## Limitations

The chapter's due-diligence advice is colored by the author's later bargaining
and betrayal narrative. Public profiles are incomplete signals, and an NDA or
lawyer does not guarantee that an acquisition will close.

## Practical Application

Track acquisition conversations by stage and evidence, not by emotional tone.
For every proposed discussion, decide what can be shared, what must remain
confidential, who owns the next action, and what event would justify exclusivity.

## Worked Example

An enterprise platform says it loves a startup's technology. Before pausing
other conversations, the founder asks for a technical workshop, a named
executive sponsor, counsel contact, diligence list, and target date for a term
sheet. If none appears, the conversation is still exploratory and the startup
keeps its options open.

## Key Takeaways

- Formality and costly effort are better deal signals than enthusiasm.
- An API can be strategic control over an ecosystem.
- Due diligence applies to acquirers and investors as much as to startups.
