# Chapter 01 — The Garden of Forking Paths

## Thesis

At Facebook, consequential product choices were made at the intersection of
weakly understood technology, deadline pressure, executive intuition, and
organizational politics.

## Core Idea

The prologue places the reader in an April 2012 meeting where Facebook's
leadership needs a new advertising engine before its IPO while its existing
social-ad experiments are disappointing. The proposed options all concern
getting better information about users: observe behavior across the Web,
import outside purchase data, or connect to other data sources. The author
argues that executive decisions do not wait for perfect technical knowledge;
they are shaped by who can make a compelling frame, who owns the decision, and
which risk is most salient at the moment. The meeting's immediate choice—to
reject one route while approving retargeting in principle—sets up the later
conflict between Facebook Exchange and Custom Audiences. The chapter's
forking-path metaphor therefore applies both to product strategy and to the
author's career: a small decision can create a path whose consequences are
visible only much later.

## Frameworks

- **Decision under asymmetric understanding:** experts supply mechanisms, but
  senior leaders often decide from compressed narratives and incentives.
- **Revenue deadline as a forcing function:** an IPO converts an exploratory
  product question into a near-term organizational survival problem.

## Key Concepts

- Retargeting and outside data
- Social plugins as behavioral observation
- Executive framing and decision rights
- Product strategy as path dependence

## Source Examples / Evidence

- The meeting occurs in the "Only Good News" room while the Ads team is under
  pressure from a slowing growth story and a pending IPO.
- Zuck rejects using Like buttons for the immediate plan but accepts
  retargeting, leaving the external-data mechanism unresolved.

## Distinctions

- **Data availability vs. data usefulness:** having many Likes does not mean
  having commercially predictive intent.
- **Approval vs. understanding:** a leader can authorize a direction without
  grasping the technical system that will implement it.
- **Local decision vs. long-term path:** the meeting's choice is tactical in
  wording but strategic in its downstream effects.

## Limitations

This is a memoirist's reconstruction of a high-level meeting, not a complete
record of every proposal or participant's reasoning. The chapter is strongest
as an account of perceived organizational decision-making and weaker as proof
that any single executive was uniquely responsible for the later outcome.

## Practical Application

When a company is choosing among data or platform strategies, write down the
decision owner, the economic hypothesis, the information each option requires,
and the deadline-induced constraint separately. Ask which facts are known,
which are merely persuasive, and what future options each decision closes.

## Worked Example

Suppose an ad platform can either collect more native engagement signals or
connect to a partner's purchase data. A decision memo should compare not just
engineering effort but signal quality, privacy boundaries, advertiser adoption,
measurement, and the opportunity cost of delaying the other path. The
prologue suggests that a clean decision process makes those trade-offs explicit
before executive intuition turns them into an unexamined narrative.

## Key Takeaways

- Data strategy is also power strategy.
- High-stakes deadlines compress technical uncertainty into political choices.
- A small product decision can determine the next several chapters of a company.
