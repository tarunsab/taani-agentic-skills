# Chapter 07 — Like Marriage, but without the Fucking

## Thesis

Founder structure is a product decision: equal authority can preserve fairness,
but a startup under pressure usually needs a clear final decision-maker.

## Core Idea

The chapter treats cofounders as people sharing a foxhole, with more exposure to
one another's weaknesses than most family members ever receive. Equal equity
and nominally equal authority can create deadlock, factional voting, and a
permanent contest over strategic pivots. The author's preferred alternative is
one leader with real authority—ideally a CEO who can make the final call—while
the other founders retain meaningful ownership and responsibility. He describes
McEachen as a highly capable but distractible engineer and designer, and Zymnis
as a broad technical hacker whose temperament can make conflict sharper; these
complementary strengths become difficult to coordinate. The central lesson is
not that engineering is easy, but that people problems are harder to debug and
more likely to determine whether the company survives.

## Frameworks

- **Authority-to-equity alignment:** decide whether ownership, operating power,
  and accountability are actually aligned before the first crisis.
- **Founder compatibility test:** assess conflict style, pace, ambition, and
  trust—not just technical skill or friendship.

## Key Concepts

- Founder deadlock
- Benevolent dictatorship
- CEO as decision right
- Complementary versus conflicting strengths
- People problems

## Source Examples / Evidence

- YC's Paul Graham identifies the author as the group's leader, clarifying a
  role that the founders had not fully formalized themselves.
- Arguments over product direction repeatedly expose differences in emotional
  resilience, technical identity, and appetite for risk.

## Distinctions

- **Equal respect vs. equal veto:** founders can be peers without giving every
  decision multiple blocking votes.
- **Technical centrality vs. leadership:** writing the most code does not make a
  person the best strategic decider.
- **Conflict avoidance vs. healthy disagreement:** a clear leader should make
  disagreement safer by ending it, not suppressing it.

## Limitations

The chapter generalizes from one difficult team and favors the author's own
leadership model. A single leader can become an unchecked bottleneck; governance,
board oversight, and explicit decision domains may be better than a blanket
majority rule.

## Practical Application

Write a founder operating agreement that names decision domains, tie-breakers,
equity vesting, departure rules, and how a founder can be removed. Run a
pressure simulation around a failed launch, a financing offer, and a personal
crisis before assuming alignment.

## Worked Example

Three founders disagree about whether to sell, pivot, or raise. If all three
have an informal veto, the company can spend its runway debating. If a CEO has
the final call but must document the rationale and obtain board approval for a
sale, the team preserves speed while retaining a governance checkpoint.

## Key Takeaways

- Cofounder choice compounds through every later decision.
- Clear authority can be more humane than endless ambiguity.
- Technical brilliance cannot compensate for persistent interpersonal failure.
