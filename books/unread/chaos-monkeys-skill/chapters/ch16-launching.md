# Chapter 16 — Launching!

## Thesis

Launch is a temporary attention event; the real startup test begins when the
spike ends and the company must acquire, serve, and retain users within runway.

## Core Idea

AdGrok's launch is staged as a PR event even though the product is already live,
with an embargoed media schedule creating a coordinated burst. The date also
coincides with the author's daughter's birthday, joining personal time and
company theater in the memoir's recurring collision of lives. Y Combinator's
"Trough of Despair" describes the expected shape: launch attention rises,
falls, and leaves a long low period before product-market fit or acquisition.
Most startups do not die because the founders lacked courage on launch day;
they die because customer acquisition, support, or repeat use cannot scale
afterward. The operational response is a fast iteration loop tied to a real
retention or revenue signal, while conserving enough cash to survive the trough.

## Frameworks

- **Launch-to-trough curve:** treat the initial spike as a measurement window,
  not as proof of fit.
- **Runway-to-learning ratio:** every post-launch experiment should be judged by
  how much uncertainty it removes per unit of cash and time.

## Key Concepts

- Launch PR
- Embargoed coverage
- Trough of Despair
- Product-market fit
- Scalable acquisition and service

## Source Examples / Evidence

- The product's public launch is synchronized with press coverage despite being
  technically available beforehand.
- The chapter warns that the hard period arrives after the publicity, when the
  company must show repeatable customer economics.

## Distinctions

- **Launch reach vs. demand:** press impressions and signups are not retained,
  paying usage.
- **Courage vs. economics:** persistence cannot compensate for an acquisition
  channel that costs more than the customer is worth.
- **Trough vs. failure:** a post-launch decline is normal; failure is the
  inability to learn or reach sustainable economics.

## Limitations

The curve is a useful heuristic, not a deterministic lifecycle. Some products
launch quietly, grow through distribution partners, or find fit before public
attention; the chapter's startup pattern should not be treated as a law.

## Practical Application

Define launch metrics in three layers: attention, activation, and retained
economic value. Pre-commit to the threshold that justifies more runway and the
threshold that triggers a change in customer, product, or channel hypothesis.

## Connections

## Worked Example

A launch produces 10,000 visitors and 1,000 signups, but only 50 return after a
week and none pay. The company should not celebrate the conversion rate alone;
it should interview retained users, test a narrower use case, and protect cash
until it knows whether the problem is value, onboarding, or customer selection.

## Key Takeaways

- Launch creates evidence; it does not create product-market fit.
- The trough tests unit economics and learning speed.
- Preserve runway for the period when attention disappears.
