# Chapter 41 — IPA > IPO

## Thesis

An IPO is a corporate coronation that converts collective labor into a public
price, producing genuine liquidity and status while exposing the dependence of
employees on a volatile market and a continuing employer.

## Core Idea

Facebook stages its IPO at the company campus rather than handing the ceremony
to Wall Street, turning the event into a self-authored ritual of technological
power. A hackathon, beer brewing, speeches, symbols, and a public countdown
create shared emotional significance, but the author reads the celebration as
capitalism placing a price tag on labor and identity. The stock opens above the
offering price and closes near it, frustrating the usual expectation that a
successful IPO should produce a dramatic first-day jump. The author's
compensation calculation shows how salary, bonuses, restricted stock, tax, and
vesting combine into a large nominal annual value that is still modest relative
to San Francisco housing and disappears if the Facebook job ends. The chapter
therefore holds two truths together: the IPO can be a life-changing wealth
event, and it can also be a new form of dependence on price, lockup, and
continued employment.

## Frameworks

- **Ritual-to-price conversion:** a shared corporate ceremony turns an
  organization into a publicly valued asset.
- **Compensation sensitivity:** calculate nominal equity, vesting, tax, housing,
  and job-loss scenarios instead of using the headline grant.

## Key Concepts

- IPO ceremony
- Restricted stock
- Lockup
- Vesting
- Indifference threshold
- Financial independence

## Source Examples / Evidence

- Facebook rings the public-market bell at its own campus while employees
  celebrate the event as a major life milestone.
- The author estimates his annual gross compensation at just under $1 million
  at the IPO price, then reduces it substantially for tax and cost of living.

## Distinctions

- **Public price vs. realized wealth:** shares can be locked, taxed as income,
  or sold at a lower price.
- **Celebration vs. meaning:** collective joy can be real even when the ritual is
  also a market transaction.
- **High income vs. independence:** a large salary does not eliminate dependence
  on the next vesting date or employer.

## Limitations

The wealth calculations use the author's grant, tax assumptions, and local
costs. The critique of capitalism is philosophical and personal, not a general
measure of IPO welfare or employee experience.

## Practical Application

Build an equity scenario table with price, tax basis, lockup, vesting, housing,
and termination assumptions. Decide what level of liquidity changes your
choices before the ceremony makes the number emotionally salient.

## Worked Example

A new hire values 40,000 restricted shares at the offer price. A scenario model
shows that the first-year cliff, ordinary-income tax, lockup, and a 40% price
decline can reduce realizable value dramatically. The hire can then compare the
grant with salary and opportunity cost without treating the IPO ceremony as
proof of cash.

## Key Takeaways

- IPOs create both liquidity and dependence.
- Price, tax, vesting, and cost of living determine equity's lived value.
- Corporate rituals can supply meaning while also serving market functions.
