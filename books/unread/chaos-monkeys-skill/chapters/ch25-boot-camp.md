# Chapter 25 — Boot Camp

## Thesis

Onboarding is culture transmission and operational control: a large company
turns new hires into citizens by teaching its values, tools, secrecy rules, and
preferred way of shipping.

## Core Idea

Facebook presents itself as a personalized newspaper built from the social
graph, a product vision that joins user experience to a future media business.
Senior leaders explain hacker norms such as ownership, truth-seeking, and not
claiming credit, while hackathons and internal tools make those norms concrete.
The onboarding process therefore resembles both a technical boot camp and a
citizenship ceremony: it teaches how to write code, how to use the repository
and development server, and what information must remain private. Rules about
profile access, one-ask/no, obscenity, and discretion reveal that the company's
freedom rhetoric has boundaries enforced by institutional power. The author's
small act of changing Like-button text to an obscene phrase demonstrates both
the cultural appetite for irreverence and the controlled environment in which
it is permitted.

## Frameworks

- **Culture as operating system:** values matter when they are encoded in
  rituals, tooling, review practices, and consequences.
- **Onboarding dual mandate:** teach the fastest path to productive work while
  transmitting the boundaries of acceptable behavior.

## Key Concepts

- Personal newspaper vision
- Hackathon
- Ownership and truth
- Secrecy and profile access
- Engineering boot camp

## Source Examples / Evidence

- New hires learn Facebook's custom stack and workflow before joining product
  teams.
- HR and engineering instruction pair permissive experimentation with strict
  rules around confidential data and unauthorized access.

## Distinctions

- **Autonomy vs. access:** employees may own their work without being allowed to
  inspect every user or system.
- **Irreverence vs. permission:** a culture can celebrate jokes while limiting
  where and how they reach production.
- **Values statement vs. behavior:** the real culture is visible in tools,
  incentives, and enforcement.

## Limitations

The chapter is an outsider's first impression and uses caricature. Large
companies vary by team, manager, and era; onboarding can communicate ideals
without making them consistently true.

## Practical Application

Audit onboarding for four layers: product mission, technical path, decision
rights, and information boundaries. For every stated value, name the ritual,
tool, metric, or consequence that makes it observable.

## Worked Example

A new data engineer receives a mission talk but no guidance on production
access, privacy review, or how to ship a small change. A good boot camp pairs a
safe first patch with explicit least-privilege access, code review, escalation
paths, and a demonstration of the company's product purpose.

## Key Takeaways

- Culture is made durable by systems and rituals.
- Freedom to hack requires clear boundaries around data and production.
- Onboarding is a control surface, not administrative paperwork.
