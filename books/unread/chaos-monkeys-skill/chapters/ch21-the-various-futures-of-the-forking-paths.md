# Chapter 21 — The Various Futures of the Forking Paths

## Thesis

An exit decision distributes value among founders, employees, investors, and
the acquirer, so the economically best path for one participant may be a
betrayal or loss for another.

## Core Idea

Twitter returns with a much larger offer while Facebook rejects the engineers
but still wants the author, creating incompatible futures inside one founding
team. Facebook's scale and addictive engagement make its opportunity feel
larger, while Twitter offers a cleaner and more legible deal. The author hears
how a hybrid acquisition can split outcomes—one person joins the buyer while a
company or cofounder continues elsewhere—and realizes that "selling the
company" need not mean everyone receives the same future. He then chooses
Facebook and initially hides the information from McEachen and Zymnis, whose
own goals and relationship to AdGrok differ. Their eventual agreement is less a
shared vision than recognition that the company has already lost momentum and
the decision has become effectively irreversible.

## Frameworks

- **Forked-outcome model:** map each stakeholder's preferred future, not just
  the aggregate sale price.
- **Stateless-machine principle:** Silicon Valley can rapidly forget old
  allegiances, allowing people to move between rivals, but that mobility does
  not erase interpersonal obligations.

## Key Concepts

- Hybrid acquisition
- Acqui-hire allocation
- Stakeholder divergence
- Decision irreversibility
- Facebook scale and engagement

## Source Examples / Evidence

- Twitter's offer grows to roughly $10 million while Facebook wants the author
  separately from the other founders.
- A prior deal pattern lets one founder join Facebook while another company and
  its remaining founder receive a different outcome.

## Distinctions

- **Company value vs. individual opportunity:** a buyer may value a person more
  than the legal entity or the rest of the team.
- **Consent vs. shared enthusiasm:** cofounders can accept a decision after
  being deprived of meaningful timing or information.
- **Mobility vs. loyalty:** a fluid labor market enables movement but does not
  make deception harmless.

## Limitations

The author narrates his own deception and has an incentive to frame it as
necessary. Facebook's scale and engagement are presented as evidence of social
power, not an independent assessment of user welfare or product quality.

## Practical Application

Before an acquisition, write individual outcome statements for every founder,
employee, investor, and buyer. Establish when information must be shared, how a
founder can take a different offer, and what constitutes consent to sell the
company or its assets.

## Worked Example

A buyer wants only the CEO, while another buyer wants the engineering team.
The founders can negotiate a coordinated process with separate packages,
retention terms, and a shared minimum, rather than allowing the first company
to convert personal ambition into a private veto over everyone else's future.

## Key Takeaways

- A single exit can contain several different economic futures.
- The acquirer's valuation of talent may exceed its valuation of the product.
- Timing and disclosure determine whether consent is real.
