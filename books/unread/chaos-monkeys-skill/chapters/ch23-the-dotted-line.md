# Chapter 23 — The Dotted Line

## Thesis

Acquisition headlines conceal a distribution problem: price, ownership,
vesting, retention, taxes, and liquidity decide who actually receives value.

## Core Idea

The author's Tesla-versus-Mini race provides a metaphor for taking competitive
risk, but the deal discussion quickly becomes more concrete. Twitter's roughly
$10 million term sheet allocates only about $1 million to investors and places
the rest inside employee hiring and retention offers, because the acquirer is
pricing scarce talent rather than simply purchasing a company. This enrages
Sacca and exposes the different objectives of founders, investors, and the
buyer. Secondary markets and liquidity matter because a nominal share price is
not cash in hand, and a founder's ownership can be taxed or locked up in ways
that change the outcome. Facebook's offer uses a restricted-stock package that
the author estimates at about $2.3 million at the then price, roughly matching
his stake, and he signs it before the Twitter meeting. The chapter makes the
contract itself the point: value becomes real only after allocation and
conditions are read, negotiated, and signed.

## Frameworks

- **Waterfall over headline:** model proceeds by stakeholder and instrument,
  including investor preference, employee offers, vesting, tax, and escrow.
- **Liquidity-adjusted value:** discount paper wealth for lockups, vesting,
  volatility, tax treatment, and the chance the deal never closes.

## Key Concepts

- Retention package
- Restricted stock
- Secondary liquidity
- Vesting and lockup
- Investor allocation

## Source Examples / Evidence

- Twitter's term sheet is described as a $10 million transaction with only a
  small investor allocation and most value tied to hiring the team.
- Facebook's restricted-stock offer is valued using its pre-IPO share price and
  the author's approximate ownership stake.

## Distinctions

- **Purchase price vs. employee compensation:** a buyer can spend money to
  hire people without paying the company or its investors proportionally.
- **Paper value vs. realized wealth:** shares remain subject to price, tax,
  vesting, and liquidity constraints.
- **Competitive risk vs. reckless disclosure:** taking a risk in a race does
  not justify violating deal confidentiality.

## Limitations

The figures are the author's estimates and a snapshot of early-2010s private
company practice. Tax, securities, fiduciary, and employment treatment differs
by instrument and jurisdiction; professional review is essential.

## Practical Application

Request a complete cap table and build a proceeds waterfall under several stock
prices and closing dates. Separate company consideration, founder consideration,
employee retention, investor return, and contingent value before comparing bids.

## Worked Example

Two buyers each advertise a $10 million value. Buyer A pays the company, while
Buyer B uses most of the budget for individual retention packages. A cap-table
waterfall may show that founders and investors prefer A even if the employees
prefer B; the team can then negotiate a shared floor or a better allocation.

## Key Takeaways

- A headline acquisition price is not a payout statement.
- Acquirers may be buying people, optionality, or strategic access rather than code.
- Vesting, tax, and liquidity can dominate nominal equity value.
