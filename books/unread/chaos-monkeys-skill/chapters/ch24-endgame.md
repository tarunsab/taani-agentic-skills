# Chapter 24 — Endgame

## Thesis

An acquisition is often a long job interview with asymmetric information, so
founders need to negotiate offers and obligations with the same care they used
to build the company.

## Core Idea

After the author tells Twitter he will not join, Twitter quickly improves its
offer to the engineers, demonstrating that the earlier number was not its
maximum. The boys accept and the sale closes, while the author hires an
employment lawyer and resigns from AdGrok to join Facebook. He describes being
unemployed for only minutes, but the apparent continuity hides a large change:
the founders' equity is subject to vesting and the acquisition places them in a
new company's hierarchy. In retrospect, he identifies a more aggressive
strategy—negotiate both paths, sign both, and then ask to leave one—but also
recognizes that it would breach noncompete or employment obligations. The
chapter's sober lesson is that morality, ignorance, and bravado all have prices,
and that the most valuable negotiating window closes before the signature.

## Frameworks

- **Acquisition as employment contract:** evaluate role, manager, vesting,
  termination, and autonomy, not just purchase consideration.
- **Option value before signature:** preserve alternatives while permitted, then
  stop once exclusivity and fiduciary duties make the choice binding.

## Key Concepts

- Noncompete
- Employment lawyer
- Founder vesting
- Acquisition integration
- Negotiation window

## Source Examples / Evidence

- Twitter raises the engineers' offer only after the author withdraws, revealing
  bargaining room that was not visible in the first package.
- The author notes that at ten months founders may own little of a nominal
  four-year vesting grant, even after building the company.

## Distinctions

- **Closing a deal vs. realizing value:** a signed agreement still leaves work,
  vesting, integration, and employment risk.
- **Aggressive strategy vs. authorized strategy:** maximizing leverage may
  conflict with confidentiality, fiduciary, or noncompete duties.
- **Fast next job vs. successful exit:** immediate employment can mask a poor
  allocation or an unhappy integration.

## Limitations

The counterfactual strategy is intentionally provocative and may be unlawful or
unethical in many settings. Contract terms, board duties, and local law must be
reviewed rather than inferred from this memoir.

## Practical Application

Use counsel before signing: list every obligation, vesting trigger, termination
right, IP assignment, non-solicit, and confidentiality clause. Negotiate the job
as if the acquisition price were zero, then negotiate the economics separately.

## Worked Example

A founder receives a large equity grant that vests over four years but has no
guaranteed role after closing. Before signing, she asks what happens on a
reorg, who controls vesting acceleration, and whether the buyer can terminate
her without paying unvested value. Those answers may matter more than a small
increase in headline price.

## Key Takeaways

- Negotiating power is greatest before exclusivity and signatures.
- Acquisition diligence must include the post-close job.
- Legal and ethical constraints are economic variables, not afterthoughts.
