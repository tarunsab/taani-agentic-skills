# Chapter 33 — Are We Savages or What?

## Thesis

A culture of bold experiments becomes credible when failures are quickly made
visible, pruned, and converted into a shared language for correction.

## Core Idea

Facebook moves from a crowded office to the Sun campus, and the physical move
becomes an experiment in how much disorder the company will tolerate. Employees
spray-paint walls, make uneven art, and leave behind artifacts, while Zuckerberg
permits the improvisation rather than imposing a polished corporate environment.
When the results are poor, blue tape marks failed murals and becomes an
internal code-review metaphor for work that needs correction. The symbol turns
an abstract value—try things, accept failure, improve—into a visible process.
The chapter's pattern is more useful than its vandalism: experimentation needs
permission to begin and a fast mechanism for identifying what should not
survive.

## Frameworks

- **Experiment–prune loop:** authorize a low-cost trial, inspect the result,
  mark failure without ambiguity, and remove or revise it quickly.
- **Cultural symbol as protocol:** a repeated artifact can teach behavior more
  effectively than a values memo.

## Key Concepts

- Sun campus
- Office experimentation
- Blue tape
- Code-review metaphor
- Failure pruning

## Source Examples / Evidence

- Graffiti and murals are tolerated as part of the office's hacker identity.
- Failed murals are marked with blue tape, turning a visual embarrassment into a
  shorthand for review and correction.

## Distinctions

- **Permission to try vs. permission to persist:** experimentation needs a
  clear stopping rule.
- **Visible failure vs. humiliation:** a symbol can improve quality if it marks
  work, not a person's worth.
- **Culture artifact vs. process:** symbols reinforce a loop but cannot replace
  review criteria or ownership.

## Limitations

The examples are anecdotal and focus on office symbolism rather than measuring
whether the culture improved product outcomes. Tolerance for disorder can harm
accessibility, safety, or inclusion if boundaries are not explicit.

## Practical Application

Give experiments an owner, budget, success metric, review date, and visible
status. Celebrate a well-run failure, then remove the failed artifact or
convert its lesson into a reusable checklist.

## Worked Example

A team pilots a new onboarding flow for two weeks. A shared marker identifies
the test as experimental, the review date is on the calendar, and the team
publishes both the lift and the failure modes. The result is either rolled out,
revised, or removed without a six-month argument over sunk cost.

## Key Takeaways

- Bold culture needs visible pruning.
- Symbols work when they point to a real quality loop.
- Failure should be attached to an experiment, not a person's identity.
