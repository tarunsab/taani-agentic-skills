# Cheatsheet

## Fast Decisions

1. **What is slowest?** Dry beans/whole grains set the pressure time; add quick vegetables, greens, peas, acid, and cream later.
2. **Will it rise?** Use Steam with the vent open, a covered/foiled pan, and an external timer.
3. **Is it thick?** Scrape/deglaze before sealing. Stage cashew/nut butter or use a slurry after opening.
4. **Do you want intact vegetables?** Cut large, use low pressure or a short cycle, and quick-release.
5. **Is it rice/pasta?** Rinse/soak rice; start pasta at half package time + 1 minute; fluff and remove the hot inner pot.
6. **No suitable insert?** Cook PIP components separately; the main food keeps its listed pressure time.

## Useful Starting Points

| Food/use | Book examples |
|---|---|
| Red lentils | 2–3 min in charts; 3 min squash curry; 9 min red-lentil chili |
| Brown/green lentils | 6–15 min depending on recipe; 11–13 min soups; 18 min tagine |
| Soaked chickpeas | About 35–55 min, with long curry recipes targeting very soft texture |
| White rice | About 4–6 min; many one-pot recipes use 5 min after a 15-min soak |
| Quinoa | About 1–2 min, usually with 5 min natural release |
| Quick vegetables | 1–4 min or post-cook; use large pieces and prompt release |
| Steam cakes/brownies | 26–37 min with vent open; external timer |

## Release and Finish

- **Natural**: beans, lentils, soups, sauces, most grains, long cooks.
- **Quick**: delicate vegetables, short pasta when specified, red-lentil squash curry.
- **Combination**: rest 2–10 minutes, then vent; common for rice and mixed vegetables.
- **After opening**: taste salt/acid, add greens/peas/herbs/cream, reduce or loosen, and remove the inner pot when starch or glaze is done.

## Minimum Safety Reminders

- Confirm the float valve has dropped before opening; vent only as the recipe directs.
- Keep the pot no more than two-thirds full, or half full for expanding beans.
- Use the correct minimum liquid for the cooker size and leave clearance around PIP inserts.
- Follow the appliance manual; these are cookbook-derived starting points, not a substitute for it.
