# Chapter 4: Basics

## Core Idea

The Basics chapter is the book's mise-en-place layer: make a stock, a fermented yogurt, a neutral cream, several spice blends, and a few shortcut sauces before attempting more elaborate meals. The Instant Pot is used differently for each job—pressure extracts stock, Yogurt ferments, Sauté reduces or blooms spices, and PIP sets chickpea-flour tofu. The recipes demonstrate a consistent design principle: create a neutral base once, then reuse it across curries, soups, pasta, fillings, and desserts. The chapter also shows how the author manages dietary constraints without making the core recipe fragile: seeds, tofu, coconut, stock, and alternate starches appear as role-based substitutes. Storage is part of the method, with blends generally kept airtight for about two months, sauces refrigerated for days to weeks, and cashew cream frozen in cubes. Chickpea Flour Tofu is the standout technique because pressure removes the need to stir a thick batter at the stove and creates a soy-free protein that can be panfried or simmered in sauce.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Batch once, finish later**: pressure-cook or blend a base, cool it, portion it, and add protein or vegetables only when serving.
- **Bloom, pressure, reduce**: sauté aromatics/spices, pressure-cook a sauce with restrained liquid, then use Sauté to correct thickness.
- **Set a batter by PIP**: use an oiled glass, ceramic, or stoneware dish—not metal—for chickpea tofu; cover lightly, pressure cook, cool, and air out.

## Recipe Cards

### Easy Veggie Stock

Combine onion, carrot, celery, potato, mushrooms, scraps, bay, herbs, salt, pepper, optional nutritional yeast, and 10 cups water; pressure cook high for 18 minutes and naturally release. Strain while warm, pressing the vegetables, and refrigerate up to 5 days or freeze up to 3 months; sautéing the aromatics first or adding dried mushrooms, poultry seasoning, or soy sauce changes the flavor direction.

### Vegan Yogurt

Soak almond flour and cashews in water, blend repeatedly with cornstarch/tapioca, heat on Sauté until bubbling, cool to about 110°F/42°C, and stir in a fresh yogurt starter. Run Yogurt for at least 8 hours, or 12–14 for tang and a firmer set; sterilize the pot first with 2 cups water on Steam for 2 minutes if fermenting often. Coconut milk or additive-free soy milk are the main alternatives, and pink film means discard.

### Large-Batch Cashew Cream

Blend 1 cup raw cashews with 2–2½ cups water, resting and blending in stages until silky. Refrigerate up to 3 days or freeze in ice-cube portions; reduce or increase water to match whether a sauce needs a thick cream or a pourable finish.

### Quick Garam Masala

Mix ground coriander, cumin, black pepper, cinnamon, cloves, cardamom, and nutmeg; optional powdered bay leaf adds another layer. It is aromatic rather than simply hot and works in Indian dishes, roasted vegetables, soups, stews, and even cookies; store airtight up to 2 months.

### Vegan Worcestershire Sauce

Boil soy sauce, apple-cider vinegar, brown sugar, Dijon, garlic/onion powder, allspice, and tamarind, then cool and refrigerate up to a month. Molasses deepens color; for soy-free sauce, increase tamarind and add coconut aminos, while recognizing that the result is a flavor adjustment rather than an exact copy.

### Barbecue Sauce

Sauté onion and garlic, add tomato sauce, vinegar, maple, water, chili blend, mustard, pepper, powders, molasses, salt, and optional hot sauce, then pressure cook high for 5 minutes with natural release. Reduce briefly on Sauté to the desired glaze; refrigerate up to 2 weeks or freeze up to 2 months, adjusting tang, sweetness, and heat after tasting.

### Curry Powder

Combine a batch of Garam Masala with turmeric, dry mustard, optional ginger, fenugreek powder, and cayenne. The blend is a customizable generic curry powder for curries, roasted vegetables, beans, hummus, and bowls; the author distinguishes it from the recipe-specific spice mixes used in more traditional Indian dishes.

### Cajun Seasoning Mix

Blend smoked/sweet paprika, cayenne, black pepper, red pepper flakes, thyme, oregano, sage, garlic powder, onion powder, and optional salt/celery salt. Store airtight up to 2 months and tune the blend for smoky or hot dishes; it is the backbone of Étouffée and Cajun-Style Red Beans & Rice.

### Enchilada Seasoning Mix

Mix smoked/sweet paprika, chipotle and ancho chile, cumin, oregano, chipotle flakes, garlic/onion powder, cinnamon, cayenne, pepper, and optional thyme. Use it in enchiladas, taco fillings, beans, and chilis; its design is layered chile heat rather than one-note spiciness.

### Taco Seasoning Mix

Combine cumin, garlic/onion powder, smoked or sweet paprika, pepper, oregano, cayenne, and red pepper flakes. Adjust cumin for earthiness and the chile ingredients for heat; optional allspice/cinnamon, chipotle, or a commercial mild chili blend adds depth.

### Barbecue Spice Blend

Mix smoked/sweet paprika, garlic, oregano or parsley, onion powder, thyme, cumin/coriander, optional celery seed, cayenne, pepper, coconut sugar, and salt. It supplies an earthy-sweet layer to barbecue sauce, lentil fillings, and jackfruit; keep airtight up to 2 months.

### Berbere Spice Blend

Combine coriander, sweet/smoked paprika, New Mexico chile or more paprika, cumin, fenugreek or mustard, black pepper, cardamom, cloves, ginger, allspice, cinnamon, cayenne, and optional onion flakes. This is a practical substitute blend using more available ingredients; store up to 3 months and reduce pepper for a milder batch.

### Shawarma Spice Blend

Mix cumin, coriander, paprika, black pepper, cinnamon, cardamom, cloves, nutmeg, allspice, and garlic powder. Use it on roasted vegetables, chickpeas, beans, or the Mushroom Gyro; store airtight up to 2 months.

### Baharat Spice Blend

Grind peppercorns, coriander, cumin, sweet paprika, cinnamon, cloves, allspice, cardamom, and nutmeg. The Middle Eastern blend is intended for stews, vegetables, beans, and burgers; grinding whole spices is part of the technique and supports longer pantry life.

### Refried Lentils

Sauté onion, garlic, jalapeño, cumin, Mexican oregano, optional epazote, and chipotle; add soaked brown lentils and stock, pressure cook high for 20 minutes, naturally release, then mash with chili powder and lime. Use as a dip or taco/burrito/nacho filling; soaked pinto beans can replace lentils with a 45-minute cook, and the sauté can be skipped for dump-and-done.

### Easy Vegan Mashed Potatoes

Pressure cook cubed Yukon Gold or russet potatoes with raw garlic, salt, and water for 4 minutes, naturally release 5 minutes, then vent. Drain and let steam escape before lightly mashing with oil/butter, pepper, herbs, nutmeg, and coconut milk; do not overwork or the mash turns gluey. Nutritional yeast, roasted garlic, cashew cream, soy milk, or vegan cream cheese change the finish.

### Chickpea Flour Tofu

Whisk or blend chickpea flour/besan, water, garlic powder, and salt; pour into an oiled glass, ceramic, or stoneware dish over 1½ cups water and cover lightly with foil. Pressure cook high for 22 minutes with natural release, cool, release the edges, and air out before slicing; panfrying improves texture. Less water makes a firmer set, and seasoning the batter works better than relying on a marinade, which the tofu absorbs slowly.

## Worked Example

For a reusable curry workflow, make Cashew Cream and Bhuna-style components ahead, freeze the cream in cubes, then cook a sauce and finish it with the cream only after pressure. For a soy-free protein, season Chickpea Flour Tofu in the batter with garam masala/cayenne or soy sauce/lemon, set it by PIP, let the raw-bean smell dissipate, and panfry before adding it to a curry. This preserves the book's intended division between neutral base, flavored protein, and final sauce.

## Anti-patterns

- **Blend cashew cream once and stop early**: staged resting and blending are what make it silky.
- **Use a metal pan for chickpea tofu**: it heats too quickly and can congeal the flour at the bottom.
- **Overwork mashed potatoes**: starch turns the texture gluey.
- **Add all sauce liquid before flavor reduction**: extra water weakens the pressure-roasting effect.

## Key Takeaways

1. Keep cashew cream and spice blends ready for modular cooking.
2. Use Sauté for blooming, deglazing, and reducing, not merely for browning.
3. Finish sauces with cream, acid, and fresh herbs after pressure when the recipe calls for it.
4. Use Chickpea Flour Tofu as a seasoned, sliceable soy-free protein rather than a bland emergency swap.
