# Chapter 2: Instant Pot

## Core Idea

The chapter starts with safe orientation: read the manual, begin with the Pressure Cook/Manual control, and perform a five-minute water test with three cups of water, a correctly installed sealing ring, the knob on Sealing, and natural release. Model and size affect capacity, liquid evaporation, insert fit, and final consistency more than they usually affect the stated pressure-cook time. The book's general minimum-liquid rule is about ¾ cup for a 3-quart pot, 1 cup for a 6-quart, and 1½ cups for an 8-quart, while a 6-quart cooker may seal with as little as ½ cup; the recipe remains the authority. Natural, quick, and combination release are not interchangeable: release determines whether beans finish gently, vegetables stay intact, or a liquid splatters. The float valve—not the display's elapsed-time “L”—decides when the lid is safe to open. PIP requires a trivet and a heatproof insert that fits with clearance, while ordinary dishes should stay below two-thirds full and expanding beans below half full. Altitude, ingredient age, soaking, acidity, density, and liquid all change doneness, so the charts are ranges and starting points rather than universal guarantees.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Water-test onboarding**: install the ring, add 3 cups water, close the lid, set Sealing, pressure cook 5 minutes, and let the pressure release naturally before opening.
- **Release selection**: natural for beans, soups, grains, and splatter-prone liquid; quick for delicate vegetables and fast ingredients; combination when a recipe needs a short rest plus a controlled vent.
- **Size/liquid adjustment**: keep cook time tied to the food's density, but adjust minimum liquid and final reduction for the pot size; a larger pot can evaporate more liquid while reaching pressure.
- **High-altitude correction**: after 2,000 feet, increase pressure time by 5% per additional 1,000 feet (the book's table runs from 5% at 3,000 feet to 50% at 12,000 feet).
- **Capacity guardrail**: stop at two-thirds full for ordinary foods and half full for foods that expand substantially, especially beans.

## Key Concepts

- **Natural pressure release**: pressure falls on its own until the float valve drops.
- **Quick pressure release**: vent remaining pressure manually after cooking; hot steam exits forcefully.
- **Combination release**: allow a specified natural interval, then vent the rest.
- **Burn signal**: commonly indicates an unsealed knob/ring, insufficient liquid, or browned food left stuck to the bottom.
- **Deglaze**: loosen sautéed or browned bits with liquid before pressure cooking.
- **Pressure Cook/Manual**: the general programmable pressure function; older models call it Manual.
- **PIP**: a second heatproof dish on a trivet, above water or another dish.
- **Float valve**: the round metal indicator that must fall before opening.
- **Sauté**: the function for aromatics, tempering, reducing, and final thickening.
- **Yogurt/Steam**: fermentation and open-vent steaming functions used repeatedly later in the book.

## Mental Models

- Think of **cook time and finished time** as different clocks: preheating and release belong in meal planning.
- Think of **liquid as both engine and texture control**: too little prevents sealing, too much dilutes or delays, and thick mixtures scorch.
- Think of **release as an ingredient decision**: it changes the final texture as surely as salt or heat.

## Worked Example

For a first-use water test, place three cups of water in the inner pot, verify the sealing ring, lock the lid, set the knob to Sealing, and choose Manual/Pressure Cook for five minutes. The display first shows `On`, then counts down only after pressure is reached. When the cycle ends, leave the pot alone until the float valve drops; only then open the lid and confirm that the water is hot. This tests the seal and the user's release routine without risking a full recipe.

## Anti-patterns

- **Force the lid**: pressure can remain even when the program has ended; wait for the float valve.
- **Cook without the inner pot or sealing ring**: the cooker cannot function safely or reach pressure.
- **Close the lid over stuck browned food**: deglaze or a Burn signal is likely.
- **Use a tall liquid-filled pot for quick release**: hot food can spray through the valve.
- **Assume a doubled recipe needs doubled time**: the book says time usually stays the same, but volume, liquid, and insert fit still require checking.

## Key Takeaways

1. Start with the manual and water test.
2. Account for heat-up and release time, not just the programmed number.
3. Use the recipe's stated liquid and release first; adapt only with the documented constraints.
4. Remove the inner pot when residual heat would continue cooking rice, pasta, or a thick sauce.
5. Treat charts as a calibration aid because food and cooker variability are real.
