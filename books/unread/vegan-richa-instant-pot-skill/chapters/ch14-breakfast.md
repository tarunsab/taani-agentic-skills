# Chapter 14: Breakfast

## Core Idea

Breakfast is where the book most clearly separates savory, sweet, and beverage workflows. Steel-cut oats, chai, golden milk, and compote use the cooker for hands-free, scalable morning preparation, while upma, scrambled chickpea flour, frittata, and sandwich fillings use PIP or steaming to avoid skillet sticking. Chickpea flour and kala namak provide egg-like texture/flavor without eggs, but the texture continues to set as it cools and often improves with a pan sear. Sweet breakfasts depend on controlled water and post-cook thickening: oats can sit, compote can cool or take chia, and steamed pancakes need room to rise. The section is practical about make-ahead storage, freezing, spice freshness, and the limits of cooking batter or dairy-free milk under pressure.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Steam/pressure for set texture**: use a covered glass, ceramic, or stainless insert for chickpea flour eggs, frittata, pancakes, or compote while water remains in the main pot.
- **Cool-to-set**: scrambled eggs, compote, sauces, and oats often firm or thicken after the pressure phase; do not overcook just because they look loose immediately.
- **Breakfast modularity**: make a protein/egg layer, smoky mushroom layer, sauce, bread, and toppings separately, then assemble or panfry at service.
- **Hands-free scaling**: chai and golden milk can be doubled or frozen, while pancake jars and loaf-like batters can be portioned into smaller containers.
- **Flavor-safe finishing**: add kala namak, lemon, fruit, syrup, herbs, or hot toppings after cooking so delicate high notes remain visible.

## Recipe Cards

### Turmeric Steel-Cut Oats

**Yield:** 2 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Toast steel-cut oats on Sauté for about two minutes, then add water, nondairy milk, turmeric, cinnamon, cardamom, salt, and maple syrup. High pressure for 10 minutes with natural release gives a creamier, less gooey texture than old-fashioned oats. Let stand 10–15 minutes or use a short extra cook if liquid remains, then serve warm or chilled with fruit, chia, pumpkin seeds, and dried fruit. Chai masala, saffron/turmeric, vanilla, pumpkin spice, or molasses/gingerbread spice provide flavor changes, and the water range controls thickness.

### Chai

**Yield:** 2 large or 4 Indian-size servings; soy-free, nut-free option, gluten-free. Combine nondairy milk, water, chai masala, sugar, loose tea or tea bags, and sliced/grated ginger, ideally in a spice bag or infuser. High pressure for 3 minutes (4 for stronger tea), followed by 2 minutes natural release and short-burst venting, gives the long boil associated with Indian chai without standing over a saucepan. Remove tea/ginger or strain, then serve hot; the batch can be doubled, tripled, quadrupled, frozen, and reheated. A single-batch masala uses cardamom with pinches of cinnamon, clove, and black pepper, or opened cardamom pods with whole clove and cinnamon.

### Haldi Doodh (Spiced Golden Milk)

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free option, gluten-free. Pressure-cook nondairy milk with turmeric, cinnamon, ginger, cardamom, black pepper, and sugar for 5 minutes at high pressure, then release naturally for 5 minutes and vent. Strain before serving and adjust sweetness or spice while warm. Fresh turmeric can be bitter, so the book recommends starting with two or three slices; good-quality ground turmeric avoids stale or rancid flavor. Vanilla, pumpkin spice, or culinary rose water make optional variations, and a spice bag prevents a second straining step.

### Upma

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free option. Dry-roast coarse semolina for 3–4 minutes, then temper mustard, curry leaves, and cashews/peanuts before adding onion, chile, ginger, carrot, peas, and the toasted grain. Use 3 cups water for semolina or 2¼ cups for broken angel hair/vermicelli; high pressure for 2 minutes with 5 minutes natural release, then fluff and rest semolina covered for five minutes. Lemon and cilantro provide the bright finish. Gluten-free pasta replaces semolina, while nuts can be omitted; the distinction between cereal-like semolina and pasta changes the liquid and resting treatment.

### Scrambled Eggs

**Yield:** 2 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Blend chickpea flour/besan, salt, garlic powder, turmeric, and water until smooth, pour into a lightly covered glass/ceramic/stoneware dish over 1½ cups water, and high-pressure cook for 15 minutes. Naturally release for 5 minutes, then whisk the set slab with kala namak, black pepper, and optional vegan butter; cooling changes it toward a scrambled-egg texture and makes it firmer. Serve with buttered toast, avocado, tomatoes, or Eggy Sauce, and fold in sautéed vegetables after cooking if desired. The PIP dish avoids the skillet’s sticky/goopy behavior, but the texture is not final until it cools.

### Breakfast Sandwich

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free option. Put smoky mushrooms, vinegar, soy/tamari, maple, optional liquid smoke, paprika, pepper, and salt in the main pot, then steam a covered chickpea-flour frittata batter with yogurt/coconut cream, green onion, bell pepper, and leavening in a greased insert above it. High pressure for 23 minutes with natural release cooks both layers; cool the frittata, slice and panfry the edges, and make Eggy Sauce by whisking butter, water, nutritional yeast, turmeric, flour, kala namak, and onion powder to a brief boil. Toast/butter bread or English muffins, layer sauce, crisp frittata, mushrooms, and more sauce, and serve; the frittata also works in tacos, burritos, or with hash browns. Cornstarch/potato starch plus rice flour make the sauce gluten-free, while extra vegetables, greens, nutritional yeast, or cheese can change the frittata.

### Mason Jar Pancakes

**Yield:** 4 servings; nut-free, gluten-free options. Whisk nondairy milk, sugar, vinegar, oil, applesauce/yogurt, and vanilla; combine flour, almond flour, baking powder, baking soda, and salt, then divide the batter into plain, cocoa-water, and warmed peanut-butter portions. Layer the batters in three oiled pint jars or heatproof cups only two-thirds full, top with chocolate chips/berries/nuts, cover lightly, and steam over 2½ cups water for 33 minutes with the vent open; because the timer will not count down, use an external timer. Cool briefly, remove safely, and serve with maple syrup and fruit. Omit almond flour or use more all-purpose flour for nut-free, sunflower butter for peanut-free, and the book also gives a steamed-cake adaptation with 32–37-minute times and a slightly rubbery top to trim.

### Breakfast Sweet Potatoes with Blueberry Compote

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Prick sweet potatoes and place them on a trivet over 1½ cups water, while blueberries, lime juice, sugar, salt, optional cinnamon, and optional chia go in a covered stainless/glass insert above. High pressure with natural release takes 14–22 minutes based on potato width: about 14 for 2-inch potatoes, 16–18 for 2½–3-inch, and up to 22 for larger ones. Mash a few berries after opening and let the compote cool; add chia only if it remains thin, then split and lightly mash the warm potatoes in their skins. Top with compote, yogurt or whipped coconut cream, granola, seeds, or nuts; raspberries, blackberries, strawberries, pineapple, or mixed fruit substitute for blueberries.

## Worked Example

Build the Breakfast Sandwich as a modular prep rather than a single recipe. Steam the frittata and mushrooms together, make the Eggy Sauce close to serving because it thickens rapidly as it cools, crisp the frittata slices in a skillet, and toast the bread only at the end. The same logic applies to a sweet breakfast: pressure-cook the potato and fruit component, then cool, thicken, and add toppings after opening. When a batter or flour mixture looks loose, first ask whether the recipe expects cooling or a brief finishing boil before adding pressure time. Breakfast is therefore a showcase for “set, cool, finish, assemble,” not simply for shortening a conventional stovetop recipe.

## Anti-patterns

- **Judge chickpea eggs, oats, or compote at the instant the lid opens**: cooling is part of the setting process.
- **Fill pancake jars to the top**: the batter rises and needs headspace.
- **Put tea leaves loose in chai without a straining plan**: use an infuser or spice bag.
- **Use a metal pan for every steamed breakfast**: material and covering affect bottom heat and texture.
- **Make Eggy Sauce far ahead without loosening it**: it thickens substantially; reheat with a spoonful of water if needed.

## Key Takeaways

1. PIP and steam turn chickpea flour, batter, and fruit into set breakfast components without skillet sticking.
2. Cooling, resting, and post-cook thickening are intentional steps, not signs of a failed recipe.
3. Breakfast sandwiches and bowls are modular: protein/egg, vegetables, sauce, carrier, and fresh topping can be prepared separately.
4. Chai and golden milk are scalable drinks whose spice/tea strength should be tuned to the ingredients.
5. Headspace, container material, and safe retrieval matter for jars, pans, and hot liquids.
