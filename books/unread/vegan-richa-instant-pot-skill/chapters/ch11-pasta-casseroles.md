# Chapter 11: Pasta & Casseroles

## Core Idea

The Instant Pot can make pasta quickly, but pasta is less forgiving than soup because a low-liquid mixture, thick tomato sauce, or uneven heating can trigger Burn and because the noodles continue cooking on the hot base after pressure ends. Hingle gives two main solutions: cook pasta directly in a measured broth/sauce, or use PIP for a lasagna/casserole when the pot is sensitive or the noodles need more protection. Timing is deliberately expressed as about half the package time plus one minute, then adjusted for the brand and the cooker. Creamy sauces are often blended separately and added after pressure, which protects cashews, tofu, cauliflower, and nutritional yeast from scorching and keeps the noodles from absorbing all the liquid. The chapter’s recipe content ranges from a reusable marinara and lentil Bolognese to one-pot mac and cheese, Alfredo, risotto, lasagna, enchilada pie, and mushroom casseroles.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Half-package-time heuristic**: start around half the stovetop time plus one minute, then calibrate to noodle brand, shape, and the particular pot.
- **Sauce-after-pasta**: cook noodles in flavorful liquid, then add blended tofu/cashew/cauliflower cream and briefly boil to thicken.
- **PIP casserole protection**: layer lasagna or enchiladas in a covered heatproof pan above water, pressure-cook, and broil afterward for browning.
- **Staged component cooking**: make a sauce or mushroom base, reserve it, steam cream ingredients above pasta, and reassemble after pressure.
- **Burn recovery**: change pot/insert, deglaze, add liquid, allow a hot pot to cool, or move the mixture to a covered PIP pan rather than repeatedly forcing a low-liquid direct cook.

## Recipe Cards

### Mac & Cheese

**Yield:** 6 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free options. Blend raw cashews, nutritional yeast, flour, tapioca/potato starch, garlic/onion powders, mustard, sugar, salt, paprika, and pepper into a powder that can be stored for quick meals. Pressure-cook elbow pasta with 2¼ cups water, vegan butter/oil, salt, garlic powder, and optional yellow miso at high pressure for 4 minutes, or half the package time plus one minute; quick-release. Stir in about one cup of the powder and ½ cup nondairy milk, then bring to a boil on Sauté, adding more powder for body. Pumpkin seeds plus flour or Vegan Queso provide a nut-free sauce, while oat/rice flour and gluten-free pasta cover gluten-free cooking; chipotle, Cajun, garam masala, and jerk variations alter the mix.

### Lasagna Soup

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free option. Sauté onion, garlic, fennel, peppers, carrot, zucchini, and split red lentils, then add tomato puree/diced tomatoes, Italian herbs, onion/garlic powder, optional tomato paste, broken no-boil lasagna sheets, stock, pepper, and red pepper. High pressure for 3 minutes with 10 minutes natural release cooks the deconstructed lasagna; separate any stuck noodles after opening. Stir in nutritional yeast, lemon, spinach, pesto/basil, and vegan ricotta or mozzarella, adding stock if the soup is too thick. Gluten-free pasta works directly, while extra vegetables require more stock; the garnishes are part of the lasagna effect rather than optional decoration.

### Green Chile Enchilada Casserole

**Yield:** 6 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free option. Sauté onion, garlic, and jalapeño, blend most of the mixture with canned green chiles, stock, cilantro, cumin, pepper, and salt, then season shredded jackfruit or sliced mushrooms with Enchilada Seasoning/Taco Seasoning. Pressure-cook the jackfruit mixture for 8 minutes (mushrooms need only a small amount of water), then layer sauce, tortillas, jackfruit, vegan cheese, and raw/roasted red and green peppers in an oiled 6½-inch heatproof pan. Cover with foil, place over 2 cups water on a trivet, and pressure-cook the assembled casserole for 20 minutes before quick release and a 2–4-minute broil. Corn tortillas make it gluten-free, black beans add protein after opening, and a conventional 350°F/175°C bake is the fallback when PIP is more convenient.

### Spinach Artichoke Pasta

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free and gluten-free options. Sauté onion and garlic, blend them with tofu, nondairy milk, flour, onion powder, mustard, basil, salt, nutritional yeast, lemon, and miso, and reserve this sauce. Pressure-cook pasta with chopped spinach, artichokes, water, garlic powder, and salt for 5 minutes or half the package time plus one, using short-burst quick release. Add the blended sauce and use Sauté for 2–4 minutes to thicken, then top with toasted breadcrumbs and optional vegan Parmesan. Cashews replace tofu for soy-free, and cornstarch/potato starch plus gluten-free pasta and crumbs cover gluten-free cooking; the topping supplies the toasted contrast the sealed pot cannot.

### Arroz con Pollo (Vegan Rice with Soy Curls)

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free and gluten-free options. Soak and squeeze soy curls, season them with poultry seasoning, garlic powder, and salt, then sauté onion, garlic, red/green peppers, tomato, sweet/smoked paprika, oregano, onion powder, black pepper, and red pepper flakes. Add white or brown rice, tomato paste, stock/water, optional saffron water, and the seasoned curls; high pressure takes 10 minutes for white rice or 22 for brown, with natural release. Fold in peas after opening, loosen with water if dry, and serve with fresh/pickled jalapeño and hot sauce. Seitan or another chicken substitute replaces soy curls for soy-free, but seitan is not gluten-free; the rice choice, not the curl, sets the main timing.

### Marinara Sauce

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Sauté garlic and onion until golden, bloom Italian seasoning, garlic powder, black pepper, and red pepper, then add diced tomatoes, water, tomato paste, sugar, basil, optional red wine, bay leaves, and salt. High pressure for 11 minutes with natural release creates a simple sauce that can be left chunky or blended with an immersion blender. Use it for pasta, lasagna, Bolognese, or any recipe that needs a tomato base. Because it is a reusable basic rather than a finished protein dish, its value is modularity: make it ahead, adjust thickness after opening, and pair it with later cream, lentils, tofu, or vegetables.

### Quick Lasagna

**Yield:** 4–6 servings; nut-free and gluten-free options. Blend pressed tofu with olive oil, nutritional yeast, lemon, optional almond flour, oregano, garlic, salt, pepper, and basil into a zesty ricotta, and toss mushrooms/spinach with salt, balsamic, garlic, and basil/Italian seasoning. Layer sauce, pliable/no-boil noodles, ricotta, vegetable filling, and more sauce/ricotta in a heatproof pan, cover with foil, and PIP over 2 cups water. High pressure for 21 minutes with 5 minutes natural release sets the lasagna, which can then be broiled for two minutes. Gluten-free noodles work, Lentil Bolognese can replace marinara, and a Bolognese/Alfredo version alternates the two sauces while steaming Alfredo ingredients alongside the sauce.

### Mushroom Stroganoff

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free options. Sauté onion, garlic, and mushrooms until golden, then add soy sauce, vegan Worcestershire, Dijon, thyme, and white wine before adding stock and dry pasta. High pressure for 5–7 minutes, depending on the pasta or half package time plus one, followed by 2 minutes natural release, cooks the noodles in the savory liquid. Fold in cashew butter, coconut cream, vegan sour cream, or blended silken tofu with lemon, then season with pepper and finish with Parmesan and parsley. Double the recipe with 4¾ cups stock rather than 5; use soy-free Worcestershire, gluten-free pasta, or tofu/coconut cream according to the dietary route.

### Lentil Bolognese

**Yield:** 6 servings; soy-free, gluten-free, with a nut-free option. Sauté onion, garlic, mushrooms, carrot, walnuts, optional fennel, oregano, basil, thyme/sage, onion powder, salt, and nutritional yeast, then add split red lentils, marinara/tomato/pizza sauce, tomato paste, water, sugar, pepper, and red pepper. High pressure for 11 minutes with tomato/pizza sauce or 12 with marinara, followed by 5 minutes natural release, creates a thick lentil sauce. Serve over pasta, baked potatoes, or mashed vegetables, and freeze individual portions for later meals. Coarsely chopped pumpkin and sunflower seeds replace walnuts; scraping browned bits before sealing is essential because the sauce is thick.

### Cauliflower Alfredo

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free options. Sauté onion, garlic, optional mushrooms, wine, and Worcestershire/soy sauce, add stock, lemon zest, bay, thyme/basil, and broken fettuccine, then place cauliflower, zucchini, and cashews in a steamer above the noodles. High pressure for 6 minutes or one minute beyond half the pasta package time, followed by 2 minutes natural release and venting, cooks both layers. Blend the steamed vegetables and cashews with onion/garlic powders, lemon, salt, nutritional yeast, and water, add the puree to the noodles, and boil only until evenly hot; remove the pot promptly. Hemp/pumpkin seeds or silken tofu replace cashews, while a Cashew Alfredo variation uses cashews instead of cauliflower/zucchini and gluten-free pasta handles the grain restriction.

### Mushroom Risotto

**Yield:** 2 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Sauté onion, garlic, and mushrooms, deglaze with wine, season with thyme, tarragon/rosemary, poultry seasoning, and pepper, then toast arborio rice in oil before adding stock. High pressure for 6 minutes and quick release replace the stovetop’s constant stirring. Mix in spinach, vegan butter, and Parmesan after opening and rest only a minute before serving so the risotto remains creamy rather than compacting. Dried mushroom powder, miso, bay, hearty greens, cream, and roasted vegetables are supported variations; the recipe’s defining distinction is that rice is toasted before the measured liquid enters.

### Creamy Sun-Dried Tomato Pasta

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free options. Blend soaked cashews, nondairy milk, salt, garlic/onion powders, oregano, thyme, oil, nutritional yeast, and sun-dried tomatoes into a cream. Cook farfalle with dry soy curls, garlic, pepper, smoked paprika, poultry seasoning, salt, and water at high pressure for 6 minutes or one beyond half the package time, using one minute natural release and short-burst venting. Add the blended sauce on Sauté for 2–3 minutes until thick, then transfer out and top with bread crumbs, vegan cheese, thyme, or basil. Seitan/mushrooms replace soy curls, tofu-based Spinach Artichoke sauce replaces cashews, and gluten-free pasta/crumbs preserve the dietary option.

### Ziti with Tomato Sauce

**Yield:** 4 servings; gluten-free option. Sauté garlic, add tomato/marinara/pizza sauce, Italian seasoning, red pepper, and salt, then submerge ziti with 1¼ cups water and press the pasta into the sauce. High pressure for 4–7 minutes or one minute beyond half the package time, followed by 4 minutes natural release and venting, cooks the direct-to-pot pasta. Add white pepper, olive oil, vegan Parmesan, mozzarella, and herbs after opening, then remove the inner pot because the hot base will keep cooking the noodles. Thick sauce may need 1½ cups water; cashew cream makes a tomato cream version, and the companion Tofu Mozzarella Cream below turns the ziti into baked ziti under a brief broil.

#### Tofu Mozzarella Cream

Blend tofu, olive oil, garlic powder, miso or nutritional yeast, salt, onion powder, lemon, flour/cornstarch, and enough water until thick and smooth. Pour over cooked ziti and broil for 5–7 minutes, or use as a creamy cheese layer in another casserole. The gluten-free version uses cornstarch, and the recipe is a finishing component rather than a pressure-cooked sauce. Its role is to create a browned, tangy surface after the Instant Pot supplies the pasta’s tender base.

### Mushroom Tetrazzini

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free options. Sauté mushrooms and onion until golden, deglaze with wine, remove them, then bring stock, vegan butter/oil, garlic/onion powders, parsley/Italian seasoning, pepper, salt, and optional soy curls to a boil. Add broken spaghetti, cover with the remaining stock, and pressure cook at high for 4 minutes or one minute beyond half the package time; release naturally for 3–4 minutes. Add cashew cream, simmer just to combine, fold in half the mushrooms, and transfer from the hot pot before folding in the rest. Silken tofu replaces cashew cream, gluten-free pasta/crumbs cover the restriction, and a flour roux can replace the cream pathway; toasted crumbs, Parmesan, herbs, and red pepper finish the casserole-like bowl.

## Worked Example

Start with the simplest direct pasta, Ziti with Tomato Sauce, and treat the package time as a calibration input rather than a law. If the pasta sticks, the sauce is unusually thick, or Burn appears, stop relying on a direct low-liquid cook: add stock, deglaze, let the pot cool, use a new/ceramic insert, or move the dish to a foil-covered PIP pan. For a richer dish, cook the noodles and protein in measured liquid while a separate tofu, cashew, cauliflower, or zucchini component steams above them, then blend and add it after pressure. For lasagna or enchiladas, assemble layers in a covered pan, pressure-cook over water, and broil at the end for the browned top that pressure cannot create.

## Anti-patterns

- **Use half package time without checking the pasta shape/brand**: it is a starting point, not a guarantee.
- **Put thick tomato, flour, cashew, or nut-butter mixtures directly against the hot base**: thin/deglaze or stage the cream for after pressure.
- **Leave pasta in Keep Warm**: the hot bottom plate keeps cooking noodles that are already done.
- **Expect pressure to brown cheese or casserole tops**: use a broiler or conventional oven after PIP.
- **Ignore a Burn signal on a low-liquid pasta**: cool, dilute, change insert, or switch to PIP rather than repeating the same setup.

## Key Takeaways

1. Direct pasta succeeds through measured liquid, submerged noodles, package-time calibration, and prompt transfer.
2. Blended cream components are usually safer and tastier after pressure.
3. PIP protects layered casseroles and provides a reliable fallback for sensitive pots.
4. Marinara and Lentil Bolognese turn one cooking session into several future pasta or casserole meals.
5. A broiler supplies the texture pressure cooking cannot: browned cheese, crumbs, and edges.
