# Chapter 15: Desserts

## Core Idea

The dessert section’s central technical claim is that vegan cakes and many baked goods should be steamed rather than pressure-cooked. With the vent open, boiling water supplies steady steam without the building pressure pressing down on a rising batter; a covered pan and parchment reduce condensation and gumminess. Pressure cooking remains useful for rice pudding, custards, fruit, and dense fillings, but cakes, brownies, pancakes, and rolls often use the Steam setting with an external timer because an open vent may prevent the appliance timer from counting down. Material and cooling are part of texture: glass/stoneware give fudgier brownies or gentler cake heat, metal can brown/crust more, and cakes/set custards need complete chilling before slicing. Across the twelve preparations, sweetness, creaminess, and structure come from cashews, tofu, chickpea flour, starch, coconut cream, flax/chia, fruit, and post-cook frosting rather than from eggs or dairy.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Steam instead of pressure for rise**: leave the vent open for cake-like batters, use a covered pan, and time externally when necessary.
- **Condensation control**: parchment or foil over the batter limits water droplets; removing the cover and trimming a steamed top are normal finishing steps.
- **Set-by-cooling**: brownies, flan, cheesecake, halwa, oats, and compotes continue to firm after cooking, so chill/rest before judging.
- **Material-driven texture**: choose glass/stoneware, stainless steel, or ceramic based on whether the desired result is fudgy, crusty, even, or protected from bottom heat.
- **Separate base and finish**: pressure/steam the main dessert, then add cream, frosting, glaze, broiled sugar, fruit, or chocolate after it has cooled enough to hold.

## Recipe Cards

### Rice Pudding (Punjabi Kheer)

**Yield:** 2 servings; soy-free, nut-free option, gluten-free. Wash white or brown basmati very well, pressure-cook it with water, sugar, and cardamom while cashews steam in a basket or heatproof bowl above. High pressure takes 19 minutes for white rice or 38 for brown, with natural release; blend the softened cashews with water and return the cream to the rice. Use Sauté briefly to boil and thicken—about one minute for white rice or 5–7 minutes for brown—and remove the inner pot so the bottom does not scorch. Serve warm or chilled with raisins, toasted nuts, cardamom, saffron, rose petals, chiroli, or slivered almonds; coconut cream is the nut-free post-cook substitute, and the recipe doubles without changing cook time.

### Brownies

**Yield:** 6 servings; soy-free, gluten-free, nut-free option. Make a flax slurry, then mix maple syrup, vanilla, sugar, almond/oat flour, cocoa, optional tapioca, baking soda, salt, almond/cashew butter, hot water, and chopped vegan chocolate; pour into a parchment-lined glass, ceramic, stoneware, or parchment-protected steel pan. Steam at high pressure with the vent open for 30 minutes (28 for steel), wait two minutes, and cool in the pot briefly before removing. Cool about 15 minutes for the intended fudgy pudding-like center or refrigerate at least an hour for firmer slices. Pan material is a true texture control—metal is crustier, glass/stoneware fudgier—and sunflower/soy butter plus oat or seed flour gives the nut-free version.

### Flan

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free option, gluten-free. Make a coconut/dark-sugar caramel with water and salt in a small pan, divide it among ramekins, and pour over a blended custard of soaked cashews, water, chickpea flour, sugar, starch, baking soda, salt, and vanilla. Pressure-cook the ramekins over 1½ cups water at high pressure for 25 minutes with natural release, then cool completely and refrigerate for at least two hours. Loosen the chilled custards with a small knife and invert them onto plates; vegan vanilla ice cream is optional. Skip caramel and torch sugar for crème brûlée, or replace cashews with silken/firm tofu plus coconut cream for a nut-free custard.

### Peach & Wild Blueberry Cobbler

**Yield:** 6 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free option. Mix a stiff biscuit batter from flour, almond/all-purpose flour, sugar, baking powder, salt, oil, and nondairy milk, then spread dollops over melted vegan butter in a ceramic/stoneware pan before adding peaches and blueberries. Sprinkle fruit with coconut/brown sugar, cinnamon, optional cardamom, and salt, cover, and steam with the vent open for 15 minutes. Switch to high-pressure cooking for 20 minutes and naturally release for at least 15, then cool, add remaining sugar, and broil 2–3 minutes until bubbling and golden. The batter sits below the fruit to avoid a rubbery texture; the gluten-free version uses almond flour, potato starch, and gluten-free oat flour, and other seasonal fruit can replace the named berries.

### Cinnamon Rolls

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free. Activate yeast in hot water with nondairy yogurt/coconut cream/aquafaba, sugar, and maple, knead in oil, flour, and salt, rest 30 minutes, roll, fill with oil, brown sugar, cinnamon, and salt, slice, and proof in a pan. Steam the covered pan at high pressure with the vent open for 32 minutes, using an external timer, then cool briefly and glaze after at least 15 minutes. The top is intentionally steamed-bun sticky; broil before glazing to evaporate moisture and crisp the surface. Make-ahead dough can be refrigerated after shaping, and fillings can include maple pecan, blueberries, apples, or strawberries; the source warns that broiling and glazing must be ordered so the glaze does not burn.

### Almond Halwa or Ladoos

**Yield:** 4 Indian-size servings or 12-plus balls; soy-free, gluten-free. Mix hot water, sugar/jaggery, lime, salt, cardamom/saffron/cinnamon/rose flavoring, and almond flour in a covered glass dish over 1½ cups water, then high-pressure cook for 5 minutes with natural release. Use three tablespoons water for crumbly halwa or ladoos, one-third cup for thick spoon fudge, and ½ cup for pudding-like halwa. Serve the warm halwa with nuts and dates/raisins, or chill 30 minutes, roll one-inch balls, and coat them in coconut, nuts, or seeds. Macadamia/cashew/pistachio flour, coconut-almond ladoos, vegan butter, and freezer-friendly ladoos are supported variations; the water ratio—not extra pressure time—controls formability.

### Gajar Halwa (Indian Carrot Halwa)

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free option, gluten-free. Sauté cashews in vegan butter/oil, add dates/raisins, then cook shredded carrots with sugar, salt, almond flour, and nondairy milk before high-pressure cooking for 7 minutes with natural release. Add cardamom, then use Sauté for 4–6 minutes to dry and caramelize the mixture, stirring frequently; optional vegan butter enriches the finish. Rest about 15 minutes, stir once, serve warm or cool, and garnish with pistachios/cashews or vegan ice cream. Sunflower seed meal replaces almond flour for nut-free, carrots can be mixed with beets, and nutritional yeast provides a mava/milk-solids note; dry carrots may need more milk.

### Oatmeal Banana Bars

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free option. Mash a ripe banana, mix in nondairy milk and maple syrup, then combine with oats, baking powder, cinnamon/pumpkin spice, salt, pecans, coconut, optional dates/raisins, and chia seeds. Spread in an oiled glass/ceramic/stoneware pan, cover with foil, and high-pressure cook over 1½ cups water for 35 minutes with natural release. Cool 15 minutes before inverting, slice, and top with pecans and cinnamon; refrigerate up to five days. Omit pecans for nut-free, and use rice or quinoa flakes for oat-free; the bars are intentionally firmer than porridge.

### Chocolate Layer Cake

**Yield:** two 6-inch cakes, 10 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free options. Mix nondairy milk, oil, applesauce/yogurt, sugar, vinegar, vanilla, flour, almond flour, baking powder, baking soda, salt, and cocoa until a batter with slight resistance forms. Steam two parchment-lined pans for 32 minutes or one tall pan for 37 with the vent open, then cool completely, remove parchment, and level the slightly rubbery steamed top with a serrated knife. Make frosting by heating coconut cream, coconut milk, coconut oil, sugar, and starch, then melting in chocolate and chilling 30–60 minutes before spreading between layers and on the sides. Parchment reduces condensation/gumminess, preserves can be added between layers, and nut-free/gluten-free routes use more all-purpose flour or a double gluten-free spice batter with cocoa and club soda.

### Carrot Cake

**Yield:** one 6–7-inch cake, 6 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free options. Make a spiced batter from nondairy milk, brown sugar, vinegar, oil, yogurt/applesauce, flour, almond flour, leaveners, cinnamon, nutmeg, clove, cardamom, and allspice, but first sauté grated carrots with brown sugar until lightly caramelized. Fold in cooled carrots, optional raisins/dates and pecans/walnuts, pour into a covered pan, and steam at high pressure with the vent open for 35–38 minutes before cooling and refrigerating. Blend soaked cashews with water, maple, lemon, sugar, cinnamon, vanilla, and salt into frosting, thickening with coconut flour only if needed. Spices meld overnight, sunflower seed meal replaces almond flour, the pecans can be omitted, and a vegan cream-cheese frosting is an alternate finish.

### Gluten-Free Spice Cake

**Yield:** one 6–6½-inch cake, 6 servings; soy-free, gluten-free. Whisk almond flour, potato starch, sugar, leaveners, salt, cinnamon, ginger/clove/nutmeg, milk, oil, vanilla, and freshly opened club soda into a flowy but not runny batter. Steam in a covered glass/ceramic/stoneware pan for 26 minutes with the vent open, cool, invert, and let the sides release before frosting with whipped coconut cream or vegan cream cheese. Carbonation supplies the air that eggs would provide in a conventional gluten-free cake; too much liquid makes the batter weak and too little leaves it dry. Vanilla, cocoa, layer-cake, and half almond/half certified gluten-free oat-flour variants are supported, but gluten-free flours are not direct 1:1 substitutes for wheat flour.

### Strawberry Cheesecake

**Yield:** one 6½–7-inch cake, 8 servings; soy-free, gluten-free. Press a crust of oat flour, almond flour, sugar, salt, maple, and coconut oil into a lined springform pan, then pour over a blended filling of cashews, nondairy yogurt/coconut cream, vegan cream cheese, sugar, starch, vanilla, lemon, and salt. Put a covered strawberry, sugar, and lemon compote pan above the cheesecake on a trivet and pressure-cook the pair for 45 minutes, or cook only the cheesecake for 40; release naturally. Add a cornstarch slurry to a thin compote while hot, cool everything completely, and refrigerate at least four hours before slicing. Other berries work, the compote can be pressure-cooked separately for 3 minutes, and chilling is essential because the cheesecake texture improves as it sets.

## Worked Example

For a steamed cake, first choose the pan, line it to control condensation, and leave the vent open; then set an external timer because the Instant Pot may not count down in this configuration. Cool the cake fully before removing parchment, leveling a steamed top, frosting, or slicing. For a pressure-set dessert, keep the dense mixture away from scorching by using a covered insert and enough main-pot water, then let cooling establish the final body. The same sequence explains why Brownies are judged after chilling, why Flan and Cheesecake need refrigeration, why Halwa’s water ratio controls shape, and why Cinnamon Rolls benefit from a final broil before glaze.

## Anti-patterns

- **Pressure-cook a rising cake with the vent closed**: building pressure can press down the batter and produce a dense result.
- **Fill jars or pans without headspace/covering**: batter rises and condensation can make the top gummy.
- **Cut custard, cheesecake, or brownies while hot**: cooling/chilling is part of setting.
- **Leave rice pudding or thick sauce on the hot inner plate**: residual heat can scorch or over-thicken the dessert.
- **Treat gluten-free flour as a direct wheat substitute**: use the book’s dedicated flour/starch/liquid/air method.

## Key Takeaways

1. Steam is the default dessert mode for rise; pressure is better for rice, custard, fruit, and dense fillings.
2. Vent position, pan material, parchment, and an external timer materially affect texture.
3. Cooling and chilling complete the set for brownies, flan, cheesecake, halwa, oats, and compote.
4. Nut-free and gluten-free substitutions change structure, so use the book’s specific flour, starch, tofu, seed, and cream routes.
5. Broiling, frosting, glazing, and fresh fruit provide the browned or bright finish that steam cannot create.
