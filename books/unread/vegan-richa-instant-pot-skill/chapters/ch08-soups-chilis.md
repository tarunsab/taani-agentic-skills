# Chapter 8: Soups & Chilis

## Core Idea

Soups and chilis are the most forgiving entry point in the book because pressure builds depth while ingredients remain submerged in a liquid that can be corrected after opening. The section moves from light broths to creamy blended soups and long-cooking bean chilis, using the same sequence repeatedly: sauté aromatics when helpful, add stock and solids, pressure-cook, then adjust thickness and brightness. Lentils and rice can thicken a soup without dairy, while cauliflower, cashew cream, coconut milk, or a blended portion create a richer body. Release timing matters less for a purée than for intact vegetables or rice, but beans still need a natural release and a tenderness check. The chapter also shows how an apparently odd flavor combination—mole with cocoa, cinnamon, and molasses—can give a protein-dense chili a deeper, less one-dimensional profile.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Broth-to-body progression**: start with stock/water, then add lentils, rice, potato, cauliflower, blended vegetables, or cream to set the final body.
- **Blend a portion, not the whole pot**: pureeing a cup or two thickens a soup while retaining beans, pasta, or vegetable texture.
- **Pressure depth plus fresh finish**: use the sealed cook for base flavor, then add spinach, lemon/lime, herbs, black salt, or cream after opening.
- **Bean tenderness checkpoint**: soak when possible, allow a natural release, sample a bean, and run a second short cook if needed.
- **Dump-and-done modularity**: long-cooking grains and beans can be jarred or layered with pantry spices, but quick vegetables should be hardy or omitted.

## Recipe Cards

### Everyday Lentil Soup

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Sauté onion, garlic, and optional chile in stock or oil, then add thyme/rosemary, oregano, black pepper, tomato, brown or green lentils, carrot, and 2½ cups stock or water. High pressure takes 13 minutes for brown lentils or 16 for green, with natural release; fold in spinach afterward and brighten with lemon/lime and cayenne. Mash some lentils or blend ½ cup back into the pot for a thicker soup, and serve with red pepper flakes and vegan Parmesan. Red lentils can be added for creaminess, while mung beans or black-eyed peas need about 20 minutes; garam masala, curry powder, Berbere, or Baharat change the profile without changing the basic architecture.

### Cream of Mushroom Soup

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, and gluten-free options. Sauté onion, garlic, and mushrooms with wine, nutritional yeast, soy sauce/Worcestershire or coconut aminos, oregano, thyme, and pepper, then add a little stock and steam cauliflower and cashews above the mushrooms. High pressure for 10 minutes with natural release caramelizes the mushroom base while softening the creamy component. Blend the steamed cauliflower/cashews with about one-third of the mushrooms, the remaining stock, and salt, return the puree to the pot, and boil briefly on Sauté to set consistency. Tofu or pumpkin seeds replace cashews, tamari replaces soy sauce for gluten-free cooking, and truffle oil is an optional finishing accent; more stock fixes an overly thick soup.

### Minestrone

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, with a gluten-free pasta option. Sauté onion and garlic, then season celery, carrot, Italian herbs, red pepper, and black pepper before adding diced tomatoes, stock, canned chickpeas/white beans/kidney beans, and small pasta. High pressure for 3–4 minutes, selected for the pasta, followed by 8 minutes of natural release, gives the pasta time to finish without disintegrating. Stir in vegan Parmesan after opening and serve with basil. The recipe is deliberately flexible about beans, vegetables, and herbs, but gluten-free pasta is the direct substitution when required.

### Chickpea Potato Soup

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Cook onion and garlic in stock, then add tomato, oregano, rosemary or thyme, onion/garlic powder, cinnamon, fennel, potato, carrot, bell pepper/mushroom/zucchini/broccoli, chickpeas, nondairy milk, and water. High pressure for 5 minutes with natural release cooks the potatoes and legumes; fold in spinach and use Sauté for one minute. Lemon and black pepper balance the creamy broth, which can be topped with vegan bacon or Parmesan. Use stable coconut, soy, or certain oat milks because some nondairy milks separate under pressure; stock plus cashew cream is the alternative, and blending one cup makes the soup thicker.

### Black Bean Soup

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Sauté onion and garlic, then bloom chili powder/chipotle, cumin, oregano, smoked paprika, black pepper, and bay leaves before adding tomato, soaked black beans, and 1¾ cups stock or water. High pressure takes 35 minutes for overnight-soaked beans or 40 without that soak, followed by 10 minutes natural release; sample a bean and repeat for 10 minutes if needed. Add lime, blend about two cups of soup, and briefly boil the puree back into the pot to create body. Use one cup liquid instead of 1¾ cups for drier beans suited to taco/burrito fillings, and add cayenne for more heat.

### Butternut Squash Soup

**Yield:** 6 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Poke a whole butternut squash, place it on a trivet over stock, apple, onion, ginger, garlic, thyme, sage, salt, pepper, and nutmeg, and pressure cook at high for 20 minutes with natural release. Split the cooked squash, remove seeds and skin, scoop the flesh into the broth, and blend with an immersion blender or in batches. Stir in coconut milk, cashew cream, or thick nondairy milk on Sauté just until boiling, then adjust with maple syrup, lime, and red pepper. The whole-squash method avoids the dangerous peeling/cubing step; peeled ¾-inch cubes can be used instead with a shorter 12-minute cook.

### Dump & Done Wild Rice Soup

**Yield:** 6 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Add wild rice or a blend, dried mushrooms, soaked or dry chickpeas, onion flakes, garlic powder, poultry seasoning, thyme, rosemary, pepper, stock, hardy vegetables, and salt directly to the pot. High pressure for 42 minutes with natural release cooks the wild rice and chickpeas, so the book warns against delicate vegetables that will overcook during that long window. Stir in cashew cream or another nondairy cream after opening, reduce on Sauté for 2–3 minutes, and loosen with stock if needed. The first nine dry ingredients can be assembled as a jar gift; taco seasoning and salsa make the supported spicier variation, with vegan cream cheese or cashew cream added at the end.

### Chickpea Rice Soup

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free and gluten-free options. Sauté onion, carrot, garlic, celery, poultry seasoning, thyme, oregano, onion powder, and black pepper, then add stock, chickpeas, washed white rice, soy sauce/tamari/coconut aminos, and salt. High pressure for 5 minutes with natural release cooks the rice and produces a light chickpea version of chicken noodle soup. Reduce on Sauté if the broth is too thin and finish with parsley/cilantro, black pepper, and lemon. Brown rice is partially cooked separately before adding, while noodles/pasta or soy curls can replace rice/chickpeas if their timing and dietary constraints are respected.

### Red Lentil Chili

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Sauté onion, garlic, jalapeño, celery, carrot, and bell pepper, then add kidney/black/pinto beans, split red lentils, cumin, coriander, paprika, chili powder, oregano, chipotle/ancho, onion/garlic powders, bay, diced tomatoes, tomato paste, and stock. High pressure for 9 minutes with natural release lets the red lentils supply thickness while the beans retain substance. Stir in lemon and half the cilantro after opening, adjust cayenne and liquid, and serve with avocado, onion, tortilla strips, hot sauce, or vegan sour cream. Cooking the tomatoes briefly before adding beans and lentils gives deeper flavor, while corn is an optional addition.

### Chili sin Carne Mole

**Yield:** 6 servings; soy-free and gluten-free options. Sauté onion, garlic, jalapeño, chili powder, cumin, and cinnamon, then add stock, tomato, bell pepper, seitan/soy curls/TVP, soaked black and kidney beans, brown lentils, cocoa, molasses, and salt. High pressure for 75 minutes with natural release handles the slowest dry beans; after opening, reduce on Sauté or blend a portion if the chili needs more body. The mole combination is the defining technique: cocoa and molasses deepen the chili while cinnamon rounds the heat rather than making it taste like dessert. Omit soy curls/TVP for soy-free, omit seitan for gluten-free, or skip sautéing for an oil-free dump-and-done version; serve with cornbread, chips, avocado, lime, and vegan cheese.

## Worked Example

Use the soup architecture to turn pantry odds and ends into a controlled meal. If the slow ingredient is an intact bean or wild rice, build the liquid and pressure cycle around that ingredient, selecting hardy vegetables or adding greens after opening. If the base is already tender, use a short cycle and make texture with a blended portion, cashew cream, coconut milk, or red lentils. Taste only after the fresh finish—lemon, lime, pepper, herbs, salt, or hot sauce—because the pressure phase mutes some high notes. The same process applies to Everyday Lentil Soup, Black Bean Soup, Red Lentil Chili, and the more elaborate Mole, but their liquid and bean timing should not be casually exchanged.

## Anti-patterns

- **Put delicate vegetables in a 42- or 75-minute cycle**: choose hardy vegetables or add quick greens after pressure.
- **Treat the recipe’s bean time as a guarantee**: bean age and soaking alter tenderness; test and re-cook when necessary.
- **Over-thicken before pressure**: use the stated stock/water, then reduce or blend after opening.
- **Use an unstable nondairy milk under pressure**: separate milk and cream into the post-cook stage when needed.
- **Forget residual heat with rice soup**: natural release and prompt serving prevent soft, swollen grains.

## Key Takeaways

1. Soup is a forgiving medium for learning pressure, release, and post-cook correction.
2. Let lentils, rice, potatoes, cauliflower, or a blended portion create body instead of relying on dairy.
3. Use a natural release for beans and long grains, and treat tenderness as an observable checkpoint.
4. Add greens, acids, herbs, cream, and hot finishes after pressure to restore contrast.
5. A dump-and-done recipe still requires ingredient timing discipline, especially when rice, pasta, or quick vegetables are involved.
