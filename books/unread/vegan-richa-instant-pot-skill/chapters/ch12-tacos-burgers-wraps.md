# Chapter 12: Tacos, Burgers & Wraps

## Core Idea

The handheld-food recipes extend the Instant Pot beyond bowls by separating a moist, pressure-cooked filling from the bread, tortilla, crisp shell, or raw topping that supplies contrast. Lentils, walnuts, mushrooms, jackfruit, tempeh, quinoa, and beans are repeatedly cooked until cohesive, then shaped, broiled, toasted, or spooned into a carrier. PIP allows Spanish rice, Buffalo tempeh, and optional greens to finish together, while other recipes pressure-cook only the filling and use a skillet or oven for the final texture. Flavor can be diluted by the water needed for pressure, so Hingle often divides spices or adds sauce, lime, herbs, glaze, or hot sauce after opening. This chapter is therefore less about a single “meat substitute” and more about building a filling with enough moisture to cook, enough structure to hold, and a fresh or crisp finishing layer.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Filling-plus-carrier architecture**: pressure-cook a flavorful, cohesive filling; add the tortilla, bun, pita, potato, or wrap only at service.
- **Moisture-to-structure conversion**: use lentils/quinoa/beans for bulk, mushrooms or nuts/seeds for texture, and breadcrumbs/oats/starch for shaping after cooling.
- **Spice split and finish**: pressure-cook part of the seasoning, then restore brightness with reserved spices, glaze, salsa, lime, hot sauce, or raw toppings.
- **PIP complete wrap**: cook rice in the main pot, steam sauced tempeh or vegetables in a covered insert, and assemble only after both are ready.
- **Cooling as a binding step**: refrigerate burger or meatloaf mixtures before adding crumbs and shaping so moisture can redistribute and the structure can set.

## Recipe Cards

### Buffalo Sauce Tempeh & Spanish Rice Wraps

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free and gluten-free options. Toast washed long-grain rice with onion and garlic, add tomato paste and spices, then use a chunky puree of tomato, water, and optional jalapeño as the rice liquid. Place a tall trivet over the rice and steam sliced tempeh or seitan in a covered container with hot sauce, sriracha, vegan butter/oil, and water; low pressure for 10 minutes or high for 5, followed by the book’s natural-release/quick-release sequence. Remove and turn the tempeh in its sauce, fluff rice, and assemble warmed tortillas/lavash with bell pepper and raw or steamed kale. Tomato rice can trigger Burn, so scrape, use cold water to cool the pot, and fold in some salsa later; a bowl version omits flatbread, and soy-free tempeh or seitan changes the protein route.

### Mushroom Lentil Walnut Burger

**Yield:** 6 patties; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free options. Toast onion and mushrooms until golden, then add brown lentils, walnuts, stock, half the taco/berbere/garam masala/Cajun spice mix, and salt; high pressure for 10 minutes with natural release cooks the filling. Evaporate any remaining liquid, mix in the reserved spices, lime zest, oats, and breadcrumbs, mash half for cohesion, and chill at least 15 minutes plus another 30 minutes after crumbs are added. Shape six patties and sauté 4–5 minutes per side or bake at 400°F/200°C for about 25 minutes. Pumpkin seeds replace walnuts, oats plus rice flour replace crumbs for gluten-free, and omitting crumbs turns the mixture into a taco/wrap filling rather than a firm burger.

### Baked Potato with Barbecue Lentils

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free and gluten-free. Steam fork-poked russet potatoes on a rack over 1½ cups water while a covered stainless container above them holds onion, mushrooms, carrot, barbecue sauce, water, split red lentils, garlic, Barbecue Spice Blend, optional sugar, and salt. High pressure for 17 minutes with natural release cooks both the potatoes and the lentil filling; stir the filling after opening because it thickens as it sits. Split warm potatoes, brush with vegan butter/oil, fill, and drizzle with sriracha mayonnaise, parsley/cilantro/chives, and red pepper. The filling can also be doubled or tripled and pressure-cooked alone for 10 minutes, then used in sandwiches, lettuce cups, or bowls; sweet potatoes and soy-free barbecue sauce are direct variations.

### Lentil Quinoa Meatloaf

**Yield:** 6 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free options. Sauté onion, garlic, and fennel, then pressure-cook brown lentils, carrot, quinoa, bay leaves, salt, and stock for 9–10 minutes with natural release. Drain excess liquid, discard bay, and mix the cooked grains with crumbs/oats, walnuts or seed meal, ketchup, flax/chia, optional raisins, Worcestershire/soy alternative, thyme, Italian seasoning, and onion powder; mash at least half so the loaf holds. Press into a parchment-lined pan and bake at 375°F/190°C for 30 minutes, glaze with ketchup/barbecue sauce, Worcestershire, sriracha, and maple, then bake 15–20 minutes more and rest 20 minutes. Slices freeze for two months; the mixture can become tacos after 3–5 minutes on Sauté, and molasses/tamarind/vinegar/mustard replace Worcestershire for soy-free cooking.

### Lentil Sloppy Joes

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free and gluten-free options. Sauté onion, pepper, celery, carrot, and garlic in stock/oil, then add tomato, tomato paste, chili/taco/barbecue seasoning, lemon, optional Worcestershire/soy/tamari/coconut aminos, smoked paprika, pepper, stock, green lentils, sugar, and salt. Use Bean/Chili for 22 minutes or high pressure for 16 minutes with natural release; the mixture looks loose at first and thickens as it cools. Reduce briefly on Sauté only if it remains watery, then serve in buns with slaw, in wraps, or over tater tots/baked potatoes. Refrigerate up to four days and freeze up to one month; barbecue sauce and lettuce cups are supported variations.

### Mushroom Gyro with Tzatziki

**Yield:** 4 servings; nut-free, gluten-free option. Pressure-cook thin mushrooms and onion with garlic/garlic powder, water, vegan Worcestershire, Shawarma Spice Blend, maple syrup, and salt for 9 minutes; naturally release, then reduce on Sauté or thicken with cornstarch slurry if watery. Blend tofu with lemon, vinegar, garlic, salt, and pepper for tzatziki, then pulse in cucumber and dill so the cucumber stays distinct. Warm pita, layer mushroom filling with red onion, cucumber, tomato, greens, and tzatziki, and serve immediately. Store filling, toppings, and sauce separately for up to three days; jackfruit, seitan, soy curls, or mushroom-free soy curls substitute, and a bowl makes the gluten-free version.

### Crunch Wrap with Jackfruit & Vegan Queso

**Yield:** 6 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free options. Sauté onion, garlic, jalapeño, and Enchilada/Taco seasoning with shredded jackfruit, add water, and place a covered bowl of sweet potato, zucchini, carrot, cashews, and water on a trivet above it. High pressure for 9 minutes with natural release cooks both components; reduce jackfruit if wet, then blend the cooled vegetables with nutritional yeast, lemon, tapioca, paprika, garlic powder, mustard, salt, and cumin, boiling the queso briefly to thicken. Layer a large tortilla with queso, jackfruit, tostada, avocado, lettuce, tomato, and optional cheese, fold overlapping edges, and toast seam-side down on a skillet. Mushrooms replace jackfruit with only 2 tablespoons water, pumpkin seeds replace cashews, refried beans can be added, and the components also become nachos or a queso mac-and-cheese bake.

### Instant Pot Barbecue Jackfruit

**Yield:** 6 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free option. Drain, rinse, squeeze, shred, and de-seed young jackfruit, brush the pot with oil, and mix the fruit with barbecue seasoning, barbecue sauce, hot sauce, optional water, and optional brown sugar. Pressure cook for 5 minutes with natural release, then taste and add more sauce/sugar before a short Sauté reduction; stir frequently because the glaze can scorch as it caramelizes. Remove the inner pot as soon as the desired sticky consistency appears and serve in buns or tacos with cabbage or Easy Slaw. Mushrooms replace jackfruit without the water, and the slaw is made by tossing shredded cabbage/carrot/Brussels sprouts/kale mix with vegan mayonnaise, vinegar/lemon, maple/sugar, salt, and pepper.

## Worked Example

Design a handheld meal in four layers: a pressure-cooked filling, a binding or thickening adjustment, a carrier, and a cool/crisp contrast. For a burger or meatloaf, let cooked lentils/quinoa/mushrooms cool, mash enough of the mixture to bind, add crumbs or oats, and finish in the oven or skillet. For a wrap, keep the pressure phase moist enough for rice or tempeh, but assemble with raw pepper, kale, slaw, salsa, hot sauce, or tzatziki after opening. For jackfruit or sloppy joes, reduce until the sauce glazes without drying the filling, then remove the pot from the hot element. This layered design explains why the same pressure-cooked base can become a bun, taco, potato, bowl, lettuce cup, or sandwich.

## Anti-patterns

- **Shape a hot, wet lentil mixture immediately**: chill and evaporate first so patties/loaves hold.
- **Expect pressure to crisp a burger, wrap, or casserole**: use a skillet, oven, broiler, or toaster for the final surface.
- **Put all flavor into the pressure phase**: reserve finishing sauce/spices and use fresh acid, herbs, salsa, or hot sauce.
- **Leave barbecue glaze on the hot base**: short Sauté reduction is enough; remove the inner pot before scorching.
- **Pack toppings into make-ahead storage**: keep filling, bread, greens, and sauces separate so texture survives.

## Key Takeaways

1. Pressure cooking makes the filling; skillet, oven, raw toppings, and carriers make the handheld meal.
2. Lentils/quinoa/beans provide bulk, while mushrooms, walnuts/seeds, jackfruit, and spices create texture and identity.
3. Cooling, mashing, crumbs, oats, and starch are structural controls, not afterthoughts.
4. PIP is especially useful when rice and a sauced protein need different textures but one cooking window.
5. Fresh and crisp finishes restore flavor lost to pressure moisture and heat.
