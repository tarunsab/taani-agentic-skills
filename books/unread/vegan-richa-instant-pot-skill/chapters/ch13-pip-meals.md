# Chapter 13: PIP (Pot-in-Pot) Meals

## Core Idea

Pot-in-pot cooking turns one pressure cycle into a coordinated meal: the main pot carries a sauce, stew, or mushroom base while one or two covered inserts steam a mash, grain, cabbage, or creamy component above it. The book distinguishes stainless steel, glass, and stoneware by heat behavior—steel heats quickly, while glass/stoneware can give gentler, more even cooking—and repeatedly uses a tall trivet, long-handled trivet, wire rack, or foil sling for safe retrieval. PIP is not an exclusive appliance technique: if the right basket or insert is unavailable, cook the components separately using the main dish’s same pressure time. This section emphasizes staged assembly, because sauces thicken after opening, mashed vegetables need seasoning after steaming, and delicate toppings may belong in a separate container. The payoff is not merely convenience; it is a way to align different textures without extending every component to the slowest ingredient.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Main-pot anchor**: place the component that needs the most direct liquid or longest pressure time in the inner pot.
- **Covered insert protection**: use foil or a lid to keep steam/water out of rice, mash, grits, or a sauce whose consistency matters.
- **Material matching**: choose stainless steel for general PIP, glass/stoneware when rapid metal heating could overcook the bottom, and a container that leaves clearance around it.
- **Vertical retrieval**: use a tall trivet, wire rack, tongs, or foil sling and silicone mini mitts so hot inserts can be removed without tipping.
- **Post-PIP reassembly**: blend, mash, thicken, season, and plate each component after the pressure cycle rather than mixing everything prematurely.

## Recipe Cards

### Mushroom Bourguignon with Potato-Cauliflower Mash

**Yield:** 2 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Sauté mushrooms, onion, and garlic until some edges brown, deglaze with red wine/brandy/whiskey/stock, then add celery, carrot, thyme, garlic powder, stock, soy/tamari/coconut aminos, tomato paste, and optional bay/rosemary. Put potato and cauliflower in a steamer above the mushrooms, pressure cook at high for 10 minutes, and release naturally. Thicken the bourguignon with cornstarch/arrowroot and wilt spinach on Sauté while coarsely mashing the steamed vegetables with milk, olive oil, herbs, garlic powder, salt, and pepper. Less liquid preserves mushroom roasting, beans can be added to either component, and mashed potato can replace starch; doubling adds two minutes to the pressure time.

### Lentil Mushroom Stew with Mashed Sweet Potatoes

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Sauté onion, garlic, mushrooms, and salt, deglaze with wine/whiskey, then add carrot/celery/zucchini, brown lentils, tomato paste, thyme, sage, onion powder, stock, optional Worcestershire/rosemary, and bay. Steam peeled sweet potatoes or Yukon Gold potatoes in a basket above the stew at high pressure for 14 minutes with natural release. Mash the potatoes with olive oil, garlic powder, salt, nondairy milk, thyme, and pepper, taste both components separately, and serve the stew over the mash with chives/parsley. The same architecture becomes shepherd’s pie after thickening the stew, topping it with mash, and baking at 425°F/220°C; doubling uses slightly less than double liquid.

### Ethiopian Lentils & Ethiopian Spiced Cabbage

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free; requires a 6-quart-or-larger cooker and two inserts. Put red/brown lentils, water, Berbere, paprika, and salt in the main pot; season cabbage, tomato, jalapeño, turmeric, salt, and water in one insert, and coat onion, garlic, ginger, Berbere, coriander, paprika, salt, sugar, and a little water in a second insert. Set the onion insert on a trivet, cabbage insert above it, cover the top with foil without touching the lid, and high-pressure cook for 12 minutes with natural release. Mix a spoonful or two of caramelized onion into the cabbage and the rest into the lentils, then adjust with lemon and serve with injera, rice, or flatbread. Do not put the onion directly into the lentils if caramelization matters; Berbere heat varies, so reduce the hot blend and supplement with mild paprika/coriander when necessary.

### Portobello Mushrooms in Garlic Sauce

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free options, gluten-free. Put cleaned portobello caps gill-side down over water, oil, garlic, balsamic, wine, Worcestershire/soy/tamari, paprika, salt, and pepper, then place cauliflower, cashews, and garlic in a steamer above them. High pressure for 12 minutes with natural release infuses the mushrooms; if they remain firm, add ½ cup water and cook five minutes more. Blend the steamed vegetables, cashews, garlic, water, arrowroot/flour, lemon, olive oil, optional nutritional yeast/wine, and salt, then boil the sauce in a skillet to thicken. Pumpkin seeds or silken tofu replace cashews, and sliced mushrooms over pasta turn the dish into a second meal; the sauce can be made cheesier with miso or nutritional yeast.

### Grits with Mushrooms & Chickpeas

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Combine stock, grits/polenta, optional nutritional yeast, salt, and pepper in a foil-covered stainless or glass insert. Sauté sage and thyme in vegan butter, add chickpeas, mushrooms, stock, wine, salt, and pepper, then place the grits insert on a trivet over the mushroom mixture. High pressure for 15 minutes with natural release cooks both, after which the grits are whisked vigorously and loosened with vegan butter or nondairy milk. Serve the mushroom/chickpea stew over the grits with oregano/basil and optional truffle oil; the covered insert protects the grain while the main pot concentrates the mushroom flavor.

### Scalloped Potatoes

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free options. Blend water, cashews, flour, oil, lemon, mustard, dried herbs, optional miso, garlic/onion powder, and salt into a cream, then layer very thin Yukon Gold slices with salt, pepper, and several spoonfuls of sauce in a greased stoneware or ceramic broiler-safe dish. Place the dish on a trivet over 1½ cups water and high-pressure cook for 45 minutes with natural release; test with a toothpick and add 10 minutes if not tender. Top with vegan cheese, pepper, and red pepper flakes and broil 2–3 minutes, then rest 10 minutes. The source specifically avoids metal because it can overcook the bottom; silken tofu/nondairy milk, cornstarch/potato starch, and nutritional yeast cover the nut-free, gluten-free, and soy-free variants.

## Worked Example

Design a PIP meal by ranking components by required contact with liquid and heat. Put the lentil, mushroom, or stew base in the main pot; put mash, grits, rice, cabbage, or cream ingredients in a covered insert; and select a pressure time that satisfies the anchor without destroying the protected component. After natural release, remove the inserts with a stable trivet, blend or mash them separately, thicken the base, and only then plate the layers. If a container is unavailable, run the main component alone and make the companion on the stovetop—the book treats PIP as a time-saving option, not a prerequisite.

## Anti-patterns

- **Use a short trivet that lets an insert touch the food or lid**: ensure clearance and use a foil sling or tall rack for retrieval.
- **Put delicate mash or rice in an uncovered metal pan by default**: cover inserts and match material to heat sensitivity.
- **Mix the components before checking them separately**: mash, cream, and stew may need different salt, liquid, or thickening corrections.
- **Assume every insert can be grabbed safely**: use silicone mini mitts, tongs, a long-handled trivet, or a sling.
- **Force PIP when the pot size cannot accommodate it**: cook components separately rather than overcrowding the cooker.

## Key Takeaways

1. PIP aligns different cooking times by giving each component its own container and heat exposure.
2. The main pot anchors the longest or most liquid-dependent component; inserts protect grains, mash, greens, and creams.
3. Container material and clearance affect texture and safety.
4. Natural release, careful retrieval, and post-cook reassembly are part of the method.
5. Any PIP recipe can be decomposed into separate stovetop/oven tasks if the equipment is missing.
