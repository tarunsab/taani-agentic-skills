# Chapter 10: Stir-Fries

## Core Idea

These recipes use the Instant Pot for fried-rice, noodle, and thick saucy dishes, even though the final texture is not the same as a wok-fried dish. The most important rice lesson is to rinse and soak first: hydration and starch removal help white rice cook faster and fluffier, while skipping the soak can produce clumping. Sauces are often split, with a portion cooking under pressure and a reserved portion added after opening so peanut, soy, chile, or sweet-and-sour notes stay vivid. Quick vegetables and cooked proteins frequently enter after pressure, while dry chickpeas, soy curls, or longer-grain timing set the sealed cook. The result is a family of adaptable one-pot meals whose “stir-fry” identity comes from sequencing, finishing sauce, and immediate transfer from the hot inner pot.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Soak before rice pressure-cooking**: rinse away surface starch and hydrate the grain before a short sealed cycle.
- **Reserve-and-finish sauce**: cook only part of a concentrated sauce with the grain/protein, then add the rest after opening for gloss and aroma.
- **Texture by stage**: pressure-cook rice/quinoa and sturdy ingredients; fold in peas, greens, crunchy vegetables, tofu egg, or thickener later.
- **Post-cook thickening**: use cornstarch slurry, residual heat, a short Sauté boil, or reserved peanut sauce to set body.
- **Carryover control**: fluff to the bottom and remove the inner pot immediately so rice does not compact and overcook.

## Recipe Cards

### Dump & Done One-Pot Rice or Quinoa & Beans

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Add oil, garlic, onion, thawed frozen vegetables, squeezed spinach, canned beans, salt, curry powder or a spice blend/paste, onion powder, cayenne/pepper, water, and soaked white basmati or rinsed quinoa. Cook at high pressure for 5 minutes with rice or 2 minutes with quinoa, then naturally release for 8 minutes. Fluff, remove the inner pot, and finish with lime, red pepper, and cilantro. Coconut milk can replace half the water, and the vegetable choice can follow the blend—celery with Cajun, stir-fry vegetables with curry powder, peppers with baharat—so the “anything goes” format still has a flavor logic.

### Fried Rice

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free and gluten-free options. Soak long-grain rice, sauté onion, garlic, and scallion whites, then briefly cook crumbled tofu coated with garlic powder, kala namak, onion powder, and turmeric as the egg-like component. Reserve half the ginger/soy/sambal/sesame sauce, cook the rice with bell peppers, carrot, frozen vegetables, peas, and water at high pressure for 5 minutes, and naturally release for 10. Add the remaining soy, reserved tofu, peas, and scallion greens only after opening, then transfer out of the pot immediately. More vegetables require more sauce, curry paste can replace the Asian seasoning, tamari makes it gluten-free, and Chickpea Flour Tofu plus coconut aminos makes it soy-free.

### General Tso’s Soy Curls

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free and gluten-free options. Mix water, low-sodium soy sauce, mirin, rice vinegar, sesame oil, sambal/sriracha, ginger, and garlic, then sauté garlic, green-onion whites, peppers, carrot/zucchini, and dry soy curls with half the sauce, water, and part of the sugar. High pressure for 6 minutes with 5 minutes natural release hydrates the curls; whisk cornstarch into the reserved sauce and add it with the remaining sugar on Sauté for 3–4 minutes until boiling and thick. Garnish with green onion and red pepper over rice or another grain. Tamari makes it gluten-free, coconut aminos and seitan make a soy-free route, and PIP white rice can cook in a covered bowl on a tall trivet above the curls.

### Peanut Sauce Fried Rice

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free options. Blend peanut/almond butter, ginger, garlic powder, soy sauce, lime, lime zest, chile sauce, salt, sweetener, sesame oil, and coconut/nondairy milk into a smooth sauce. Sauté onion, garlic, peppers, carrot, scallion whites, and optional mixed vegetables, cook two-thirds of the sauce with soaked rice and 1½ cups water at high pressure for 5 minutes, then naturally release for 8. Fold in the reserved sauce, fluff from the bottom, and transfer out of the warm pot before garnishing. Coconut aminos and sunflower seed butter cover soy-free/nut-free needs, and cubed baked tofu turns the rice into a more substantial meal.

### Sweet & Sour Chickpeas

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free and gluten-free options. Soak dried chickpeas, sauté garlic and bell pepper, then add chickpeas, sugar, maple syrup, ketchup, soy sauce, garlic powder, and ¾ cup water. High pressure for 35 minutes with natural release cooks the chickpeas in the glaze; add apple cider/rice vinegars and broccoli on Sauté for about 2 minutes, then finish with a cornstarch slurry and the reserved 2 tablespoons water. Scallions sharpen the sweet-tangy sauce, which works over rice or crunchy cabbage salad. Canned chickpeas shorten the pressure cycle to 10 minutes, while tamari or coconut aminos and additional stir-fry vegetables provide the supported variations.

### Spicy Peanut Noodles

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free options. Stir soy sauce, lime, rice vinegar, sambal, maple syrup, peanut butter, and hot water into a sauce, then sauté onion, garlic, ginger, carrot, bell pepper, and snow peas before adding half the sauce, water, salt, and broken linguine. Cook at high pressure for 4 minutes, or half the package time plus one minute, then release naturally for 2 minutes. Toss the noodles with the reserved sauce, close the lid for 3–4 minutes so the sauce can absorb, and add thin raw vegetables on Sauté for one minute if crunch is wanted. Coconut aminos, sunflower butter, gluten-free noodles/tamari, tofu, Thai basil, lime, and peanuts/sunflower seeds make the flavor and dietary switches explicit.

### Thai-Inspired Curry Ramen

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free options. Sauté garlic and mushrooms until the edges color, immediately mix in peanut butter, curry paste, coriander, pepper, salt, and stock, then add carrot, bell pepper, coconut milk, soy sauce, sugar, and half the lime. High pressure for 5 minutes with 5 minutes natural release builds the broth; ramen noodles, spinach, and remaining lime enter on Sauté and boil only 2–3 minutes, with extra stock for a soupier bowl. Finish with sesame, cilantro, Thai basil, jalapeño, or peanuts. Sunflower butter and seeds replace peanut, coconut aminos replace soy, and gluten-free noodles may be added under pressure if their brand needs a longer cook.

### Curried Quinoa

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free and gluten-free options. Sauté onion, carrot, pepper, snow peas/green beans/zucchini/bok choy/cabbage, garlic, curry powder, turmeric, and red pepper, then add rinsed quinoa, Chinese cooking wine/mirin, soy sauce, sweetener, coconut milk, water, and salt. High pressure for only 1 minute with 5 minutes natural release cooks the quinoa, so the grain is fluffed and moved out of the hot inner pot immediately. Scallions, lime, and optional peanuts finish the Singapore-style fried-rice/noodle-inspired profile. Chickpeas or baked tofu make it a meal, while tamari or coconut aminos preserve the gluten-free/soy-free variants.

## Worked Example

Start a one-pot stir-fry by deciding whether the slow component is soaked rice, quinoa, dry chickpeas, soy curls, or noodles. If it is rice or quinoa, rinse/soak first, keep the liquid measured, and protect fresh sauce by reserving a portion; if it is a dry chickpea, accept a long bean cycle and add broccoli or vinegar only afterward. Use the Sauté stage for aromatics and the post-cook stage for cornstarch, peanut butter, tofu egg, peas, greens, or crunchy vegetables. Fluff and transfer promptly because the warm pot can erase the texture the short pressure cycle was designed to preserve.

## Anti-patterns

- **Skip soaking rice and expect wok-like grains**: starch and hydration affect clumping more than the pressure button itself.
- **Put all thick peanut sauce at the bottom**: reserve part and deglaze to limit scorching.
- **Pressure-cook peas, ramen, broccoli, or greens for the full bean time**: add them after opening or in a short Sauté stage.
- **Leave fried rice in Keep Warm**: carryover heat compacts and softens it.
- **Assume a “five-minute” recipe means five minutes total**: pressure build, natural release, and post-cook thickening are part of the result.

## Key Takeaways

1. Rice success begins with rinsing and soaking, then ends with immediate fluffing and transfer.
2. Reserve-and-finish sauces keep peanut, soy, chile, sweet, and sour notes distinct.
3. Use the pressure cycle for hydration and integration, not for every ingredient in the dish.
4. A cornstarch slurry, blended sauce, or short boil sets body after pressure.
5. The chapter’s flexibility is structured by grain, liquid, and stage—not by adding unlimited ingredients.
