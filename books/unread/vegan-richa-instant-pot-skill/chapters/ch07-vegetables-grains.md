# Chapter 7: Vegetables & Grains

## Core Idea

This section is a study in restraint: many vegetables cook faster than an Instant Pot can build pressure, so one or two extra minutes can turn a side into mush. Hingle therefore uses hearty potatoes, eggplant, cabbage, roots, and grains, cuts cauliflower large, and uses low pressure or quick release when a vegetable must remain distinct. Several recipes make a sauce or masala first and layer the vegetable on top; others deliberately embrace mash, as in baingan bharta and pav bhaji. The vegetable sides are usually lightly sauced because they are meant to accompany dal or curry, while the rice dishes are self-contained and finish with a careful fluff and removal from the hot inner pot. Condiments such as tomato and mango chutney supply concentrated acid, heat, sweetness, and spice without extending the pressure time of the main meal.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Vegetable pressure triage**: use short times, large pieces, low pressure, and quick release for fragile produce; reserve long pressure cycles for ingredients that benefit from collapse.
- **Sauce/vegetable separation**: cook a tomato or spice base first and place the vegetable above it when the vegetable needs less time or less direct liquid.
- **Mash as a recovery mode**: when potatoes/cauliflower or eggplant go soft, change the target to bharta or pav bhaji rather than treating softness as failure.
- **Grain carryover control**: release on the recipe’s schedule, fluff immediately, and remove the inner pot so residual heat does not overcook rice.
- **Condiment concentration**: pressure-cook fruit or tomato, then use Sauté to evaporate water and tune the vinegar/sugar/salt balance.

## Recipe Cards

### Saag Aloo (Sweet Potato & Chard Curry)

**Yield:** 2 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Sauté onion, ginger, garlic, cumin, cinnamon, cardamom, cayenne, cloves, turmeric, and salt, then add ¾-inch sweet-potato or potato cubes, chopped chard/spinach, ½ cup water, and nondairy yogurt or coconut cream. High pressure for 4 minutes with 5 minutes natural release gives a soft side without a long curry cycle. Finish with garam masala, lemon, and cilantro and serve beside dal or curry. White potatoes substitute directly; extra water makes it saucier, and chickpeas or cooked lentils turn it into a meal with doubled spices and salt.

### Aloo Gobi (Curried Potatoes & Cauliflower)

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Brown potato cubes with cumin and onion on Sauté, then add ginger, garlic, chile, turmeric, coriander, garam masala, tomato, and water, scraping the pot well. Toss large cauliflower florets with oil, turmeric, cumin, optional amchoor, paprika/cayenne, and salt, and layer them over the potatoes; cook at low pressure for 2 minutes and quick-release. Lemon, cilantro, cayenne, and garam masala finish the restaurant-style sauce. Peas or coconut milk can be added after opening, and if the cauliflower overcooks, mash the mixture with onion, lemon, butter, and garam masala into Pav Bhaji.

### Baingan Bharta (Spiced Mashed Eggplant)

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Sauté cumin, onion, ginger, garlic, chile, turmeric, garam masala, coriander, and smoked paprika with tomato, then add cubed eggplant and ½ cup water. High pressure for 12 minutes followed by natural release makes the eggplant tender; mash thoroughly, then use Sauté for about 3 minutes to evaporate water and develop the roasted profile. Fold in peas and cilantro, and garnish with more smoked paprika and red pepper flakes. Smoked paprika approximates the traditional flame-char, liquid smoke supplies a stronger alternative, and chickpeas can be added after mashing to make a meal; for oil-free cooking, dry-toast cumin and deglaze with stock or water.

### Gobi Matar (Cauliflower Pea Curry)

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free and gluten-free. Blend coconut milk, ginger, tomato paste, onion flakes/powder, cayenne, garam masala, cumin, coriander, turmeric, optional fenugreek, salt, and sugar into a sauce, then sauté fresh onion and garlic before adding it with medium/large cauliflower and tomato. Cook at low pressure for 2 minutes and quick-release, fold in peas, and thicken with flour or cornstarch whisked into cashew cream. Lemon, red pepper, and cilantro balance the rich sauce. Yogurt or another nondairy cream replaces cashew cream, chickpeas replace peas, and thin nondairy milk can be blended with cashews; softer zucchini, peppers, roots, and greens are valid variations but may need stage changes.

### Avial (South Indian Vegetable Coconut Curry)

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Blend coconut, green chile, salt, optional turmeric, cumin, and water; if using dried coconut, let it rehydrate before the second blend, while avoiding over-blending fresh coconut so it does not release too much oil. Temper mustard seeds, chile, and 10–12 curry leaves, then add firm vegetables such as potato, pumpkin, carrots, beans, summer squash, moringa stems, or unripe banana with the coconut sauce and ¼ cup water. High pressure for 4 minutes with 2 minutes natural release and quick release preserves the vegetable assortment. Lime is the only required garnish, and the author emphasizes that curry leaves are essential to the South Indian flavor; serve with rice or dosa.

### Masala Potatoes

**Yield:** 2 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Temper mustard and dried red chile, then cook thin onion, curry leaves, cashews, ginger, turmeric, potatoes, salt, and ⅓ cup water. High pressure for 4 minutes with 5 minutes natural release softens the small cubes, after which lemon juice and a light mash of half the potatoes create the dosa-filling texture. Cilantro and extra lemon provide the finish. The recipe doubles with scant ⅔ cup water while keeping the same cook time, making it a practical batch filling for dosa, tacos, or flatbread.

### Potato Sabzi (Onion- & Garlic-Free Spiced Potato)

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Crush or grind fennel, coriander, and fenugreek with turmeric, cayenne, chickpea flour, ginger, chile, and amchoor, then toast the mixture with small potato cubes and enough water to prevent scorching. High pressure for about 8 minutes with natural release produces a dry, strongly spiced potato; mash some of it to bind the sauce and finish with lemon or amchoor. The recipe is deliberately allium-free, so the aromatic structure comes from whole spices, ginger, chile, and chickpea flour rather than onion/garlic. Serve as a side or stuffing, and loosen with water or nondairy yogurt if a more saucy sabzi is wanted.

### Masala Eggplant Curry

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, gluten-free, with nut-free substitutions. Grind coriander, cumin, mustard, chickpea flour, nuts or seeds, coconut, cardamom, cinnamon, cayenne, turmeric, sugar, and salt into a stuffing sauce, then bloom it in oil. Sauté onion, ginger, garlic, chile, and lime, add chopped eggplant and water, and pressure cook at high for 6 minutes with natural release. The eggplant collapses into the masala while the toasted flour/nut mixture supplies body; garnish with cilantro and lemon. Chickpeas turn it into a meal, pumpkin or sunflower seeds replace nuts, and other hearty vegetables can share the method if they can tolerate the six-minute pressure cycle.

### Patta Gobi Sabzi (Indian Cabbage & Peas)

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Temper mustard and curry leaves, then add garlic, chile, hing, turmeric, cabbage, a small amount of water, and salt. High pressure for 2 minutes or 3 minutes for a softer result, followed by 5 minutes natural release, keeps cabbage tender without requiring a sauce. Fold in peas after opening and finish with lemon, cilantro, or coconut. Cumin and extra coconut are supported variations, while the short timing makes this an everyday side that should not be held on Keep Warm.

### Poriyal (Carrot & Green Beans with Mustard Seeds)

**Yield:** 4 side servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Temper mustard, urad or mung dal, and optional cinnamon, cook onion with turmeric and cayenne, then add sliced carrots, green beans, salt, and ¼ cup water. High pressure for only 1 minute with 5 minutes natural release leaves the vegetables bright and lightly crisp. Stir in shredded coconut after opening and garnish with cilantro. Beet replaces carrot, but if beet is not wanted, the book suggests simply increasing the green beans; red lentils can replace the toasted urad dal or it can be omitted.

### Pav Bhaji (Street-Style Spiced Vegetable Mash)

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Sauté onion, garlic, ginger, pav bhaji masala, paprika, and coriander, then add tomato, passata, 4–5 cups mixed vegetables, salt, black salt, and water. High pressure for 10 minutes with natural release deliberately turns the vegetable assortment into a mash; coarsely mash after opening and reduce on Sauté if watery. Vegan butter and lime supply the street-stall finish, and the mash is served on toasted buttered bread with raw onion, cilantro, and lime. The homemade pav bhaji masala combines coriander, cumin, cinnamon, cardamom, clove, black pepper, and amchoor; this recipe is also the recovery destination for overcooked aloo gobi.

### Dump & Done Spinach Rice & Chickpeas

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Soak rinsed white basmati for 15 minutes, then put oil, ginger, garlic, chile, thawed squeezed spinach, tomato, garam masala, chickpeas, bay leaves, salt, and 1 cup water into the pot before adding the drained rice. High pressure for 5 minutes followed by 7–8 minutes natural release cooks the meal without a sautéed base. Fluff thoroughly and remove the inner pot or transfer the rice immediately so the hot element does not continue cooking it, then garnish with lemon, red pepper, and cilantro. Black-eyed peas, mung beans, kidney beans, or white beans replace chickpeas, but cooked rather than dry beans preserve the rice timing.

### Tawa Pulao (Spiced Tomato Rice)

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Sauté cumin and fennel, onion, ginger, garlic, pav bhaji masala, cayenne, tomato, bell peppers, carrot, and green beans until the tomato is saucy, then add salt, water, and soaked rice. High pressure for 5 minutes with 5 minutes natural release, followed by venting, cooks the vegetables with the rice; fold in peas only after opening. Fluff immediately and remove the inner pot to stop carryover cooking, then finish with lemon and cilantro. Garam masala, curry powder, or Berbere replace pav bhaji masala, and a drained can of chickpeas converts the side into a meal.

### Tomato Chutney

**Yield:** about 1¼ cups; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Temper mustard, fennel, hing, chopped ginger, garlic, chile, onion, turmeric, and coriander, then add chopped or blended tomatoes, sugar/jaggery, salt, and water. High pressure for 8 minutes with 5 minutes natural release is followed by Sauté and mashing until the chutney reaches the desired thickness; cayenne and kala namak go in at the end. Finely chopped rather than minced ginger and garlic are used because the finer mince is more likely to stick to the pot. Cool and refrigerate up to five days; the chutney doubles as a stir-fry base for potatoes, cauliflower, zucchini, or green beans.

### Indian Pickled Onions

**Yield:** one pint jar; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. This condiment is not made in the Instant Pot: boil water with cinnamon, black pepper, cayenne, and cloves for four minutes, then pour it into a heatproof jar with ginger, green chile, salt, sugar, pearl or sliced red onions, and vinegar. Chill for 24 hours before serving so the onions become tangy but retain a raw crunch. The author positions them as a counterpoint to spicy or sweet sauces, not as a shelf-stable canning recipe. Keep the jar refrigerated and use the stated vinegar-based method rather than improvising pressure canning.

### Mango Chutney

**Yield:** 6 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Put ripe diced mango, ginger, red chile, garlic, salt, vinegar, most of the sugar, optional golden raisins, and cinnamon/cardamom/clove/nutmeg into the pot with a little oil. High pressure for 7 minutes with 5 minutes natural release makes the fruit jammy; use Sauté with the reserved sugar to evaporate liquid, then tune the vinegar/sugar balance. Very ripe mango gives the intended sweet condiment, while less ripe fruit makes a more sweet-sour result. Cool and refrigerate up to one week; the chutney supplies an acid-sugar contrast beside curries, dals, or sandwiches.

### Achari Vegetables (Indian Pickled Spiced Veggies)

**Yield:** 4 servings; soy-free, nut-free, gluten-free. Temper fennel, nigella, mustard, cumin, and fenugreek, then add ginger, garlic, tomato, turmeric, cayenne, water, yogurt or blended silken tofu, similarly sized vegetables, and salt. High pressure for 3 minutes with 3 minutes natural release cooks the vegetables while preserving the achari spice profile. Rest the dish for a few hours before judging it because the whole spices need time to infuse; cilantro is the final garnish. The recipe borrows the oil-and-spice logic of Indian pickles without using vinegar, supports cooked chickpeas for protein, and warns that vegetables should have similar cooking times.

## Worked Example

Build a balanced spread around a long-cooking dal: put Dal Fry or another lentil dish in the main pot, make a vegetable side with a short pressure window, and use tomato or mango chutney as the bright condiment. If the vegetable is rice-based, fluff it and remove the inner pot as soon as the release window ends; if it is cauliflower or beans, use low pressure or one minute and vent promptly. When a side is softer than intended, change the serving identity to bharta or pav bhaji and add acid, fresh onion, herbs, or butter for contrast. The book’s vegetable logic is therefore less about one universal timing chart than about matching shape, water, pressure level, and release to the desired final texture.

## Anti-patterns

- **Use long bean-style pressure times for ordinary vegetables**: most vegetables overcook before the cooker even finishes depressurizing.
- **Cut cauliflower or other quick vegetables too small**: large florets create a useful buffer.
- **Leave finished rice in the hot inner pot**: carryover heat continues the cook after pressure is gone.
- **Expect raw chutney texture straight from the cooker**: reduce on Sauté and cool before evaluating thickness.
- **Treat Indian pickled onions as an Instant Pot recipe**: they are a refrigerated vinegar condiment with a 24-hour resting period.

## Key Takeaways

1. Choose pressure level, piece size, and release method before choosing the spice profile.
2. Use the cooker for collapse when the dish wants mash, and protect vegetables when it wants definition.
3. Rice needs immediate fluffing and removal from residual heat.
4. Chutneys are concentration tools: pressure softens fruit/tomato, while Sauté sets the final body.
5. Condiments and cooked potato fillings let one pressure-cooked spread become several meals.
