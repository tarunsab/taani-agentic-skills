# Chapter 5: Indian Comfort Food

## Core Idea

This section is both a restaurant-favorite collection and a lesson in how to reproduce stovetop Indian flavor in a pressure cooker. Many sauces traditionally gain depth by slowly caramelizing onion, roasting tomato, and infusing whole spices; Hingle uses pressure with very little moisture to approximate that effect through the Maillard reaction, then uses Sauté to reduce excess liquid. The trade-off is timing: watery vegetables can disintegrate during the long pressure cycle, while tofu can add enough moisture to interrupt roasting, so the recipes say explicitly what goes in before and after pressure. Cashew cream is the neutral creamy default, but yogurt, blended tofu, pumpkin seeds, coconut cream, or coconut milk fit different sauces and dietary constraints. Indian spice flavors improve after resting, so a dish that tastes sharply of spinach or spice immediately after opening may be substantially better a few hours later or the next day. A tall-trivet PIP rice setup works for recipes with roughly 7–12 minutes of pressure time, allowing a curry and basmati rice to finish together. The section therefore teaches a modular restaurant-style workflow: pressure-roast or cook the base, add the right protein/vegetable at the right stage, correct consistency, and finish with cream, acid, herbs, and whole-spice fragrance.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Pressure-roasted sauce**: blend aromatics, tomato, spices, and minimal water; pressure cook; reduce on Sauté if needed; use the sauce as a freezer-friendly base.
- **Steam the cashews while cooking**: put cashews in a basket or equal-water bowl above the sauce, then blend them after pressure so the cream is smooth and the sauce is less likely to burn.
- **Protein timing**: add low-moisture soy curls or seitan before pressure when the sauce allows it; add tofu, roasted vegetables, peas, greens, or cream afterward when they would overcook or dilute the roast.
- **Rest for infusion**: judge heavily spiced tomato, spinach, or cream sauces after they sit, not only at the first taste.
- **PIP rice with curry**: soak and rinse basmati, cover in a tall insert with water and a little oil, and cook it above a curry whose pressure time is compatible.

## Recipe Cards

### Tikka Masala Sauce (Spicy Tangy Tomato Sauce)

**Yield:** 6 servings. Sauté onion, ginger, garlic, green chile, bell pepper, turmeric, sweet/smoked paprika, coriander, cayenne, garam masala, and dried fenugreek; add tomatoes, yogurt, and salt, then pressure cook high for 12 minutes and naturally release. Finish with sugar if the yogurt/tomatoes are tart and cashew cream for a rounder sauce; use it with baked tofu paneer, vegetables, chickpeas, seitan, or vegan chicken, or add dry soy curls plus water before pressure. For a smoother or less acidic result, puree part of the sauce, add tomato paste when using fresh tomatoes, or replace fenugreek with dry mustard.

### Palak Tofu (Tofu in Creamy Spinach Sauce)

**Yield:** 4 servings. Coat firm tofu in salt, cumin, garam masala, garlic powder, nutritional yeast, and cayenne; bake at 400°F/200°C for 20–25 minutes until crisp. Blend half the spinach with nondairy milk, cashews, ginger, and tomatoes, bloom cumin/coriander with garlic and chile, then pressure cook the blended and chopped spinach for 2 minutes fresh or 4 minutes frozen, followed by quick release. Boil briefly on Sauté, fold in the baked tofu, and finish with cashew cream; the author specifically recommends resting the dish for hours so spinach stops dominating and the spices emerge. Chickpea tofu, chickpeas, beans, or vegetables replace tofu; coconut cream or dry-toasted spices support nut-free or oil-free versions.

### Mushroom Masala (Easy Mushroom Gravy)

**Yield:** 2 servings. Blend onion, ginger, garlic, chile, and water; sauté the puree, then add garam masala or another blend, paprika, turmeric, fenugreek, tomato puree, mushrooms, optional vegetables/chickpeas, and salt without adding extra liquid. Pressure cook high for 8 minutes, naturally release for 10, then add peas, spinach, cashew cream, sugar, and cayenne on Sauté and bring to a boil. Mushrooms release enough water to cook and their low-liquid treatment helps them develop a roasted quality; if doubling, increase pressure time only to 9 minutes. Pumpkin-seed puree, silken tofu, coconut cream, or plain water replace cashew cream, and the dish can stay noncreamy.

### Large-Batch Butter Chicken Sauce (Silky Tomato Sauce)

**Yield:** 10–12 servings. Blend tomatoes, ginger, garlic, chiles, nondairy milk, garam masala, paprika, cayenne, salt, sugar, and fenugreek, then pressure cook high for 16 minutes and naturally release. Add cashew cream on Sauté, bring to a boil, and fold in fresh ginger, sliced chile, garam masala, fenugreek, and cilantro; this late finish restores fragrance after the long cook. Refrigerate up to 4 days or freeze up to 2 months, then use with soy curls/seitan before pressure, baked tofu by simmering, or roasted vegetables by stovetop cooking or roasting first. Vegan butter, coconut cream, or blended silken tofu make richer or nut-free versions.

### Vegetable Biryani (Royal Rice with Veggies & Nuts)

**Yield:** 4 servings. Rinse/soak basmati, then sauté bay, cumin, cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, pepper, onion, chile, cashews, salt, and sugar until the onion and nuts color; reserve some of that mixture for garnish. Cook ginger-garlic paste, tomato, optional raisins/yogurt, sturdy vegetables, peas, herbs, garam masala, paprika, rice, and water at high pressure for 5 minutes, naturally release 5, then quick release and fluff. The book simplifies traditional layering into a mixed one-pot rice while preserving whole-spice, mint, cilantro, nut, and onion contrast. Add chickpeas with the rice to turn the side into a meal; omit cashews for nut-free biryani.

### Plain, Garlic, or Avocado Naan (Dairy-Free Indian Flatbread)

**Yield:** 8–10 naans. Activate yeast in warm water and sugar, mix flour, salt, leaveners, oil, and nondairy yogurt or mashed ripe avocado into a soft slightly sticky dough, and proof it in an oiled Instant Pot on Yogurt for 1 hour, adding 30 minutes if it has not doubled. Divide, lightly roll oval breads, wet and oil the tops, and add nigella; bake on a very hot pizza stone for 2–3 minutes plus a brief broil, or cook on a hot griddle for 2–3 minutes per side. Garlic naan adds chopped garlic and cilantro; coconut/cashew cream, silken tofu, or thinned vegan sour cream can replace yogurt. The dough also works as pizza crust with oregano, and the cooked naan stores briefly at room temperature or longer refrigerated.

### Dump & Done Creamy Indian Butter Soy Curls

**Yield:** 4 servings. Blend tomatoes, ginger, garlic, chile, and water, pour into the pot, and add dry soy curls, cooked chickpeas, garam masala, paprika, cayenne, and salt. Pressure cook high for 10 minutes, naturally release for 10, then add cashew cream, more spice, sugar, fenugreek, fresh chile, ginger, and cilantro on Sauté and boil briefly. This is the low-effort counterpart to the batch sauce: the soy curls hydrate in the tomato base, while the finishing ingredients keep the final flavor bright. Use seitan or extra chickpeas for soy-free versions, coconut cream for nut-free, and extra butter/oil for richness.

### Kadhi Pakora (Spiced Yogurt Sauce with Onion Fritters)

**Yield:** 4 servings. Whisk nondairy yogurt, chickpea flour/besan, turmeric, chile, salt, and water; temper cumin, fenugreek, dried chile, ginger, garlic, optional hing, and red onion before adding the yogurt mixture. Pressure cook high for 7 minutes, naturally release, whisk, and use Sauté briefly if the flour tastes raw or the sauce is loose; serve with cilantro, chile, pappadum pieces, or baked onion pakoras. For the pakoras, coat thin onion rings with chickpea/rice flour, ginger, chile, turmeric, cayenne, oil, and just enough water to make a paste, then bake at 400°F/200°C for 20–25 minutes. Spinach, a chile-oil finish, or tall-trivet PIP rice are supported variations; the yogurt sauce can also be served without fritters.

### Bhuna Masala Sauce (Roasted Curry Sauce)

**Yield:** 2½–3 cups. Blend onion, ginger, garlic, chile, tomatoes, garam masala, coriander, cumin, turmeric, cinnamon, cayenne, paprika, fennel, fenugreek seeds, salt, sugar, and only ½ cup water. Sauté the puree 2–4 minutes to drive off raw onion fumes, pressure cook high for 25 minutes, naturally release, and reduce 5–8 minutes more if a deeper roast is wanted. Minimal water is the key constraint: dilution weakens the pressure-roasted effect, while post-cook reduction restores concentration. Use it for chole, rajma, tofu curry, beans, dals, or a freezer-ready shortcut; the author also gives a quick tofu-and-coconut-milk finish.

### Amritsari Masala (Spicy & Smoky Sauce)

**Yield:** 4 servings. Bloom cayenne, smoked/Kashmiri paprika, cumin, and coriander in hot oil before adding onion, ginger, chile, tomatoes, cashews, nondairy milk, and salt; this order is the defining contrast with sauces where spices follow the aromatics. Pressure cook high for 10 minutes, naturally release, blend in the pot, then simmer tofu, chickpeas, chickpea tofu, vegetables, or veggie balls for about 5 minutes. Bake vegetables in the tofu marinade when a roasted component is desired, and use pumpkin seeds, macadamias, or coconut cream for nut-free alternatives. Panned tofu should be pressed, cubed, and sautéed until golden before joining the sauce.

### Shahi Royal Sauce with Chickpeas or Tofu

**Yield:** 4 servings. Pressure cook onion, ginger, garlic, chile, cashews, almonds, pumpkin/watermelon seeds, water, whole spices, ground spices, saffron if using, and salt for 12 minutes; remove bay leaves and blend the softened nuts/seeds into a smooth white sauce. Add lemon and sugar for balance, then return chickpeas or tofu to the sauce and simmer 5 minutes. The technique lets the pressure cycle soften the nuts so no separate long soak is required. Add spinach/peas for color, raw vegetables plus water for a cooked curry, or use pumpkin/hemp seeds and cauliflower instead of the nuts.

### Balti Sauce with Jackfruit, Soy Curls, Chickpeas, or Seitan

**Yield:** 4 servings. Blend onion, ginger, garlic, nigella, coriander, garam masala, fenugreek, cardamom, cloves, pepper, cinnamon, paprika, salt, and chile with a few tablespoons of water. Sauté the sauce 3–4 minutes, add tomatoes and cook until thickening, then add bell peppers, sugar, and the selected protein; pressure cook 16 minutes for jackfruit, soy curls, or chickpeas, or 20 for seitan, with natural release. The pressure cycle stands in for the high-heat searing associated with a balti wok, while the whole spices benefit from sitting before serving. Finish with lime, cilantro, and red pepper flakes; grind the hard spices first if a smoother method is preferred.

### Dal Makhani (Buttery Black Lentils)

**Yield:** 4 servings. Soak whole black gram and kidney beans overnight, pressure cook with water, salt, and cayenne for 30 minutes, and reserve the cooked legumes and liquid. Sauté cumin, ginger, garlic, chile, onion, optional hing, turmeric, tomatoes, salt, garam masala, and fenugreek; return the legumes and cashew cream, pressure cook another 5 minutes, naturally release, and mash some of the lentils. The second pressure cycle lets the sauce and legumes become the creamy, buttery texture implied by “makhani.” Green mung, adzuki, caviar, brown, or green lentils can replace black gram with shorter times, while coconut cream or dry-toasted spices provide nut-free/oil-free routes.

### Baked Tofu Paneer

**Yield:** 4 servings. Press firm or extra-firm tofu, marinate it in garam masala, paprika, turmeric, salt, ginger, garlic, lemon, water, starch, and optional nutritional yeast, then rest 15 minutes. Bake at 400°F/200°C for 15–20 minutes until dry/crisp and simmer in an Indian sauce for 4–6 minutes to warm through. Baking rather than pressure cooking supplies the paneer-like exterior and lets the marinade flavor the tofu before a wet curry. Chickpea Flour Tofu is the supported soy-free replacement.

### Dum Aloo (New Potatoes in Fenugreek Sauce)

**Yield:** 4 servings. Sauté onion, ginger, garlic, tomato puree, paprika, turmeric, garam masala, and salt; coat baby potatoes in the sauce, add ½ cup water, and pressure cook high for 9 minutes for 1-inch potatoes or 10 for larger ones. Use a five-minute natural release, then vent, add cashew cream, and boil briefly on Sauté before folding in fenugreek. The Instant Pot version avoids the traditional pre-frying of the potatoes, though light panfrying is possible. Coconut cream replaces cashew cream for a nut-free finish.

### Tofu Pasanda (Tofu in Velvety Pepita Seed Sauce)

**Yield:** 3 servings. Blend onion, ginger, garlic, cumin, fennel, coriander, garam masala, cinnamon, paprika, cayenne, salt, sugar, pumpkin/sunflower seeds, poppy seeds, tomato, and coconut milk; let the mixture rest five minutes and blend again so the whole spices break down. Sear cubed tofu, add the sauce and blender-rinse water, pressure cook high for 17 minutes, naturally release, and finish with optional cream, cilantro, and pepper flakes. The sauce is intentionally seed-based and velvety, not simply a cashew curry. For soy-free pasanda, use overnight-soaked chickpeas and pressure cook about 25 minutes instead.

### Tofu & Sweet Potato Korma (Luxurious Onion Sauce)

**Yield:** 4 servings. Bake tofu and sweet potato coated with oil, garam masala, garlic powder, and salt at 400°F/200°C for 20–25 minutes. Blend cardamom, cloves, poppy seeds, coriander, garam masala, cinnamon, ginger, garlic, cashews, yogurt, water, salt, and cayenne; sauté onion and bay leaf, simmer in the blended sauce, then pressure cook high for 9 minutes. Add the baked components and quick vegetables/peas/cooked chickpeas afterward, boil 2–3 minutes, and rest 5 minutes before garnishing with cilantro, chile, nuts, or dried fruit. If the cooker tends to Burn, steam the cashews separately and add them blended at the end; pumpkin seeds replace cashews.

### Chole (Indian Chickpea Curry)

**Yield:** 3 servings. Soak chickpeas overnight, then pressure cook them with Bhuna Masala, water, lemon, amchoor, cinnamon, cloves, bay, cardamom, cayenne, chana masala/garam masala, and optional tomato for 45–55 minutes on Bean/high pressure with natural release. Add the second portion of lemon, adjust salt, reduce on Sauté, and mash some chickpeas for the traditional melt-in-the-mouth sauce. The long timing is intentional: chole is not aiming for merely tender chickpeas. The dump-and-done variation layers onion, ginger, garlic, spices, tomatoes, water, and salt without mixing the tomato at the bottom; serve with cilantro and red onion.

### Rajma Masala (Kidney Bean Curry)

**Yield:** 4–6 servings. Soak kidney beans in hot water overnight, pressure cook beans and water alone for 50 minutes (60 if old), then add bay, garam masala, cumin, turmeric, paprika, cayenne, hing, fenugreek, salt, Bhuna Masala, and optional potato. Pressure cook again for 10 minutes, naturally release for 10, mash some beans, and reduce if watery. The author flags kidney-bean texture as a genuine limitation: age and sauce ingredients can prevent the desired melt-in-the-mouth result, so baking soda soaking and additional Bean-mode cooking may be needed. Baharat, ginger/chile, rice, and flatbread are supported flavor/serving variations.

### Lentil Keema with Peas

**Yield:** 4 servings. Sauté finely chopped onion/garlic, then coarse walnut-carrot meal; add peas, brown lentils, sage, garam masala, cinnamon, cardamom, nutmeg, pepper, cayenne, soy sauce, ketchup, cilantro, and water. Pressure cook high for 11 minutes, naturally release for 5, vent, and reduce any remaining liquid on Sauté. The lentil/walnut mixture is intended to evoke minced-meat keema and can fill tacos, sandwiches, or burritos rather than only being served as a curry. Pumpkin/hemp seed meal replaces walnuts, and doubling needs only two additional pressure minutes.

### Malai Kofta (Kofta Balls in Cream Sauce)

**Yield:** 4 servings. Pulse nuts/seeds, potato, garlic, chickpeas, salt, garam masala, toasted cumin, chile, flour, breadcrumbs, starch, and baking soda into a mixture that forms soft balls; shape promptly because potato releases moisture. Dust and sear the balls on Sauté, add already-prepared Butter Chicken Sauce or Amritsari Masala, scrape the bottom, and pressure cook high for 2 minutes with natural release. Finish with cashew cream and cilantro; the intended kofta texture is soft and melt-in-the-mouth, not firm. If Burn appears, release carefully and finish on Sauté 4–6 minutes; steaming the balls above the sauce makes them softer like dumplings, while oat flour and gluten-free crumbs make them gluten-free.

### Mulligatawny Soup (Curried Soy Curl Lentil Soup)

**Yield:** 4 servings. Sauté onion, ginger, garlic, chile, celery, carrot, apple, curry powder, nutmeg, thyme, and tomato, then add red lentils, rice if using, seitan or soy curls, stock, coconut milk, salt, pepper, and cilantro. Pressure cook high for 12 minutes with natural release, then loosen if too thick and finish with lime and cilantro. The soup combines peppery South Indian inspiration with the later thickening influence of lentils/rice, coconut milk, and a meat substitute. Replace curry powder with garam masala, turmeric, and coriander; omit soy curls for soy-free and seitan for gluten-free.

## Worked Example

To assemble a restaurant-style weeknight curry, make Baked Tofu Paneer first: press, season, marinate, and bake the cubes until crisp. At the same time or earlier, pressure-cook Tikka Masala Sauce with minimal tomato moisture, naturally release, add cream, and simmer the tofu in the sauce for about 10 minutes. Add fenugreek, cilantro, lemon, or garam masala only at the finish, then let the curry rest so the spices and tofu absorb the sauce. The same architecture transfers to Butter Chicken, Amritsari, Shahi, or Korma, but their liquid, cream, and protein timing are not interchangeable.

## Anti-patterns

- **Add watery vegetables to a pressure-roasted sauce at the start**: they release liquid and can disintegrate before the sauce develops.
- **Blend or add thick cashew/nut butter mixtures without enough clearance/deglazing**: dense mixtures can stick and trigger Burn.
- **Judge spinach or heavily spiced curry immediately after opening**: the flavors may need hours to meld.
- **Treat chole or rajma as quick bean recipes**: their target texture is softer, and kidney beans are notably variable.

## Key Takeaways

1. Use minimal water for pressure-roasted Indian bases and reduce after opening.
2. Separate roasted/flavored proteins and fragile vegetables from the long sauce cycle.
3. Keep cashew cream, Bhuna Masala, and spice blends ready for modular meals.
4. Finish with acid, herbs, fenugreek, cream, or fresh spice to restore contrast.
5. When a bean texture is wrong, adjust soaking, age assumptions, or a second cook rather than blaming the seasoning.
