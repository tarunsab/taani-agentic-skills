# Glossary

## Terms and Ingredients

| Term | Working meaning in this book |
|---|---|
| **Achari** | Pickle-spice profile built from fennel, nigella, mustard, cumin, and fenugreek; the vegetable recipe borrows the flavor without vinegar pickling. |
| **Amchoor** | Dried green-mango powder; it supplies dry, tangy acidity in potato, chaat, and curry finishes. |
| **Appam / dosa / idli** | South Indian breads or cakes; dosa is a skillet crepe, idli is a steamed cake, and both use fermented rice-lentil batter. |
| **Besan** | Chickpea flour, commonly used for batters, pakoras, sauces, and the book’s chickpea-flour tofu. |
| **Bhuna masala** | A pressure-roasted onion-tomato-spice base; minimal water and later reduction concentrate it. |
| **Chai masala** | Small spice blend centered on cardamom with cinnamon, clove, and black pepper; blend strength varies. |
| **Chana dal** | Split chickpeas; a slow, hearty legume used in dals and spice blends. |
| **Chole** | Chickpea curry; the book targets very soft, long-cooked chickpeas and often mashes some for body. |
| **Dalia / dal** | Dal can mean a lentil/pea/bean or its cooked soup/stew; the meaning depends on context. |
| **Dhania-jeera** | Coriander-and-cumin powder, optionally finished with cinnamon, clove, and pepper. |
| **Hing / asafetida** | Strong allium-like spice used in small pinches; use certified gluten-free hing when needed. |
| **Kala namak** | Indian black salt with sulfurous, egg-like aroma; used after pressure in scrambled eggs and sauces. |
| **Kasuri methi** | Dried fenugreek leaves; bittersweet, aromatic finishing herb for Indian sauces. |
| **Kheer / halwa / ladoo** | Kheer is a soft rice pudding; halwa spans crumbly-to-fudge textures; ladoos are rolled sweet balls. |
| **Kitchari** | Rice-and-dal preparation; the book gives both porridge and more distinct rice textures. |
| **Makhani** | “Buttery” sauce style; in Dal Makhani, long bean cooking and a second pressure cycle create creamy body. |
| **Masala** | A seasoned mixture rather than one fixed spice; it may be dry, wet, roasted, or a finished sauce. |
| **Nondairy cream** | Cashew cream, coconut cream, yogurt, blended tofu, seed cream, or commercial cream used for richness/thickening. |
| **Paanch phoron** | Five-seed tempering, usually mustard, cumin, fennel, nigella, and fenugreek. |
| **Pav bhaji masala** | Blend used in street-style vegetable mash; coriander, cumin, cinnamon, cardamom, clove, pepper, and amchoor are the book’s homemade basis. |
| **PIP** | Pot-in-pot: one or two covered heatproof inserts on a trivet above water or another food. |
| **Pressure Cook vs Steam** | Pressure Cook seals the pot and cooks under pressure; Steam with the vent open is the dessert/batter method that permits rise. |
| **Quick release** | Manually venting pressure after cooking; useful for quick vegetables but can overcook or spray liquid-heavy food. |
| **Natural release** | Letting pressure fall on its own; preferred for beans, lentils, soups, sauces, and many grains. |
| **Combination release** | Rest naturally for a stated interval, then vent; common for rice and vegetables. |
| **Roux** | Flour cooked in fat to thicken and flavor a sauce; Japanese Curry and Étouffée use it differently. |
| **Saag** | Greens; paired with aloo (potato) in the spinach/chard curry. |
| **Sambar** | South Indian split-pea/lentil stew with tamarind, curry leaves, and sambar spice. |
| **Soy curls / seitan / jackfruit** | Protein or meat-texture ingredients with different liquid and timing needs; jackfruit shreds, soy curls hydrate, and seitan is wheat-based. |
| **Tadka / tempering** | Hot oil infused with whole spices and aromatics, added before or after the dal. |
| **Yogurt function** | Low, warm incubation used here for vegan yogurt, sprouts, and idli/dosa fermentation—not a pressure cycle. |

## Dietary and Texture Shortcuts

- **Soy-free** usually means chickpea tofu, beans, mushrooms, jackfruit, coconut aminos, or seed/cream alternatives; seitan is not gluten-free.
- **Nut-free** commonly uses pumpkin/hemp/sunflower seeds, nondairy yogurt, blended tofu, or coconut cream, but coconut changes flavor and richness.
- **Gluten-free** often requires both a starch/flour swap and a carrier swap: tamari, gluten-free pasta/bread, rice/oat flour, or corn tortillas.
- **Oil-free** savory recipes generally sauté with stock or water and dry-toast whole spices; oil-free baking is not always a neutral substitution.
