# Reusable Patterns

## Pressure-Cooking Patterns

### 1. Sauté → deglaze → pressure → finish

Use Sauté to bloom whole spices and soften aromatics, then add tomato, wine, or a spoonful of stock and scrape every browned bit from the bottom. Add the pressure liquid and slow ingredients, cancel Sauté, and seal only after the base is loose enough to avoid Burn. After release, add greens, peas, cream, acid, herbs, and fresh chile, then reduce or loosen to the desired consistency. This is the default pattern for dals, curries, soups, and many sauces.

### 2. Pressure-roasted sauce

Blend onion, ginger, garlic, tomato, spices, and minimal water, pressure-cook, naturally release, and reduce on Sauté if needed. The low-moisture setup helps reproduce some roasted/caramelized flavor, especially for Bhuna Masala and Indian restaurant-style sauces. Watery vegetables and tofu often belong after the sauce has developed, or they will dilute and overcook it. This is a flavor-concentration method, not a universal replacement for pan roasting.

### 3. Legume calibration

Soak beans when the recipe calls for it, use the slowest legume to set the time, and sample a bean after natural release. If it is still firm, reseal and cook in ten-minute increments rather than changing the seasoning blindly. Red lentils cook quickly and dissolve for body; brown/green lentils and whole beans need longer windows. Bean age, water, altitude, and cooker differences make the book’s times starting points rather than guarantees.

### 4. Rice and grain control

Rinse and soak white rice for about 15 minutes unless a recipe says otherwise; quinoa is rinsed but generally not soaked. Use the recipe’s liquid, combine grains with a sauce only after scraping the pot, and follow the specified natural or combination release. Fluff to the bottom immediately and remove the inner pot so carryover heat does not compact the grain. For PIP rice, cover a tall insert with foil and keep it above the main food on a tall trivet.

### 5. Direct pasta timing

Submerge broken or short pasta in measured liquid and begin around half the package time plus one minute, adjusting for the brand and shape. Use quick or short-burst release only when the recipe specifies it, then stir apart any stuck noodles. Add blended cream or thickener afterward and remove the inner pot as soon as the pasta is done. If Burn repeats, add liquid, cool the pot, change the insert, or switch to PIP.

### 6. PIP component stack

Put the longest-cooking or most liquid-dependent food in the main pot. Put rice, mash, grits, frittata, cashews, cabbage, queso, or compote in a covered insert above it, choosing glass/stoneware when metal would heat too aggressively. Leave clearance around the insert and below the lid; use a tall trivet, wire rack, or foil sling for removal. Finish each component separately after opening, then assemble.

### 7. Steam-to-set baking

For cakes, brownies, pancakes, and rolls, use the Steam setting with the vent open, cover the pan, and time externally because the appliance timer may not count down. Parchment reduces condensation; pan material changes crust versus fudginess. Cool before inverting, leveling, frosting, or slicing, and broil afterward when a crisp/browned top is wanted. Pressure is better for custards, fruit, rice pudding, and dense fillings than for a rising cake.

### 8. Thickener staging

Avoid putting a full quantity of flour, cashew butter, or nut butter against the hot base. Use a measured slurry, blend a portion of the cooked food, or reserve most of a nut/cream component for after pressure. Bring the final mixture to a brief boil on Sauté and remove the pot once it reaches the target body. A sauce that looks loose immediately after opening may thicken while it cools.

## Failure Modes and Repairs

| Symptom | Likely cause | Book-grounded repair |
|---|---|---|
| Burn signal | Stuck sauté bits, thick tomato/nut mixture, insufficient liquid, or an over-hot base | Cancel, vent only when safe, inspect; deglaze/add liquid, cool the pot, change insert, or finish PIP/Sauté. |
| Mushy vegetables | Pieces too small or pressure/release too long | Use larger pieces, low pressure, quick release, or add the vegetable after pressure; repurpose as mash when appropriate. |
| Firm beans | Old beans, too little soaking, altitude, or an underpowered time | Natural release, sample, then reseal in ten-minute increments; adjust only after texture is fixed. |
| Gummy rice/pasta | Skipped soak, excess starch, wrong timing, or carryover heat | Rinse/soak, calibrate to package, fluff, and remove inner pot immediately. |
| Rubbery steamed cake | Condensation, closed vent, wrong pan, or too much liquid | Steam vent-open, cover with parchment/foil, match pan, cool fully, and trim the top if needed. |
| Thin finished sauce | Watery produce or too much release liquid | Reduce on Sauté, blend a portion, add starch/cream at the stated stage, or wait for cooling. |
