# Cheatsheet: Haidt's Decision Rules

## Default decisions

| If… | Prefer… | Because… |
|---|---|---|
| Child is below high school | Basic phone only; no browser or app ecosystem | Delay round-the-clock internet access and preserve real-world experience. |
| Child is below 16 | No social-media account | Avoid the most vulnerable puberty years of public comparison and algorithmic socialization. |
| School allows phones in pockets | Full-day lockers or lockable pouches | Remove attention, comparison, and peer-pressure loops for everyone. |
| Free time is adult-scheduled or screen-filled | Mixed-age, child-directed outdoor play | Build competence, conflict repair, self-governance, and antifragility. |
| One family wants to change alone | Coordinate with other families/schools | Reduce the social cost and break the collective-action trap. |

## Fast diagnostic: what to fix first

1. Check sleep: remove devices from bedrooms and set a fixed evening cutoff.
2. Check embodied friendship: restore regular in-person play, sport, meals, and third places.
3. Check attention: silence notifications and remove visible devices from work/class.
4. Check control: look for craving, deception, withdrawal, conflict, or inability to stop.
5. If several domains are impaired, pause access and seek specialized or clinical guidance.

## Age-graded ladder

| Age | Developmental default |
|---|---|
| 6 | Household responsibility and chores |
| 8 | Local unsupervised play and small errands |
| 10 | Wider roaming; basic phone if needed |
| 12 | Apprenticeship and trusted adults beyond parents |
| 14 | High school, work/teams, possible first smartphone |
| 16 | Internet account adulthood and social-media access |
| 18 | Legal adulthood |
| 21 | Full legal adulthood in U.S. alcohol/gambling norms |

Adapt the ladder to local safety and the individual child; pair every new freedom with responsibility.

## Tells and smells

- “Everyone has one” → likely collective-action pressure, not evidence of readiness.
- Phone in the pocket during class → attention is still being spent even if the screen is dark.
- More contacts but fewer close friends → quantity has displaced quality.
- Fewer injuries plus less adventure → possible loss of healthy risk practice, especially for boys.
- A product that removes stopping cues → race-to-the-bottom attention design.
- A child can browse but cannot self-regulate a feed → distinguish internet access from account adulthood.
- Parent fear rises after each small independence step → use repeated, bounded exposure to update trust.
- School adds another wellness program but leaves phones and recess unchanged → it may be standing on a whale and fishing for minnows.

## Core allocation rule

Protect children more from the virtual world, while giving them more autonomy in the real world. Use “as safe as necessary, not as safe as possible” for play, and treat chronic or overwhelming stress as a boundary—not as antifragility training.
