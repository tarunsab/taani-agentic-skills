# Glossary

**Addiction** — Compulsive use marked by craving, loss of control, withdrawal, or impairment; not every heavy user meets this threshold (Ch. 5, 7, 12).

**Age Appropriate Design Code (AADC)** — U.K. framework requiring child-best-interest design, high privacy defaults, geolocation protection, and clear explanations (Ch. 10).

**Age check** — Proposed device-level signal that lets a platform verify a minor's age status without receiving identity or browsing history (Ch. 10).

**Agency** — Motive to individuate, master skills, assert, compete, and affect the world (Ch. 6, 7).

**Anomie** — Normlessness or loss of stable, binding social order, associated with meaninglessness and despair (Ch. 7, 8, 9).

**Antifragility** — A complex system's ability to become stronger through appropriately sized shocks, unlike mere resilience (Ch. 3).

**Attachment system** — Mammalian system that balances exploration away from a secure base with retreat for protection (Ch. 3, 12).

**Attention fragmentation** — Repeated switching among tasks and notifications that prevents sustained thought and weakens executive function (Ch. 5, 8).

**Attunement** — Mutual tuning of bodies, emotions, attention, and timing through responsive interaction (Ch. 2, 8).

**Behavioral activation system (BAS)** — Opportunity-seeking system Haidt calls discover mode (Ch. 3).

**Behavioral inhibition system (BIS)** — Threat-monitoring system Haidt calls defend mode (Ch. 3).

**Collective action problem** — Individually rational choices that create a bad group outcome, such as universal early smartphone adoption (Ch. 9, 13).

**Communion** — Motive to belong, care, cooperate, and integrate the self into a social unit (Ch. 6–8).

**Concept creep** — Expansion of a concept downward to minor cases and outward to new phenomena (Ch. 3).

**Discover mode** — Curiosity and approach state generated by the BAS; the mode of opportunity, learning, and growth (Ch. 3).

**Dopamine** — Neurotransmitter involved in wanting, reward prediction, and learning; release increases craving rather than simple satisfaction (Ch. 5).

**Experience blocker** — Safetyism or connected technology that crowds out developmentally necessary experiences (Ch. 2, 4).

**Experience-expectant development** — Brain tuning that assumes species-typical experiences during malleable periods (Ch. 2).

**Externalizing disorder/symptom** — Distress expressed outwardly through conduct problems, anger, violence, or risk-taking (Ch. 1, 7).

**Failure to launch** — Failure to move from adolescence into employment, independence, and adult responsibility (Ch. 4, 7).

**Free play** — Activity freely chosen and directed by participants for its own sake, with child-led rules and low-cost mistakes (Ch. 2, 11).

**Great Rewiring of Childhood** — Rapid 2010–2015 transition from play-based to phone-based childhood (Ch. 0, 1, 5).

**Hikikomori** — Extreme retreat from society into a bedroom or home, originally described in Japan (Ch. 7).

**Internalizing disorder/symptom** — Distress experienced inwardly through anxiety, sadness, rumination, and withdrawal (Ch. 1, 7).

**Internet adulthood** — Threshold for opening accounts, accepting terms, and giving data; Haidt argues for 16 rather than 13 (Ch. 4, 10).

**Internet gaming disorder** — Gaming pattern causing substantial impairment or distress; cited estimates are higher for boys (Ch. 7).

**Mentalizing / social comparison** — Monitoring how one's status, appearance, or behavior compares with others (Ch. 6).

**NEET** — Young person not in education, employment, or training (Ch. 7).

**Opportunity cost** — Benefits forgone when time and attention go to screen activity instead of sleep, play, friends, or work (Ch. 5, 12).

**Phone-based childhood** — Childhood and adolescence organized around internet-connected personal devices (Ch. 0, 2, 5).

**Play-based childhood** — Childhood organized around free, embodied, real-world play and local community (Ch. 0, 2, 3).

**Prestige bias** — Tendency to copy people perceived as excellent or high-status in a valued domain (Ch. 2, 6).

**Psychological safety** — Shared belief that members can speak and take interpersonal risks without humiliation; distinct from immunity to negative feelings (Ch. 3).

**Reasonable Childhood Independence law** — Proposed clarification that neglect requires blatant, willful, or reckless disregard of obvious danger, not ordinary unsupervised freedom (Ch. 10).

**Rites of passage** — Community processes that mark a shift in status; often separation, transition, and reincorporation (Ch. 4).

**Risky play** — Child-led, uncertain, thrilling play involving possible physical injury and six recurring risk types (Ch. 3, 11).

**Safetyism** — Belief system that treats safety as sacred and refuses trade-offs needed for autonomy, conflict, or learning (Ch. 3, 4).

**Secure base** — Reliable adult from whom a child explores and to whom the child returns after a setback or threat (Ch. 3, 12).

**Sensitive period** — Developmental window when learning or identity formation is easier and more durable (Ch. 2, 4, 6).

**Social deprivation** — Loss of face-to-face, embodied, synchronous social time and practice (Ch. 2, 5, 6).

**Socially prescribed perfectionism** — Feeling required to meet unrealistically high standards set by others or society (Ch. 6).

**Sociogenic illness** — Symptoms generated or amplified by social forces rather than a direct biological cause (Ch. 6).

**Sociometer** — Hypothetical internal gauge of social standing that triggers anxiety when status appears to fall (Ch. 6).

**Spiritual degradation** — Downward movement toward ego, contempt, triviality, judgment, and fragmented attention (Ch. 8).

**Synchrony** — Coordinated timing and movement that can increase trust, cooperation, and communion (Ch. 2, 8).

**Withdrawal** — Anxiety, irritability, insomnia, and dysphoria after removing an addictive digital activity (Ch. 5, 7).
