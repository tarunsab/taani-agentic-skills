# Chapter 9: The L6

**Contents position**: NINE | **Part**: Part II

## Core Idea

The L6 is Todd Park’s name for the capable person hidden six layers below an organization’s nominal decision maker. In the pandemic, Charity Dean discovers that she is an L6 while Carter Mecher becomes the invisible author whose analysis is repeated by officials who never credit him. The Diamond Princess gives the Wolverines a rare, relatively bounded view of transmission, asymptomatic infection, age, and lethality, and confirms Carter’s warning that timing is the decisive variable. Charity then turns the same reasoning into a choice between containment and mitigation: contain while the virus is still traceable, or use targeted layers, local metrics, and shared responsibility once it is widespread. Todd Park’s model of California, built with DJ Patil and a volunteer technology team, turns Charity’s assumptions into the evidence behind Governor Newsom’s March 19 stay-at-home order. The chapter’s organizational thesis is that large systems often have the talent they need, but bury it, deny it, or ask it to work without authority; good crisis management excavates the L6 and gives the person a direct path to action.

## Narrative movement 1 — Carter’s network and the fire extinguisher

The Red Dawn emails grow to include governors’ advisers, federal officials, the surgeon general, and people near the president, but Carter keeps directing attention to the evidence rather than himself. The weekend calls give participants a place to say what they cannot safely say in public, though no one knows exactly who is listening. Carter compares layered interventions to a fire extinguisher: useful on a small stove fire, nearly useless once half the house is burning. The Diamond Princess is valuable because its 2,666 passengers resemble an older residential population while its 1,045 crew resemble a younger healthy one. Its itinerary and testing make visible a cluster that the United States would have missed if the original passenger had simply flown home.

## Narrative movement 2 — The ship as a denominator

The ship’s confirmed cases rise from 61 to 135 and then to 355, 621, and beyond while authorities debate what the numbers mean. A Japanese field report records ages, cabins, symptoms, and the striking fact that 51 percent of those testing positive were asymptomatic at that point, though some might later develop symptoms. The attack rate reaches about 20 percent in the confined setting, and Carter uses ICU outcomes plus known respiratory-failure mortality to estimate a ship infection-fatality rate of roughly 1.5–2 percent. After adjusting for the ship’s older population, he estimates about 0.5–1 percent for the United States. The ship is artificial and not a city, but it supplies a denominator and a check on the mental model that Wuhan’s incomplete data cannot provide. Carter concludes that time is transmission and that a week of delay can turn a fire extinguisher into a gesture.

## Narrative movement 3 — The Churchill Plan

Charity wants California to try containment while it is still possible: broaden testing, create local laboratory capacity, trace backward to superspreaders, use quarantine authority, and borrow Asian practices such as monitored isolation. If containment fails, she proposes a national dashboard that classifies communities as hot, warm, or cool according to cases per capita, test positivity, and hospital occupancy, with restrictions scaling up or down by local heat. The point is radical accountability: residents should see the conditions that govern their zip code and own the work of reducing its reproductive rate rather than imagine that “the government” is imposing an arbitrary order. Charity’s draft, first imagined as the Churchill Plan and later titled “Everyone Owns Their Own R Naught,” also calls for genomic information to reveal how clusters entered a safe space. Its sharpest distinction is between managing spread and beating it; she prefers containment, but she recognizes that local, targeted mitigation is the fallback once spread is broad.

## Narrative movement 4 — The L6 reaches the governor

Todd Park and DJ Patil find Charity after Governor Newsom asks them to help solve California’s testing and forecasting problem. A volunteer team rewrites a Johns Hopkins model with California data and shows a huge difference between doing nothing—roughly 700,000 hospital beds needed by mid-May—and acting like St. Louis, when the peak is around 70,000. The forecast supplies “new information” for Newsom’s March 19 statewide stay-at-home order. Park sees Charity as the L6 because she is doing the work associated with the chief health officer while being excluded from the leadership table. The episode shows how an obscure operator becomes consequential when a model, a decision maker, and a bridge person align. It also exposes the fragility of the arrangement: Charity writes a national plan under a secrecy constraint, and the federal system can copy its language without granting the underlying authority.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **L6**: Find the buried operator with the best situational knowledge and move that knowledge to the decision maker.
- **Containment/mitigation fork**:
  - Containment: extinguish clusters while testing and tracing can still find the source.
  - Mitigation: reduce harm through targeted layers after widespread transmission.
- **Radical accountability**: Give communities local data and clear conditions so they can own their response.
- **Hot/warm/cool dashboard**: Scale interventions to local heat rather than impose one permanent rule.

## Key Concepts

- **Diamond Princess**: Bounded outbreak that supplies a denominator and age-stratified clues.
- **Attack rate**: Share infected in the ship’s population.
- **Superspreader**: Person responsible for disproportionate transmission.
- **Safe space**: School, nursing home, office, building, or neighborhood whose internal and external transmission must be distinguished.
- **Time = transmission**: Carter’s rule for exponential delay.
- **Everyone Owns Their Own R Naught**: Charity’s proposed title for shared local responsibility.

## Mental Models

- Use a **fire extinguisher** only while the fire is small; pair it with a smoke detector and a route to escape.
- Treat the Diamond Princess as a **calibration instrument**, not as a perfect miniature city.
- Ask whether a crisis plan is **owned locally** or merely imposed centrally.

## Worked Example

Charity’s California calculation starts with one known death and a 0.5 percent fatality assumption, implying about 199 other infections. With a reproductive rate of 3, those cases triple each week; seven weeks later the source’s arithmetic reaches 11,809,800 cases, more than a million hospitalizations at a 10 percent rate, and roughly 59,000 deaths. The point is not the exact forecast but the shape of the hidden process: one visible death is a delayed signal of a much larger upstream wave. The whiteboard turns a statistic that looks small into an argument for immediate action.

## Distinctions & Limits

The cruise ship’s confined population, age structure, and unusual exposure pattern limit how directly its rates transfer to a whole country. The 51 percent asymptomatic figure is provisional because some passengers had not yet developed symptoms. Charity’s dashboard and privacy proposals depend on data quality, legal authority, local leadership, and public trust; the chapter does not show them fully implemented. The L6 concept can locate expertise, but discovery alone does not repair the hierarchy that buried it. A national plan may also be less effective than locally owned rules when communities differ in resources, behavior, and transmission.

## Anti-patterns

- **Waiting for the perfect city-scale denominator**.
- **Treating a bounded natural experiment as the whole world**.
- **Calling containment impossible before widening testing and tracing**.
- **Finding the L6 but leaving the person without access or authority**.
- **Borrowing the language of a plan without implementing its ownership model**.

## Key Takeaways

1. Use bounded outbreaks to calibrate the hidden process.
2. Convert deaths and hospital strain into upstream estimates.
3. Separate containment from mitigation and choose based on spread.
4. Give local communities metrics they can understand and act on.
5. Move the buried expert to the decision maker before the window closes.
