# Cheatsheet

## Decision rules

| If you see… | Do… | Because… |
|---|---|---|
| A novel communicable disease plus credible suspicion | Act under the authority already available; do not wait for perfect confirmation | The law and the outbreak both move before certainty |
| Reported cases rising slowly while deaths, travel, hospital strain, or emergency visits disagree | Estimate the unseen cases and widen surveillance | Reported numbers are lagging and can be selectively incomplete |
| One intervention looks like a silver bullet | Build a targeted stack of partially effective layers | Transmission can route around any single barrier |
| A decision feels reversible but delay compounds | Ask which wrong choice creates the greatest regret | Inaction is also a bet |
| A model reveals a surprising leverage point | Use it to decide where to gather real-world data | A model is a flashlight, not the room |
| The official expert says “no evidence” while refusing to look | Test the target directly using a local lab, proxy, or field observation | You cannot find what your definition excludes |
| A breakthrough has been discovered | Trace its approval, supplies, staffing, data, billing, and user | Discovery without the last mile saves nobody |
| A capable person is absent from the meeting | Find the L6 and give them direct access to the decision maker | Hierarchy can hide the person who has the answer |

## Containment or mitigation?

- **Containment**: choose it while transmission is limited and backward tracing, testing, quarantine, or border controls can still extinguish clusters.
- **Mitigation**: choose layered measures when the virus is widespread; reduce transmission, hospital overload, and deaths.
- **Do not confuse them**: declaring containment impossible can excuse failure to try; declaring containment possible after spread can waste time.

## Quick quantitative checks

- Drive effective R below 1 if the goal is to make spread decline.
- Use the herd-immunity relation 1 − 1/R0 only as a conceptual guide; higher R0 means a larger immune share.
- Source-specific pandemic-plan thresholds: Cat 1 was below 90,000 projected U.S. deaths; Cat 4 was 900,000 or more; Cat 5 was above 1.8 million and called for all available measures.
- In the source’s California example, one death at a 0.5% fatality rate implied about 199 other infections; a weekly reproductive rate of 3 made delay enormous.

## Tells and smells

- “Risk is low” while asymptomatic people are not being tested.
- A test result takes longer than the intervention window.
- A supply truck is announced before anyone verifies the inventory.
- A unit offers free help and the recipient cannot enter zero cost in its system.
- The agency that claims ownership keeps the data but does not share it.
- Leaders want to study which intervention worked one at a time while people are being infected.
- A local operator is doing the chief’s job but cannot be named or empowered.
- A successful outcome is used to prove the process was correct.
