# Glossary

**Agent-based model** — A model that represents individual entities, their schedules, and interaction rules to observe system behavior (Prologue).

**Attack rate** — The percentage of a population infected during an outbreak (Ch 5, Ch 9).

**Asymptomatic spreader** — An infected person able to transmit without recognizable symptoms, making detection and isolation harder (Ch 5, Ch 9).

**Case fatality rate** — Deaths divided by detected cases; it can exaggerate apparent lethality when many infections are missed (Ch 5, Ch 7).

**Communicable disease** — An infectious disease that can pass from one person to another; this distinction makes it a public-health crisis (Ch 1, Ch 2).

**Community mitigation** — The CDC’s later name for layered social interventions such as school closure, distancing, and limits on gatherings (Ch 4).

**Containment** — Preventing a pathogen from establishing or expanding transmission; Charity preferred this to merely managing inevitable spread (Ch 9).

**Effective R0** — The reproductive rate at a particular moment after behavior, immunity, or interventions have changed transmission (Ch 4).

**Infection fatality rate** — Deaths divided by all infections, including those not detected; the denominator matters (Ch 5, Ch 9).

**L6** — Park’s term for the capable person buried roughly six layers below an organization’s nominal decision maker (Ch 9).

**Last-mile problem** — The gap between discovering a useful intervention and getting it approved, delivered, and used in time (Ch 6, Ch 10).

**Learning Exchange** — Carter Mecher’s proposed institution for surfacing the important, often taboo learning happening outside formal meetings (Ch 3).

**Mann Gulch / escape fire** — A 1949 wildfire and the counterintuitive fire a leader lit to survive; a model for acting before exponential danger is visible (Ch 7, Ch 8).

**Non-pharmaceutical intervention (NPI)** — A social or behavioral measure used before or without drugs and vaccines, including isolation, distancing, and school closure (Ch 3, Ch 7).

**Red Phone** — Joe DeRisi’s informal channel for urgent, unusual diagnostic requests that reached his lab after ordinary systems failed (Ch 6, Ch 10).

**Redneck epidemiology** — Fast, improvised analysis using public fragments, analogies, and rough calculations to support time-critical decisions (Ch 4, Ch 7).

**Reproductive rate / R0** — The average number of people infected by each infected person at the start of an epidemic; driving it below 1 makes spread decline (Ch 3, Ch 4).

**Social distance** — Hatchett’s term for physically reducing contact in a population, not eliminating all contact (Ch 3, Ch 4).

**Superspreader** — A person or event responsible for a disproportionate share of transmission (Ch 1, Ch 9).

**Swiss-cheese model** — Layered defenses whose holes do not line up, so no single failure reaches the patient or population (Ch 3, Ch 4).

**Targeted Layered Containment (TLC)** — A strategy that combines semi-effective interventions and targets dense transmission networks rather than relying on a silver bullet (Ch 4).

**Virochip** — A glass slide carrying genetic sequences from known viruses, allowing unknown material to be matched to a viral family without a prior hypothesis (Ch 6).

**Wolverines** — The informal network of doctors who shared pandemic analysis and acted outside a formal White House chain of command (Ch 7, Ch 8).
