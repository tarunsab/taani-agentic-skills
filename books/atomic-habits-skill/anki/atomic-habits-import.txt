atomic-habits::four-laws::concept::01	What does the book mean by “Four Laws of Behavior Change” and why does it matter?	<b>Answer</b><br>The Four Laws are four separate levers for building a desired habit: 1) Make it obvious—create a cue that is visible and specific; 2) Make it attractive—connect the behavior to an anticipated benefit or a social norm; 3) Make it easy—reduce the steps and effort required for the response; 4) Make it satisfying—provide immediate feedback that teaches repetition. To weaken an unwanted habit, invert the same levers: make the cue invisible, the outcome unattractive, the response difficult, and the consequence unsatisfying.<br><br><b>Re-teach</b><br>The laws separate visibility, motivation, effort, and repetition so a plan can target the observed bottleneck. The first three laws increase the chance of starting now, while satisfaction supplies feedback that increases the chance of repeating later.<br><br><b>Example</b><br>The book’s central model moves from an obvious cue to an attractive craving, an easy response, and a satisfying reward.<br><br><b>Remember</b><br>For bad habits, invert the same laws: make the cue invisible, outcome unattractive, response difficult, and consequence unsatisfying.<br><br><b>Connections</b><br>Related ideas: habit-loop, environment-design.<br><br><span class="source">Source</span><br>Chapter 3 — How to Build Better Habits in 4 Simple Steps (ch03)	atomic-habits atomic-habits::ch03 atomic-habits::concept::four-laws atomic-habits::type::concept atomic-habits::tier::core
